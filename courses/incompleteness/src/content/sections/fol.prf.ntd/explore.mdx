Build a derivation the way the book develops its examples: write the goal at the bottom, then choose, for a goal, the rule that could have produced it. Its premises become new goals. Close a goal by an assumption — labelled, if an inference below will discharge it. You can also work **top-down**: assume sentences as *pieces*, apply rules to them, and use a finished piece for a goal.

After every change the whole tree goes to the checker. Its messages appear exactly as it states them; <Prov kind="checked" /> appears only when it accepts every inference and every undischarged assumption is in $\Gamma$. Undo walks back through every change — for a loaded book example, back through its construction, goal by goal.
