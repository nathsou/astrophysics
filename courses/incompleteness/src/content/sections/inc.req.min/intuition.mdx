$$
A_f(z, y) \;\equiv\; A_g(y, z, 0) \land \forall w\,(w < y \rightarrow \lnot A_g(w, z, 0))
$$

## Why this needs more lemmas

Clause (a) is the easy half. If $f(n) = m$, then $g(m, n) = 0$, so clause (a) for $g$ gives the first conjunct; and $g(k, n) \neq 0$ for each $k < m$, so clause (b) for $g$ refutes $A_g(\overline k, \overline n, 0)$ for each of them. To pass from "each $\overline k$ with $k < m$" to "every $w < \overline m$", $\mathbf Q$ must know that anything below $\overline m$ is one of $\overline 0, \ldots, \overline{m-1}$ (<Ref k="inc:req:min:lem:less-nsucc" />), or, when $m = 0$, that nothing is below $0$ (<Ref k="inc:req:min:lem:less-zero" />).

Clause (b) has to exclude every other candidate value $b$, including non-numerals: $\mathbf Q$ cannot look at $b$ and compare. <Ref k="inc:req:min:lem:trichotomy" /> does the comparison inside $\mathbf Q$: $b < \overline m$, or $\overline m < b$, or $b = \overline m$. The first contradicts the first conjunct of $A_f(\overline n, b)$ (by the fact just derived for clause (a)); the second, instantiated at $\overline m$, contradicts clause (a) for $g$; only the third is left.

## Induction outside Q

$\mathbf Q$ has no induction axiom, and it cannot prove $\forall x\, \forall y\,(x' + y) = (x + y)'$. The lemmas are proved *by induction on $n$ in the metalanguage*: for each particular $n$ there is a derivation, and the induction says how to build the one for $n + 1$ from the one for $n$. Following that recipe for a particular $n$ gives an actual derivation — and Explore mode does exactly this.
