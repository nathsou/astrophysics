<Added label="Added for this edition">

A derivation is built inductively: an assumption on its own is a derivation, and one, two or three derivations followed by a correct inference form a bigger one. $\Gamma \vdash A$ means some derivation ends in $A$ and all its undischarged assumptions are in $\Gamma$.

</Added>

## The three conditions

The book's definition asks of a finite tree of sentences that (1) its topmost sentences are in $\Gamma$ or discharged by an inference in the tree, (2) its bottom sentence is $A$, and (3) every other sentence is a premise of a correct inference whose conclusion stands directly below it. The checker tests exactly these: (3) inference by inference, (1) by computing, for every step, the undischarged assumptions it depends on — and the builder then compares those at the root with $\Gamma$.

## Discharging nothing

A rule may discharge assumptions; it need not. The book: "we can apply a rule even if the assumptions are not present in the derivation."

<ExampleCheck id="der-vacuous" />

Here $\rightarrow$Intro carries the label $1$ though no assumption $[A]^1$ exists. The checker accepts it: the inference is correct, and it discharges nothing, so $B \vdash A \rightarrow B$.
