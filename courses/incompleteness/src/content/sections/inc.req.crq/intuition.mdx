This short section puts the chapter together. Every computable function is general recursive (the Church–Turing thesis, used here as a definition). Primitive recursion is eliminated with the β-function. What remains is built from representable basic functions by operations that preserve representability. Together with <Ref k="inc:req:rpc:sec" />, this gives <Ref k="inc:req:int:thm:representable-iff-comp" />: the functions representable in $\mathbf Q$ are exactly the computable ones.

</Added>

## An induction on definitions

The proof is an induction, not on numbers but on the way a function is defined. It has to use a basis in which every clause is covered by an earlier section:

| clause | covered by |
|---|---|
| $\mathrm{zero}$, $\mathrm{succ}$, $P^n_i$, $+$, $\times$, $\chi_=$ | <Ref k="inc:req:bre:sec" /> |
| composition | <Ref k="inc:req:cmp:prop:rep-composition" /> |
| regular minimization | <Ref k="inc:req:min:prop:rep-minimization" /> |
| primitive recursion | eliminated: <Ref k="inc:req:pri:lem:prim-rec" /> |

Primitive recursion has no clause of its own. A direct representing formula for $\mathrm{Rec}(f, g)$ would have to say “there is a sequence of values, each obtained from the previous one by $g$”, and the language of arithmetic has no sequences — until the β-function supplies them. The book's route rewrites the definition first, so that only composition and minimization remain; the representing formula is then built from the rewritten definition.

## What it means

Representability is a statement about *every* computable function and about a *very weak* theory. Anything that extends $\mathbf Q$ inherits it: Peano arithmetic, set theory. Conversely, by the argument of <Ref k="inc:req:rpc:sec" />, a consistent extension of $\mathbf Q$ whose derivations can be checked mechanically represents only computable functions. So across all these theories the representable functions are the same — the computable ones — even though what the theories can *prove* differs enormously.
