<Ref k="sol:syn:siz:sec" /> expressed that the *domain* is infinite or countable. This section does the same for a subset $s(X)$, and uses it to express that a set has size $\aleph_0$ or $\aleph_1$. Checking the formulas on small domains turns up a problem with three of them as printed: Inf(X), Count(X) and Aleph₁(X).

</Added>

## Inf(X), as printed

The book’s formula is

$$\exists u\,(\forall x\,\forall y\,(u(x) = u(y) \rightarrow x = y) \land \exists y\,(X(y) \land \forall x\,(X(x) \rightarrow y \neq u(x)))).$$

It asks for an injective $u$ and an element $y$ of $X$ that is not $u(x)$ for any $x \in X$. But it does not ask $u$ to map $X$ **into** $X$, and then finite sets satisfy it. On $\{0, 1, 2\}$ take $X = \{0\}$ and let $u$ swap $0$ and $1$: $u$ is injective, $0 \in X$, and $0 \neq u(0) = 1$. The intended formula adds $\forall x\,(X(x) \rightarrow X(u(x)))$; with it, $u$ restricted to $X$ is an injection of $X$ into itself that misses $y$, which exists iff $X$ is infinite.

## Count(X), as printed

$$\exists z\,\exists u\,(X(z) \land \forall x\,(X(x) \rightarrow X(u(x))) \land \forall Y\,((Y(z) \land \forall x\,(Y(x) \rightarrow Y(u(x)))) \rightarrow X = Y))$$

The last conjunct is meant to say that $X$ is the *smallest* set containing $z$ and closed under $u$. But $Y$ may be the whole domain, which always contains $z$ and is closed under $u$ — so as printed, the formula forces $X = |\mathfrak M|$. Replacing $X = Y$ by $X \subseteq Y$ gives the intended meaning: $s(X)$ is $\{z, u(z), u(u(z)), \ldots\}$, i.e., countable (and non-empty).

## Sizes

With the repaired formulas, $\mathrm{Aleph}_0(X) \equiv \mathrm{Inf}(X) \land \mathrm{Count}(X)$ says that $s(X)$ is countably infinite.

## Aleph₁(X), as printed

The book says that $X$ has size $\aleph_1$ iff all its subsets are finite or of size $\aleph_0$ but $X$ itself is not of size $\aleph_0$, and writes

$$\mathrm{Aleph}_1(X) \equiv \forall Y\,(Y \subseteq X \rightarrow (\lnot\mathrm{Inf}(Y) \lor \mathrm{Aleph}_0(Y))) \land \lnot\mathrm{Aleph}_0(X).$$

But $X$ is one of its own subsets. Taking $Y = X$, the first conjunct makes $X$ finite or of size $\aleph_0$, and the second rules out $\aleph_0$: as printed, $\mathrm{Aleph}_1(X)$ holds exactly when $s(X)$ is finite. (A set of size $\aleph_1$ has uncountable subsets, itself among them.) What is meant is that $X$ is uncountable and each of its subsets is countable or as large as $X$:

$$\mathrm{Aleph}_1(X) \equiv \mathrm{Inf}(X) \land \lnot\mathrm{Count}(X) \land \forall Y\,(Y \subseteq X \rightarrow (\lnot\mathrm{Inf}(Y) \lor \mathrm{Count}(Y) \lor Y \approx X)).$$

(With the axiom of choice, an uncountable set has a subset of size $\aleph_1$; if that subset is as large as $X$, so is $X$.) The same pattern defines $\aleph_2$ and beyond.
