A set of sentences $\Gamma$ is **closed** if it contains everything it entails. The closure of $\Gamma$ is $\{A : \Gamma \vDash A\}$, and a theory is **axiomatized by** $\Delta$ if it is the closure of $\Delta$. The incompleteness theorems are about such theories — especially those, like $\mathbf Q$ and $\mathbf{PA}$, whose axioms can be listed by a computer.

</Added>

## Example: strict linear orders

The theory of strict linear orders in the language with $<$ is axiomatized by three sentences: irreflexivity $\forall x\, \lnot x < x$, connectedness $\forall x\,\forall y\,((x < y \lor y < x) \lor x = y)$, and transitivity. Its models are exactly the strict linear orders: $\mathbb N$, $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb R$ with their usual orders, and every finite linear order.

The theory contains every sentence true in all of them: for instance, asymmetry $\forall x\,\forall y\,(x < y \rightarrow \lnot y < x)$. It does *not* contain “there is a least element” (false in $\mathbb Z$), nor its negation (false in $\mathbb N$): a theory need not be complete. Compare the **theory of a structure** in chapter 7, $\{A : \mathfrak M \vDash A\}$, which is always complete.

## Axioms and theorems

Axiomatizing is a way of describing an infinite set by a small, often finite, one. For $\mathbf Q$ the axioms are eight sentences; for $\mathbf{PA}$ they are infinitely many, but decidable. What makes theories interesting for this book is the gap between the axioms and the closure: the closure is what follows, and the incompleteness theorems show that for arithmetic it can never be all of the truth.

Explore mode looks for small models of an axiom system and small countermodels to a claimed consequence. Every finite linear order is a countermodel to “every element has something above it”, and the search finds the smallest one. But it finds no countermodel to “there is a least element”: every *finite* linear order has one. The countermodel $\mathbb Z$ is infinite — a reminder that a fruitless search of small structures proves nothing.
