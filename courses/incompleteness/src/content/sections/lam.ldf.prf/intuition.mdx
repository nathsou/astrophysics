<Added label="Added for this edition">

The primitive recursive functions are generated from zero, successor and projections by composition and primitive recursion. So it suffices to λ-define the basic functions (easy) and to show that λ-definable functions are closed under the two operations. Composition is substitution of terms into terms; primitive recursion is *iteration on pairs*, the same idea as the predecessor.

</Added>

## The basic functions

$$
\mathrm{Zero} \equiv \lambda a.\lambda f x.\,x,\qquad \mathrm{Succ} \equiv \lambda a.\lambda f x.\,f(a f x),\qquad \mathrm{Proj}^n_i \equiv \lambda x_0\ldots x_{n-1}.\,x_i .
$$

## Composition

If $F$ defines $f$ and $G_0, \ldots, G_{k-1}$ define $g_0, \ldots, g_{k-1}$, then

$$
H \equiv \lambda x_0 \ldots x_{n-1}.\,F\,(G_0\,x_0\ldots x_{n-1})\ldots(G_{k-1}\,x_0\ldots x_{n-1})
$$

defines their composition: substitute the numerals for the $x_i$, let each $G_j$ term reduce to its numeral, and let $F$ finish. (The section's problem asks you to write this out.) The argument uses that the $g_j$ are *total*: <Ref k="lam:ldf:par:sec" /> explains what goes wrong otherwise.

## Primitive recursion

$h(x, 0) = f(x)$ and $h(x, y+1) = g(x, y, h(x, y))$. A numeral $\overline y$ can iterate a step $y$ times, but the step needs to know *which* stage it is at, and the previous value. So iterate on pairs $\langle \text{stage}, \text{value}\rangle$:

$$
D \equiv \lambda p.\,\langle \mathrm{Succ}(\mathrm{Fst}\,p),\ G\,x\,(\mathrm{Fst}\,p)\,(\mathrm{Snd}\,p)\rangle,\qquad H \equiv \lambda x.\lambda y.\,\mathrm{Snd}(y\,D\,\langle\overline 0, F x\rangle).
$$

Starting from $\langle \overline 0, \overline{f(x)}\rangle$, each application of $D$ moves from $\langle \overline m, \overline{h(x, m)}\rangle$ to $\langle \overline{m+1}, \overline{h(x, m+1)}\rangle$ — this is the induction in the proof of <Ref k="lam:ldf:prf:lem:prim" /> — and after $y$ steps $\mathrm{Snd}$ reads off $h(x, y)$.

Explore mode builds $H$ with the engine for addition, multiplication, exponentiation and truncated subtraction, and runs it on a few inputs. Each row is an instance, computed by normal order; the lemma is the proof that it always works.
