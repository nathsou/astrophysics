$\forall$Elim and $\exists$Intro are unrestricted: any closed term will do. $\forall$Intro and $\exists$Elim reason about an *arbitrary* object, named by a constant $a$ — the **eigenvariable** — and they are only correct if $a$ really is arbitrary: it must not occur in the conclusion or in any undischarged assumption.

</Added>

## Why constants, and why closed terms

Derivations in this book contain sentences only. So the instance $A(t)$ in $\forall$Elim must be a sentence, which is why $t$ is a *closed* term; and $\forall$Intro generalises on a *constant* $a$, not a free variable. $A(t)$ is short for $A(x)[t/x]$. In $\exists$Intro the premise $A(t)$ may contain $t$ in places that stay in the conclusion: from $P(a, a)$ one may infer $\exists x\, P(a, x)$, replacing only some occurrences of $a$.
