This table is a reading guide: for each stage of Gödel’s paper, where the same idea appears in this book. The mapping follows the book’s own summary in this section.

## Differences worth noticing

- Gödel’s system is higher-order and has more axioms than needed; the book shows that the weak theory $\mathbf Q$ already represents every computable function, so every consistent computably axiomatized extension of $\mathbf Q$ is incomplete.
- Gödel does not use a general fixed-point lemma; he constructs his sentence directly. The lemma in the form used here was isolated later (it is often credited to Carnap).
- Gödel promised the details of §4 in a sequel that never appeared; this book, too, takes the derivability conditions on faith (<Ref k="inc:inp:prc:sec" />).
