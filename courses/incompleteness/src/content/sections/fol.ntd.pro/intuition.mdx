<Added label="Added for this edition">

The book develops its derivations **backwards**: write the goal at the bottom, look at its main connective, and ask which rule could have produced it. The premises of that rule are the new goals. When the goal's shape suggests nothing, look at the assumptions and use an elimination rule; and when nothing else works, use $\bot_C$.

</Added>

## Strategy, in the book's order

1. **Introduce the main connective.** For $(A \land B) \rightarrow A$ the main connective is $\rightarrow$, so the last inference is $\rightarrow$Intro, discharging $[A \land B]^1$.
2. **Use the assumptions.** For $(\lnot A \lor B) \rightarrow (A \rightarrow B)$, after $\rightarrow$Intro the assumption $\lnot A \lor B$ is a disjunction, so reason by cases ($\lor$Elim): derive $A \rightarrow B$ from $\lnot A$ and from $B$.
3. **Remember that discharging is optional.** In the case $B$, the derivation of $B$ from $B$ is just $B$ itself; the assumption $A$ need not be used.
4. **$\bot_C$ as a last resort.** $A \lor \lnot A$ cannot be obtained by $\lor$Intro — neither $A$ nor $\lnot A$ is derivable on its own. Instead, assume $\lnot(A \lor \lnot A)$ and derive $\bot$.

Formal mode hands each of the book's finished trees to the checker. In the finished tree of the second example the rightmost $\rightarrow$Intro has **no label**: it discharges nothing, so there is nothing for a label to point to. The rules allow this — discharging is "a permission, but not a requirement", and $\rightarrow$Intro may discharge "any number of assumptions … including zero" (<Ref k="fol:ntd:prl:sec" />).
