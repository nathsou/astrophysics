# Course plan: *The Orphan Species* (draft for review)

**Working title:** *The Orphan Species: how* Homo sapiens *came to be the only human*
**Working slug:** `orphan-species` (published at `/orphan-species/`)
**Source:** Jean-Jacques Hublin, *Homo sapiens : l'espèce orpheline*, Collège de France, chaire internationale de Paléoanthropologie, 4 October to 13 December 2016. It has nine lectures, and each was followed the same evening by a guest seminar: 18 sessions, about 18 hours in all.
**Status:** vision and plan, nothing built yet. The decisions marked *proposed* are waiting for the owner's approval.

---

## 1. The vision in one paragraph

For seven million years there were usually several kinds of human alive at once. Today there is one. Hublin's course explains how that came about. A large-brained African population became *Homo sapiens* across the whole continent. It slipped out of Africa several times. It met, mixed with and replaced Neanderthals, Denisovans and the last *erectus*-like islanders. It crossed open sea to Australia and reached the Americas through Beringia. Along the way it remade the planet's fauna, vegetation and atmosphere.

The course retells that story as a **detective case with three witnesses**: bones, stones and genes. It adds a **clock** (dating) and a **stage** (climate, sea level and ice). Each step is taken in the learner's hands with real data and honest simulations. It follows Hublin's nine evenings in order, gives his guest speakers their own chapters, and has one thing no lecture series can have: **ten years of hindsight**. Hublin made specific calls in 2016. He gave Jebel Irhoud an age of about 300 ka eight months before the *Nature* papers. He predicted that the first modern wave into Europe "failed". He was sceptical of the early Chinese teeth, of the corridor route into the Americas, and of a 1950 boundary for the Anthropocene. The learner gets to see which of these calls held up by 2026. The result is a course about human origins and, just as much, about **how a science corrects itself**.

## 4. Through-lines

1. **The Atlas.** One space–time map runs through the whole course, from 320 ka to today, with layers that each chapter switches on. The layers are:
   - sea level and the coastline it implies;
   - ice sheets;
   - Green Sahara and Arabian humid phases;
   - sites, which appear at their dates, carry error bars and are coloured by which witness they support;
   - hominin populations (Neanderthal, Denisovan, *sapiens*, island hominins);
   - routes.

   The Atlas is both the flagship of several chapters and a standalone page (`#/atlas`), like Particle Physics' Control Room. Every site name in the text links to it. A prototype from real NOAA bathymetry is in `docs/prototypes/paleocoast-test.png`: Sundaland and Sahul with the open-water gap of Wallacea, Beringia, Doggerland and a dry Persian Gulf, all at −120 m.

2. **Three witnesses, one clock, one stage ("How do we know?").** Every claim carries a badge for the evidence behind it:
   - 🦴 bones (morphology)
   - 🪨 stones (archaeology and behaviour)
   - 🧬 genes (living or ancient DNA)
   - ⏳ clock (the dating method, and whether it dates the layer or the fossil, and gives a minimum or a maximum)
   - 🌍 stage (environment)

   Each chapter has at least one *How do we know?* exercise in which the learner weighs the evidence.

3. **The Scorecard: 2016 → 2026.** A running ledger of the specific calls in the lectures. Before each reveal the learner bets on the outcome (*held / revised / overturned / still open*). Sample entries:

   | Hublin, 2016 | 2026 status |
   |---|---|
   | The first *sapiens* faces date to about 300 ka at Jebel Irhoud (L2, before publication) | **Held**: 315 ± 34 ka (Richter et al. 2017; Hublin et al. 2017) |
   | The later large-faced Africans (Kabwe) are poor candidates for our ancestors (L2) | **Strengthened**: Kabwe redated to 299 ± 25 ka (Grün et al. 2020), contemporary with Irhoud |
   | The first modern wave into Europe (Initial Upper Palaeolithic) failed and left almost no descendants (L5) | **Held**, and confirmed by his own Bacho Kiro excavation (2020–21), Zlatý kůň (2021) and Ranis (2024–25) |
   | The LRJ industry is probably Neanderthal (L5, tentative) | **Overturned**: made by *H. sapiens* at Ranis (Mylopotamitaki et al. 2024) |
   | The Daoxian "modern" teeth at 80–120 ka are suspect (L4) | **Held**: shown to be much younger (Sun et al. 2021) |
   | The Iberomaurusians of North Africa crossed from Sicily (L3, speculative) | **Overturned**: Taforalt genomes show Near Eastern plus sub-Saharan ancestry (van de Loosdrecht et al. 2018) |
   | Neanderthal ancestry kept declining for 40,000 years (L1, L6) | **Revised**: most of the purging happened in the first generations (Petr et al. 2019; Iasi et al. 2024) |
   | People reached the Americas before Clovis, "but not as early as some would like"; entry by the coast at about 15 ka (L8) | **Open, under strain**: White Sands footprints at 23–21 ka (Bennett et al. 2021; Pigati et al. 2023) |
   | Arrival in Australia "nearer 50 ka than 40" (L7) | **Open**: Madjedbebe at about 65 ka (2017) versus admixture dating at about 47 ka (2024) |
   | Many Chinese fossils are probably Denisovans (L5) | **Strongly supported**: the Harbin cranium identified as Denisovan by proteins and mtDNA (2025) |
   | A 1950 boundary for the Anthropocene is arbitrary (L9) | **Vindicated, in a way**: the Anthropocene was rejected as an epoch (SQS/IUGS, March 2024) |

   The full Scorecard is an appendix. The reader's own bets are saved, and the epilogue shows how their calibration compares with the field's.

4. **The orphan question.** Each part ends by adding evidence to one ledger of explanations for why *sapiens* is alone:
   - climate and catastrophes;
   - competition;
   - absorption through interbreeding;
   - demography (small, inbred archaic populations);
   - networks and culture;
   - disease;
   - predation and niche construction.

   The epilogue weighs them. The answer is multi-causal, and Hublin's own later framing ("an invasive species", his 2022 course) gets a closing look.

5. **Predict, then reveal**, placed at the historical moments where intuition fails:
   - Qafzeh is older than the Neanderthals of the same region;
   - you cannot walk across Bab el-Mandeb at the LGM;
   - Oase had a Neanderthal ancestor 4–6 generations back;
   - Kennewick's genome is Native American;
   - Africa lost the *fewest* large animals;
   - "35,000 BP" means about 40,000 calendar years.

6. **Ideas have politics.** A thread runs from Linnaeus, Voltaire and Coon through Weidenreich's exile and the Kennewick and Mungo repatriations. It shows how origin stories were bent by religion, racism and colonialism, and how they were corrected. The tone is factual, not preachy.

## 5. Curriculum

The prologue is followed by nine parts, one per evening at the Collège de France. Each part has two chapters built on the lecture and one guest-lecture chapter, reordered where prerequisites require. That makes 27 chapters plus a prologue and an epilogue, of roughly 3,000–5,000 words each and about 110k words in total. Particle Physics has about 227k words. Each chapter has named sessions with goals and a natural stopping point (root `CONTRIBUTING.md`). ★ marks the flagship interactive.

### Prologue
**0. The last human standing.** Hook: a timeline of species diversity, showing how many kinds of human were alive at each moment (★ *Hominin census*). It drops to one. The prologue introduces the five badges, the Atlas and the Scorecard, and says who Hublin is and what the 2016 course was.

### Part I: Models (L1, S1)
1. **Adam, the hat-rack and the candelabra.** Linnaeus, monogenism and polygenism, Haeckel's Lemuria, Weidenreich's trellis, Coon, Wolpoff and Brace. ★ *Model morph*: one diagram that morphs between the models, with fossils dropped in by date; the Qafzeh TL date breaks the "Neanderthal phase".
2. **Eve, and what she isn't.** mtDNA; Cann, Stoneking and Wilson 1987; rooting the tree yourself. ★ *Coalescent playground*: "Eve" was never alone, and the date of the most recent common ancestor is not a date of origin. The serial founder effect, with real HGDP heterozygosity plotted against distance from Africa. Hublin's continuity–replacement slider ("> 95 %").
3. **Guest lecture: Quintana-Murci, history written in our genomes.** Diversity, structure, the Pygmy and farmer split. Ordering exercise: did farming cause the Bantu expansion? (No: the arrow is reversed.) ★ *Drift vs selection* (malaria, lactase); hunting the EPAS1 outlier in Tibetan versus Han Chinese frequencies; Neanderthal DNA at immune genes.

### Part II: Archaic Africans (L2, S2)
4. **What is a *Homo sapiens*?** Biological and phenotypic species; there is no type specimen; Day & Stringer's checklist tested against living people (some fail); primitive and derived traits. The surprise: the modern face is primitive.
5. **Shape, size and the face.** ★ *Morpho lab*: Procrustes superimposition hands-on, shape space versus form space, "can you shrink Kabwe into a modern face?", and growing the Gran Dolina child to adulthood. Ends with the Jebel Irhoud reveal (predict its age).
6. **Guest lecture: Gunz, the round head.** Endocasts, the globularisation phase, Neanderthal newborns, the obstetric dilemma. ★ *Mosaic timeline*: face, then teeth, then braincase, in both lineages.

### Part III: The first moderns (L3, S3)
7. **No moment of creation.** Omo 1 and 2, Herto, Singa, LH 18, Klasies: all mosaics. ★ *Neutral-drift clock* (after Weaver 2012). Structure versus bottleneck: a metapopulation that produces a false dip in Ne. Toba, and why it is no longer blamed.
8. **A continent-sized network.** ★ Atlas: the Green Sahara slider with the *Nassarius* bead network from the Cape to Morocco and the Levant. The Aterian. The streetlight effect (where the digging has been done). Relic populations. African "multiregionalism", which the Scorecard confirms (Scerri 2018; Ragsdale 2023), and the Sicily hypothesis, which it refutes.
9. **Guest lecture: Parkington, symptoms of modernity.** Pinnacle Point middens, heat-treated silcrete, ochre adhesives. ★ *Railway-line decoder* for the Diepkloof eggshell engravings. A symptom timeline comparing 2016 and 2026 (Border Cave bedding at about 200 ka). "Eden was a beach."

### Part IV: Out of Africa (L4, S4)
10. **When Arabia was Africa.** ★ Atlas: Bab el-Mandeb at a high-resolution sea level (predict "walkable?"), with humid windows from the sapropels and speleothems. Skhul and Qafzeh, the Mousterian puzzle, Tabun C2, and the first gene flow from moderns into Neanderthals. Since 2016: Misliya, Al Wusta, Alathar. A dispersal model driven by climate alone versus one that includes competition, testing Timmermann's "Europe by 80 ka".
11. **Claims in the East.** ★ *Fossil-claim tribunal*: Jwalapuram, Liujiang, Zhirendong, Daoxian and Tam Pa Ling scored on context, what was dated, method and replication, followed by the 2026 verdicts. The three 2016 genome papers. A ghost-lineage simulator: an early wave that vanishes from living mtDNA.
12. **Guest lecture: Marks, the Arabian crossroads.** ★ *Knap-along*: Nubian Levallois versus classic Levallois versus the Emiran. Jebel Faya. A surface-site patina puzzle. Where Marks and Hublin disagree (climate as a pull or as a barrier).

### Part V: The replacement (S5 first, then L5)
13. **Guest lecture: Meyer, reading ancient DNA.** How bone preserves DNA. ★ *Deamination detective* (C→T damage at fragment ends filters out contamination). Coverage and error rates. An ABBA–BABA counter. The Denisova finger. Sima de los Huesos: mtDNA against nuclear DNA ("one page torn from a book").
14. **A leaky replacement.** ★ *Recombination clock*: chop Neanderthal blocks generation by generation and recover the "4–6 generations" for Oase. Selection against introgressed DNA; deserts on the X chromosome and in testis-expressed genes; a short list of adaptive introgressions.
15. **Waves that failed.** Climate in MIS 3 (Greenland Interstadials, Heinrich events). ★ Atlas: the waves from 55 to 38 ka (the Initial Upper Palaeolithic from Boker Tachtit to the Bohunician, then the Proto-Aurignacian and the Aurignacian). A dating-integrity puzzle at the Grotte du Renne. "Who made it?" attribution cards, 2016 versus 2026 (Ranis flips the LRJ). Stimulus diffusion. A test of whether the catastrophes are guilty (the Campanian Ignimbrite and Heinrich 4 against site dates).

### Part VI: Europe (L6, S6)
16. **The radiocarbon clock.** ★ *Radiocarbon lab*: decay, BP versus cal BP with IntCal20, a contamination slider. "Redated!" cards for Vogelherd, Combe-Capelle, Mezzena and Cro-Magnon.
17. **Cultures are not peoples.** The techno-complexes; art boundaries versus stone-tool boundaries. ★ *Cultures vs clusters*: sort about 30 dated genomes by culture, then by genetic cluster (Fu 2016; Posth 2023). The LGM refugia on the Atlas. Phenotypic inertia: body proportions and a timeline of skin-colour alleles.
18. **Guest lecture: Svoboda, mammoth hunters.** ★ *Recount the fauna*. Gully hunting. Matching each "Neolithic" invention to its Gravettian precursor. A test of "hunting magic" (the animals in the art against the animals eaten).

### Part VII: Australia (L7, S7)
19. **Crossing to Sahul.** Gondwana and endemism. ★ Atlas: Wallacea at any sea level, with intervisibility (can you see the next island from a hilltop?). Founding population size. A timeline of arrival estimates, where the learner places their own guess first. The Liang Bua stratigraphy puzzle. Sulawesi art.
20. **Bones of contention.** Multiregionalism in Australia (Weidenreich's gouache, Thorne, Kow Swamp). ★ *Cranial-deformation morpher*: binding the skull mimics only one *erectus* trait and inverts the others. Coobool Creek. Denisovan ancestry. The Lapita epilogue.
21. **Guest lecture: Fitzsimmons, Lake Mungo.** ★ *OSL battery* (luminescence explained correctly). A sediment detective. Flooding the lunette to make the mega-lake island. Why fishing peaked while the lake was drying.

### Part VIII: The Americas (S8 first, then L8)
22. **Guest lecture: Zwyns, the northern road.** Neanderthals in the Altai, Denisovans, the Initial Upper Palaeolithic at Tolbor. ★ *Least-cost path explorer*. Site dates against Greenland interstadials (GI-12 and GI-8). Yana. Sopochnaya Karga.
23. **Beringia and the ice.** ★ Atlas: from 30 to 8 ka, with the land bridge, the Laurentide and Cordilleran ice, the coast, and the corridor's physical opening versus the date it became habitable. The menu revealed by sediment DNA from the corridor. Clovis. A pre-Clovis tribunal. Geofact, human or capuchin? A calibration trap. The White Sands reveal.
24. **Genes of the first Americans.** Mal'ta and the Ancient North Eurasians, Anzick-1. Kennewick: predict the skull's answer, then the genome's. ★ *Build the admixture graph*. The Beringian standstill. Later waves. The Solutrean myth. The ethics of NAGPRA.

### Part IX: The dawn of the Anthropocene (L9, S9)
25. **The peculiar predator.** A mortality ternary plot (prime-age adults). The East African carnivore guild. ★ *Overkill simulator*: harvest rate, age targeting and life history. Body-size extinction histograms. Extinction plotted against arrival date (Sandom; Bartlett). Africa spared through co-evolution.
26. **Fire, methane and the golden spike.** A core detective at Lynch's Crater. ★ *Ruddiman's test*: Holocene CH₄ against earlier interglacials, plus the gradient between the poles. A biomass waffle chart. Pick the golden spike, then the 2024 vote is revealed.
27. **Guest lecture: Geneste, living inside the animal.** ★ *Menu vs bestiary* at Chauvet. Where to paint the bear. The chaîne opératoire of ivory working. A palimpsest peeler. Art "made not to be seen".

### Epilogue
28. **Why are we alone?** Weighing the orphan-question ledger, the reader's Scorecard calibration, and a capstone: judge a *fictional* fossil press release using the course's rubric.

## 6. Chapter template

Hook (a fossil, an object, a site or a historical moment) → 🔮 predict → explore (the flagship) → explain → 🧭 *How do we know?* (an evidence exercise, with badges) → 🎙 *In the lecture* (what Hublin argued, with a link to the recording at the right minute) → 📅 *Since 2016* (Scorecard entries) → ⚖️ *Debate* (both sides, named) → what comes next → further reading.

Standards per chapter:
- one flagship and 2–4 smaller figures;
- at least one predict question, one *How do we know?* exercise, one history card and one Scorecard entry where the lecture made a call;
- every number and date cited;
- every date given with its method and, where it matters, its error and whether it is a minimum or a maximum age;
- sessions of about 15–25 minutes with goals in the front matter.

New exercise kinds, added to Particle Physics' quiz, predict and numeric:
- `place`: put a site on the map or a date on the timeline;
- `order`: put events in sequence;
- `sort`: assign items to classes;
- `tribunal`: score a claim against a rubric, then see the verdict;
- `estimate`: a number with a tolerance, such as generations since admixture.

## 7. The science library (`src/lib/sapiens`)

Pure TypeScript with no DOM, so it runs in workers and Vitest. All randomness is seeded and every module has tests.

| `chronology` | Deep-time scale (ka, log or linear), MIS bands, conversions between BP and cal BP | Everywhere |

| Genetic summaries | Published values (HGDP heterozygosity, SGDP archaic proportions, a simplified PhyloTree, Fu 2016 / Posth 2023 supplementary tables, AADR allele frequencies summarised) | Per paper (licences checked) |
| Extinctions | Faith & Surovell 2009; Sandom et al. 2014; Bartlett et al. 2016 supplementary tables; PHYLACINE body masses | Open supplementary data |
| Australia | AustArch radiocarbon database (Archaeology Data Service) | Open |
| Carnivore guild | Werdelin & Lewis 2013 (*PLoS ONE*) | CC BY |

**Caveat:** paleo-coastlines from present-day bathymetry ignore glacio-isostatic adjustment and sedimentation. That is fine at continental scale and wrong for narrow straits. The prototype shows the problem: at 4′ resolution Bab el-Mandeb looks closed at −120 m, whereas regional reconstructions leave water in the strait. The course says this openly and uses published regional reconstructions for the straits that matter.

## 9. Look and feel: "Ochre and charcoal"

- **Dark theme:** a cave wall by lamplight. Charcoal background, ochre and red highlights, a warm amber glow on hover.

## 10. Accuracy

- Every claim is cited. There are no invented references or quotations. When unsure, say less, or compute it in a test (house rule).
- Three tenses are always kept apart: *what Hublin said in 2016*, *what the field says in 2026*, and *what is still disputed*.
- **Known errors in the talks**, which the course corrects rather than repeats. The digests list them all; examples:
  - Haeckel's map is from 1868, not 1937.
  - Hublin's slip "the last interglacial at 18–20 ka" means the LGM.
  - The figure for modern DNA in the Altai Neanderthal (0.1–2.1 % as spoken; check against Kuhlwilm et al. 2016).
  - The age of Mungo 1 (about 25 ka in both talks against about 40 ka in Bowler et al. 2003).
  - The Austronesian start date (about 3,000 years ago as spoken; it is older).
  - Fitzsimmons's loose description of how OSL works.
  - Gunz's "Neanderthals ate no fish".
  - Marks's "Manot 1 is a clear hybrid".
  - Geneste's African shell beads at "> 200 ka".
  - The 3 km of ice over Montreal.
  - The Hohle Fels rope tool has four holes, not three.
- **Fact-check pass:** each chapter is reviewed against primary sources by a separate agent before it is marked done, and the claims each author is least sure of are listed in the chapter's README.

## 11. Sensitivity and ethics

- **Human remains of Aboriginal people and Native Americans** (Mungo, Kow Swamp, Kennewick, Anzick): no photographs. Drawings only where scientifically necessary, plus a respectful note on repatriation and the communities' wishes. Use current terminology (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, First Peoples).
- **The history of racial science** (Linnaeus's varieties, Voltaire, Coon): presented as history and analysed, never reproduced uncritically. The course's science says it plainly: variation among living humans is recent, mostly found within populations, and largely "cosmetic" (Hublin's word).
- **Living foragers** (San, Hadza, Mbuti, Aka): never "living fossils". This is one of Hublin's own points: they are displaced relic populations.
