## 1. Thesis

This is the opening lecture, and it is mostly about the history of ideas. Hublin argues that the scientific debate over where *Homo sapiens* came from is an old ideological quarrel under new labels. The 18th-century fight between **monogenism** (one origin, tied to the Bible) and **polygenism** (separate origins, often anticlerical and openly racist, e.g. Voltaire) carried on into 20th-century palaeoanthropology. It surfaced as the "hat-rack" (linear succession) versus "candelabra" (deep separate regional lineages) models. Weidenreich's polycentric trellis, Coon's racialised version and Wolpoff's **multiregional** "single species" school dominated from the Second World War until the 1980s. Several lines of evidence then converged on a **recent African origin**: Howells' worldwide craniometrics (1973), the Qafzeh moderns turning out older than Near Eastern and European Neanderthals, Bräuer's "Afro-European" hypothesis (1978–84), Cann, Stoneking and Wilson's mtDNA tree (1987), Stringer and Andrews (1988), and the fall in diversity with distance from Africa. Ancient-DNA evidence of Neanderthal and Denisovan introgression (from 2010) was taken up by multiregionalists such as John Hawks and by the "assimilation" model as a vindication. Hublin calls this an over-interpretation. Archaic ancestry is only about 1–2 % and was selected against, so the picture is still essentially **replacement**: on his own sliding scale between total continuity and total replacement, "more than 95 %" replacement. Today's human differences are "cosmetic" local adaptations (skin colour, body proportions, immunity). He also observes that the winning model came wrapped in biblical language ("African Eve", "Garden of Eden").

## 2. Timestamped outline (lecture)

**[0:00] Introduction: an orphan species**
- The series runs for 9 weeks rather than the usual 6. Today gives the historical, ideological and philosophical background.
- He shows the familiar "bushy tree" of hominin species over the last 7 Myr. These are palaeontological species, many of them probably interfertile. Through most of the past several hominin forms lived at the same time, sometimes in the same region. *H. sapiens* being alone is exceptional.
- Precedent: *H. erectus*, around 1.2 Ma, was nearly the only hominin. It was the first to leave Africa, a little more than 1.8 Ma, into the mid-latitudes of Eurasia.
- *H. sapiens* reached every habitable place, plus the Arctic, remote islands and the Moon. He predicts other planets "in a not so distant future".
- Biomass claim: humans plus domestic animals make up "more than 95 %, maybe 97 %" of the vertebrate biomass on land and in the air. (This holds for mammals; "vertebrates" overstates it; see the table.)
- He argues the expansion was cultural and behavioural rather than biological. Neanderthals were well adapted and persisted for hundreds of thousands of years. Modern humans "squeeze the lemon" ("presser le citron"), intensifying their exploitation of resources.
- He shows a slide of Arctic hunter-gatherer equipment and clothing. Social networks matter most and leave the fewest traces: "we don't know a Neanderthal's relations with his in-laws". Large solidarity networks built on shared language and beliefs are, in his view, a key reason for sapiens' success.
- He previews a recurring problem: "anatomically modern human" and "*Homo sapiens*" are merged by some researchers and kept apart by others.

**[0:10] Linnaeus, monogenism and polygenism**
- Linnaeus, Swedish, 18th century, binomial nomenclature. He created *Homo sapiens* with varieties (in the 1758 10th edition: Americanus, Europaeus, Asiaticus, Afer, plus ferus and monstrosus), as well as *Homo troglodytes*, a "wild cave man".
- The biblical constraint was a single creation only a little over 6,000 years ago. How could all human variety come from Adam and Eve so quickly? A Cranach the Elder Adam-and-Eve painting is referred back to later, so it was presumably shown here.
- Proposed answers: pre-Adamites; degeneration of non-European groups (the most popular); an Irish scholar who suggested Eve's sin with the devil (identity unclear; verify).
- **Polygenism** held that human groups had separate origins, even from different primate species. It was fashionable with Voltaire, whom Hublin calls "rather racist", and tinged with anti-Christianity. He quotes Voltaire predicting the Bible would vanish within 100 years (probably apocryphal).
- The debate ran into the 19th century. In the 1930s some authors were still publishing trees with human groups rooted in different apes.
- **Haeckel's map** shows a "monophyletic" human origin from a centre in the Indian Ocean ("Lemuria"). The transcript says 1937, which is wrong: it comes from *Natürliche Schöpfungsgeschichte*, 1868, with the map in later editions. That idea sent Eugène Dubois to Java ("the miracle is that he really found something"). Haeckel's monophyly was shallow: the root was a very primitive form that existed before language.

**[0:20] Fossils enter the debate: hat-rack versus candelabra**
- Gibraltar (Forbes' Quarry) skull, found in 1848. Neanderthal child remains from Belgium (Engis, around 1829–30). Recognition of the Feldhofer Neanderthal, 1856. It took a generation for an extinct, different human form to be accepted.
- The monogenism/polygenism debate came back in a "more reasonable" form: no longer separate ape ancestors, but early versus late divergence of modern groups.
- **"Porte-chapeau" (hat-rack) model**: a linear succession, the "march of progress" parade. Neanderthals are either ancestors or a dead-end side peg. Popular until the second half of the 20th century.
- **Candelabra model**, shown from a popular book: separate regional lineages leading to Africans, San, Europeans and so on, all joined at a Pliocene root (>2.5 Ma). It is polygenism rescaled.
- **Franz Weidenreich.** He trained in Strasbourg (German until 1918, hence "he could have been a great French palaeoanthropologist"). He was Jewish. His 1931 article in *Der Morgen* showed that blood-group frequencies varied by region, not between Jews and non-Jews, which made him unpopular with the Nazis. He emigrated to Chicago and then took over Zhoukoudian ("Sinanthropus pekinensis", later *H. erectus*) after Davidson Black died young (1934). He also studied Javanese *H. erectus*.
- Weidenreich's **four-centre model** rests on perceived regional continuity, e.g. Java *erectus* to Aboriginal Australians and Chinese *erectus* to modern Chinese. Hublin points out that the trellis arrows of gene flow make it more than a candelabra. It fits the "phases" idea: archanthropian, palaeoanthropian, neanthropian.

**[0:30] Polycentrism, multiregionalism and the turn**
- **Hrdlička** proposed a "Neanderthal phase" of humanity. Hublin says "of Hungarian origin"; he was in fact born in Bohemia. Weidenreich carefully left Neanderthal off his diagram.
- Polycentrism dominated from after WWII until about the 1980s.
- **Carleton Coon**, *The Origin of Races* (1962), Philadelphia. He removed most of Weidenreich's horizontal arrows, made races separate and deep, and set them in a hierarchy. Hublin shows a quote and calls him "carrément" racist.
- **Multiregionalism**, the "Michigan school": C. Loring Brace and Milford Wolpoff at Ann Arbor. They hold the **single-species hypothesis**: since the human niche is cultural, two human species cannot coexist, so there has been one species for about 2 Myr. This avoids the biological oddity of one species arising several times in parallel. Wolpoff and Rachel Caspari wrote a book (*Race and Human Evolution*, 1997) to rebut charges of racism. Hublin notes the shift from religious and anticlerical conflict to mutual accusations of racism.
- **W. W. Howells (Harvard), 1973**, *Cranial Variation in Man*: the first large multivariate craniometric survey. Recent humans "look damned alike" ("se ressemblent fichtrement") worldwide, and their small differences cannot be tied to regional fossils. Howells therefore supported a single origin.
- **Levant**: a map of Near Eastern Neanderthal sites in red. Skhul (1930s) yielded modern-looking remains, at first read as local Neanderthal descendants. **Qafzeh** near Nazareth was excavated by **Bernard Vandermeersch** from the 1960s–70s: abundant robust moderns in spectacular burials, with Mousterian tools. Dating showed it was older than Neanderthal sites in Europe and the region. This undermined both the hat-rack and the phase models: "un sacré coup de pied dans la fourmilière".
- **Günter Bräuer**, late 1970s and early 1980s.

**[0:40] Africa and genetics**
- Some had argued for a European origin of modern Europeans. Bräuer proposed instead an African origin with Neanderthals replaced: the **Afro-European sapiens hypothesis**. Hublin shows his 1978 paper. Its impact was limited because he wrote mostly in German.
- **Cann, Stoneking and Wilson, 1987 (Berkeley)**. Hublin explains mtDNA: a captured "battery" with its own short genome, maternally inherited, non-recombining and abundant. All non-African mtDNA forms a sub-branch of African diversity, and the first split separates Africans from everyone else. Their sampling was criticised, but every later study confirmed the result.
- He shows a world mtDNA phylogeny: the African root lineages (L) highlighted in green, and **L3** as the source of all non-African lineages.
- "Last week" three *Nature* papers with dozens of authors each hammered home the same result. These are the Sept 2016 papers: Mallick et al. (SGDP), Pagani et al., Malaspinas et al. They disagree only on secondary matters such as chronology and the route to Australia.
- **Diversity declines with distance from Africa**, in both genetic and phenotypic data (cranium, pelvis). This is the expected signature of serial pioneer colonisation with bottlenecks, drift and local selection.
- Conclusion: all humans have a recent African origin, and non-Africans are descendants of Africans. The differences Linnaeus and Voltaire made much of are "cosmetic": skin colour, body proportions, immune defences. He shows a Benetton advert in which make-up turns one model into four "races".

**[0:50] Synthesis, biblical echoes, introgression and the scale of models**
- Summary map: moderns originate in Africa at the end of the Middle Pleistocene, Neanderthals are in western Eurasia, Denisovans in the east. Dispersal starts at least 100 ka and ends recently with remote islands, largely replacing local populations.
- After Cann et al. and **Stringer and Andrews (1988)** came the "African Eve" and "Garden of Eden" vocabulary, and maps hunting for Eden in southern or eastern Africa. "In a way the anticlericals lost."
- From 2010, palaeogenetics by his MPI-EVA colleagues showed some Neanderthal DNA in modern genomes, and since then many introgressions in both directions.
- Multiregionalists seized on this. Hawks' blog shows images of a braided stream (*"tout se mélange avec tout"*, so trees are pointless). The **assimilation model** accepts an African origin but with substantial local mixing.
- Hublin's rebuttal: introgression is about 1–2 %, sometimes much less. It was strongly selected against and "has not stopped decreasing for 40,000 years". So it is still replacement plus a little leakage.
- **His continuity-replacement scale**: 100 % continuity (candelabra) at one end, total replacement at the other, with multiregional and assimilation in between. He places reality near the replacement end: RAO supporters "won at more than 95 %".
- Closing image: a South African artist ("Karine Muller"; identity unverified) revisits Cranach's Adam and Eve.

## 4. Hublin's positions, debates and rhetoric

- **He sides firmly with recent African origin and replacement.** He rejects the reading of introgression as reviving multiregionalism, naming John Hawks (a Wolpoff student) and the assimilation model (associated with Fred Smith; not named in the lecture). His argument: the archaic fraction is small (1–2 %), selected against and declining. Counter-arguments a learner should weigh:
  - Even small percentages can matter adaptively (immune genes, EPAS1; see the seminar).
  - The multiregionalists' core claim was gene flow, not lineage continuity, so they can claim partial vindication.
  - Since 2016 the evidence for decline over time has weakened.
- **Choice of terms**: "multirégionalisme" is reserved for Wolpoff, "polycentrisme" for Weidenreich and Coon. He credits Weidenreich with seriousness, since the gene-flow arrows mean it is not a pure candelabra, and keeps the charge of outright racism for Coon.
- **Ideology runs through the science.** His recurring move is to show that each scientific position carries a religious or political charge:
  - monogenism goes with the Bible;
  - polygenism goes with anticlericalism and racism (Voltaire);
  - later the camps traded accusations of racism.
  - His punchline: the RAO model brought back biblical iconography (Eve, Eden), so "the anticlericals lost".
- **He pushes back on the heroic view of the Enlightenment.** His aside "Voltaire... assez raciste" punctures the progressive image of Enlightenment polygenism.
- **Neglected pioneer.** Bräuer was ignored because he wrote in German. This is a gentle point about the dominance of English in science.
- **Weidenreich's life story**: a Strasbourg-trained, would-be French anatomist; a Jewish scientist whose serology undercut Nazi race ideology; an exile who ended up at Zhoukoudian.
- **Analogies**:
  - porte-chapeau (hat-rack);
  - candélabre (candelabra);
  - Hawks' braided river;
  - Benetton make-up (variation is skin-deep);
  - "presser le citron" (exploitation intensification);
  - "la belle-famille" of a Neanderthal (social life matters more than scrapers).
- **Personal stance**: he says he knows several of the protagonists, hopes "not to betray their thought", and calls Wolpoff "toujours en vie". He speaks from MPI-EVA, the home of the Neanderthal genome ("mes collègues").
- **Scope he defers to later lectures**: which Africans, when and how; definitions of AMH versus *H. sapiens*.

## 5. Concepts a learner must master (in prerequisite order)

1. **Biological versus palaeontological species**: interbreeding populations versus morphologically defined fossil taxa. Many fossil "species" were probably interfertile.
2. **Binomial nomenclature, varieties and subspecies** (Linnaeus): genus plus species names, and lower ranks used to label human "races".
3. **Monogenism / polygenism**: a single origin of all humans versus separate origins (even from different species).
4. **Monophyly**: a group descending from one common ancestor. Haeckel's "monophyletic" origin.
5. **Hat-rack (linear) versus candelabra versus trellis models**: succession; deep parallel lineages; parallel lineages connected by gene flow.
6. **Evolutionary "phases"** (archanthropian, palaeoanthropian, neanthropian; Hrdlička's "Neanderthal phase"): grades every lineage was thought to pass through.
7. **Single-species hypothesis**: the cultural niche forbids coexisting hominin species (Brace, Wolpoff).
8. **Multiregional evolution / Recent African Origin (RAO, Out of Africa) / assimilation**: the three modern models along the continuity-replacement axis.
9. **Mousterian**: the Middle Palaeolithic flake industry, shared by Neanderthals and the Qafzeh/Skhul moderns. Tools do not equal species.
10. **Thermoluminescence (TL) and ESR dating**: the methods that made Qafzeh older than the Neanderthal sites (~90–120 ka).
11. **Mitochondrial DNA**: maternal, non-recombining, high copy number. **Haplogroup** (L0–L6, L3, M, N).
12. **Coalescence and MRCA ("Mitochondrial Eve")**: the point where lineages trace back to one ancestor. This is not a single first woman.
13. **Bottleneck, founder effect and serial founder effect; genetic drift**: why diversity falls with distance from Africa.
14. **Introgression / admixture / gene flow**: the transfer of archaic DNA into modern genomes; the percentage of archaic ancestry.
15. **Purifying selection against introgressed DNA**: why archaic segments are depleted, especially near genes.
16. **Middle and Late Pleistocene** (780–129 ka; 129–11.7 ka): the time frame of the course.

## 6. Misconceptions

- **"Linear march of progress"**: Hublin's hat-rack. Several hominins usually coexisted.
- **"Neanderthals are our ancestors"** (the phase model): Qafzeh moderns predate many Neanderthals. Neanderthals are a parallel lineage that contributed ~2 %.
- **"Human races are deep, separate lineages"** (candelabra, Coon): variation is recent, mostly within populations, and "cosmetic".
- **"Mitochondrial Eve was the only woman alive / the first human"**: the biblical vocabulary encourages this. She is a coalescent point, not a founder couple.
- **"Admixture proves multiregionalism"**: Hublin's main target. Small, selected-against introgression is consistent with near-total replacement.
- **"The Enlightenment equals anti-racism; monogenism equals science"**: Voltaire was a polygenist and racist. Monogenism was rooted in dogma. Both camps mixed science and ideology.
- **"Multiregionalists are racist"**: Hublin notes Wolpoff's extensive defence and the symmetrical accusations. The model is not inherently racist, though Coon's version was.
- **"Out of Africa means Africans are 'ancestral' or less evolved"**: all living groups are equally recent descendants. Africans hold most of the diversity.
- **"Africa is a single population"**: implicit here, developed in later lectures. African populations are deeply structured (see the seminar: the Pygmy and farmer ancestors split ~60 ka).
- **"Tools identify species"**: Mousterian tools are found with both Neanderthals and moderns.

## 7. Since 2016 (relevant to this lecture)

- **Jebel Irhoud re-dated to ~315 ka (TL on burnt flint) and attributed to early *H. sapiens*.** Hublin et al. 2017 *Nature*; Richter et al. 2017 *Nature*. This supports a "pan-African" emergence, which Hublin already hints at in the annuaire. Confidence: high.
- **African structured-stem / "African multiregionalism".** Scerri et al. 2018 *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* ("Did our species evolve in subdivided populations across Africa…?"); Ragsdale et al. 2023 *Nature* ("A weakly structured stem for human origins in Africa"). The multiregional idea has been reborn *inside* Africa, which makes a good discussion point against the lecture's dichotomy. Confidence: high.
- **Deep African population splits.** Schlebusch et al. 2017 *Science* (Ballito Bay boy; KhoeSan divergence ~260–350 ka). Confidence: high.
- **Early sapiens outside Africa**:
  - Misliya, ~177–194 ka (Hershkovitz et al. 2018 *Science*);
  - Apidima 1, ~210 ka (Harvati et al. 2019 *Nature*; disputed).
  - Both make "at least 100 ka" an underestimate for the first forays. Confidence: high (Apidima's interpretation is medium).
- **Timing of Neanderthal admixture.** A single extended pulse ~50–43 ka (Iasi et al. 2024 *Science*), and genomes from Ranis/Zlatý kůň constraining it to ~45–49 ka (Sümer et al. 2025 *Nature*). Confidence: high; verify the exact figures.
- **Decline of Neanderthal ancestry questioned.** Petr et al. 2019 *PNAS* found ancestry roughly stable over the last ~45 kyr once biases are corrected. Hublin's "continuous decline" now needs qualifying; most selection happened in the first generations (Harris & Nielsen 2016 *Genetics*; Juric et al. 2016 *PLoS Genetics*). Confidence: medium-high.
- **Neanderthal ancestry in Africans** via back-migration (Chen et al. 2020 *Cell*). Introgression in the other direction: Neanderthal mtDNA and Y chromosome were replaced by early sapiens-related lineages (Posth et al. 2017 *Nature Communications*; Petr et al. 2020 *Science*). This fits his remark about "introgression in the other direction" and the annuaire's ~300 ka mtDNA introgression. Confidence: high.
- **Denisovans get a face.** Harbin cranium (*"Homo longi"*, Ji et al. 2021 *The Innovation*) identified as Denisovan from ancient proteins and dental-calculus mtDNA (Fu et al. 2025, *Science* and *Cell*; verify the venue split). Confidence: medium-high.
- **Earlier *H. erectus*-grade dispersal.** Shangchen ~2.1 Ma (Zhu et al. 2018 *Nature*). Confidence: high.
- **Biomass figures quantified.** Bar-On, Phillips & Milo 2018 *PNAS*. Confidence: high.
- **Synthesis reviews.** Bergström, Stringer, Hajdinjak, Scerri & Skoglund 2021 *Nature* ("Origins of modern human ancestry"). Confidence: high.

## 8. Interactive ideas (lecture)

1. **Model gallery morph.** Interpolate one diagram through hat-rack, candelabra, Weidenreich trellis, Coon, multiregional, assimilation and RAO. The learner drops in fossils (Feldhofer, Zhoukoudian, Ngandong/Java, Skhul, Qafzeh) with dates, and models turn red when contradicted. Discovery: the Qafzeh TL date (~92 ka) breaks both the hat-rack and the phase models. Data: a small hand-built table.
2. **Predict, then reveal: "How much of you is archaic?"** Sliders for the % Neanderthal or Denisovan ancestry in a French, Han Chinese, Papuan and Yoruba genome. Reveal about 2 %, ~2.3 %, ~2 % + ~4 % Denisovan, and ~0.3 % (via back-migration). Then place each model on Hublin's continuity-replacement scale and see where ">95 % replacement" lands. Data: published estimates (SGDP).
3. **Serial founder effect simulator.** Click to send founder groups along a map from East Africa. Each hop samples a subset of alleles, and a live plot of heterozygosity against distance builds up. Then overlay the real HGDP points (Ramachandran et al. 2005) and Howells/Manica-style cranial diversity. Open data: HGDP and SGDP genotypes, plus the Howells craniometric dataset, which is freely available online.
4. **"How do we know?" The mtDNA tree.** Show a trimmed PhyloTree (open). The learner must root the tree and find where the non-African lineages branch, discovering that L3 sits inside African diversity. A follow-up sets Cann et al.'s 1987 sample of 147 people against the critique of their sampling.
5. **Howells challenge.** Show unlabelled crania measurements (or 3D shapes). Can you assign them to continents? Compare the within-group versus between-group spread. Data: the Howells dataset.
6. **Quote-matching timeline (history of ideas).** Match quotations and positions to Linnaeus, Voltaire, Haeckel, Hrdlička, Weidenreich, Coon, Howells, Wolpoff, Bräuer, Cann/Wilson, Stringer and Hawks, then place each on the monogenism-polygenism and continuity-replacement axes. Discovery: the ideology does not line up with "progressive versus conservative".
7. **Dating detective: Qafzeh.** A stratigraphy with burnt flints. The learner picks a method (14C, TL, ESR) given the age range, sees why radiocarbon fails beyond ~50 ka, and gets the TL result. Then they decide what the result means for the "Neanderthal phase".
8. **"Cosmetic" variation map.** Skin-reflectance against UV-intensity scatter plot (Jablonski & Chaplin data). The learner predicts the relationship, then sees it, and contrasts it with neutral markers that show no such pattern.

## 9. The seminar: Lluís Quintana-Murci (Institut Pasteur; CNRS; since about 2020 also holder of the Collège de France chair "Génomique humaine et évolution", verify the date)

**Thesis.** Population genetics reads two histories from living genomes:
- **Demographic**: a recent African origin, an out-of-Africa bottleneck, admixture with archaic humans, and the Bantu expansion. Using Central African Pygmies and Bantu-speaking farmers, his lab argues that demographic expansion *preceded* agriculture rather than being caused by it.
- **Adaptive**: drift explains most allele-frequency differences, but a few loci show selection. These include pigmentation, lactase persistence, high-altitude EPAS1 and, above all, immunity. Pathogens are a major selective force; most immune-gene selection dates to the Neolithic transition (6–13 ka); and Neanderthal introgression was *enriched* at innate-immunity genes, especially TLR1-6-10 and regulatory variants for antiviral responses.

**Outline**

- **[0:00] Genomic diversity basics**
  - The human genome was sequenced "almost 15 years ago". HapMap and 1000 Genomes consortia followed.
  - Humans have very low diversity compared with chimpanzees and gorillas.
  - Most variation lies between individuals rather than between populations, unlike in great apes (the Lewontin apportionment).
  - Two people differ at about 0.1 %, roughly 3 million sites, mostly neutral.
  - Each person carries ~10,000 amino-acid-changing variants, up to ~300 genes with inactivating variants (a redundancy question), and up to ~100 known disease mutations in heterozygous form.
  - Founder populations (Québécois, Finns, Icelanders) carry more deleterious load.
  - Forces acting on diversity: mutation, demography, selection.
  - Out of Africa happened 50–60 ka with a strong bottleneck, the first great founder effect. South Asia and Australia were reached early, the Americas later, probably in two waves.
- **[0:10] Structure and admixture**
  - Diversity declines towards southern South America.
  - In his PhD days, sequencing 200 bp of mtDNA was hard; now 5 million variants take hours.
  - Population structure: in Africa the main axis separates hunter-gatherers (Pygmies, Hadza) from Bantu-speaking farmers. In Europe, genetic structure mirrors the map (Novembre et al. 2008). North Africa and the Middle East show structure linked to endogamy.
  - Archaic admixture is 2 % to about 6 % (Denisovan in Southeast Asia and Oceania).
  - Neanderthal "deserts" near genes, but some regions reach ~64 % Neanderthal ancestry (Sankararaman et al. 2014; Vernot & Akey 2014).
  - SGDP: 300 genomes from 142 populations, Neanderthal ancestry up to ~3 %, higher in East Asia than in Europe, Denisovan ancestry highest in Papua and Melanesia.
  - His lab's focus is the hunter-gatherer to farmer transition (~10 ka in the Middle East), which brought population growth, migration and crowd diseases.
  - Western Pygmies live in Gabon and Cameroon, eastern Pygmies further east (he says the largest group is in "Uganda"; the Mbuti are in the DRC's Ituri forest; flag).
