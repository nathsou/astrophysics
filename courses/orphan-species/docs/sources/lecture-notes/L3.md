## 1. Thesis

Hublin argues that there was **no single moment, place or event at which "modern humans" emerged** within *Homo sapiens*. The fossil record from about 195 ka onwards (Omo Kibish, Herto, Singa, Ngaloba, Klasies, Border Cave, the Aterian remains) shows individuals that are modern in many traits but always keep some primitive ones. That is what a **gradual, largely neutral accumulation of changes** predicts (Tim Weaver 2012), not punctuated equilibrium. The genetic arguments for a ~200 ka "event" do not hold up. A coalescence date says nothing about when a population began, and dips in effective population size (Ne) may reflect **population structure** (growing cultural differentiation between groups) rather than real crashes. Behavioural "modernity" builds up gradually within the African Middle Stone Age (MSA). It is regionalised, but some behaviours spread across the whole continent: perforated *Nassarius* shells appear from the Cape to Morocco and the Levant. This points to a **network of populations, alternately isolated and connected by climate (the Green Sahara)**, swapping behaviours and probably genes. In other words, a "multiregional model at the scale of Africa", not a Garden of Eden. "Origin zone" arguments drawn from living African hunter-gatherers (the San, the Hadza, the Pygmies) are flawed. Those groups are **relic populations**, displaced by the Bantu expansion and by climate change. The Sahara once held large populations, such as the Aterian, that have left no descendants. Present-day North Africans, for instance, largely descend from recent back-migrations. North Africa therefore cannot be ruled out as part of the source area for the dispersal out of Africa.

## 2. Timestamped outline

**[0:00] Framing: what is a "modern human"?**
- Africa is paradoxically less well known than Neanderthal Europe. His three questions are how, when and where.
- He revisits the confusion between *H. sapiens* and the "anatomically modern human" (AMH). Researchers who separate the two want a **discontinuity** on the sapiens lineage after it split from the Neanderthal one.
- Lineage recap: *H. rhodesiensis/heidelbergensis*, which probably survived in Africa after the split, then *H. sapiens*.
- Two models. (a) A **bottleneck / demographic crash** with new mutations producing moderns quickly ("adamique"). (b) **Gradual** change with no singular point.
- Anatomical criteria recalled: Stringer & Day 1982; Stringer, Hublin & Vandermeersch 1984. They cover a short, flat face tucked under the braincase, a globular neurocranium, smaller teeth, a chin and postcranial traits. Some living people fall outside the quantitative definitions.
- Recap from L2: by 250–300 ka there are forms with modern faces but archaic braincases.

**[~0:05] The East African "early moderns"**
- **Omo Kibish 1** (Kibish Formation, Omo valley, Ethiopia), dated to about 195 ka. He probably shows the reconstruction, with the restored parts in blue. It is very fragmentary and perhaps "trop moderne" (too modern) for its age.
- **Omo 2** is a calvaria said to come from the same level but looks far more primitive: a receding frontal and an occipital that is not rounded. There are two readings. Either one population was very variable, or one specimen is out of stratigraphic place (Omo 2 older, or Omo 1 intrusive). The discoverers insist both are in situ.
- A gap remains between about 250–300 ka (Florisbad, Kenyan and Moroccan fossils) and 195 ka. It was presumably filled by intermediate forms.

**[0:10] More specimens**
- **Herto** (Ethiopia), about 160–154 ka: modern overall plan, very robust, archaic brow and occipital. Cut marks on the adult and on a child's skull from the same level, which he presents as Pleistocene cannibalism, or at least mortuary processing.
- **Singa** (Sudan), about 135 ka or more: globular vault with a rounded frontal. It is **pathological**. It has no right bony labyrinth (no inner ear on that side) and thickened parietals with an expanded diploë, which suggests anaemia. Some of its "modern" shape may be due to the pathology.
- **Ngaloba LH 18** (near Laetoli, Tanzania): short, flat face, but a long, low braincase with a flat, receding frontal, like Omo 2.
- **Klasies River Mouth** (South Africa) mandibles, about 100 ka: very variable, some with a bony chin.
- **Border Cave** (South Africa): modern-looking skull recovered from old spoil heaps, so its stratigraphic position is unclear. Possibly about 80 ka.
- Take-home: none of these fossils has all the traits of living humans.

**[~0:16] Gradualism vs. punctuated equilibrium: Tim Weaver**
- **Weaver 2012** borrows methods from quantitative genetics. Modernity is defined as falling within ±1 SD of the recent human mean on every measurement.
- He models phenotypic divergence under **neutral drift**, with inputs for generation time, mutation rate and heritability. Distance from present-day humans grows steadily as one goes back in time.
- Result: fossils "enter" the modern range gradually around **150–200 ka** with nothing special happening. Because the process is stochastic, individuals are modern for most traits but keep a few primitive ones, exactly as the fossils show. He probably shows a graph of distance against time with the threshold crossed.

**[0:20] Genetic arguments examined**
- **Coalescence.** mtDNA, Y and other loci give a most recent common ancestor around 180–200 ka. That is used "pas vraiment à bon escient" (not really wisely), because other lineages existed then and were lost. The parallel case: late Neanderthals (40–50 ka) coalesce in MIS 6 (more than 120 ka), yet Neanderthals existed at 250–300 ka. A coalescence date fits a bottleneck and a constant-size population equally well.
- **Effective population size (Ne)** is the number of ancestors needed to explain present-day diversity. Reconstructions (probably a PSMC-type curve) show a maximum, then an apparent **bottleneck around 70 ka** before expansion. That is not at 200 ka, so it does not fit the "bottleneck = origin" story.
- The **Toba super-eruption** (Sumatra, about 70 ka, "hiver volcanique") is mentioned as a "very, very debated" explanation. Aside: "Les paléoanthropologues ont beaucoup d'imagination" (palaeoanthropologists have plenty of imagination).
- **Structured populations.** Humans mate preferentially within cultural groups (language, religion, customs). His comparison: a female chimpanzee can simply move to another group, and males fare worse. This decouples Ne from census size. He cites his own work with **Luke Premo** (Premo & Hublin 2009) and a 2016 paper (Mazet et al. 2016, *Heredity*).

**[0:30] Behavioural modernity and the MSA**
- The 70 ka "dip" may instead reflect **rising cultural differentiation**: group markers that need not be adaptive.
- The MSA appears at about 300 ka alongside the first *H. sapiens*. **Kapthurin** (Kenya) still has bifaces, but also Levallois, points and blades. Herto still has bifaces made on large Levallois flakes.
- Regionalisation: he shows points from different regions (bifacial and tanged). He refers to **Still Bay** and **Howiesons Poort** (transcribed "Stilbé" and "Ovier-Sonne-Portes"): heat-treated bifacial points, geometric backed pieces used as projectile inserts, and very small points perhaps used for long-range weapons.
- In Europe, "modernity" (after 40 ka) is a clean break because of population replacement. In Africa it emerges piecemeal within the MSA.
- **Blombos**: more than 70 ka, engraved ochre blocks with geometric designs and bone points. **Diepkloof** (transcribed "Deep Plouf"): repeated engraved motifs. **Perforated *Nassarius* shells**: a single genus chosen again and again at many sites.
- **Francesco d'Errico** (his "colleague and friend") argues that Neanderthals did much the same. Hublin disagrees: repeated, standardised geometric signs and ornaments used as group markers or exchange items "n'existent pas" (do not exist) among Neanderthals.
- The **"Garden of Eden" in South Africa**, with internet maps of moderns leaving the Cape. Hublin calls it largely a **streetlight effect** (drunk man looking for his keys under the lamp). Research intensity is uneven, and digging in Angola or Mali is hard.

**[0:40] Genetic "origin zones" and why they mislead**
- mtDNA **haplogroup L3** (the African lineage ancestral to all non-African lineages) is densest in NE Africa, hence an "African Eve" there.
- Nuclear-DNA work by American researchers (Henn et al. 2011) puts the origin in **southern Africa**: the San of the Kalahari are closest to the root.
- His objections. (1) It assumes living populations descend from all past ones, which coalescence logic shows is false. (2) It assumes past movements can be reconstructed.
- **Environment**: in the last 125 ka the Sahara was repeatedly covered in steppe, savanna, wooded savanna, lakes and rivers, with many hunter-gatherers. In arid phases (**MIS 6, MIS 2**) the deserts expanded and the equatorial forest almost disappeared, so now-unexplored regions were inhabited. He probably shows maps of vegetation at about 20 ka and about 130 ka.
- The **Bantu expansion** over the last ~5,000 years, from near N Cameroon, south then east, through a savanna corridor opened by drying. It displaced hunter-gatherers (cross-reference to Quintana-Murci, L1). Today's foragers (Pygmies, Hadza, San) are **vestigial**. Khoisan genetic markers in Bantu-speaking Zambians show the Khoisan once lived much further north.
- **The Aterian**: an MSA of North Africa and the Sahara, from at least 100 ka to about 40 ka. He overlays a map of the USA to convey its extent, a big set of populations even if density was low. It is best known in Morocco thanks to stratified caves; in the Sahara it is mostly surface finds. Human remains date to about 100–80 ka.

**[0:50] The Aterian people, *Nassarius*, an African "multiregionalism"**
- Aterian mandibles show the same mosaic as the Klasies mandibles. Morphometrics of the **Dar es-Soltane II H5** face resemble **Skhul/Qafzeh** (the first moderns outside Africa, about 100+ ka, next lecture).
- Aterian behaviour: foliate and tanged points (some look like Neolithic arrowheads), bone tools, pigments, and **perforated *Nassarius***. Several Moroccan sites have them at about 100 ka, older than the South African ones. Algeria and further east have them too, as do Skhul/Qafzeh. "En archéologie, on trouve ce qu'on cherche" (in archaeology you find what you look for).
- Long-distance spread from the Cape to Rabat or Tangier, at least along the coasts, can only be explained by **diffusion through connected populations**.
- The **87 genes** (2014) with fixed modern-specific changes absent in Neanderthals and Denisovans. The Garden-of-Eden reading: all arose in one population that then replaced everyone. His alternative: the mutations arose in different places and spread through an intermittently connected pan-African network before the dispersal out of Africa. "Un espèce de modèle multirégional à l'échelle de l'Afrique" (a sort of multiregional model at the scale of Africa), notable because he criticised global multiregionalism in L1.
- **Iberomaurusian** (transcribed "libéromorussien"): bladelets and large cemeteries (Taforalt, Afalou). Faces resemble European Upper Palaeolithic people, not the Aterian or Skhul/Qafzeh. This points to population replacement in the Maghreb, where no relic foragers survive.
- Evidence: Neanderthal ancestry in North Africans, less than the roughly 2% in Europeans. On the mtDNA tree, two Eurasian lineages are found in Africa (Kabyles, Mauritania, Berbers), notably **haplogroup U6**. [The transcript is cut off here.]

**[1:00] Back-migration and conclusions**
- In 2016 U6 was found in a **35 ka Romanian fossil from Peștera Muierii**. This supports U6 reaching North Africa **from Europe** rather than directly from the Near East.
- Last image: the **Strait of Sicily at the LGM**, with emerged islands. A crossing by Gravettians from Italy and Sicily to Tunisia (where he places the earliest Iberomaurusian dates) would not have been hard.
- Consequence: present-day North African genomes cannot tell us about the region's history beyond 20–50 ka.
- Conclusions. (1) Gradual evolution: the face became modern early and the braincase changed gradually. (2) From at least 100 ka there was a pan-African network exchanging behaviours and probably genes. *Nassarius* reflects transmission, not independent invention. (3) The distribution of hunter-gatherers is vestigial. Had we Aterian genomes, they might show an mtDNA sister group to the Pygmy or San lineages. (4) North Africa cannot be excluded from the source zone of the dispersal out of Africa.

## 4. Hublin's positions and debates

- **Against punctuated / "Adamic" origins of moderns** (bottleneck plus key mutations). He relies on Weaver's neutral modelling and on mosaic fossils. Opponents are not named, but the targets are the "200 ka speciation" readings of mtDNA Eve and Klein-style "late revolution" scenarios.
- **Sceptical of coalescence dates as origin dates**, and of reading Ne as census size. Toba is dismissed with irony.
- **Against a single African Eden**, whether in South Africa (archaeology plus Henn-type genomics) or East Africa (mtDNA L3). Rhetorical move: the **streetlight effect** anecdote. Political and logistical reasons explain where people dig (Angola, Mali).
- **Pro African "multiregionalism"**: an intermittently connected network. He highlights the irony himself, since he criticised Weidenreich/Wolpoff multiregionalism in L1.
- **Against d'Errico's equivalence of Neanderthal and early modern symbolism.** Neanderthals lack *repeated, standardised* signs and ornaments used as social markers. He is polite ("collègue et ami") but firm.
- **Pro-North Africa**: a personal stake, given his Moroccan fieldwork (Irhoud, the Aterian with McPherron). He shows the USA overlay to make the Aterian feel big, and argues that North Africa belongs in the out-of-Africa source zone.
- **Iberomaurusians from Europe via Sicily.** This was a speculative position (Gravettian crossing). It has since been undercut by aDNA (see §7).
- Memorable moves: the chimpanzee female migration joke; "on trouve ce qu'on cherche"; the drunk man and his keys; the Aterian–USA map; Herto cannibalism as an aside; Singa's missing inner ear.

## 5. Concepts a learner must master (prerequisite order)

1. **Species vs. "anatomically modern human" (AMH)**: *H. sapiens* is a lineage, while AMH is a fuzzy endpoint defined by living variation.
2. **Mosaic evolution**: traits change at different times and rates (face before braincase).
3. **Gradualism vs. punctuated equilibrium**: steady change vs. stasis broken by rapid speciation events.
4. **Neutral drift and heritability**: phenotypes can diverge with no selection, at a rate set by mutation, heritability and generation time.
5. **Standard deviation as a definition boundary**: Weaver's "±1 SD on every trait" criterion, and why some living people fail it.
6. **Haplotype / haplogroup (L3, U6)**: lineages of non-recombining DNA (mtDNA, Y).
7. **Coalescence / TMRCA**: tracing lineages back to their most recent common ancestor. That ancestor's date is not the date the population began.
8. **Effective population size (Ne)**: the size of an idealised population with the same diversity. It is not a headcount.
9. **Population structure / metapopulation**: subgroups that mate mostly internally. Structure can create false bottleneck signals.
10. **Marine isotope stages (MIS 6, 5, 2)**: glacial/interglacial numbering. In Africa, glacials meant aridity and contracted forest.
11. **Green Sahara / African Humid Periods**: recurrent wet phases that connected north and south.
12. **Chronostratigraphic dating** (Ar/Ar on tuffs, ESR, U-series, OSL, TL): how each fossil gets its age, and why "found in spoil" matters.
13. **Stratigraphic intrusion**: material moving between levels (Omo 1 vs Omo 2; Diepkloof critics).
14. **MSA vs ESA/LSA**: African Stone Age stages. The MSA is the Levallois-based, flake/point technology of roughly 300–30 ka. Regional industries include Still Bay, Howiesons Poort and the Aterian.
15. **Levallois, bifacial points, backed geometrics, tanged points, heat treatment, hafting**.
16. **Behavioural modernity**: the "trait list" approach (symbols, ornaments, long-distance exchange, composite tools) and its critiques.
17. **Ornaments as social markers**: why standardised *Nassarius* beads imply shared codes and networks.
18. **Back-migration into Africa**: Eurasian lineages (U6, M1) and Neanderthal ancestry in North Africa.

## 6. Misconceptions

- "Modern humans appeared 200,000 years ago in East Africa." This is a coincidence of the Omo date and mtDNA coalescence. Neither marks an event.
- "The mtDNA Eve was the only woman alive / the first modern human."
- "A low Ne means few people." Structure inflates apparent bottlenecks.
- "Toba nearly wiped out humanity."
- "Fossils are either modern or archaic." Real fossils are mosaics, and a reconstruction (Omo 1, mostly plaster) can exaggerate modernity.
- "Today's San or Hadza are living fossils of the ancestral population / tell us where we originated."
- "Where we find the most evidence is where things started" (the streetlight effect; preservation in caves).
- "North Africans are just one African population among others." They carry Eurasian back-migration ancestry and recent replacement.
- "Behavioural modernity arrived as a package at 50 ka (the 'Upper Palaeolithic revolution')." Europe's sharp break is a replacement artefact.
- "Neanderthals never made anything symbolic." Hublin's line is narrower: no *repeated, standardised* sign systems. Post-2016 Neanderthal finds complicate this (§7).

## 7. Since 2016 (relevant to this lecture)

- **Jebel Irhoud** (Hublin et al. 2017, *Nature*; Richter et al. 2017, *Nature*): *H. sapiens* and MSA at ~315 ka (TL on burnt flints). This makes his "pan-African" origin explicit and closes the Moroccan part of the "gap". Confidence: high.
- **Omo Kibish 1 redated** (Vidal et al. 2022, *Nature*): the Kamoya's Hominid Site level lies above a tephra correlated with the Shala eruption, giving a minimum age of 233 ± 22 ka. The "195 ka" figure he used is obsolete. High.
- **Olorgesailie** (Brooks et al. 2018; Deino et al. 2018; Potts et al. 2018, all *Science*): MSA by ~320 ka, with long-distance obsidian transport and pigment. This strengthens early regional networks. High.
- **African multiregionalism formalised**: Scerri et al. 2018, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution* ("Did our species evolve in subdivided populations across Africa…"). Ragsdale et al. 2023, *Nature* ("A weakly structured stem for human origins in Africa") models deep, weakly connected African stems, matching his network view and his scepticism of single-origin models. High (whether Hublin is a co-author of Scerri 2018: verify).
- **Deep African structure from aDNA**: Schlebusch et al. 2017, *Science* (a 2,000-year-old Ballito Bay genome; Khoe-San divergence ~260–350 ka). Lipson et al. 2022, *Nature* (deep population structure in sub-Saharan foragers). High.
- **Taforalt genomes** (van de Loosdrecht et al. 2018, *Science*): 15 ka Iberomaurusians carry ~2/3 Natufian-related (Near Eastern) and ~1/3 sub-Saharan ancestry, with mtDNA U6 and M1, and **no detectable European Upper Palaeolithic contribution**. This **contradicts Hublin's Sicily-crossing scenario**, but supports his wider point about recent replacement and back-migration. High.
- **U6 in Peștera Muierii**: Hervella et al. 2016 ("The mitogenome of a 35,000-year-old *Homo sapiens* from Europe supports a Palaeolithic back-migration to Africa"; journal believed to be *Scientific Reports*, verify).
- **Neanderthal ancestry in all Africans**: Chen et al. 2020, *Cell*. Back-migration from Eurasia left Neanderthal-derived segments across Africa, not only in the north. High.
- **Toba**: Smith et al. 2018, *Nature*. Pinnacle Point 5–6 (South Africa) occupation continued through the Toba cryptotephra horizon, so there is no catastrophe signal. High.
- **Older bottleneck claim**: Hu et al. 2023, *Science*. A severe bottleneck at ~930–813 ka (FitCoal method). It is heavily contested, and a good case for his warnings about reading Ne. Medium.
- **Older *Nassarius*-type beads**: Bizmoune Cave, Morocco, ~142–150 ka (Sehasseh et al. 2021, *Science Advances*). This reinforces his North African priority argument. High.
- **Blombos drawing**: ochre crayon cross-hatching on silcrete at ~73 ka (Henshilwood et al. 2018, *Nature*). High.
- **East Africa filling in** (streetlight effect): Panga ya Saidi, Kenya, has a ~78 ka child burial ("Mtoto"; Martinón-Torres et al. 2021, *Nature*) and a long MSA–LSA sequence (Shipton et al. 2018, *Nature Communications*). High.
- **Neanderthal symbolism challenges**: Hoffmann et al. 2018, *Science* (U-Th ages >64 ka for Spanish cave markings, contested) and Hoffmann et al. 2018, *Science Advances* (perforated and pigmented shells, Cueva de los Aviones, ~115 ka). These fuel d'Errico's side of the debate. Medium-high (the dates are disputed).
- **Pan-African character of the earliest sapiens**: many syntheses, e.g. Stringer 2016 *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* (verify) and Bergström et al. 2021, *Nature* ("Origins of modern human ancestry"), which concludes that no single place or time can yet be identified. High.

## 8. Interactive ideas (lecture)

1. **"Modern or not?" mosaic sorter.** Learners score Omo 1, Omo 2, Herto, Singa, LH 18, Dar es-Soltane 5 and a living person on 6–8 traits (face height, frontal curvature, occipital rounding, brow, chin…). The widget applies Weaver's ±1 SD rule. Discovery: no fossil passes every trait, and some living people fail too. Data: published trait descriptions (qualitative) or simplified metric ranges, approximated where needed.
2. **Predict, then reveal: the neutral-drift clock.** Learners first guess when fossils should "become modern". A simulator then runs neutral drift on N traits (sliders for heritability, generation time and mutational variance) and shows fossils crossing the threshold trait by trait around 150–200 ka. Real fossil dates are overlaid. Implementable as a simple random walk; no special data needed.
3. **Coalescence playground.** A Wright–Fisher simulation of mitochondrial lineages over time. Switch between a constant population and a bottleneck. Learners see that a TMRCA appears in both cases and that the "Eve" was never alone. Classic, open, easy.
4. **Structure vs. bottleneck.** Simulate an island metapopulation and lower the migration rate. A PSMC-like Ne curve "dips" even though the census size is constant. Grounded in Premo & Hublin 2009 and Mazet et al. 2016.
5. **How do we know? Dating the fossils.** Cards for Omo 1 (Ar/Ar tuffs, redated 2022), Herto (Ar/Ar), Singa (U-series/ESR), Border Cave (from spoil), Irhoud (TL). Learners rank each age by reliability and see why "found in spoil" or "above a tephra" matters. Includes the Omo 1 195 → ≥233 ka reveal.
6. **Green Sahara time-slider.** A map of Africa toggling ~130 ka (MIS 5e humid), ~70 ka, ~20 ka (MIS 2 arid; forest contracted) and today. Site dots (Aterian, Still Bay/Howiesons Poort, *Nassarius* sites) appear or vanish. Learners judge when Cape and Maghreb populations could have been connected. Open data: palaeovegetation reconstructions (e.g. Larrasoaña et al. 2013 humid-period maps, verify), site coordinates from ROAD (ROCEEH database).
7. ***Nassarius* network map.** Plot dated perforated-shell sites (Bizmoune, Taforalt, Contrebandiers, Oued Djebbana, Skhul, Qafzeh, Blombos, Sibudu) with an age slider. Prompt: independent invention or diffusion? The Cape–Tangier distance is shown with a scale bar. Open data from the literature (~15 sites).
8. **Streetlight effect.** A heat map of excavated MSA sites against the area of Africa, alongside political, accessibility and preservation layers. The learner predicts where the "origin" would be if evidence tracked effort. Data: ROAD database.
9. **Who lives where now vs. then.** An animation of the Bantu expansion (5 ka to present) pushing forager ranges. A toggle shows the Aterian footprint (USA overlay) with "0 living descendants sampled". Ends with the Taforalt 2018 result, as a self-correction exercise: Hublin's Sicily hypothesis tested by aDNA.

## 9. The seminar — John Parkington (University of Cape Town)

"Symptoms of Modernity: behavioural innovations in the South African Middle Stone Age". The speaker is Emeritus Professor of Archaeology at UCT, a long-time excavator of Western Cape sites and co-director of the Diepkloof Rock Shelter project with the Bordeaux team (Texier, Rigaud, Porraz).

**Thesis.** Behavioural modernity means the point when past foragers "start behaving like recent hunter-gatherers" and become lost in their range of variation. He uses a medical metaphor: modernity is an "illness", the archaeological record is the "patient" (patchy memory, unreliable witness), and archaeologists diagnose "symptoms". In the southern African MSA a **cluster** of symptoms appears from about 160 ka and accelerates after about 120 ka in the later MSA (Still Bay, Howiesons Poort). The cluster: preference for caves and rocky areas, transported shell middens, organised hearths and bedding, heat-treated and selected stone, soft-hammer blades, compound ochre-loaded mastics, standardised engraved ostrich eggshell and ochre, and perforated beads. These are the behaviours that **persist** until colonialism ended the Stone Age sequence about 350 years ago. His explanation is social (organisation of space, place, the "social brain") plus a nutritional hypothesis: **coastal shellfish supplied DHA and other long-chain omega-3 fatty acids for brain growth**, mediated by women's diets.
