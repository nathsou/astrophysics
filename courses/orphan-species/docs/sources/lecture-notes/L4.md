# Lecture 4 (25 Oct 2016): "La sortie d'Afrique" (Out of Africa)
Seminar: Anthony E. ("Tony") Marks (Southern Methodist University, Dallas), "Out of Africa: how, when and why"

Sources: transcripts/L4.txt (about 63 min, 7 chunks), transcripts/S4-marks.txt (about 55 min, 6 chunks), plus annuaire.txt and summaries.txt. Note: the annuaire dates the Marks seminar to 18 Oct 2016, but in the lecture Hublin says Marks will speak "tout à l'heure" (later this evening), so lecture and seminar were almost certainly on the same evening, 25 Oct. Hublin's written annuaire summary (written later) already includes post-lecture findings: Israeli fossils "100,000–170,000", African mtDNA introgression into Neanderthals "around 300,000", Tam Pa Ling "from 70,000", Australia "about 60,000". ## 1. Thesis

Hublin argues that there was no single "Out of Africa" event. There were several exits, and the earliest of them was not really an exit at all. In the humid "Green Sahara" phases, above all early MIS 5 (about 130–120 ka), north-east Africa, the Arabian Peninsula and the southern Levant formed one continuous habitable zone. Arabia was then "more a piece of Africa than of Eurasia". Early *Homo sapiens* at Skhul and Qafzeh, and Nubian-core industries in Arabia, are the first traces of our species outside Africa proper. The modern-to-Neanderthal gene flow seen in the Altai Neanderthal genome shows that these early groups met archaic humans. Climate opened and closed the "gates" (the Sinai and, by boat, Bab el-Mandeb). But Hublin rejects pure climatic determinism, because other hominins (Neanderthals, Denisovans) already occupied Eurasia. Claims for very early moderns further east (India before Toba, China at 100–140 ka) do not convince him. Problems include stratigraphic association, dating, and the assumption that a stone-tool type identifies a species. He accepts Tam Pa Ling (Laos, about 50–60 ka) as solid. Finally, he warns that present-day genomes cannot see populations that left no descendants. This makes the three 2016 *Nature* genome papers useful but structurally limited for counting waves.

## 2. Timestamped outline (lecture)

**[0:00] Recap and framing**
- Recap of L3: modern anatomy emerged gradually across Africa over about 300 ka, with sub-Saharan Africa perhaps playing a larger role. Herto (about 160 ka) is a mosaic of modern and archaic traits.
- The popular "200 ka" date for modern humans is mainly an artefact of the gaps in the fossil record before 200 ka.
- The big picture: moderns in Africa while Eurasia held Neanderthals (Europe, Central Asia, Near East) and "mysterious" Denisovans further east. Neanderthals spread east to the Altai, probably during the interglacial from 130 ka. Moderns later replace them, with some genetic assimilation and probably cultural interaction.
- Four questions: where did they leave, when, how many times, and why (the "why" is deferred to the lecture on replacement).
- He proposes speaking of "les sorties d'Afrique", in the plural.

**[~0:04] Geography of exits**
- He shows a map. Sicily and Gibraltar crossings are set aside, as there is no evidence for this period. The later Upper Palaeolithic Europe-to-North Africa link (L3) is much younger.
- Two candidate exits. The first is the Sinai/Suez isthmus, the "natural" dry-land route, with satellite views of the Nile delta and Sinai. The second is Bab el-Mandeb ("Gate of Lamentations/Grief"), about 30 km wide today with an intermediate islet (Perim).
- Bab el-Mandeb was not dry land in the late Middle or Upper Pleistocene. If the Red Sea had been closed off, it would have dried out and left evaporites, and the evidence for these is weak at best. Sea-level reconstructions leave at least 15–20 m of water even at the lowest stands, so any crossing needed watercraft. Watercraft existed by 50 ka (Australia).
- The low-sea-level phases are arid in Africa, and those are exactly the times when people were not there. The favourable windows are the humid phases.

**[0:10] Routes within Asia and the scenario zoo**
- Southern coastal route versus northern route (Central Asia to the Altai), separated by the Himalaya, the Tibetan Plateau and the deserts to its north-west.
- Europe: a Mediterranean entry, or a higher-latitude route via eastern Europe and southern Russia.
- He shows a figure of combined scenarios "from a recent article" (likely Reyes-Centeno et al. 2014 *PNAS* or Groucutt et al. 2015 *Evol. Anthropol.*; verify). It includes northern, southern and both routes, and variants on whether Aboriginal Australians carry an early coastal-wave legacy.
- The key point: the first obstacles were inside Africa. Saharan and Arabian desertification limited population density in north-east Africa.

**[~0:14] Green Sahara**
- Pluvial phases in the Middle and Upper Pleistocene. The most spectacular is the start of the last interglacial (Eemian, MIS 5e, about 130 ka): slightly warmer than today in Europe, and the African summer monsoon pushed far north.
- He shows a map (very likely Larrasoaña, Roberts & Rohling 2013, *PLoS ONE*): wooded savanna with 10–40% tree cover across today's Sahara, river networks draining to the Mediterranean and southwards, and mega-lakes. Mega-Lake Chad was "the size of present-day Germany".
- The same happened in Arabia (deferred to Marks).
- Later humid episodes: late MIS 5 and MIS 3.
- Proxies: speleothems (oxygen isotopes along stalagmites). There are no caves in the central Sahara, hence other proxies are needed.

**[0:20] Sapropels and the Timmermann & Friedrich model**
- Sapropels: dark, organic-rich layers in eastern Mediterranean sediments. He shows a Miocene section from Sicily to illustrate them. They form when monsoon-fed Nile floods (and other rivers) cap the basin with warmer, fresher water, so bottom water stagnates and turns anoxic.
- One sapropel falls squarely in the Eemian (about 128–121 ka; S5). At least two more fall in later MIS 5 (S4 and S3). Speleothems indicate another humid spell around 60–50 ka.
- Timmermann & Friedrich (2016, *Nature*, published "a few weeks ago") coupled a climate model with a human-dispersal model. They identified windows of easy passage from north-east Africa into Arabia and the Levant, including again around 130–120 ka.
- **Garbled passage**: the transcript jumps from "the door is open" to a map with red dots (most likely the Levantine Neanderthal sites Tabun, Kebara, Amud, Dederiyeh) versus two sites with moderns, Skhul and Qafzeh. Some content is lost here.
- Skhul (Mount Carmel, next to Tabun), excavated by Dorothy Garrod's team (1929–34). Hublin shows the Skhul V skull: modern, but with many primitive traits. There are skeletons in apparent burials, with many proposed dates, mostly falling in the last interglacial.
- Qafzeh, near Nazareth at the foot of Mount Precipice. First excavated by the French consul René Neuville (transcribed "Neville"), then by Bernard Vandermeersch in the 1960s–70s. Qafzeh 9 is the "very modern" showcase skull.

**[0:30] Qafzeh, Skhul and Tabun C2: behaviour and the Mousterian puzzle**
- The Qafzeh sample spans a range: some individuals, like Skhul, are more robust and archaic. Mostly thermoluminescence dates (around 90–100 ka), slightly younger than Skhul.
- Burials: the Qafzeh double burial (young woman with a child at her feet, Qafzeh 9 and 10). Also a child burial with what seems an offering of deer antlers attached to the skull (Qafzeh 11; transcribed "massacre de dents" for *massacre de daim*, a fallow-deer antler frontlet).
- The puzzle: both sites have Tabun C-type Levallois Mousterian, very similar to what Neanderthals later made in the region. "No good explanation."
- *Nassarius* shell beads at Skhul are like those from North and South Africa, and possibly older. Ornament shells moved across a zone from the Cape to Morocco to Israel. This is unknown among Neanderthals and implies social signalling and exchange.
- The Tabun C2 mandible (Garrod) is believed to come genuinely from layer C. It is not Neanderthal: it has a tear-drop symphysis section and a decreasing corpus height. He shows a virtual 3D cross-section that he made himself. It aligns with the early moderns.
- History of science: Sir Arthur Keith (with McCown) wavered between one variable population and two groups. Some today still argue for a single Levantine population with a Neanderthal pole, a modern pole and hybrids. Hublin points out that the dates give no proof the two groups were present at the same time, and that fossils sort easily into one "box" or the other.
- New in 2016: gene flow from early moderns into Neanderthals (Kuhlwilm et al. 2016, *Nature*). The Altai Neanderthal carries a small fraction of modern DNA (he says "0.1 to 2.1%"). The dated introgression matches the Levantine arrival at about 120–130 ka. It was then the only known flow in that direction.
- Qafzeh microfauna are "Arabo-African" (Tchernov), i.e. coming up from the south (Arabia) more than from Africa proper.
- There are no human fossils in Arabia (in 2016), but Nubian Levallois cores show a technological community across the Red Sea. Arabia at 120–110 ka is "more a piece of Africa than of Eurasia". Puzzle repeated: Skhul and Qafzeh people did not carry "Nubian cores in their pockets".

**[0:40] From "aborted exit" to early eastern claims: India**
- The textbook view of his student days was an aborted exit. Moderns "put their nose out of the door", then Neanderthals returned from about 80 ka, and the real exit was about 50 ka. That view changed over the past 10–20 years.
- India: Michael Petraglia (Cambridge, Oxford, now MPI Jena) argued for moderns before the Toba super-eruption (Sumatra, "a bit before 70 ka"; about 74 ka). He relies on Jwalapuram (Jurreru valley, Andhra Pradesh), where Middle Palaeolithic Levallois industries with points lie under Toba ash and look African to him.
- Critique by Paul Mellars and colleagues. Hublin finds neither side fully convincing. Simple lithics converge, and with no human remains you cannot tie an industry to a species.
- Mellars' genetic argument: the coalescence of the non-African descendants of mtDNA haplogroup L3 (M and N) postdates Toba, at about 60–65 ka. Hublin's rebuttal: present-day lineages cannot exclude earlier waves that died out. Mellars instead links the arrival of moderns to later microlithic and geometric industries in India (50–40 ka), but geometrics are also produced by many unrelated groups.

**[~0:45] China**
- He shows a figure of very old dates claimed for Chinese sites.
- Liujiang skull: found in 1958 by farmers digging cave sediment for fertiliser, with no excavation context. Claimed ages range from about 139 ka (he attributes this to "Bai", probably Shen Guanjun et al. 2002; verify) to perhaps a little over 15 ka.
- Zhirendong mandible (Guangxi; transcribed "Ziran Dong"; Liu Wu et al. 2010 *PNAS*): over 100 ka if in situ. It has a small mental trigone but a thick symphysis and corpus, and the variation of Chinese archaic humans is poorly known.

**[0:50] Daoxian, Tam Pa Ling, and the three 2016 genome papers**
- Daoxian (Fuyan Cave, Hunan; Liu Wu et al. 2015 *Nature*): 47 isolated teeth below a dated flowstone, so at least 80 ka, perhaps 100 ka. Is the site intact? The teeth are "almost too modern": small, with reduced roots and spectacular caries. In all the Skhul and Qafzeh teeth, by contrast, there are only about two tiny caries.
- Aside on replicability: archaeological observations cannot be re-run like experiments.
- Tam Pa Ling (northern Laos; Franco-American team, with Fabrice Demeter in the audience and Anne-Marie Bacon mentioned). Methodical excavation. The TPL1 skull has a fully modern frontal and brow region. Dates: OSL about 47 ka, direct U-Th on bone over 60 ka ("always a bit delicate"), and radiocarbon just over 50 ka in the same level. So moderns were in the region by 50–60 ka, consistent with Australia at 50 ka. The TPL2 mandible has a true chin but a thick corpus, i.e. a mosaic.
- Three *Nature* papers from "a few weeks ago" (21 Sep 2016 issue): Mallick et al. (Reich lab, Simons Genome Diversity Project), Malaspinas et al. (Aboriginal Australian genomes) and Pagani et al. (Estonian Biocentre). All agree on an African origin, with non-Africans as a subset of African variation.
- Mallick: no trace of an early dispersal in present-day people, i.e. one exit under 80 ka. There is deep African population structure going back about 200 ka, which complicates coalescence dating and estimates of effective population size. Khoisan and Mbuti divergence exceeds 100 ka, implying that "modern behaviours" were exchanged between groups.

**[1:00] Tree topologies, the Pagani signal and conclusions**
- Mallick: the first split is East versus West Eurasia, and Australians branch off the Asian side.
- Malaspinas: Australians split first from all other non-Africans, which favours a southern coastal colonisation.
- Pagani: Papuan coalescence with Africans is older than for other Eurasians. They explain this by about 2% ancestry from an earlier modern wave (xOoA), though they admit an archaic (Denisovan-like) source is also possible.
- Conclusions:
  - Climate mattered: hunter-gatherers need a productive environment to grow in number.
  - Moderns were out of Africa by 120–130 ka in the southern Levant and very probably in Arabia.
  - Climatic determinism is too simple. Timmermann & Friedrich's model predicted moderns in Europe by about 80 ka, which did not happen, because Neanderthals (and Denisovans in Asia) were in the way. "Not a dispersal on an empty planet."
  - On early moderns in the Far East he is conservative: 50–60 ka yes, Tam Pa Ling convincing. Claims of 80, 100 and 150 ka are "generally heavily criticised within months or years".
  - Living-population genetics is blind to waves that left no descendants. Ancient DNA already shows early modern populations unrelated to any living group (to be developed for western Eurasia).

## 4. Hublin's positions and debates

- **Plural exits, and Arabia as Africa.** His main conceptual move is that the MIS 5 "exit" was a range extension of an African biome. He partly echoes Richard Klein's quip, quoted by Marks: "Africa expanded".
- **Against the "aborted exit" orthodoxy** of the 1980s–90s, in which Levantine moderns retreated and the real exit happened at 50 ka. But he still treats the Levantine record as a mosaic and keeps the later main dispersal.
- **Against a single variable Levantine population with hybrids** (the Keith tradition, and some modern authors). His argument: no demonstrated contemporaneity, and the specimens sort cleanly.
- **Sceptical of lithics as species markers.** This applies to Petraglia's African-looking Jwalapuram industries, to Mellars' microliths, and implicitly to the Nubian argument. Convergence in simple technologies is the problem. He also admits that the Skhul/Qafzeh moderns making a Neanderthal-like Mousterian is unexplained.
- **Even-handed on Petraglia vs Mellars.** "Neither entirely convincing". He uses the case to make the methodological point about present-day genetics (lost lineages).
- **Conservative on China.** The issues are stratigraphic association (Liujiang farmers), dating by overlying flowstone (Zhirendong, Daoxian), suspiciously modern teeth with caries, and poor knowledge of archaic variation. His pattern claim: early-date papers get torn down "within months or years". In hindsight he was vindicated for Daoxian.
- **Against climatic determinism.** He uses Timmermann & Friedrich's own failed prediction (Europe at 80 ka) to argue that biotic competition with Neanderthals and Denisovans must enter the model. Marks openly disagrees in emphasis: for Marks, good climate pulls people outwards, while Hublin sees deserts as barriers.
- **On present-day genetics.** It is powerful but blind to extinct waves. He sets this up for later lectures on ancient DNA from early Europeans.
- **Rhetorical moves and anecdotes:**
  - The meaning of "Bab el-Mandeb" ("not a very cheerful name").
  - "They went to Australia 50 ka, so boats existed."
  - Hand-cut 3D model of Tabun C2.
  - Farmers digging fertiliser at Liujiang.
  - The caries comparison (Daoxian vs about 2 tiny caries in all of Skhul and Qafzeh).
  - Replicability: you cannot re-run an excavation the way you re-run an astronomical observation.
  - Greeting Fabrice Demeter in the audience.
  - The image of moderns "putting their nose out of the door" and then "pof, back in their box".

## 5. Concepts a learner must master (in prerequisite order)

1. **Pleistocene subdivisions**: Middle Pleistocene (to about 129 ka) and Upper/Late Pleistocene (129–11.7 ka).
2. **Marine Isotope Stages (MIS)**: odd numbers are warm, even numbers cold. MIS 6 is the glacial at about 190–130 ka; MIS 5e (Eemian) is about 130–116 ka; MIS 4 is about 71–57 ka; MIS 3 is about 57–29 ka.
3. **Glacio-eustatic sea level**: ice volume lowers sea level (up to about −120 m), which changes straits and coastlines.
4. **Monsoon and orbital forcing (precession)**: drives the northward reach of the African monsoon and hence Green Sahara phases.
5. **Palaeoclimate proxies**: speleothem δ¹⁸O, sapropels, lake shorelines.
6. **Dating methods**: TL, OSL, ESR, U-series (on flowstone versus bone), radiocarbon (limit about 50 ka). Includes the difference between dating a layer and dating a fossil, and *terminus ante quem* from an overlying flowstone.
7. **Stratigraphic association / taphonomy**: intrusive burials, reworked material, in-situ context.
8. **Mosaic morphology**: modern traits (globular vault, chin, tear-drop symphysis) co-occur with archaic ones.
9. **Levallois / Mousterian / MSA**: prepared-core technologies, with the Levantine Mousterian facies Tabun D, C and B.
10. **Nubian Levallois core**: a point core whose distal convexity is shaped by two converging removals, creating a steep median ridge.
11. **Emiran / IUP**: the earliest Levantine Upper Palaeolithic, combining bidirectional Levallois points with blades.
12. **Convergence (equifinality)** in lithics.
13. **Coalescence time** of a lineage (mtDNA L3 and its daughters M and N), and why it is not the same as the population split or exit date.
14. **Population structure and effective population size (Ne)**.
15. **Introgression / gene flow** and admixture dating.
16. **Ghost populations / extinct waves**: lineages with no living descendants.
17. **Climatic determinism vs range expansion vs competition** as explanatory frameworks.

## 6. Misconceptions

- "Humans walked out of Africa once, about 60–50 ka, via Suez." Both speakers say: several episodes, starting at 130–120 ka.
- "Modern humans appeared at 200 ka." This is a sampling artefact.
- "Bab el-Mandeb was a land bridge at low sea level." Not in the relevant period, and low sea level coincides with aridity anyway.
- "The Sahara and Arabia were always barriers." They were savannas with mega-lakes during pluvials.
- "Anatomically modern means behaviourally modern, and Mousterian means Neanderthal." Skhul and Qafzeh break both equations (Marks develops this).
- "A tool type identifies who made it." The problem is convergence (India, microliths).
- "Genetics of living people shows every migration." Extinct waves are invisible.
- "Coalescence date = migration date."
- "An old date for a layer dates the fossil in it." Liujiang, Zhirendong and Daoxian show why not.
- "Climate alone explains dispersal." The failed Europe-at-80-ka prediction counters this.
- "Gene flow only went from Neanderthals into us." The Altai evidence shows modern-to-Neanderthal flow too.
- Map projection: Africa is far larger than Mercator suggests (Marks).

## 7. Since 2016 (relevant to this lecture)

- **Jebel Irhoud, about 315 ka** (Hublin et al. 2017 *Nature* 546:289; Richter et al. 2017 *Nature* 546:293). Confirms that "200 ka" was a gap artefact. Confidence high.
- **Misliya-1 maxilla, Israel, 177–194 ka** (Hershkovitz et al. 2018 *Science* 359:456). The earliest *H. sapiens* outside Africa, pushing Levantine presence well before the Eemian. Explains the annuaire's "100,000–170,000". Confidence high.
- **Apidima 1, Greece, about 210 ka** (Harvati et al. 2019 *Nature* 571:500). A claimed early sapiens in Europe; contested. Confidence medium.
- **Neanderthal mtDNA replaced by an early African-related lineage, about 270–470 ka** (Posth et al. 2017 *Nature Communications* 8:16046), and Y-chromosome replacement (Petr et al. 2020 *Science* 369:1653). The source of Hublin's "around 300,000" in the annuaire. Confidence high.
- **Nesher Ramla Homo, Israel, about 140–120 ka** (Hershkovitz et al. 2021 *Science* 372:1424; Zaidner et al. 2021 *Science* 372:1429). An archaic, non-sapiens population contemporaneous with Skhul and Qafzeh, using similar Levallois technology. Relevant to the Mousterian puzzle and to cohabitation. Interpretation contested. Confidence high on the find.
- **First Arabian human fossil: Al Wusta phalanx, Nefud, about 88 ka** (Groucutt et al. 2018 *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 2:800). Answers Hublin's "we have no human fossils in Arabia, I hope we will". Confidence high.
- **Human footprints at Alathar palaeolake, Nefud, about 120–112 ka** (Stewart et al. 2020 *Science Advances*). Confidence high.
- **Multiple hominin incursions into Arabia over 400 ka at Khall Amayshan 4** (Groucutt et al. 2021 *Nature* 597:376). Confidence high.
- **Tam Pa Ling redated to 86–68 ka** (Freidline et al. 2023 *Nature Communications*). Older than Hublin's 47–60 ka, and matches the annuaire's "dès 70 000". Confidence high.
- **Lida Ajer, Sumatra, 73–63 ka** (Westaway et al. 2017 *Nature* 548:322). Early moderns in island Southeast Asia. Confidence high.
- **Fuyan/Daoxian teeth shown to be much younger** (Sun X. et al. 2021 *PNAS* 118:e2019158118; ancient DNA plus multi-method dating). Vindicates Hublin's scepticism. Confidence medium-high.
- **Madjedbebe, Australia, about 65 ka** (Clarkson et al. 2017 *Nature* 547:306), challenged by O'Connell et al. 2018 (*PNAS*). The annuaire's "Malakunanja II ≥60 ka" refers to the same site. Confidence medium.
- **Toba**: occupation continues across the eruption in northern India at Dhaba (Clarkson et al. 2020 *Nature Communications* 11:961). Revives Petraglia's case, but still with no fossils. Confidence medium-high.
- **Main Neanderthal admixture dated about 50–43 ka.** Iasi et al. 2024 *Science* (about 47 ka, lasting about 7,000 years), and Sümer et al. 2025 *Nature* (genomes from Ranis and Zlatý kůň). Places the main successful non-African dispersal after about 50 ka, consistent with Mallick's single-late-exit picture. Confidence high (verify exact volume and pages).
- **Bacho Kiro (Hublin et al. 2020 *Nature* 581:299; Hajdinjak et al. 2021 *Nature*) and Ranis (Mylopotamitaki et al. 2024 *Nature*)**: early modern Europeans from about 45 ka, some lineages without living descendants. Confirms his "ghost wave" point. Also the IUP spreading from the Levant. Boker Tachtit Emiran redated to about 50–49 ka (Boaretto et al. 2021 *PNAS*). Confidence high.
- **Grotte Mandrin, France, about 54 ka** (Slimak et al. 2022 *Science Advances*). A possible early, failed modern incursion into Europe, though still not the 80 ka that Timmermann predicted. Confidence medium.
- **Climate-dispersal modelling**: follow-ups by Timmermann and colleagues (e.g. Beyer et al. 2021 *PNAS* on habitability windows; verify), and Arabian humid-phase speleothem series (Nicholson et al. 2020 *Nature Ecology & Evolution*; verify). Confidence medium.
- **Pagani's 2% xOoA signal** has not been widely replicated, and most later work favours one main exit plus Denisovan admixture. Confidence medium.
- **Denisovans**: the Harbin cranium identified as Denisovan by proteins and mtDNA (Fu Q. et al. 2025, *Science* and *Cell*; verify exact venues). Gives the "mysterious" eastern population a face. Confidence medium-high.

## 8. Interactive ideas

1. **Sea-level and strait slider (predict, then reveal).** The learner drags a sea-level curve (Spratt & Lisiecki 2016 stack, open data) and watches the bathymetry of Bab el-Mandeb and Suez. First prompt: "At the Last Glacial Maximum, could you walk across?" Reveal: about 15–20 m of water remains, so you need a boat. Second reveal: lowstands coincide with arid phases. Data: GEBCO bathymetry (open) plus the sea-level stack.
2. **Green Sahara time machine.** A timeline from 150 to 40 ka linking vegetation/lake maps, sapropel layers, speleothem humid phases and dated sites (Skhul, Qafzeh, Misliya, Jebel Faya, Aybut, Al Wusta, Alathar). The learner finds the windows and sees sites cluster within them. Data: the Larrasoaña 2013 maps are published figures; site dates can be compiled (ROAD database).
3. **"How do we know?" fossil-claim tribunal.** Cards for Liujiang, Zhirendong, Daoxian, Tam Pa Ling, Qafzeh and Al Wusta. The learner scores each on excavation context, what was dated (layer or fossil), method, replication and morphology. Then reveal the 2026 status (Daoxian redated, Tam Pa Ling made older). Teaches Hublin's criteria.
4. **Ghost-lineage coalescent simulator.** Simulate an early exit at 120 ka and a later one at 60 ka, with an extinction probability for the first. Show that present-day mtDNA coalescence lands at about 60 ka whatever happened earlier. A "Mellars vs Petraglia" reveal. Can be built client-side with a simple coalescent; no external data needed.

## 9. The seminar: Anthony E. Marks (SMU, Dallas), "Out of Africa: how, when and why"

Speaker: Anthony E. Marks, Professor Emeritus, Southern Methodist University. Transcribed "Tony Marx". He worked in Sudanese Nubia, the Negev (Boker Tachtit, the type Emiran site), Crimea, Portugal, and in Oman (Dhofar) and the UAE (Jebel Faya, with H.-P. Uerpmann, S. Armitage, K. Bretzke and J. Rose). He spoke in English. **The first 10-minute chunk was machine-translated into French by the transcriber**; from 0:10 the text is in English.

**Thesis.** Our species arose in Africa and is now alone; everything in between is uncertain. Marks dismantles the post-1980s package that linked anatomical modernity, Upper Palaeolithic culture (the Aurignacian or Emiran as the template for "modern behaviour") and a single exit at about 50 ka. He argues there were at least three exits between 130 and 100 ka: Sinai to Skhul and Qafzeh, a Jebel Faya Assemblage C-type movement, and the Nubian Complex into Arabia. All were technologically "pre-modern" and best explained as simple range expansion into newly green, empty Arabia. There is no evidence for an exit at 50 ka. Behavioural modernity then developed *in situ* in Arabia and the southern Levant over about 50 ka, through fusion of Arabian Nubian and Levantine traditions (via the "Mudayyan"), producing the Emiran. It was driven by climatic swings that alternated dispersal and aggregation in refugia.

**Outline.**
- **[0:00] The 1980s paradigm.** Genetics placed origins in Africa, and Skhul and Qafzeh "progressive Neanderthals" were reclassified as moderns, which overturned multiregionalism. Mousterian equalled Neanderthal (Kenneth Oakley, 1950s) and Upper Palaeolithic equalled modern. Since the Emiran is the first Upper Palaeolithic and the closest to Africa, it was assumed to be the African carriers' culture, with a genetic exit date of 60–40 ka matching. This produced cognitive dissonance: moderns making Mousterian, and Africa full of "Mousterian" with no Neanderthals. The field chose to decouple anatomical from behavioural modernity. Hence Richard Klein's neural switch at about 50 ka.
- **[0:10] Critique.** Africa is huge (Mercator distortion), so a "plague-like" spread of a switch is implausible. McBrearty & Brooks (2000) showed "the revolution that wasn't". The Levantine 130–80 ka moderns were cast as "cultural underachievers" replaced by Neanderthals. The Sinai route was favoured: Ramesses II, the Hittites, Moses ("it took him 42 years"). It was testable, and it failed: the Emiran is absent from Africa. Its closest African analogue, the Taramsan (transcribed "Paramson"; Taramsa Hill, Egypt), lacks Levallois points and bidirectional reduction.
- **[0:20] The southern route via Howiesons Poort fails too.** Mellars' backed lunates are about 60 ka, about 4,000 miles away from Arabia, inland and not maritime. Abdur (Eritrea) bivalves turned out not to be humanly exploited. A survey of the Dhofar coast and its roughly 2,000 m escarpment found only Lower Palaeolithic or Holocene sites, with a trivial submerged shelf. New picture: Arabia had grasslands and lakes (the largest over 1,000 km²), with wet phases at about 125, 100 and 80 ka, and pockets at 70–60 ka. **Jebel Faya** (Sharjah, UAE) is the only Arabian rock shelter with intact Upper Pleistocene deposits spanning about 100 ka; almost all others are strictly Holocene. Assemblage C (about 125 ka) combines bifacial foliates, Levallois, blades and "core axes", similar to the East African MSA.
- **[0:30] Nubian Complex.** Nubian cores (two distal removals giving a steep ridge) first appear on Sai Island (Nile) at about 160 ka and in central Egypt by 125 ka. Northern Egypt is a taphonomic blank. The Arabian Nubian dates to at least 106 ka (Aybut al-Auwal, Dhofar, OSL), maybe 125 ka. The Arabian variant makes points and more elongated products, whereas the African one makes flakes. It is confined to western and central Arabia, so it is "not the group that went to Australia". It developed into the **Mudayyan**: surface sites only, lighter patina, clustered at fossil springs (drier climate, lower mobility), so younger than about 80 ka, maybe about 50 ka. Flatter cores yield points plus blades, as in the Emiran. But there are few retouched tools.
- **[0:40] Links.** The nearest Mudayyan and Emiran sites are about 2,000 km apart and the Mudayyan is undated. Nubian-like sites occur in northern Arabia and the Negev, and Levantine "Tabun B" (Late Levantine Mousterian, about 65–60 ka) assemblages occur in central and southern Arabia. Contact was "proven" by Manot 1 (about 55 ka), which Marks calls "a clear Neanderthal-sapiens hybrid". **This is an overstatement**: Hershkovitz et al. 2015 described it as modern, and hybrid status is not established. Our Neanderthal genes are not "stimulus diffusion". Conclusion: three exits at 130–100 ka, none at 50 ka. The shells ("I would argue about those shells") do not count for him. He disagrees with Hublin: good climate *encourages* movement.
- **[0:50] Why Arabia mattered.** Klein's quip on the Faya *Science* paper (Armitage et al. 2011): "Africa expanded". Pan-Saharo-Arabian savanna. Situational emergence of modern behaviour: varied topography, low densities, encounters between African and Levantine groups. Refugia in the Yemeni highlands, Dhofar and the "Ur-Schatt" valley (transcribed "Urshak"), now the Persian Gulf, flooded only after about 10 ka (Rose's "Gulf Oasis"). Wet-phase dispersal and dry-phase aggregation spread innovations. By 50 ka Arabians and southern Levantines had interbred and merged technologies into the Emiran. As Arabia dried and the northern Negev became wetter, people moved north, and the two regions' cultural paths split at about 50 ka.

**How it complements the lecture.** Marks supplies the Arabian archaeology that Hublin explicitly hands over to him (Nubian cores, Faya, lake phases). He also gives a possible answer to Hublin's puzzle about why Skhul and Qafzeh lack Nubian cores: different routes and populations, Sinai versus Arabia. The two disagree on climate (barrier versus attractor) and on the value of shell beads as signs of modernity. Marks is far more radical than Hublin in denying any exit at about 50 ka. Post-2016 admixture dating (Iasi 2024; Sümer 2025) and the Bacho Kiro and Ranis genomes favour Hublin's (and Mallick's) late main dispersal. The Arabian fossils and footprints (Al Wusta, Alathar) back Marks' early Arabian expansions.
