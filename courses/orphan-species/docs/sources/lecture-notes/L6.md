# Lecture 6 (15 Nov 2016): "Les peuplements modernes de l'Europe"
*Seminar: Jiří Svoboda (Institute of Archaeology, Czech Academy of Sciences, Brno; also Masaryk University, Brno), "At the eve of the last glaciation: mammoth hunters in Central Europe"*

Sources: `transcripts/L6.txt` (about 67 min, 7 chunks), `transcripts/S6-svoboda.txt` (about 33 min, 4 chunks), annuaire §12–13 and the seminar abstract in `summaries.txt`. There is no Q&A in either transcript.

## 1. Thesis

Once the Neanderthals are gone (c. 40 ka cal BP), the European Upper Palaeolithic is a quick succession of techno-complexes: Aurignacian, Gravettian, Solutrean/Epigravettian, Magdalenian, Epipalaeolithic. Three things run through all of them: blade and bladelet production, the systematic use of bone, antler and ivory, and an explosion of ornaments, art and music with no Neanderthal equivalent. Hublin argues that these named cultures are **not** ethno-linguistic peoples who replaced one another. Ancient genomes show that, from about 37 ka onwards, one interlinked set of European lineages carried on through changing "fashions" in stone tools. The real break was climatic. The Last Glacial Maximum (LGM, about 21 ka, the peak of MIS 2) split Europe into southern refugia, shrank populations, cut the cultural sequence in two (Solutrean in the west, Epigravettian in Italy and the east) and opened the way for gene flow from the Near East after about 14 ka. Two methodological lessons sit alongside this. First, radiocarbon ages must be read carefully (BP versus cal BP, contamination), and direct AMS dating has thrown many "classic" Upper Palaeolithic fossils out of the record. Second, the earliest modern Europeans (Ust'-Ishim, Oase) belong to pioneer waves with few or no descendants. He closes on **biological inertia**: the immigrants from the tropics kept tropical limb proportions and dark skin for tens of millennia, so "becoming European" in phenotype took 30,000 to 40,000 years.

## 2. Timestamped outline

**[0:00] Framing: climate after the replacement**
- Today begins a trilogy on modern human settlement: Europe, then Australia (L7), then the Americas and Arctic latitudes (L8).
- Recap: Neanderthal replacement happened between about 50 and 40 ka, in mid-MIS 3, a milder but unstable stage that was still much colder than today.
- After that comes the slide into MIS 2 and the LGM, then rapid warming towards the Holocene, interrupted by the Younger Dryas ("dryas récent"; the transcript has "leçène" for Holocène). This warming ends both the Pleistocene and the Palaeolithic.
- Aside: the speed of the late-glacial warming gives a sense of what accelerated modern warming could do.
- Key framing: the LGM "cuts the Upper Palaeolithic in two", culturally and biologically.

**[0:04] Maps of LGM Europe**
- He shows a map of Europe at maximum ice extent. The Channel is dry land, parts of the Mediterranean shelf are exposed, and northern and central Europe are polar desert where humans cannot live.
- "Retreat" need not mean long migrations. It can mean local extinction in some areas and survival in others.
- He shows a map of permafrost types in France: seasonal near the Atlantic, continuous or even deep permafrost in the continental interior. This is probably from Bertran et al.'s permafrost mapping (verify).
- He shows an **eco-cultural niche model** that uses the eco-geographic profile of known LGM sites to predict habitable zones. It picks out three separated southern areas: south-west Europe, the Italian peninsula, and the Balkans plus the east. This is probably Banks, d'Errico, Zilhão et al. (verify exact paper).
- Consequences: smaller populations and divergent cultures.

**[0:07] The techno-complex sequence**
- He shows a simplified chart. The Aurignacian (Proto-, Early, later) is pan-European at first, then comes the Gravettian (with a plug for Svoboda's talk). Near the LGM the west goes Solutrean (he shows bifacial "feuilles de laurier" points) while Italy and the east go Epigravettian. After that come the Magdalenian (west) and the Epipalaeolithic.
- The framework is old, built by Gabriel de Mortillet and Henri Breuil on *fossiles directeurs* (diagnostic artefact types such as laurel leaves or Gravette points). The Aurignacian was already named in the late 19th century.
- Aside: "the Upper Palaeolithic alone could fill a year of lectures."
- The old assumption was that each techno-complex was a people, sometimes replacing the previous one, because they have neat boundaries in time and space.
- That assumption is now challenged. (1) Art does not respect the stone-tool boundaries: there is an Aurignaco-Gravettian art block and a Solutreo-Magdalenian block. (2) Genetics shows continuity of the same populations across several techno-complexes.

**[0:13] Technology**
- Blades come from volumetrically managed cores (recalling the IUP lecture). They were used as they were, retouched into end-scrapers and burins, or snapped into segments.
- A parallel bladelet line comes from small cores. Aurignacian "carinated scrapers" (*grattoirs carénés*) are really bladelet cores. The bladelets armed composite projectiles at the tip or along the edges.
- Blades and bladelets are also present in the Châtelperronian, which he attributes to late Neanderthals.
- Osseous technology uses bone, ivory and antler (mostly reindeer). Split-based antler points (*sagaies à base fendue*) appear from the Aurignacian, probably thrown with spear-throwers. Single- and double-row barbed harpoons appear at the end of the Upper Palaeolithic.
- Magdalenian spear-throwers (*propulseurs*) have hooks carved as animals, which makes them works of art. Simpler throwers, including flexible thong or textile slings, may be older but leave no trace.
- His favourite object is the **Early Aurignacian rope-making tool from the Swabian Jura** (Hohle Fels; the transcript has "Jura-Suabe"). It is ivory, the holes carry spiral grooves, and fibres pulled through it twist into cord of any length. He says three holes, but the published piece has four (see §10).
- "On est à 40 000." Neanderthals were still here at 42–43 ka and never made anything remotely like it.

**[0:20] Ornaments, music, cave art**
- Hard animal materials are also used for ornaments and portable art, which implies a corpus of myth that is now lost.
- First musical instruments: bird-bone flutes and composite ivory flutes made of several parts (Hohle Fels and Geissenklösterle).
- He debunks "Mousterian flutes" (Divje Babe is implied). The holes match the spacing of hyena canines. The mustard-glass analogy (paraphrased): you can make music with mustard jars, but that does not make them instruments.
- Cave art starts early, but preservation is biased because sites near entrances were lost. Aurignacian examples are **Chauvet-Pont-d'Arc** and **Arcy-sur-Cure** (the transcript has "Arcis"). Their technical and stylistic complexity implies a long prior history.

**[0:23] Parenthesis: radiocarbon dating**
- Radiocarbon is the method of choice for the Upper Palaeolithic, usable back to about 45–50 ka "with luck".
- Principle: cosmic rays turn nitrogen into ¹⁴C (6 protons, 8 neutrons). There is about one ¹⁴C atom per 10¹² carbon atoms. Living organisms stay in equilibrium with the atmosphere, uptake stops at death, and the half-life is 5,730 years.
- ¹⁴C ages are not calendar ages because atmospheric ¹⁴C has varied. Post-1950 bomb carbon is a special case. Dendrochronology showed the mismatch for the Holocene, and calibration curves followed.
- Until a few years before 2016, the curve did not cover the full range, so ages older than about 25 ka in the literature were uncalibrated. By 2016 it reached about 50 ka (IntCal13).
- **[0:30] "BP" means radiocarbon years before 1950, and "cal BP" means calibrated.** For example, 35 ka ¹⁴C BP is about 40 ka cal BP, and the gap widens further back in time.
- Contamination: he shows a graph. With 1% modern carbon, a 20 ka sample comes out about 1,000 years too young. At 35–40 ka the error makes the date useless, hence careful chemical pre-treatment of charcoal or bone.
- **AMS** counts atoms and allows direct dating of objects and fossils, but still needs about 500 mg of bone ("a piece this big" on a skull). Curators balk, so small ornaments and art objects are not dated directly. His lab (MPI-EVA, with Sahra Talamo) was working on much smaller samples.

**[0:33] Direct dating dethrones "classic" fossils**
- Many fossils in Upper Palaeolithic layers turned out to be intrusive, either later burials dug in or the result of hasty old excavation.
- **Vogelherd / "Stetten"** (Germany) was long held to be Aurignacian and is Neolithic. **Combe-Capelle**, thought to be Châtelperronian, is Mesolithic. Other cases are listed on his slide.
- **Riparo Mezzena mandible** (Monti Lessini, Veneto; the place is not named in the transcript) had been published as a Neanderthal–modern hybrid. His team redated it to about 6,300–6,400 years, "weeks ago" (Talamo et al. 2016).
- **Cro-Magnon** (Les Eyzies, 1868; he shows Cro-Magnon 1) is the emblem of the Aurignacian "replacer". Dating of the *Littorina* shell beads in the burial shows it is Upper Palaeolithic, but much younger than the Aurignacian and probably Gravettian.

**[0:37] Earliest moderns: the IUP**
- **Ksar Akil** (Lebanon) and **Üçağızlı** (Turkey, near the Syrian border) have very fragmentary remains. He doubts Ksar Akil's IUP remains are informative. He has seen the Üçağızlı teeth and they are "modern, these modern humans".
- **Bacho Kiro** (Bulgaria, Bachokirian) had human remains that have been lost. (This was soon to change; see §7.)
- The **Bohunician** (Moravia) occurs in loess, which does not preserve bone, so there are no fossils.
- **Ust'-Ishim** femur (western Siberia) was picked up on a riverbank without context but is directly dated to about 45 ka. It is the oldest morphologically and genetically modern fossil in this part of Eurasia, with a high-coverage genome.

**[0:40] Ust'-Ishim and D-statistics**
- Comparing it with present-day populations misleads, because Neolithic and later migrations reshuffled Eurasia.
- Compared instead with a Western European Mesolithic genome and an Upper Palaeolithic Siberian genome, Ust'-Ishim has **D ≈ 0**. It is equally distant from both, so it predates the split between Europeans and Asians and possibly left no descendants. It belongs to a pioneer wave that died out.

**[0:42] Proto-Aurignacian and Oase**
- The Proto-Aurignacian is rooted by many in the Levantine **Early Ahmarian**. **Egbert** (Ksar Akil) comes from Early Ahmarian levels. He shows a front page of the *Milwaukee Journal*'s "Green Sheet". Egbert is lost, but casts show it was modern.
- Proto-Aurignacian teeth from northern Italy are modern (Benazzi et al. 2015).
- **Peştera cu Oase** (Romania) has a cranium and a mandible, found without context. The mandible is best dated, at about 40 ka cal. The individual had a Neanderthal ancestor only a few generations back, with Neanderthal DNA far above that of other Upper Palaeolithic Europeans.
- Morphology as a hybrid detector: Erik Trinkaus listed Neanderthal-like features (receding frontal, dental and mandibular traits). Hublin is sceptical. Nobody knows how much Neanderthal DNA becomes visible in bone, and odd features may just be archaic.
- He shows **Nazlet Khater 2** (Egypt, about 38 ka), which has an Oase-like frontal but no plausible Neanderthal ancestry.
- He then "maliciously" shows **Vogelherd/Stetten**. Its horizontal-oval (H-O) mandibular foramen, a feature common in Neanderthals (for example La Chapelle-aux-Saints), had been used to call it a hybrid, yet it is Neolithic. Oase also has the H-O foramen. "Je vous laisse juge."
- Oase's genetics show the same trap as Ust'-Ishim. Against present-day populations it looks "Asian". Against ancient genomes it falls between groups, so it probably has few descendants.

**[0:47] Aurignacian people and how they treated the dead**
- Fragmentary but clearly modern remains come from France. He shows four sites, including the **La Quina-Aval** child mandible with its chin; "Quinard à Val" in the transcript is La Quina-Aval. There are also remains from the Swabian Jura.
- There are no complete Aurignacian burials. Instead there are fragmented bones with cut marks made on fresh bone, and **perforated human teeth worn as pendants** (Brassempouy, La Combe).
- Skulls and mandibles are over-represented, which suggests complex secondary mortuary rites. Oase is seen as deposited deep in the cave in the same spirit.
- **Mladeč** (Moravia; the transcript has "Bladesh") has crania in karst with Aurignacian (Mladeč-type) points, directly dated to about 36 ka cal. Svoboda's idea, from a site visit, is that skulls were displayed and fell into the karst.
- **Kostenki 14** (Markina Gora, Russian Don) is the only certain inhumation from an Aurignacian-like context: flexed burial, about 37 ka, studied anatomically, dated directly and sequenced.

**[0:51] The 2016 synthesis: Fu et al., "The genetic history of Ice Age Europe"**
- He shows a tree, with older individuals at the top and an African outgroup. **Ust'-Ishim and Oase sit apart** with no link to later Europeans. **Kostenki 14 already belongs to the European bundle of lineages.**
- From about 37 to 14 ka there is one interrelated set of European groups. A lineage known before the LGM in the north (**Goyet**, Belgium; the transcript has "Goye") reappears in the south during the LGM and helps repopulate Europe afterwards.
- **From about 14 ka**, these lineages mix with incoming groups with Near Eastern affinities (the Villabruna cluster in Fu et al.).
- So the LGM caused contraction and re-expansion *from within Europe*, not extinction followed by replacement. Neolithic and Bronze Age changes are "off topic".

**[0:55] Mitochondrial haplogroup U6, revisited ("la pédagogie, c'est la répétition")**
- U6 is North African but does not root in the African mtDNA tree.
- Two 2016 papers report a basal U6 in about 35 ka individuals from **Peştera Muierii**, Romania (the transcript has "Pestérinuri"). This supports a back-migration from western Eurasia into North Africa, perhaps around the LGM.

**[0:56] Gravettian burials and social complexity**
- From the mid-Upper Palaeolithic, and especially in the Gravettian, burials become lavish. Grave goods represent hundreds of hours of bead-making, which Hublin finds hard to square with egalitarian societies, so some hierarchy is likely.
- **Sunghir** (about 200 km from Moscow) dates to about 33–35 ka. It shows people already at high latitude before the LGM.
- Burials are found across France, Italy and Moravia. The transcript says "épigravétien" here, but Gravettian is meant at least in part. Bodies are covered in ornaments, ochre and clothing, perhaps with ochre masks.
- **Dolní Věstonice triple burial**: multiple burials often include individuals with anomalies or pathologies, and dwarfs are buried with others. The dwarf allusion is presumably Romito 2, an Epigravettian burial in Calabria.
- **[1:00] Krems-Wachtberg** (Austria; the transcript has "Wartberg") holds two newborns, probably twins, covered in red ochre under a mammoth scapula. They are about 25–27 ka ¹⁴C, which is about 30 ka cal. Again: "je fais exprès de vous rabâcher ça".

**[1:01] Physical evolution: body proportions**
- At the end of the glacial period the environment changes dramatically. Upper Palaeolithic traditions break up, cave art disappears, and figurative portable art gives way to geometric motifs as people turn to forest game.
- Recap: the Neanderthal alleles that were adaptively kept concern pigmentation, UV and immunity.
- He shows **Trenton Holliday's PCA of body proportions**. PC1 runs from East and West Africans (linear, long-limbed) to high-latitude people (Norwegians, British). Neanderthals are "hyper-arctic". **Gravettians plot near Sudanese.** This was already an argument in the 1980s and 1990s for a tropical origin.
- Change during the Upper Palaeolithic was slow and "disordered". Limb length changes first, but the brachial and crural indices stay tropical until late, through the Magdalenian and Mesolithic.

**[1:04] Skin colour**
- Light skin in present-day Europeans depends mainly on three genes. Mesolithic genomes overturned expectations.
- **La Braña 1** (Spain, about 7 ka; the transcript has "La Bragna") carries the European allele at only one of the three genes.
- **Loschbour** (Luxembourg, about 8 ka; the transcript has "Lochebourg") carries it at none. Reconstructions show dark skin with light, probably blue, eyes.
- The light-skin mutations had been dated to about 15 ka. They must have stayed confined to some groups and regions before spreading.
- Conclusion: there was **great phenotypic inertia** in proportions, skin and eyes. Tropical immigrants arriving 45–50 ka took tens of thousands of years to look like present-day Europeans.

## 4. Hublin's positions and debates

- **Cultures are not peoples.** He argues against the de Mortillet/Breuil-era idea that each techno-complex was an ethnic group replacing the last. His evidence is (a) art boundaries that do not match lithic boundaries and (b) genetic continuity from about 37 to 14 ka. Caveat for learners: Posth et al. 2023 shows Gravettian-period Europe held at least two distinct gene pools (Fournol and Věstonice), so partial correlation between culture and population does exist.
- **Against morphological hybrid diagnoses.** This is a gentle but pointed dig at Erik Trinkaus, whom he calls "mon collègue", over Oase. His argument: we have no calibration of how much admixture shows in bone, archaic ≠ Neanderthal (Nazlet Khater 2), and the H-O mandibular foramen "evidence" collapsed when Vogelherd turned out Neolithic. The Mezzena redating by his own team is a second example.
- **Sceptical of Mousterian flutes.** Divje Babe-type holes are hyena tooth marks. The mustard-glass analogy is memorable.
- **Pioneer waves left no descendants.** Ust'-Ishim and Oase are presented as dead-end lineages, consistent with his L5 view of an IUP first wave.
- **LGM as contraction, not replacement.** Repopulation came from southern European refugia, and exogenous (Near Eastern) input arrived only after 14 ka.
- **Social hierarchy among hunter-gatherers.** Gravettian burial investment, such as Sunghir's beads, makes egalitarianism hard to sustain in his view. This is open to debate, since it could be ritual investment rather than rank.
- **Phenotypic inertia.** Immigrants adapted slowly to the north (proportions, pigmentation). This implicitly argues against expecting quick climatic adaptation and against reading cold-adapted morphology into early moderns.
- **Rhetorical moves.** He keeps saying he is deliberately repeating the BP versus cal BP point ("je fais exprès de vous rabâcher ça"). He shows the newspaper page for Egbert as a history-of-science moment. He tells the museum curator anecdote about 500 mg. He mentions a site visit to Mladeč with Svoboda. He admits that "the Upper Palaeolithic could fill a year". He is self-ironic about finding the rope tool "extraordinary".

## 5. Concepts a learner must master (in prerequisite order)

1. **Marine Isotope Stages (MIS 3, MIS 2)**: numbered warm and cold phases from ocean-core δ¹⁸O. MIS 3 (about 57–29 ka) was mild but unstable; MIS 2 (about 29–14 ka) holds the LGM.
2. **Last Glacial Maximum (LGM)**: the period of maximum ice volume, about 26–19 ka, peaking around 21 ka. Sea level was about 120 m lower.
3. **Glacial refugia / niche modelling**: areas where populations survived, predicted from the environmental profile of known sites.
4. **Younger Dryas and Holocene**: a brief cold reversal (about 12.9–11.7 ka) before the Holocene began at 11.7 ka.
5. **Techno-complex and *fossile directeur***: a cultural unit defined by recurring artefact types and technology, recognised by type-fossils.
6. **Sequence**: Initial Upper Palaeolithic (IUP), then Proto/Early Aurignacian, then Gravettian (with Pavlovian in Moravia), then Solutrean or Epigravettian, then Magdalenian, then Epipalaeolithic/Mesolithic.
7. **Blade versus bladelet; volumetric core; carinated "scraper" as core**: the two production lines of the Upper Palaeolithic.
8. **Composite projectile, sagaie, spear-thrower, harpoon**: Upper Palaeolithic weapon systems.
9. **Radiocarbon basics**: production, half-life, equilibrium, decay after death.
10. **Calibration (BP versus cal BP), IntCal**: converting ¹⁴C years to calendar years. ¹⁴C BP means before AD 1950.
11. **AMS and pre-treatment** (ultrafiltration, contamination): how small, clean samples allow direct dating.
12. **Intrusive burial / direct dating**: why stratigraphic association is not enough.
13. **Ancient DNA clusters; D-statistics** (ABBA-BABA): a test of whether a sample shares more alleles with one population than another. D = 0 means equidistant.
14. **Admixture and introgression**: the generations-since-admixture logic (Oase).
15. **mtDNA haplogroups (U, U5, U2, U6, M)**: maternal lineages used to trace migrations.
16. **Primary versus secondary burial; grave goods**: reading mortuary behaviour.
17. **Ecogeographic rules (Bergmann, Allen); brachial and crural indices**: body shape follows climate. Brachial index = radius/humerus; crural index = tibia/femur.
18. **Pigmentation genetics (SLC24A5, SLC45A2, HERC2/OCA2)**: alleles behind light skin and blue eyes in Europeans.

## 6. Misconceptions

- "Aurignacians, Gravettians, Magdalenians were different peoples." Largely false for 37–14 ka in his account. It is partly true for sub-structure (Posth et al. 2023).
- "Cro-Magnon Man was the first modern European who replaced the Neanderthals." Cro-Magnon is Gravettian, about 32 ka cal.
- "A radiocarbon date of 35,000 means 35,000 calendar years." It is about 40,000 cal BP.
- "A fossil found in an Upper Palaeolithic layer is Upper Palaeolithic." There are many intrusive Holocene burials (Vogelherd, Combe-Capelle, Mezzena).
- "Odd archaic-looking features mean Neanderthal hybridity." The H-O foramen and receding frontal are poor diagnostics.
- "The earliest moderns are the ancestors of today's Europeans." Ust'-Ishim and Oase have no or few descendants.
- "Neanderthals made flutes." The Divje Babe holes are carnivore damage, in his view.
- "Ice Age Europeans were pale and stocky because it was cold." Gravettians were tall and linear, with tropical proportions. Mesolithic people were dark-skinned with blue eyes.
- "The LGM emptied Europe and it was recolonised from outside." Repopulation came from southern refugia, with Near Eastern input after 14 ka.
- "Comparing ancient genomes with present-day populations tells you ancestry directly." Neolithic and later migrations distort this (Oase looks "Asian").

## 9. The seminar: Jiří Svoboda, "At the eve of the last glaciation: mammoth hunters in Central Europe"

*Speaker: Jiří Svoboda, Institute of Archaeology of the Czech Academy of Sciences (Brno; Dolní Věstonice research centre) and Masaryk University. The transcript mangles his name as "Girizvogoda" and "Jiris Voboda". He speaks in English after a French apology.*

**Thesis.** The Gravettian (about 33–25 ka cal; in Moravia, the Pavlovian of about 31–29 ka cal) was a major social and technological transformation before the LGM. Its people were genetically coherent (the Věstonice cluster), tall and robust with strong sexual dimorphism, and buried their dead with care. In Moravia they were specialised mammoth exploiters living in the gullied foothills of the Pavlov Hills. They already had in principle many "Neolithic" inventions (fired clay, weaving and nets, plant grinding, polished stone, possibly dog-like canids), used for symbolic rather than practical ends. Their art (figurines, possible maps, discs) unites Eurasia from France to Siberia.

**Outline**
- **[0:00] Roots.** He agrees with Hublin ("rare in the jungle of disputes"). He shows a map of two technological lines around 40 ka. The first is IUP (Emiran, Bohunician, Siberia, northern China; the transcript has "Atherian", see §10), with almost no fossils. The second is backed-blade/microblade, which has modern fossils at the start and Neanderthals at the end (Châtelperronian). He calls acculturation "a reasonable answer". These backed-blade industries may be the roots of the Gravettian 10 kyr later.
- **Genetics.** He shows Fu et al. 2016. Around 30 ka there is the "Dolní Věstonice cluster" (Moravia, Italy, Belgium) as the base of the Gravettian population. mtDNA is haplogroup U (U5, U2), with a bottleneck at the LGM and some survival into present-day northern Europeans.
- **Bodies and burials.** **Předmostí** mass burial (late 19th century, over 20 individuals; about 99% destroyed in World War II). Tall, robust, "East African-style" stature with pronounced dimorphism. **Dolní Věstonice triple burial** (excavated in the 1980s; "30 years ago"): all three are male. The central individual (DV 15) could only be sexed genetically because his skeleton was anomalous and intermediate, perhaps the reason he was placed in the middle. A male of about 40 (DV 16) was buried by a hearth inside a hut within a three-unit settlement. Faces are rectangular with quadrangular orbits and massive mandibles, matching faces in the art.
- **Innovations "before Childe's Neolithic".** The transcript's "childhood's claim" is **V. Gordon Childe**. Examples:
  - starch grains of *Typha latifolia* (cattail; "Tifa") on grindstones from Pavlov and Dolní Věstonice, showing flour-making without cultivation;
  - a "strange" range of wolf sizes, and wolf skeletons deposited like human burials at Předmostí (one intact one excavated about two years earlier); he leaves domestication open;
  - fired-clay figurines (animal and human) but no vessels, found broken around kilns or hearths and bearing fingerprints;
  - textile and net impressions identified by **Olga Soffer**, implying weaving and net hunting;
  - polished stone used for ornaments, not axes.
- **[0:10] Lifestyles.** The fauna differs across Europe (horse and reindeer in the north; ibex, gazelle and cervids in the Mediterranean). The Moravian Gravettian, unlike the Aurignacian and Magdalenian before and after it, is mammoth-based. The century-old debate between hunting and scavenging of natural deaths continues. His landscape argument: the sites sit on the slopes of the Pavlov Hills, cut by wet, muddy, spring-fed gullies. Animals singled out from herds along the river could be driven into the gullies, where even a mammoth becomes vulnerable. **Mammoth bone deposits** lie beside settlements (Dolní Věstonice I, Milovice). Counting these would push mammoth to 90–100% of the fauna.
  - Weapons in ivory, stone and probably wood vary in size down to microliths.
  - The fauna is bimodal: mammoth plus hares and birds (nets), with few mid-sized animals.
- **Sites.**
  - Pavlov I was excavated by two major campaigns, which allows a full site plan; its mammoth bone deposit is now displayed in a museum (Archeopark Pavlov).
  - A small 2007 excavation found a single unit: a stone-filled pit, kettle-shaped "boiling pits" and two mammoths. Large sites are palimpsests of such units.
  - **[0:20]** Dolní Věstonice II (excavated up to 2012): a gully mammoth deposit that lay underwater (aquatic snails), so the bones were dumped into a small lake, and fine microstratigraphy of earlier Gravettian layers.
  - Collapsed wine cellars under a village exposed mammoth bones in the ceiling. The dig was too dangerous to continue.
  - Předmostí re-excavation: a mammoth bone deposit about 40 m from the 1894 mass grave suggests the dead were laid in the depository zone, not in the living area. There is now a museum on site.
- **Ethnoarchaeology.** Reindeer herders' camps in the north (chums, hearths, a male/female split of the tent, a sacred place, children's play areas, traces across the landscape, antler deposits marking sacred places). He compares Bohuslav Klíma's stone circle around a hearth at Dolní Věstonice with Arctic stone rings and North American "tipi rings".
- **Art.**
  - Gravettian figurines unite Eurasia from France to Siberia, and there is some Gravettian cave art in the Urals.
  - Obese versus pregnant females, with repeated stone impacts on pregnant bellies; old and young women.
  - Ceramic females wear belts or cords.
  - The "Venus of Dolní Věstonice" (probably the ivory piece meant here) mixes male and female symbolism.
  - **[0:30]** A monumental mammoth. Bestiary: mammoth, rhino and lion are prominent, but rhino and lion were not hunted, so "hunting magic doesn't work here". Art shows the most imposing animals, not prey.
  - **Maps**: engraved mammoth tusks (Pavlov; Předmostí) match local topography, with a circle where the site should be. He compares a Native American pictographic map of a horse raid.
  - Geometric body decoration typical of the Central European Gravettian.
  - Last slide: perforated stone discs from Gravettian burials, compared with Chinese and Korean *bi* discs (symbols of heaven) and Siberian shamans' discs, as possible archetypes.

**How the seminar complements the lecture.** Hublin gives the Europe-wide frame (climate, chronology, dating, genetics and phenotype). Svoboda zooms into the richest Gravettian region and gives the "lived" picture: subsistence, settlement units, technology and art. Both talks use the Fu et al. 2016 genetic tree, agree on the IUP "first line" and the role of acculturation, and use the Gravettian burials (Předmostí, DV triple) as evidence of social complexity. Svoboda's tall "East African-style" statures confirm Hublin's tropical body-proportion argument.
