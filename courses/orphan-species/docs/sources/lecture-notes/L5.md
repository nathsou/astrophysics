# Lecture 5 — "Le remplacement" (8 November 2016)

Seminar: Matthias Meyer (Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology, Leipzig), "Insights into Recent Human Evolutionary History from Sequencing Archaic Human Genomes".

Sources read in full: `transcripts/L5.txt` (about 70 min, 7 chunks) and `transcripts/S5-meyer.txt` (about 55 min, 6 chunks). I checked them against the official abstract (`summaries.txt`) and Hublin's annuaire summary (§10–11).

## 1. Thesis

Hublin argues that modern humans did replace Neanderthals (and Denisovans), but the replacement was neither simple nor instantaneous. He calls it a "remplacement avec des fuites", a leaky replacement. First, there was some genetic absorption of archaic groups. Neanderthal DNA makes up about 2% of non-African genomes, but selection filtered it heavily, leaving archaic-free deserts (especially on the X chromosome) and a few dozen positively selected genes, mostly for immunity and local adaptation. Second, the replacement came in several waves with different fates. In the Levant, early moderns at about 130–90 ka gave way to Neanderthals again (Kebara, Tabun, Amud). Then an Initial Upper Palaeolithic (IUP) wave of about 50–44 ka, rooted in the Negev (Boker Tachtit, Emirian) and reaching Moravia (Bohunician), left almost no descendants (Ust'-Ishim). The decisive wave was the Early Ahmarian / Proto-Aurignacian / Early Aurignacian from about 46–42 ka. Third, archaic and modern populations overlapped for at least about 5,000 years at continental scale. Hublin argues that this explains why the late Neanderthal "transitional" industries, especially the Châtelperronian with its ornaments and bone tools, look Upper Palaeolithic. He reads this as acculturation by stimulus diffusion (Kroeber), not independent invention. Natural catastrophes (Heinrich Event 4, the Campanian Ignimbrite eruption) cannot explain the replacement, because modern humans were already in Italy before them. All other human forms disappeared wherever *H. sapiens* arrived, which points to the newcomers themselves. The Denisovan side of the story is still almost entirely unknown.

## 2. Timestamped outline

**[0:00] Introduction and framing**
- Recap of L4: moderns out of Africa, first into the Levant and Arabia, possibly early into southern Asia.
- Scope: mostly the Near East and Europe, "the only region of the world where we roughly know what happened". Elsewhere we only see a "before" and an "after".
- Announces Meyer's seminar and a lecture heavy on prehistory and archaeology.
- Key message: "un remplacement avec des fuites" (a replacement with leaks). Several waves, interactions with locals. Continues next week with the European Upper Palaeolithic (L6).
- Shows the familiar phylogeny slide. Neanderthals plus an Asian sister group (Denisovans) evolving in Eurasia for at least 500 kyr. *H. sapiens* in Africa. Late *H. erectus*-like dwarf forms on "one Indonesian island" (Flores). Every branch except ours ends around 50–40 ka.

**[~0:05] Climate backdrop: MIS 5–2**
- Even-numbered MIS are cold, odd-numbered are temperate. MIS 5e is the last interglacial, when moderns reached the Levant at about 130–125 ka. MIS 4 is a strong cold peak, Neanderthal times. MIS 3 is "supposed to be warm" but is cool and highly unstable. MIS 2 is the last glaciation. Hublin misspeaks, saying the "last interglacial" peaks at 18–20 ka; he means the Last Glacial Maximum.
- Shows a North Atlantic benthic δ¹⁸O curve from marine carbonates (foraminifera). It is blurred by bioturbation ("animals burrowing the seafloor").
- Then shows the Greenland ice-core δ¹⁸O curve: annual layers, far higher resolution. MIS 3 is full of abrupt jumps (Dansgaard–Oeschger oscillations, not named).
- Climatic instability probably modulated movements and demography.

**[~0:09] The Levantine see-saw**
- Moderns in the southern Levant (Israel only, i.e. Skhul/Qafzeh) from about 130–120 ka until about 90–80 ka.
- Then Neanderthals return: Kebara, Tabun, Amud, between about 80 and 60–50 ka.
- Moderns restart from about 60–50 ka. With similar technologies, the expansion was "not an irresistible movement".

**[~0:11] Palaeogenetics of admixture**
- Shows a gene-flow diagram: Neanderthal→modern, an earlier modern→Neanderthal flow, Denisovan↔Neanderthal, Denisovan→modern. Archaic proportions are tenths of a percent to a few percent.
- Shows a 2016 figure: % Neanderthal DNA in Upper Palaeolithic individuals from 45 ka to the present (Fu et al. 2016). Oase is set aside as an outlier "caught a few generations after" admixture. Equilibrated individuals carry about 4–5% at about 40 ka, declining under selection to just under 2% in living Europeans, with individual variation.
- If everyone's ~2% were pooled, 20–40% of the Neanderthal genome survives, but nobody carries it all.
- Shows a chromosome painting with white "deserts" of Neanderthal ancestry, due to drift in the founding admixture plus selection.
- Shows a 2014 scatterplot of archaic-derived high-frequency alleles in Europeans (y-axis) vs East Asians (x-axis): signatures of positive selection.
- Aside on gene count: an early guess of ~2 million genes, now about 20,000 protein-coding genes, about 1.5% of DNA. Only "two or three dozen" coding genes show clear archaic adaptive introgression. None is fixed in all non-Africans.
- Examples: a Denisovan allele for high altitude in Tibetans (EPAS1, not named); UV adaptation, skin quality and colour, freckles ("rather cosmetic"); and above all immunity genes (refers back to Quintana-Murci, S1).

**[~0:20] X chromosome and testis deserts**
- Far less Neanderthal ancestry on X than on autosomes. This is a classic signature of reduced fertility in the heterogametic sex (male hybrids).
- Genes expressed in testes are depleted of Neanderthal alleles.
- Two explanations: partial male hybrid sterility, or demographic/social factors limiting male hybrids' reproduction.

**[~0:21] Archaeology: why it matters**
- Human remains are often missing, so archaeological "proxies" are used.
- The Middle-to-Upper Palaeolithic change is "probably the most important thing" in Pleistocene prehistory before the Neolithic.
- New in the Upper Palaeolithic: symbolic objects, musical instruments, cave art, systematic use of osseous materials, and long-range projectile weapons (spear-thrower, sling and bow vs Neanderthal thrusting and throwing spears).
- The Middle/Upper Palaeolithic boundary is much sharper than the Lower/Middle one.

**[~0:25] The stratigraphic sandwich and the "transitional" industries**
- Late Middle Palaeolithic, about 50–45 ka: Mousterian of Acheulean Tradition (MTA), Discoid-Denticulate Mousterian, Micoquian (Germany, Central Europe).
- Above it, the Aurignacian, long taken as the first Upper Palaeolithic and the marker of modern arrival.
- In between are "transitional" industries, named so because "we don't know where to put them". Some are genuine assemblages, not mixtures: Châtelperronian, Uluzzian, LRJ (Lincombian–Ranisian–Jerzmanowician, "a terrible name"), Szeletian.
- The IUP has been split off from these because it looks intrusive in Europe, not rooted in local Middle Palaeolithic. The boundary is fuzzy, e.g. the Bohunician.

**[~0:30] The Initial Upper Palaeolithic wave**
- Bohunician (Moravia, around Brno): blades with Levallois-like typology, Upper Palaeolithic tools (endscrapers, burins), and bifacial leaf points like the Micoquian (and some African MSA).
- Its closest parallel is Boker Tachtit in the Negev, excavated by Anthony Marks (the S4 speaker). It shows an in-situ transition from Levallois-flavoured debitage to volumetric blade production (bipolar cores, faceted platforms). Marks sees this as the source of a modern population spreading east and west.
- Dating: Boker Tachtit's published dates are about 50 ka with huge errors, and unpublished dates exist. The Emirian groups similar Levantine sites. The oldest Bohunician dates are about 48 ka by TL.
- Chain of similar sites between the Negev and Brno. Üçağızlı (Hatay, Turkey, near the Syrian border) has shell ornaments.
- Makers: Ksar Akil (Lebanon) is lost to archaeology through the civil war, and many finds are lost too. A maxilla from IUP levels (about 45–50 ka, disputed), described as rather modern; this is presumably "Ethelruda".
- Manot 1 (western Galilee): fully modern calvaria with high maximum breadth in posterior view. U-Th on its calcite crust gives about 55 ka. The problem is that it was found outside stratigraphy.
- Ust'-Ishim (transcript conflates it with Manot; text likely dropped): femur found on a riverbank by mammoth-ivory hunters. Directly dated to 45 ka, morphologically and genetically modern.
- Conclusion: a first modern wave between about 50 and 44 ka from the Near East through Anatolia into Eastern and Central Europe. Nothing yet in Western Europe; "some people have ideas" (almost certainly an allusion to Slimak's Neronian at Grotte Mandrin, published 2022).
- At the same time Neanderthals were a few hundred to thousand km away, making MTA and Micoquian-derived industries.

**[~0:40] The second wave: Early Ahmarian, Proto-Aurignacian, Early Aurignacian**
- The chunk opens mid-sentence; text has probably been lost.
- Proto-Aurignacian: large straight pointed bladelets, almost certainly projectile armatures. Found from the Near East to Western Europe under regional names.
- Several authors link the Early Ahmarian (Levant) and the Proto-Aurignacian as one wave moving along the northern Mediterranean shore.
- Remains: fragmentary teeth in the Western European Proto-Aurignacian. The Ksar Akil skull (lost; photo, description and cast survive) is a modern human associated with the Early Ahmarian. This is "Egbert", Ksar Akil 1.
- Dates: Early Ahmarian about 47–46 ka. Proto-Aurignacian in northern Italy and southern France about 42 ka. North of the Alps the Early Aurignacian; south of them the Proto-Aurignacian. Two sites, one in Germany and one in Austria (Geissenklösterle, Willendorf II), give about 43 ka, roughly 5,000 years after the Bohunician.
- In France the Proto-Aurignacian usually lies below the Early Aurignacian, with some late Proto-Aurignacian in central France. The Aurignacian spreads to Britain and everywhere.
- Every human remain associated with the Aurignacian is modern.
- Peștera cu Oase 1 mandible: about 40 ka, outside stratigraphy, age fits early Aurignacian. A modern human with a Neanderthal ancestor 4–6 generations back, so Neanderthals were still around when the second wave arrived.

**[~0:45] Transitional industries in detail**
- Shows a map. Châtelperronian: central France and northern Spain, not east of the Rhône. Uluzzian: southern Italy, one northern Italian site (Fumane), Greece. LRJ: north-west Europe, England to Poland. These overlap in time with the Proto- and Early Aurignacian (from about 43–42 ka).
- **Châtelperronian**: the most debated. The Châtelperron point/knife is a curved backed blade. The lithics have a very Upper Palaeolithic look.
- Neanderthal remains at Saint-Césaire (Charente-Maritime; partial skeleton with skull and mandible) and Arcy-sur-Cure, Grotte du Renne (Yonne; fragmentary, many isolated teeth). They are Neanderthal by morphology, genetics and palaeoproteomics.
- Grotte du Renne has pendants of perforated teeth, fossils and worked ivory, unique in the Neanderthal world. It also has a bone industry (awls) and bladelets.

**[~0:51] The integrity debate and stimulus diffusion**
- Sceptics invoke layer mixing.
- Hublin's team re-dated Grotte du Renne: more than 40 dates respecting stratigraphy, incompatible with large-scale mixing. Neanderthal bones would have had to move up from the Mousterian, or Aurignacian ornaments down.
- "A few weeks ago" hominin remains from a Châtelperronian level were directly dated to a Châtelperronian age, matching the Saint-Césaire skeleton (Welker et al. 2016, PNAS, Sept 2016).
- Two interpretations. (a) Independent convergent evolution: Neanderthals invented the same things just centuries before moderns arrived. Hublin calls this an extraordinary coincidence after 400–500 kyr of separation. (b) Hublin's view: **stimulus diffusion** (A. L. Kroeber). Contact with a concept leads a group to adopt it and implement it with its own methods, not by direct imitation. He floats the possibility of hybrids within Châtelperronian groups: "the future will tell".
- **LRJ**: bifacial and unifacial leaf points plus Upper Palaeolithic burins and endscrapers. No certain human association. At Spy (Meuse valley, Belgium), two Neanderthal skeletons were dug out by 19th-century methods, so they cannot be assigned to a layer. Direct dates fit the LRJ better than the local Mousterian, but this is "still to be demonstrated".
- **Uluzzian**: "a world of passion and combat". It is rooted in the local Mousterian, with bone tools and shell ornaments (*Dentalium*), about 45/44–40 ka. Long thought to be an Italian Châtelperronian. In 2011 a re-study of two deciduous teeth from Grotta del Cavallo found them modern (Benazzi et al. 2011). The morphology is not disputed, but their stratigraphic integrity is "vehemently" contested.

**[~0:59–1:00] Extinction causes**
- Text is lost at the chunk boundary. It resumes with the key argument: not only Neanderthals but *all* other human forms disappeared wherever moderns spread.
- A special issue of *La Recherche* listed about ten possible causes. It concluded that the cause was unknown but certainly not modern humans. Hublin calls this a wish to exonerate ourselves.
- Heinrich events: collapse of North Atlantic overturning circulation (the Gulf Stream system), with icebergs off Western Europe. Sea-surface temperatures fell by about 15 °C in about 70 years, perceptible within 2–3 generations. H4 (just after 40 ka) has been linked to Neanderthal extinction ("an empty continent").
- Campanian Ignimbrite (Phlegraean Fields, near Naples), about 39 ka. Ash across the eastern Mediterranean and as far as Kostenki (Russia).
- Refutation: shows a chart of industries against the CI tephra (red line) and H4 (dark band). The Proto-Aurignacian in Italy predates both, and the eruption had little effect in Western Europe.
- What is plausible: temperate phases helped. GI/GIS 12 may have favoured the IUP entry into Central Europe. That attempt aborted with a cold episode, followed by the "true" modern peopling with the Proto-Aurignacian and Aurignacian.

**[~1:05] Denisovans and close**
- Anatomy is barely known. Many Chinese fossils older than 50–100 ka are probably Denisovans.
- Elevated Denisovan ancestry is found in Australia, Papua New Guinea and a little in southern Asia, far from the Altai. Their range was vast, and their replacement is "all still to learn".
- Closes with a photo of a grizzly × polar bear hybrid (a wink at interspecific hybridisation).

## 4. Hublin's positions and debates

- **Against simple replacement, and against multiregionalism.** "Remplacement avec des fuites" (leaky replacement) is his middle position: the genome is overwhelmingly African-derived, with a minor, selection-filtered archaic component. He keeps stressing how small and "cosmetic" the adaptive archaic contribution is (a few dozen genes, none universal). This implicitly pushes back on media hype about Neanderthal heritage.
- **Several waves, not one front.** First, the Levantine see-saw at about 130–50 ka. Second, the IUP wave (about 50–44 ka), "une tentative avortée" (an aborted attempt). Third, the Ahmarian/Aurignacian wave that stuck. This framework was later strongly vindicated by his own Bacho Kiro excavation (2020) and by aDNA (Zlatý kůň, Bacho Kiro and Ranis show no or little descendance).
- **Neanderthals made the Châtelperronian.** He takes this side firmly against Zilhão, d'Errico, Bar-Yosef and Higham et al. 2010 (who argued for mixing at Grotte du Renne). He relies on his own 2012 re-dating and the 2016 palaeoproteomic and aDNA work.
- **Stimulus diffusion, not independent invention.** This is directed at the d'Errico and Zilhão school, which argues that Neanderthal symbolic behaviour emerged independently before contact. His rhetorical move is the improbability of the coincidence: after 400–500 kyr of separate evolution, Neanderthals would have invented Aurignacian-like ornaments "a few centuries" before moderns showed up. Kroeber's concept (1940, "Stimulus diffusion", *American Anthropologist*) is the history-of-anthropology anchor.
- **Uluzzian made by moderns.** He presents Benazzi et al. 2011 and acknowledges the "vehement" contestation without taking a hard line. His co-authorship with Benazzi suggests his sympathy.
- **Against climatic and volcanic explanations of extinction.** Moderns predate H4 and the CI in Italy. He mocks the *La Recherche* conclusion ("certainly not modern humans") as self-exoneration. He accepts climate as a modulator (GI 12 facilitating entry) but not as the cause. His implicit argument is the global pattern: all archaic humans vanish where *sapiens* arrives.
- **Methodological caution.** He repeatedly flags problem fossils: Manot, Ust'-Ishim and Oase are all "hors stratigraphie" (outside stratigraphy), and Spy was dug with brutal 19th-century methods. Ksar Akil was destroyed by war and its fossils lost.
- **Memorable moments**: "certains d'entre nous sont plus néandertaliens que les autres" (some of us are more Neanderthal than others), a joke; the human gene-count anecdote (we have barely more genes than a houseplant); "le monde des industries de transition, c'est un monde de passion et de combat" (the world of transitional industries is one of passion and combat); and the closing pizzly/grolar bear photo as an emblem of hybridisation between "good species".

## 5. Concepts a learner must master (in prerequisite order)

1. **Marine Isotope Stages (MIS)**: alternating cold (even) and warm (odd) stages defined from δ¹⁸O. MIS 5e is the last interglacial, MIS 3 (~57–29 ka) the unstable interval in which the replacement happens.
2. **δ¹⁸O proxies, marine vs ice core**: heavier oxygen is stored in ocean/foraminifera when ice volume is high. Ice cores give annual resolution; marine cores are blurred by bioturbation.
3. **Dansgaard–Oeschger cycles / Greenland Interstadials (GI) and Heinrich events**: millennial warm spikes vs iceberg-discharge cold events (H4 ~40 ka).
4. **Tephrochronology**: volcanic ash layers (Campanian Ignimbrite) as a single-instant time marker across sites.
5. **Radiocarbon limit and calibration**, plus TL and U-Th: why ~50 ka is the edge of ¹⁴C. Direct-dating of bones. U-Th on calcite crusts gives minimum ages.
6. **Middle vs Upper Palaeolithic**: Levallois flake technology vs volumetric blade/bladelet production, osseous tools, ornaments, art.
7. **Technocomplex / industry**: an archaeological unit defined by tool types and methods (Mousterian, MTA, Micoquian, Aurignacian). Industry does not equal species.
8. **Transitional industries vs IUP**: locally rooted Middle/Upper Palaeolithic hybrids (Châtelperronian, Uluzzian, LRJ, Szeletian) vs an intrusive blade industry with Levantine affinities (Emirian, Bohunician).
9. **Proto-/Early Aurignacian, Early Ahmarian**: bladelet-based modern-human complexes. The Proto-Aurignacian has straight pointed bladelets.
10. **Stratigraphic integrity / taphonomic mixing**: why "association" of fossils and artefacts can be challenged.
11. **Stimulus diffusion vs acculturation vs convergent invention**.
12. **Admixture/introgression**: gene flow between populations. Admixture %.
13. **Recombination and tract length**: archaic segments shorten each generation, so block length dates the admixture (the Oase logic).
14. **Purifying vs positive selection; introgression deserts**: removal of deleterious archaic DNA vs adaptive introgression.
15. **Haldane's rule / hybrid male sterility**: the heterogametic sex suffers first, hence the X and testis depletion.
16. **Palaeoproteomics (ZooMS)**: identifying bone fragments to taxon by collagen peptides. This is how hominin bones were found among Grotte du Renne debris.

## 6. Misconceptions

- "Moderns swept Neanderthals away in one go." Hublin's answer is several waves, including failed ones, and a Neanderthal comeback in the Levant.
- "Neanderthal DNA makes Europeans special." Asians carry slightly more, and adaptive archaic genes number only a few dozen.
- "Everyone carries the same 2%." Different segments survive in different individuals, covering 20–40% of the Neanderthal genome collectively.
- "Upper Palaeolithic tools mean modern humans." The Châtelperronian has Upper Palaeolithic traits but Neanderthal makers. Conversely, early modern industries can look Middle Palaeolithic.
- "Aurignacian = first moderns in Europe." The IUP (Bohunician) is about 5 kyr earlier.
- "A fossil found with tools was made by those tools' makers." Mixing, old excavations and out-of-context finds (Manot, Oase, Ust'-Ishim, Spy) complicate this.
- "Climate or a super-eruption killed the Neanderthals." The chronology does not fit.
- "The last interglacial peaked at 18–20 ka." This is Hublin's slip; that was the Last Glacial Maximum. Learners must not copy it.
- "Denisovans lived in the Altai." Their genetic legacy is in Oceania, so their range was huge.

## 8. Interactive ideas

## 9. The seminar — Matthias Meyer (MPI-EVA Leipzig)

**Thesis.** Advances in sequencing and sample preparation turned ancient DNA from 200-bp mtDNA snippets into high-coverage archaic genomes. Meyer was central to these advances with single-stranded library preparation and short-fragment extraction. They reveal three things. Neanderthals and Denisovans were distinct, small, low-diversity populations. All groups exchanged genes, always at below 10%. And mtDNA alone can mislead. Sima de los Huesos shows this: a Denisovan-like mtDNA but a Neanderthal-like nuclear genome. That pushes the archaic–modern split back to roughly 650–800+ ka.

**Outline**
- **[0:00]** Hublin introduces him. DNA basics: nuclear genome ~3 Gb vs multi-copy mtDNA. About 2,000–3,000 damage events per cell per hour, mostly repaired. A parent–child comparison shows about 50–100 new mutations. Most mutations are neutral (neutral theory). Two humans differ at about 3 million sites, human vs chimp at about 30 million. Modern mtDNA tree: deepest branches in Africa, shallow outside, hence Out of Africa.
- **[~0:07]** Why bone preserves DNA: hydroxyapatite binds DNA tightly. A student's experiment shows DNA "disappears" onto HAP powder and only returns after dissolving the mineral. This works even on 600 ka and dinosaur bone. Decay by depurination leads to strand breaks and fragmentation.
- **[~0:10]** Fragment-length simulations. Strong temperature dependence: permafrost allows DNA approaching 1 Ma, but no archaic humans are known from permafrost. Outside permafrost, convincing results come from only about 100–120 ka. First Neanderthal mtDNA: 1997, about 200 bp from Feldhofer 1 (the type specimen). It falls outside modern variation, hence "not our ancestors".
- **[~0:14]** NGS revolution: about 3 billion reads per run vs 96 before 2005. Library = adapters ligated, amplified ("immortalised"), then mapping assembly to the human reference and the concept of coverage. Draft Neanderthal genome in 2010 from three Vindija bones at 1.3× coverage. Divergence: 0.8 per 1,000 between non-Africans, 1.2 involving Africans, 1.5 to a Neanderthal.
- **[~0:19]** Admixture test (ABBA–BABA / D-statistic). Two Africans give D ≈ 0. San vs French shows a Neanderthal affinity, and the same holds for Han and Papuans. About 1.5–2% Neanderthal ancestry everywhere outside Africa, slightly higher in East Asia. The 2010 inference was early admixture, perhaps in the Levant. Admixture dated by tract length to about 40–90 ka.
- **[~0:23]** Oase 1 (Romania, ~40 ka): highest Neanderthal ancestry ever seen, with half of chromosome 12 in one Neanderthal block. A Neanderthal ancestor 4–6 generations back means a second, local admixture. Ust'-Ishim (45 ka) has 4.5–5% with longer blocks than moderns. Decline over time suggests purifying selection.
- **[~0:26]** Denisova discovery from a juvenile distal phalanx screened because it was undiagnostic. mtDNA: a basal lineage with a TMRCA of about 1 Ma vs about 500 ka for human–Neanderthal. Endogenous DNA 70% (vs typical 1%, best Neanderthal 5%). Nuclear genome: sister to Neanderthals. No contribution to Europe, a trace in East Asia, a large one in Oceania (about 3× the Neanderthal share, "6%"). Large molars support a distinct group. He calls naming a group from DNA alone "a bit of a scandal in palaeoanthropology".
- **[~0:33]** Synthesis map: out of Africa about 50 ka or earlier, Neanderthal admixture in the Middle East, Denisovan admixture before reaching Island SE Asia and Sahul. Up to about 8–10% archaic ancestry in Oceanians.
- **[~0:34]** The 1× coverage problem: errors outnumber real differences. Only 30 mg of the Denisova finger was available. The single-stranded library gave about 10× more data and a 30× Denisovan genome (heterozygous sites resolvable). High-coverage Altai Neanderthal; Vindija (Croatia) in progress; an early modern human from Siberia (Ust'-Ishim).
- **[~0:37]** Heterozygosity: Denisovan about one third of modern African diversity, lower than non-Africans. Long runs of homozygosity in the Altai Neanderthal mean parents were related at the half-sibling level (or uncle–niece, grandfather–granddaughter, double first cousins). Even so, inbreeding does not explain the low diversity. PSMC (attributed to "Reich's lab"; actually Li & Durbin 2011) shows long-term small archaic populations.
- **[~0:41]** Gene-flow web: Neanderthal→Denisovan; an unknown archaic (>1 Ma divergent, "maybe *H. erectus*") → Denisovan; modern→Neanderthal (recent finding). Always below 10%. Functional work on negatively selected regions may show "what makes humans human".
- **[~0:44]** Going deeper: the 700 ka Thistle Creek permafrost horse (Copenhagen) with ~70 bp fragments. Their extraction modification recovers 35 bp ("add a little schnapps": a binding-buffer change with alcohol, i.e. isopropanol in guanidine buffer).
- **[~0:46]** Sima de los Huesos (Atapuerca): 28 near-complete individuals at about 400 ka, 30 m underground, a constant 11 °C "perfect fridge". Cave bears first, then humans.
- **[~0:50]** Raw reads: mixed C/T positions caused by contamination. C→T at fragment ends from cytosine deamination. Filtering for deaminated reads removes modern contamination. Fragments of 30–40 bp. Two grams of bone needed for full mtDNA. Short branch length gives a genetic date of about 400 ka. mtDNA is closer to Denisovans. Surname analogy: judging ancestry by a patrilineal surname, or reading one page torn from a book.
- **[~0:54]** Nuclear DNA (below 0.1% of genome) using diagnostic-site counting. Of about 100 reads at Neanderthal-informative sites, 40 show the derived Neanderthal state. Of a similar number at Denisovan sites, only 10. So Sima is early Neanderthal, or close to it. Implications: the Neanderthal–Denisovan split predates 400 ka, and the archaic–modern split is about 650–800 (maybe 900) ka. Acknowledges Svante Pääbo and the palaeontologists.

**Interactive ideas (seminar)**
1. **ABBA–BABA counter (how do we know?).** Learners count toy site patterns for San/French/Neanderthal/chimp, compute D and see it deviate from 0. Then swap in San/Yoruba (D ≈ 0) and Papuan/Denisovan. Data can be simulated, or come from real allele tables (SGDP, Altai and Vindija VCFs are public).
2. **Deamination detective.** Present a pileup of reads with C→T at the ends. The learner filters for "damaged" reads and watches a contaminated C/T position resolve to the true base.
3. **Coverage vs error slider.** At 1× vs 30×, can you tell a true heterozygote from a sequencing error? This motivates diagnostic-site counting (Sima: 40/100 vs 10/100, with a binomial test).
4. **mtDNA-vs-nuclear "surname" game (predict, then reveal).** Predict where Sima falls from morphology. Reveal the mtDNA (Denisovan-like), then the nuclear result (Neanderthal). Uses Meyer's surname analogy.
5. **Fragment-length decay simulator.** Damage rate × time × temperature gives a length distribution against a 35 bp usability threshold. Compare permafrost, Sima at 11 °C and a tropical cave.

**How it complements the lecture.** Meyer supplies the genetic mechanics behind Hublin's "leaky replacement": D-statistics, tract-length dating, Oase as local admixture, the Denisovan discovery and its Oceanian legacy, and low archaic diversity (small populations, which matters for why archaics were vulnerable). His mtDNA warning reinforces Hublin's caution about single lines of evidence. Sima's deep split time links back to L2–L3 on Middle Pleistocene ancestry.
