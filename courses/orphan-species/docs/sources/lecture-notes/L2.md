## 1. Thesis

Hublin asks where in the varied, large-brained hominins of the African Middle Pleistocene our species takes root, and how we would recognise its earliest members. He first deals with definitions. *Homo sapiens* is a **phenotypic species**, not a reproductively isolated biological species, and the morphological lists used to diagnose it (Day & Stringer 1982; Lieberman 2002) are imperfect. "Anatomically modern" is a convenient label but misleading. He then turns to the **face** and uses 3D geometric morphometrics, mostly the work of Sarah Freidline in his Leipzig department, to make three points. (a) The modern face is in many respects **primitive**, close to *H. erectus*. It is the Neanderthal face that is derived. (b) The modern face **cannot be produced just by shrinking** the big prognathic faces of Bodo or Kabwe, so allometry does not explain it. This makes the African "*H. rhodesiensis/heidelbergensis*" fossils, and especially the later ones, poor candidates for direct ancestors. (c) The supposedly modern face of the 800 ka Gran Dolina child (*H. antecessor*) is mostly a juvenile effect. Faces that really are modern appear only around **300 ka in Africa**, with Jebel Irhoud as the best example. There the **face comes before the braincase**: the Irhoud neurocrania are still elongated and not globular. The same mosaic, with the face first and the braincase later, is seen in the Neanderthal lineage (Sima de los Huesos). The jump in encephalisation is late (last 500 kyr) and happened independently in the two lineages. Irhoud dental microstructure already shows a modern pace of growth. This archaic-*sapiens* stage coincides with the **Early Stone Age to Middle Stone Age transition** (Kapthurin, about 285 ka). Overall he argues for a gradual, mosaic emergence of *H. sapiens* within Africa.

## 2. Timestamped outline (times are approximate and placed within each 10-min chunk)

**[0:00] Recap and the species problem**
- He shows his phylogenetic tree again (it was also used in 2015–16). A Middle Pleistocene common ancestor splits into a Eurasian branch (Neanderthals plus their sister group, the Denisovans) and an African lineage that leads to *H. sapiens* through the late Middle and much of the Late Pleistocene. All of this is rooted in *H. erectus*, the first species to leave Africa.
- Linnaeus coined *H. sapiens* but there is **no type specimen**. Gene flow between almost all these branches makes the biological species concept unworkable.
- Some colleagues include Neanderthals in *H. sapiens*. Hublin's reply is the slippery slope: if Neanderthals go in, so must the common ancestor, then by gene flow *erectus* and Denisovans, and in the end "everything in the last two million years". In his words that is "jeter le bébé avec l'eau du bain" (throwing the baby out with the bathwater). He keeps a **restricted, phenotypic** usage and notes that hybridising "species" are common among Pleistocene mammals.

**[~0:05] What a modern human looks like**
- He shows a photo of a young woman at the Oktoberfest as an example of a modern human. Her face is reduced, her anterior teeth are reduced and she has a chin. He adds that reproduction and life history are also distinctive.
- Cro-Magnon 1 (Upper Palaeolithic, Dordogne) is compared with a slightly older Neanderthal skull. Both are large-brained, but the striking difference is the face: in moderns it is **retracted under the neurocranium**. The chin is probably a by-product of that retraction and of smaller teeth.

**[~0:08] Diagnostic lists: Stringer, Wolpoff, Lieberman**
- Stringer's list (Day & Stringer 1982; then Stringer, Hublin & Vandermeersch 1984, which Hublin co-signed). It includes a short, high, globular vault; a high, convex frontal; brow ridges that are divided rather than forming a continuous torus; a rounded occipital with an external occipital protuberance; an infraorbital region concave in several directions; a projecting bony chin; reduced teeth; and a few postcranial traits, which are rarely available for archaic fossils.
- The list was built to interpret the **Omo Kibish** (Ethiopia) remains.
- Milford Wolpoff (multiregionalist, who also puts 1.5 Ma fossils into *H. sapiens*) objected that some living people fail the criteria. Hublin calls this objection "pas du tout négligeable" (not at all negligible).
- Daniel Lieberman (Lieberman, McBratney & Krovitz 2002) offered a shorter, structural definition based on GM analysis. A developmental reorganisation of the cranial base, with flexion and shortening of the anterior cranial fossa, causes the face to retract and the braincase to become more globular. This could in principle be a rapid change. Lieberman would call everything *H. sapiens* and drop "modern".

**[~0:14] A working nomenclature**
- He sets out three tiers. (1) The archaic common ancestor (*heidelbergensis/rhodesiensis*): strong mid-facial prognathism, non-globular braincase, brain larger than most *erectus*. (2) "Archaic" *H. sapiens*: carries part of the Stringer list, but no fossil meets every criterion. (3) "Anatomically modern" humans: within present-day variation for almost all traits.
- Tiers 2 and 3 form a continuum. Whether the change was gradual or abrupt is held over for Lecture 3. If it was gradual, "AMH" loses its rationale, "but everyone keeps using it."

**[~0:17] Allometry**
- An adult is not an enlarged baby. He distinguishes **ontogenetic** allometry (shape changes during growth), **static** allometry (shape varies with size among adults, including male versus female), and **evolutionary** allometry (size change over time). The key question is whether shape changes in fossils simply follow size.

**[~0:21] Method: geometric morphometrics (a methods excursus)**
- When Hublin was a student there were two schools: descriptive morphology, which was strong in France, and linear or angular biometrics, which was rigorous but lost information. 3D GM reconciles them and was made possible by personal computers.
- Landmarks (e.g. bregma) and **semilandmarks** slid over curves and surfaces. ("No French term exists," he says.)
- **Procrustes superimposition**: shapes are centred on the centroid, scaled and rotated to minimise landmark distances. Shape is defined as what remains after removing size, position and orientation.
- **PCA** reduces hundreds of variables to two or three components.
- **Shape space** compared with **form space**, which adds back the natural log of centroid size so that PC1 is largely size. He apologises for using English terms because French "forme" covers both shape and form.
- Ontogenetic trajectories per group (fossil and modern). Any point in the space can be visualised as a 3D surface, which opens the way to **simulation**.

**[~0:27] Modern and Neanderthal faces compared**
- The Neanderthal (and many Middle Pleistocene hominins) has a projecting **midface** and oblique, swept-back, "inflated" lateral parts. The zygomaticoalveolar crest is nearly straight.
- The modern face is small and flat, set "en façade" (frontally oriented). Its infraorbital region is concave, often with a true **canine fossa**. The zygomaticoalveolar crest forms a notch (the incisura malaris).

**[~0:29] "Modern" means partly primitive**
- In a GM analysis of the **maxilla** alone (zygomatic excluded), the cloud of recent humans overlaps much of the *H. erectus* morphology, and frontally oriented, flat maxillae are already present in early *erectus*. **Neanderthals are the derived group.**
- Middle Pleistocene fossils (pink in the plot) are extremely variable even with a small sample, straddling the modern and Neanderthal ranges. He develops the idea that each descendant lineage **inherited part of the ancestral variability**, and that variability then shrank independently in each (mechanism held over to next week).

**[~0:33] A tour of Middle Pleistocene faces (about 800 to 125 ka)**
- Europe: **Steinheim** (Germany, late Middle Pleistocene) has long been described as having a concave infraorbital region. Taphonomic deformation is debated: "certainly, but not only". It is less Neanderthal-like than the older **Sima de los Huesos** crania (430 ka).
- Africa: **Bodo** (Ethiopia, about 600 ka); **Kabwe/Broken Hill** (Zambia, "600–400 ka, some think much younger"); the small, damaged **Ndutu** cranium (Tanzania), which has a somewhat modern- or *erectus*-like face.
- An aside: African dates are "excessivement discutables" (highly debatable) and are revised regularly.

**[~0:36] Testing the allometric hypothesis**
- **Maddux & Franciscus (2009)** found that larger faces have flatter infraorbital regions. They proposed a size-driven continuum rather than separate Neanderthal and modern morphs.
- Freidline's test: take the mean modern face and extrapolate it along the modern ontogenetic and allometric vector to the size of Kabwe, then of Bodo. The enlarged modern face gains stronger brow ridges and a changed infraorbital region, **but it still differs**: Kabwe and Bodo remain less concave below the orbit and much more prognathic.
- In form space the large Middle Pleistocene crania sit with the archaic group and **not on the extension of the modern growth axis**.
- Conclusion: allometry explains much variation *within* the Middle Pleistocene and Neanderthal group, but there is no route from *rhodesiensis/heidelbergensis* to the modern face by size reduction alone.

**[~0:42] The Gran Dolina child (ATD6-69, *H. antecessor*)**
- A juvenile face, about 10–11 years by modern dental standards, about 800 ka, from Atapuerca (Spain). It has a marked infraorbital concavity.
- The describers (Bermúdez de Castro, Arsuaga et al. 1997) named *H. antecessor* and claimed a fully modern face at 800 ka in Europe. That raised geographic problems, given that sapiens originated in Africa, and an odd "aller-retour morphologique" (morphological round trip) through *rhodesiensis*.
- He shows the Buck & Stringer (2014) tree and comments that "they were somewhat conservative; the divergence is older."
- In shape space ATD6-69 sits at the edge of the modern range but overlaps Neanderthals and Middle Pleistocene forms. In form space it lies near the Neanderthal ontogenetic trajectory, running from the **Pech-de-l'Azé** Neanderthal infant to adults. The infraorbital region alone is still peripheral to the Neanderthal and Middle Pleistocene range.
- Simulated growth to adulthood:
  - Along the modern trajectory, the adult is still much more prognathic than the mean modern face.
  - Along the Neanderthal trajectory, the result is a very long, "hyper-Neanderthal" face that keeps an unusual infraorbital concavity.
- Verdict: an end-Lower-Pleistocene hominin at one extreme of variability. It would not have looked modern as an adult (Freidline et al. 2013).

**[~0:48] Jebel Irhoud: the first modern faces, about 300 ka**
- Faces you "could practically meet in the street" appear in Africa around 300 ka. The best preserved is from **Jebel Irhoud** (Morocco), where his team has worked for years.
- In GM the Irhoud face falls entirely within recent human variation. In one analysis its nearest neighbour was an Aboriginal Australian.
- Note: he cites about 300 ka **eight months before** the June 2017 Nature papers made it public.

**[~0:50] The braincase comes later than the face**
- He shows an endocranial-shape plot. **Irhoud 1 and Irhoud 2** lie on a path towards moderns but between *H. erectus* (green) and the Neanderthal and large-faced African Middle Pleistocene group. Gunz will give details.
- The African *rhodesiensis* fossils are **not intermediate** between *erectus* and moderns. This points to a bifurcation within Africa, and that "other thing" may not be ancestral.
- The same mosaic is found in the Neanderthal lineage: Sima de los Huesos has a Neanderthal face at 430 ka with a non-Neanderthal braincase.
- He shows an encephalisation quotient (EQ) curve. He gives a human EQ of about 5.5. The steep rise independent of body mass belongs to the **last 500 kyr** and happened **independently** in the Neanderthal and sapiens lineages, *after* each had fixed its facial pattern.
- Genes that changed after the Neanderthal–modern split and are fixed or nearly fixed in living humans must have been strongly positively selected. Some of them (shown in red and green) are linked to brain development or function.

**[~0:54] Life history**
- Dental microstructure of the **Irhoud juvenile mandible** (Irhoud 3, about 8 years) shows a **modern rate of dental development**, unlike Neanderthals (Smith et al. 2007). He refers to his 2014 course on this topic.

**[~0:56] Behaviour: the ESA to MSA transition**
- About 300 ka is also the time of the ESA/MSA transition. **Kapthurin** (Kenya, about 284 ka) is the most often cited site, and other sites fall at about 250–300 ka.
- The MSA is defined by **Levallois** flakes, retouched points and sometimes blades, while large cutting tools persist, including bifaces made on Levallois flakes. This resembles the European situation.
- He "tends to think" the appearance of the MSA coincides with archaic *H. sapiens*. The MSA is the setting in which "modern behaviours" will appear (Lecture 3).

**[~0:59] Conclusions and credits**
- The modern face is primitive in many respects but much reduced in size.
- *H. antecessor* would not have grown into a modern adult face.
- Modern facial morphology appears in the second half of the African Middle Pleistocene.
- The later large-faced African fossils are not ideal ancestors.
- The earliest *H. sapiens* coincide with the MSA.
- He credits **Dr Sarah Freidline** (MPI-EVA) for most of the analyses shown.

## 4. Hublin's positions and debates

- **Restricted, phenotypic *H. sapiens*.** He is against lumping Neanderthals, Denisovans or *erectus* into *sapiens* (Wolpoff and others). He argues by reductio ad absurdum: lumping makes phylogeny meaningless. He concedes the biological species concept fails.
- **"Anatomically modern human" is a convenient but irrational term.** It wrongly equates "modern" with "evolved" when many modern facial traits are plesiomorphic. He predicts AMH will become hard to defend if change was gradual.
- **Against a purely allometric explanation** of the modern face (Maddux & Franciscus 2009). He uses form-space extrapolation as the decisive test.
- **Against the Spanish reading of *H. antecessor*** (Bermúdez de Castro, Arsuaga, Carbonell) as a modern-faced ancestor of *sapiens*. He reads it as a juvenile effect at one end of Lower or Middle Pleistocene variability. He sides with "many people" who see *antecessor* as an *erectus*-like form.
- **Sceptical that the African "*rhodesiensis*" fossils are ancestors**, especially the more recent ones. He hints at a bifurcation within Africa. He says this "problem will come back often in coming years", and it did, with Kabwe at 300 ka (see section 7).
- **Mosaic evolution**: the face is first, then life history, with brain shape last. He draws an explicit parallel with the Neanderthal "accretion" pattern taught in 2015–16.
- **Cautious on behaviour**: he says the ESA/MSA transition "seems to coincide ... c'est peut-être significatif" ("perhaps significant"). He does not claim causation.
- **Rhetorical moves**: the Oktoberfest woman as the "type"; "you could meet the Irhoud face in the métro"; the Aboriginal Australian as nearest neighbour; history of the discipline (descriptive French school against biometricians); his apology for shape/form vocabulary; "just for fun" growth simulations; generous credit to Freidline.

## 6. Misconceptions

- "*Homo sapiens* equals anatomically modern human." Hublin separates archaic *sapiens* from AMH.
- "Modern traits are evolved traits." In fact the modern face keeps *erectus*-like features and Neanderthals are the derived ones.
- "Smaller faces are just shrunk big faces." The allometry test refutes this.
- "Big brain means modern." Irhoud has a large brain but an archaic shape, and Neanderthals had brains as large as ours.
- "A juvenile fossil's shape tells you its adult shape." The Gran Dolina case shows it does not.
- "Hybridisation means they were all one species." He rejects this for phylogenetic purposes.
- "Brain growth increased steadily over 2 Myr." Most of the early increase follows body size, and the true EQ jump is late and parallel in two lineages.
- "African fossil dates are firm." He says they are regularly revised, and Kabwe proved him right.
- "Behavioural modernity arrived with anatomical modernity." He defers this to Lecture 3. MSA onset is not the same as "modern behaviour".

## 7. Since 2016

- **Jebel Irhoud published**: Hublin et al. 2017, *Nature* 546:289–292 (new fossils, pan-African origin); Richter et al. 2017, *Nature* 546:293–296 (TL on heated flints, 315 ± 34 ka; ESR on Irhoud 3 tooth about 286 ± 32 ka). This confirms what he previewed. Confidence: high.
- **Brain-shape evolution**: Neubauer, Hublin & Gunz 2018, *Science Advances* ("The evolution of modern human brain shape"). Globularity evolved gradually within *H. sapiens* and reached the present-day range only about 100–35 ka. This formalises the "braincase lags face" claim. Confidence: high.
- **Kabwe redated**: Grün et al. 2020, *Nature* ("Dating the skull from Broken Hill, Zambia…"), 299 ± 25 ka. Kabwe is therefore contemporary with Irhoud, which supports Hublin's scepticism that later *rhodesiensis* forms are ancestors. Confidence: high.
- **Omo Kibish redated**: Vidal et al. 2022, *Nature*. Omo I is at least about 233 ka, beneath the Shala tuff (earlier McDougall, Brown & Fleagle 2005, *Nature*, about 195 ka). Confidence: high.
- ***H. naledi*** at 236–335 ka (Dirks et al. 2017, *eLife*) adds to late Middle Pleistocene African diversity. Confidence: high. It is tangential.
- **African Middle Pleistocene diversity and pan-African models**: Scerri et al. 2018, *Trends Ecol. Evol.* (subdivided populations); Mounier & Mirazón Lahr 2019, *Nature Communications* (virtual ancestor; Kabwe and Bodo). Ragsdale et al. 2023, *Nature* ("A weakly structured stem for human origins in Africa") is genetic support for a structured, multi-region African stem. Confidence: high (Mounier & Lahr: medium–high, verify volume).
- **Early MSA**: Brooks et al. 2018, Deino et al. 2018 and Potts et al. 2018, all *Science*, report Olorgesailie (Kenya) MSA from about 320 ka. This pushes the ESA/MSA transition back from Kapthurin's about 285 ka. Confidence: high.
- ***H. antecessor***: Welker et al. 2020, *Nature* ("The dental proteome of *Homo antecessor*"). It places *antecessor* as a sister lineage to the clade of *sapiens*, Neanderthals and Denisovans, not directly ancestral to us. This partly supports Hublin against the Spanish ancestral reading. The modern-like facial remodelling pattern of ATD6-69 (Lacruz et al. 2013, *PLoS ONE*) is still cited by the other side. Confidence: high (Welker); medium (Lacruz details, verify).
- **Deeper divergences and East Asian archaics**: the Harbin cranium (*H. longi*, Ji et al. 2021, *The Innovation*) was identified as **Denisovan** by ancient proteins and mtDNA in 2025 (Fu et al. 2025, *Science* and *Cell*; verify exact venue split). The Yunxian 2 reconstruction (Feng et al. 2025, *Science*) argues for a sapiens/longi split well before 1 Ma. Both support his remark that the Buck & Stringer divergence was "conservative". Confidence: medium; verify.
- **Neanderthal–modern brain gene variants**: Pinson et al. 2022, *Science* (TKTL1 modern variant increases neocortical basal radial glia); Kuhlwilm & Boeckx 2019, *Scientific Reports* (catalogue of fixed modern-specific changes). These update his slide of fixed brain-related genes. Confidence: high (Pinson); medium–high (Kuhlwilm; reproducibility of TKTL1 effect has been questioned, verify).
- **Life history**: no major revision of the Irhoud 3 result is known to me. Its date is now about 300 ka rather than the 160 ka used by Smith et al. 2007. Confidence: medium.

## 8. Interactive ideas

1. **"Spot the modern"** (predict, then reveal). Show 8 frontal face renders (Cro-Magnon 1, La Ferrassie 1, Kabwe, Bodo, Irhoud 1, ATD6-69, a *H. erectus* such as Sangiran 17, and a modern person) with labels hidden. The learner ranks them from "most modern" to "least modern" by infraorbital concavity. The reveal shows that Neanderthals, not *erectus*, are the most "derived" and that Irhoud falls inside modern variation. Data: published photos or casts and the 3D models on MorphoSource and the Smithsonian (Kabwe and Irhoud surface models are available; check licences).
2. **Procrustes playground**. The learner drags landmarks on two 2D outlines (a cup and a skull, as in his "bouteilles, tasses, chaussures" aside, i.e. bottles, cups, shoes) and steps through centring, scaling and rotation, watching the Procrustes distance fall. The concept learned is that shape is what survives the removal of size, position and orientation. No external data is needed.
3. **Shape space vs form space toggle**. A scatter of points (modern ontogenetic series, Neanderthal series, Middle Pleistocene adults) with a toggle that adds ln(centroid size). The learner sees trajectories appear and Bodo and Kabwe sitting off the modern axis. Synthetic data shaped after Freidline et al. 2012 would work, clearly labelled as illustrative.
4. **"Grow the Gran Dolina child"** (simulation). A slider takes ATD6-69 from juvenile to adult along the modern or the Neanderthal trajectory, with a 2D lateral profile morph. The learner predicts the adult first, then sees the result: prognathic, or "hyper-Neanderthal" with a persistent infraorbital concavity. Thin-plate-spline warping of profile landmarks from published figures would serve.
5. **"Can you shrink Kabwe into a modern?"** (allometry test). The learner scales a mean modern profile up to Kabwe size, overlays the real Kabwe profile, and tries to close the gap with only a size slider. They cannot. A second slider adds non-allometric "prognathism" and closes the gap. Lesson: shape change is independent of size.
6. **"How do we know Irhoud is about 300 ka?"** (evidence exercise). A card sort of evidence types: TL on burnt flints, ESR on a tooth, fauna, stratigraphy, and the old 160 ka estimate. The learner judges which evidence dates what (the fossil or the layer) and why the 2017 revision happened. A companion card covers Kabwe (direct U-series on bone, 2020).
7. **Mosaic timeline builder**. The learner places trait "flags" (modern face, modern dental growth, MSA tools, globular braincase, chin) on a 0–500 ka axis for both the Neanderthal and sapiens lineages. The reveal shows that face comes first and braincase last in both lineages. Data: dates from Hublin et al. 2017, Neubauer et al. 2018 and Arsuaga et al. 2014.
8. **Check Stringer's checklist against living people**. Interactive tick-boxes for the Day & Stringer criteria applied to a random set of recent crania and fossils. The learner discovers Wolpoff's point that some living humans fail the list and no fossil passes every criterion. Data: published descriptions; open recent-cranial datasets such as Howells' craniometric data for metric proxies.
9. **ESA vs MSA toolkit sorter**. The learner drags images of tools (handaxe, Levallois core, Levallois point, blade, a biface on a Levallois flake) into "ESA", "MSA" or "both". This brings out the transitional overlap he stresses. Images from open museum collections.

## 9. The seminar: Philipp Gunz (Dept. of Human Evolution, MPI for Evolutionary Anthropology, Leipzig), "Evolution and development of the human brain" (in English)

**Thesis.** Modern humans are distinguished from Neanderthals not by brain *size*, which overlaps, but by brain and braincase *shape*: a globular braincase with **bulging parietals and an expanded cerebellum**. That shape is **not present at birth**. Human and Neanderthal neonate endocasts are nearly identical in size and shape. It emerges in a uniquely human **"globularisation phase"** in roughly the first year after birth (and from about week 27 of gestation), on top of a "shared" postnatal pattern common to all hominoids, Neanderthals included. Longitudinal ultrasound of bonobo fetuses shows this is not just heterochrony. Globularisation tracks phases of rapid (cerebellar) growth. Gunz rejects any "new phrenology" and argues that shape is a marker of *when and how fast* the brain grows during the period when its wiring self-organises.

**Outline**
- **[0:00] Big picture.** Humans have two unusual traits: a big brain and bipedalism. They are separated by about 4 Myr.
  - Brain size stays ape-like in australopiths and *H. habilis*. Much of the early increase tracks body size. The late increase independent of body size happened **independently in the Neanderthal and sapiens lineages**.
  - Bipedalism: possibly 6 Ma in Chad (*Sahelanthropus*, anterior foramen magnum, debated); certain by 3.6 Ma from the Laetoli footprints (he says "Lucy and her partner", i.e. *A. afarensis*).
- **[~0:05] Obstetric dilemma.**
  - A narrow bipedal pelvis means a tight birth canal. Chimpanzee births are easy and rarely noticed; he says only one zoo birth has ever been filmed.
  - Human neonates have brains about the size of an adult chimpanzee's and must rotate twice through the canal.
  - The "solution" is to shift growth after birth. Human brains grow at near-fetal rates for about a year and reach adult size around 6 years, a longer period of plasticity than in chimpanzees.
- **[~0:10] Neanderthals vs moderns.**
  - Ecology: he states that Neanderthals ate little or no fish and marine food, while moderns were broad-spectrum.
  - Upper Palaeolithic art (Lascaux replica, Chauvet in the Ardèche, the Venus of Willendorf). "As an Austrian", he notes, Willendorf matters most to him.
  - Similar brain sizes but different shapes. A neonate endocast is elongated, and scaled to Neanderthal size it resembles a Neanderthal.
- **[~0:14] GM of endocasts** (CT-based, hundreds of semilandmarks, cross-sectional chimpanzee and human series).
  - From the eruption of the deciduous teeth onwards, both species follow a **shared phase**.
  - From birth to deciduous eruption, humans alone show the **globularisation phase**: cerebellar expansion and parietal bulging.
- **[~0:17] Neanderthal neonates.**
  - Gunz spent "three months with migraines" virtually reconstructing **Le Moustier 2** (France) and **Mezmaiskaya 1** (Russia).
  - Faces and noses are already Neanderthal at birth, but the endocasts match modern neonates in shape and volume.
  - Neanderthal juveniles follow the shared phase only, with **no globularisation phase**. Gibbons, gorillas and orangutans share that phase too.
- **[~0:22] The parietal "bulge" is a displacement.** In colour-coded size-change maps it reflects displacement by surrounding growth, chiefly the cerebellum, rather than parietal expansion.
- **[~0:23] Bonobo fetal study.**
  - The question: is human globularisation just a heterochronic shift of a prenatal ape phase?
  - Method: zoo collaboration in Europe and the US with no sedation; animals were trained by positive reinforcement. Ultrasound was done by veterinarian **Barbara Drews** (transcribed "Drez"; likely IZW Berlin, verify).
  - Images were too noisy for semilandmarks, so the team used the clinical **cephalic index** (biparietal ÷ fronto-occipital diameter; 1 = round).
  - Humans (clinical data from hundreds of fetuses) globularise from **week 27**, coinciding with a prenatal cerebellar growth spurt, and continue after birth.
  - Bonobos become rounder early, then elongate, and stop changing shape after an inflection at **week 20**, when their brain growth slows.
- **[~0:30] Same bonobo before and after birth.**
  - One bonobo was scanned at 22 weeks of gestation. It died of infection 5 months after birth, and its CT scan shows **no shape change** in midsagittal profile, only growth.
  - Conclusion: this is not simple heterochrony. Globularisation goes with rapid growth.
- **[~0:32] Not phrenology.**
  - Neurons are mostly present at birth but poorly connected. Wiring is self-organising, guided by spatial chemical cues; there are far too many synapses for roughly 25,000 genes to specify them.
  - He uses a "fireflies" screensaver demo: small changes in growth speed produce very different network topology (local vs large-scale graphs).
  - DTI tractography of a fetus vs a 3-year-old shows large-scale networks established after birth under environmental input.
  - Take-home: be careful with children in their first year.

**How it complements the lecture.** Hublin says the face became modern by 300 ka while the braincase lagged. Gunz explains what "braincase becoming modern" means developmentally: a postnatal globularisation phase absent in Neanderthals. This explains why Irhoud can have a modern face with an archaic endocast. Both talks share the GM toolkit (semilandmarks, PCA, trajectories) and the theme of independent parallel encephalisation.

**Debates and since 2016**
- Ponce de León, Bienvenu, Akazawa & Zollikofer 2016, *Current Biology* ("Brain development is similar in Neanderthals and modern humans") disputes the Neanderthal/modern ontogenetic difference. Learners should see both sides.
- Neubauer et al. 2018 (*Sci. Adv.*) shows that globularity arose late within *H. sapiens*. Gunz et al. 2019, *Current Biology* ("Neandertal introgression sheds light on modern human endocranial globularity") links Neanderthal alleles near *UBR4* and *PHLPP1* to slightly less globular heads in living people. Gunz et al. 2020, *Science Advances* (*A. afarensis* endocasts, Dikika child: ape-like organisation with prolonged growth) also follows on. Confidence: high for all three.
- The obstetric dilemma is challenged by the metabolic (EGG) hypothesis, Dunsworth et al. 2012, *PNAS*. Confidence: high.
- *Sahelanthropus* bipedality is still debated (Daver et al. 2022, *Nature*, for; Macchiarelli et al. 2020, *JHE*, against). Confidence: high.
