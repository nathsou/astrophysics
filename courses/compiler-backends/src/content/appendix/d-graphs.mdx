## Traversals and orders

**Depth-first search** visits a node, then recursively each unvisited successor. It runs in O(V + E). It gives:
- **preorder** and **postorder** numberings;
- **reverse postorder** (RPO), which is a topological order of any DAG;
- a classification of edges into **tree**, **back**, **forward** and **cross** (Chapter 2).

In a CFG, back edges identify loops. Postorder drives backward dataflow such as liveness; RPO drives forward dataflow and dominator computation. Implement DFS iteratively in production: real CFGs can be deep enough to overflow a recursive implementation.

**Topological sort** of a DAG is RPO, or Kahn’s algorithm (repeatedly remove a node with no incoming edges). Used by list scheduling (Chapter 16) to process instructions in dependence order.

## Strongly connected components

**Tarjan’s algorithm** (1972) finds all SCCs in one DFS in O(V + E), tracking each node’s *lowlink* — the smallest preorder number reachable. SCCs of a CFG are its maximal cyclic regions (loops, including irreducible ones). The call graph’s SCCs are the mutually recursive function groups, which interprocedural passes process bottom-up. Kiln implements it in `analysis/graph.ts`.

## Dominators

- **Iterative dataflow** over sets: simple, O(V²) space.
- **Lengauer–Tarjan** (1979): O(E α(E, V)) using semi-dominators and path compression.
- **Cooper–Harvey–Kennedy** (2001): iterative, storing only the idom, and intersecting via RPO numbers; fast in practice (Chapter 3).
- **Semi-NCA** (Georgiadis et al.): used by LLVM, with incremental updates.

**Dominance frontiers** come from walking up the dominator tree from each predecessor of each join node. **Iterated** frontiers come from a worklist.

## Graph colouring

*K*-colourability is NP-complete for *K* ≥ 3. Useful facts:

- **Kempe’s rule**: a node of degree < *K* can always be coloured after the rest; removing such nodes repeatedly is the basis of Chaitin-style allocation.
- **Greedy colouring** in a given order uses at most Δ + 1 colours, where Δ is the maximum degree.
- **Interval graphs** (intersection graphs of intervals on a line) are coloured optimally by sorting intervals by start point and taking the lowest free colour. This is the theory behind linear scan.
- **Chordal graphs** (every cycle longer than 3 has a chord) have perfect elimination orders and can be coloured optimally in linear time. SSA interference graphs are chordal.

## Union–find

Disjoint-set forests with union by rank and path compression give near-constant amortised merges and lookups. Register coalescing uses them for alias chains (in kiln, `getAlias`), and so do type unification and equivalence classes of values.

## Worklist fixpoints

Many analyses iterate monotone equations until they stabilise. A **worklist** holds only the nodes whose inputs changed:
- dataflow analyses;
- SCCP;
- the iterated dominance frontier;
- the simplify worklists of iterated register coalescing.

Termination follows from monotonicity over a finite-height lattice; efficiency follows from a good initial order (RPO or postorder).

## Shortest and longest paths

List scheduling’s priority is a **longest path** (critical path) in a DAG. Compute it in O(V + E) in reverse topological order. In general graphs, longest path is NP-hard; in DAGs it is trivial.
