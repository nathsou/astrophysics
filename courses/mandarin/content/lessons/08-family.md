English has one word, *brother*. Chinese has two: 哥哥 for an older brother and 弟弟 for a younger one. And there is no single everyday word for *grandma*: your father's mother is 奶奶, your mother's mother is 外婆. Chinese family words carry age and side of the family because, traditionally, those mattered: who is senior, whose family you belong to.

That precision makes talking about family a great way to practise the grammar in this lesson: having, counting, and belonging.

## Who's who

```words

妹妹 | younger sister

```

Explore the tree, then switch to **Who is it?** and follow the chains: 爸爸的妈妈 is "dad's mum".

::family-tree

:::culture
Older siblings are often addressed by their title rather than their name: a younger sister calls her brother 哥哥 and would rarely use his name. The words also stretch to friends: young men call a slightly older friend 哥, and a stall holder might call a young woman 小妹妹. You'll also meet 外公 and 外婆 for your mother's parents: 外 means "outside", a reminder that a mother traditionally married *into* her husband's family.
:::

## Having: 有 and 没有

有 (yǒu) means "to have" and also "there is / there are". Its negative is special: not 不有, but **没有** (méiyǒu), often shortened to 没.

| Chinese | Means |
| --- | --- |
| 我有一个哥哥。 | I have an older brother. |
| 我没有妹妹。 | I don't have a younger sister. |
| 你有孩子吗？ | Do you have children? |
| 我家有五口人。 | There are five people in my family. |

```words
有 | to have; there is
没有 | to not have; there isn't
```

:::mistake
不 negates almost every verb, but never 有. "I don't have" is always 没有, never 不有.
:::

## Measure words: you can't just say "one brother"

In Chinese you can't put a number straight in front of a noun. You need a **measure word** (also called a classifier) in between, a bit like English "two **cups** of tea" or "a **piece** of paper", except Chinese does it for everything:

> 一**个**哥哥 one (measure word) older brother · 三**个**人 three people · 两**个**孩子 two children

个 (gè) is the general-purpose measure word, and you can use it for most people and many things. Others are more specific: 口 counts family members (我家有四口人), 本 counts books, 杯 cups, 只 animals. You'll collect them as you go; when in doubt, 个 is understood.

Measure words also follow 这 (this), 那 (that), 哪 (which) and 几 (how many): 这个人 (this person), 那个孩子 (that child), 哪个？ (which one?), 几个？ (how many?).

```words
个 | the general measure word
口 | measure word for family members
这 | this
那 | that
这些 | these
那些 | those
只 | measure word for animals; only
狗 | dog
猫 | cat
```

```order
title: Count them
items:
  - en: I have two older sisters.
    zh: 我 有 两 个 姐姐
  - en: There are four people in my family.
    zh: 我 家 有 四 口 人
  - en: How many children do you have?
    zh: 你 有 几 个 孩子
    extra: [多少]
  - en: I don't have a younger brother.
    zh: 我 没有 弟弟
    extra: [不]
  - en: That child is my son.
    zh: 那 个 孩子 是 我 儿子
```

## Belonging: 的

的 (de) is the Chinese apostrophe-s. Put it between the owner and the thing:

- 我的书 my book · 妈妈的猫 mum's cat · 老师的名字 the teacher's name

With close relationships, 的 is usually dropped: 我妈妈 (my mum), 你哥哥 (your brother), 我家 (my family). 我的妈妈 is not wrong, just a little emphatic.

和 (hé) means "and", but **only between nouns**: 爸爸和妈妈 (dad and mum). You can't use it to join sentences, which trips up English speakers constantly.

```words
的 | 's, of (possession)
和 | and (between nouns)
男 | male
女 | female
大 | big; old (of age)
小 | small; young
```

```fill
title: 的, 和, 有 or 没?
items:
  - zh: 这是我___书。
    en: This is my book.
    options: [的, 和, 有]
    answer: 0
  - zh: 我有一个哥哥___一个妹妹。
    en: I have an older brother and a younger sister.
    options: [的, 和, 也]
    answer: 1
  - zh: 他___有孩子。
    en: He doesn't have children.
    options: [不, 没]
    answer: 1
  - zh: 你家有几___人？
    en: How many people are in your family?
    options: [口, 个, 只]
    answer: 0
    explain: 个 would also be understood, but 口 is what people say for family members.
  - zh: 那___猫很小。
    en: That cat is very small.
    options: [只, 口, 本]
    answer: 0
    explain: 只 counts animals.
```

## Talking about your family

```dialogue
title: Family photos
小红: 这是你家人吗？ | Is this your family?
马克: 对。这是我爸爸，这是我妈妈。 | Yes. This is my dad, this is my mum.
小红: 这个男孩子是谁？ | Who is this boy?
马克: 他是我弟弟。他十二岁。 | He's my younger brother. He's twelve.
小红: 你有姐姐吗？ | Do you have an older sister?
马克: 没有。我家有四口人，还有一只狗！ | No. There are four of us, and a dog too!
小红: 你们家的狗真大！ | Your family's dog is really big!
```

```story
title: 小明的家
zh: 小明的家
paragraphs:
  - zh: 我叫王小明，我是中国人。我家有五口人：爸爸、妈妈、一个姐姐、一个弟弟和我。
    en: My name is Wang Xiaoming, I'm Chinese. There are five people in my family, dad, mum, an older sister, a younger brother and me.
  - zh: 我爸爸是老师，我妈妈是医生。我姐姐二十岁，她是大学生。我弟弟八岁，他是小学生。
    en: My dad is a teacher and my mum is a doctor. My older sister is twenty, she's a university student. My younger brother is eight, he's a primary school pupil.
  - zh: 我们家还有一只猫。它很小，它叫“大大”。
    en: Our family also has a cat. It's very small; it's called "Big Big".

```

:::key
- 哥哥/姐姐 are older, 弟弟/妹妹 younger. Dad's parents: 爷爷/奶奶.
- 有 = have / there is; its negative is 没有, never 不有.
- Number + measure word + noun: 两个人, 四口人, 一只猫. 个 is the default.
- 的 shows possession (我的书), but drops with close relations (我妈妈).
- 和 joins nouns only.
:::
