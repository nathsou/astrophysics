Chinese verbs never change for tense. 我吃 can mean "I eat", "I ate" or "I'll eat", and time words like 昨天 usually make it clear. But Chinese does have small markers that say *how* an action sits in time: whether it's done, or whether you've ever done it. This lesson covers the two you'll use constantly.

## Done: verb + 了

了 right after a verb says the action is **completed**:

- 我买了一本书。 I bought a book.
- 他喝了三杯咖啡。 He drank three cups of coffee.
- 我们吃了饭就走。 We'll leave once we've eaten.

Notice the last one: it's not about the past, it's about completion. 了 doesn't mean "past tense", it means "done".

A sentence with verb + 了 usually needs something after the object (a number, a description) or another clause, otherwise it sounds unfinished. 我买了书 hangs in the air; 我买了一本书 or 我买了书就回家 sounds complete.

**To negate, use 没 (or 没有) and drop 了**: 我没买书 (I didn't buy a book). Never 没…了.

To ask, add 了吗？ or 了没有？: 你吃饭了吗？ / 你吃饭了没有？ Have you eaten?

```words
已经 | already
完 | to finish
开始 | to begin
走 | to walk; to leave
事情 | matter, thing
忘 | to forget
记得 | to remember
```

已经…了 means "already": 我已经吃了 (I've already eaten). 完 after a verb means "finish doing": 吃完 (finish eating), 看完了 (finished watching).

:::grammar[Two kinds of 了]
In lesson 15 you met 了 at the end of a sentence, marking a **change** (下雨了, it's started raining). Here it's after a verb, marking **completion**. They often appear together: 我已经吃了三个饺子了 "I've already eaten three dumplings (and counting)". Even teachers argue about the finer points; for now, completion after the verb, change at the end.
:::

## Ever: verb + 过

过 (guo) after a verb means you have **experienced** something at some point in your life:

- 我去过中国。 I've been to China.
- 你吃过饺子吗？ Have you ever eaten dumplings?
- 我没去过上海。 I've never been to Shanghai. (没 + verb + 过, keeping 过)

To say how many times, use 次 (time, occurrence) after 过: 我去过两次北京 (I've been to Beijing twice).

```words
过 | (after a verb) have ever done
次 | time, occurrence
```


## Coming and going: directions after verbs

来 (towards the speaker) and 去 (away from the speaker) attach to verbs of movement to show direction. Combine them with 进 (enter), 出 (exit), 上 (up), 下 (down), 回 (return) and 过 (across), and you get a little system:

| | towards me (来) | away from me (去) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 进 in | 进来 come in | 进去 go in |
| 出 out | 出来 come out | 出去 go out |
| 上 up | 上来 come up | 上去 go up |
| 下 down | 下来 come down | 下去 go down |
| 回 back | 回来 come back | 回去 go back |
| 过 across | 过来 come over | 过去 go over |

If you're inside, a friend at the door hears 请进来！ (come in!). If you're both outside, it's 我们进去吧 (let's go in).

```words
进 | to enter
出 | to go out, to come out
进来 | to come in
进去 | to go in
出来 | to come out
出去 | to go out
上来 | to come up
上去 | to go up
下来 | to come down
下去 | to go down; to carry on
回来 | to come back
回去 | to go back
过来 | to come over
过去 | to go over; (in the) past
打开 | to open
门 | door
出门 | to go out (of the house)
```

## A busy Saturday

```dialogue
title: What did you do?
小红: 你昨天做什么了？ | What did you do yesterday?
马克: 我去了一个新饭店，吃了北京烤鸭。 | I went to a new restaurant and had Peking duck.
小红: 你以前吃过烤鸭吗？ | Had you ever had duck before?
马克: 没吃过，这是第一次！太好吃了。 | Never, it was the first time! Delicious.
小红: 你看完那个电影了吗？ | Did you finish that film?
马克: 还没有。我忘了！ | Not yet. I forgot!
```

:::key
- Verb + 了 = the action is done. Negative: 没 + verb (no 了). Question: …了吗？ / …了没有？
- 已经…了 = already. Verb + 完 = finish doing.
- Verb + 过 = have ever done. Negative: 没 + verb + 过. Times: 次.
- 来 = towards the speaker, 去 = away: 进来, 出去, 回来.
:::
