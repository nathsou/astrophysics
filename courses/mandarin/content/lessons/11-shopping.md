Prices are where numbers get real. A market stall holder in China will not say "twelve yuan fifty" but 十二块五 (twelve lumps five), fast, with a calculator held up for good measure. By the end of this lesson you'll understand them, and you'll know how to ask for a better price.

## Money

China's currency is the 人民币 (rénmínbì, "the people's money"), counted in 元 (yuán). But in speech almost nobody says 元: they say **块** (kuài, literally "a lump").

| Written | Spoken | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 元 yuán | 块 kuài | one yuan |
| 角 jiǎo | 毛 máo | a tenth of a yuan |
| 分 fēn | 分 fēn | a hundredth (rarely used now) |

Prices are said from the biggest unit down, and the last unit is usually dropped:

| Price | Said | Literally |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ¥3 | 三块 | three lumps |
| ¥2 | 两块 | two lumps (两, not 二!) |
| ¥12.50 | 十二块五 | twelve lumps five (tenths) |
| ¥0.80 | 八毛 | eight tenths |
| ¥105 | 一百零五块 | a hundred and five lumps |

```words
钱 | money
块 | kuai, a yuan (spoken)
元 | yuan (written)
多少钱 | how much (money)?
贵 | expensive
便宜 | cheap
```

:::culture
Cash has almost disappeared from Chinese cities. People pay by scanning a QR code with WeChat Pay or Alipay, even at street stalls, temples and for buskers. Visitors can now link foreign cards to these apps. You will still need to understand spoken prices, though.
:::

## This one, that one

这 (this) and 那 (that) need a measure word before a noun, just like numbers: 这个 (this one), 那件衣服 (that piece of clothing), 这本书 (this book). To ask the price, point and say:

> 这个多少钱？ How much is this?

```words
买 | to buy
卖 | to sell
东西 | thing, stuff
衣服 | clothes
件 | measure word for clothes
本 | measure word for books
书 | book
穿 | to wear, to put on
好看 | good-looking, nice
漂亮 | pretty, beautiful
新 | new
```

买 (mǎi, buy) and 卖 (mài, sell) differ only in tone, and the character for "sell" is "buy" with an extra bit on top (as if you add something to the deal). Getting the tone wrong in a shop gives you the opposite meaning. Worth one more listen:

```tones

  - zh: 卖
    en: to sell

    en: to buy
  - zh: 卖
    en: to sell
```

## Too expensive!

太 (tài) means "too", and it comes with 了 at the end in exclamations: **太…了！**

- 太贵了！ Too expensive!
- 太好了！ Great! (too good!)
- 太漂亮了！ So pretty!

To ask for a discount, the classic line is 便宜一点儿吧！ "(Make it) a little cheaper!". 一点儿 (a little) comes **after** the adjective.

```words
太 | too (much)
一点儿 | a little, a bit
再 | again; more
只 | only
真 | really
对 | correct, right
```

## At the market

```dialogue
title: Bargaining for a jacket
马克: 你好，这件衣服多少钱？ | Hi, how much is this jacket?
老板: 三百块。 | 300 kuai.
马克: 三百？太贵了！便宜一点儿吧。 | 300? That's too expensive! A bit cheaper, please.
老板: 两百八，怎么样？ | 280, how about that?
马克: 两百。 | 200.
老板: 不行不行……两百五！ | No way… 250!
马克: 两百二，我就买。 | 220 and I'll buy it.
老板: 好吧，两百二。 | Fine, 220.
```

:::fun
Never offer 250 (二百五, èrbǎiwǔ) for anything. It's slang for an idiot, for reasons historians still argue about. A shopkeeper asking for 250 is probably not insulting you, but a customer offering it might get a laugh.
:::

```roleplay
title: Bargain hunter
setting: A clothes market in Shanghai. You like a T-shirt on a stall.

goal: Ask the price of the T-shirt, say it's too expensive, and agree on a lower price.
opener: 你好！这件衣服很漂亮，你喜欢吗？
openerEn: Hi! This top is lovely, do you like it?
words: [多少钱, 太贵了, 便宜一点儿, 块, 买]
checks: [asked the price, said it was too expensive, agreed on a price]
```

:::key
- Spoken prices: 块 (yuan) and 毛 (tenth), last unit dropped: 十二块五. Two yuan is 两块.
- 这/那 + measure word + noun: 这件衣服, 那本书. "How much is this?" 这个多少钱？
- 太…了 for "too…!" and exclamations; adjective + 一点儿 for "a bit more…".
- 买 mǎi buy, 卖 mài sell: mind the tone.
:::
