Single syllables are the gym; words are the match. In real speech tones sit next to each other, and like neighbours they adjust a little. The good news: there are only three adjustments worth learning, and one light, short tone that makes Mandarin sound relaxed instead of robotic.

By the end of this lesson you will say 你好 the way Chinese people do, and you will know why it is not written that way.

## The neutral tone: short and light

Some syllables have no tone of their own. They are said quickly and lightly, as if tucked behind the syllable before them. Pinyin writes them with **no mark**.

You have already met a few. The neutral tone turns up in four places:

1. **Little grammar words**: 吗 (question), 呢 (and you?), 吧 (let's), 的 ('s), 了 (done).
2. **The second half of doubled words**: 妈妈, 爸爸, 谢谢, 看看 (have a look).
3. **Common suffixes**: 子 in 桌子 (table) and 儿子 (son), 们 in 我们 (we).
4. **The end of some everyday words**, by habit: 东西 dōngxi (thing), 朋友 péngyou (friend), 喜欢 xǐhuan (to like).

Its pitch depends on what comes before it: a little lower after first, second and fourth tones, a little higher after a third. You do not need to think about that. Just keep it short, like the *-ing* in *walking*, and it comes out right.

```words
我们 | we, us
朋友 | friend
喜欢 | to like
东西 | thing
看看 | to have a look
```

:::fun
Neutral tones can change meaning too. 东西 dōngxī, with a full first tone on the second syllable, is "east and west". 东西 dōngxi, with a neutral tone, is "thing, stuff". So "buying things" is literally "buying east-west".
:::

## When two third tones meet

Try saying *nǐ hǎo* as written: two full dips, down-up, down-up. It is slow and awkward, and nobody does it. When a third tone comes right before another third tone, the first one **becomes a second tone**:

> 你好 is written *nǐ hǎo* but said *ní hǎo*.

Pinyin keeps writing the original tones. It is like the *t* in English *water*: written the same, said differently, and everyone knows.

And when a third tone comes before **any other tone**, it keeps only its low part, the dip without the bounce: a "half third". This is actually the third tone you will say most often. In 老师 lǎoshī (teacher), the 老 sinks low and stays there, then 师 jumps up high.

Flip the switch to compare what is written with what is said:

::sandhi-lab{phrases="你好,很好,老师,我也很好,可以,美国,水果,喜欢"}

:::tip
Three or more third tones in a row (我也很好, "I am fine too") get grouped by meaning, and speakers vary. Do not overthink it: say the last one fully, and make the others rise or stay low, whichever flows. Nobody will misunderstand you.
:::

## 不 and 一: the two shape-shifters

Two very common characters change their tone so predictably that pinyin **does** write the change.

**不 bù (not)** is fourth tone, except before another fourth tone, where it becomes second:

- 不好 *bù hǎo* (not good), 不忙 *bù máng* (not busy)
- 不是 ***bú*** *shì* (is not), 不对 ***bú*** *duì* (not right)

Two falls in a row are hard, so the first one rises instead.

**一 yī (one)** is the shapeshifter. It stays first tone when you are counting, in numbers, in dates and when it means "first" (一, 十一, 第一, 一月). Otherwise it becomes:

- ***yí*** before a fourth tone: 一个 *yí gè* (one of), 一样 *yíyàng* (the same)
- ***yì*** before the other tones: 一天 *yì tiān* (one day), 一起 *yìqǐ* (together), 一年 *yì nián* (one year)

::sandhi-lab{phrases="不好,不是,不对,一,一个,一天,一起,一样,第一,十一"}

The pinyin in this course follows the standard: 一 and 不 are written as said, everything else as the dictionary has it.

## Two-syllable words

Most Chinese words have two syllables, so the most useful ear training is on pairs. There are 4 × 4 = 16 combinations of full tones (and more with neutral tones). Each one has its own little melody, and you will start to hear them as shapes rather than as two separate tones.

Some words to listen to, one for each first tone:

| Starts with | Example |
| --- | --- |
| 1st | 飞机 fēijī (plane): high-high, flat as a runway |
| 2nd | 学生 xuésheng (student): rising, then a light neutral |
| 3rd | 老师 lǎoshī (teacher): low, then high |
| 4th | 再见 zàijiàn (goodbye): fall, fall |

```words
飞机 | plane
学生 | student
老师 | teacher
再见 | goodbye
不 | not, no
是 | to be
对 | correct, right
很 | very
```

## Say it

Try the tone mirror on a few words. With two syllables the mirror does not mark you right or wrong. Instead it shows each syllable's target shape in its own slot, so you can see if your melody matches.

```speak
title: Words out loud
items:
  - 老师
  - 飞机
  - 再见
  - 你好
```

:::key
- Neutral-tone syllables are short and light, with no mark: grammar words, doubled words, suffixes, some word endings.
- 3rd + 3rd: the first becomes 2nd (你好 *ní hǎo*), but it is still written 3rd.
- A 3rd before other tones is just low: a "half third".
- 不 becomes *bú* before a 4th tone. 一 becomes *yí* before a 4th tone and *yì* before others, but stays *yī* in counting and ordinals.
:::
