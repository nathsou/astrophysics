Chinese grammar has a reputation for being hard. In fact it is wonderfully free of the things that make European languages painful: no verb conjugations (*I am, you are, she is*: all just 是), no plurals, no genders, no tenses in the verb. Word order does most of the work, and it is close to English.

In this lesson you will have your first real conversation.

## Hello and goodbye

```dialogue
title: First meeting
王小明: 你好！ | Hello!
安娜: 你好！ | Hello!
王小明: 我叫王小明。你叫什么名字？ | My name is Wang Xiaoming. What's your name?
安娜: 我叫安娜。 | My name is Anna.
王小明: 认识你很高兴！ | Nice to meet you!
安娜: 认识你，我也很高兴。 | Nice to meet you too.
```

Play it, then **shadow** it: the course plays each line and gives you time to say it back. Shadowing is the single most effective speaking drill there is, because you copy the rhythm and tones of whole sentences rather than assembling them word by word.

```words
你好 | hello
你 | you
我 | I, me
叫 | to be called
什么 | what
名字 | name
认识 | to know (a person), to meet
高兴 | happy, glad
很 | very
也 | also, too
再见 | goodbye
```

A few things to notice:

- 你叫什么名字？ is literally "you are-called what name?". In Chinese, **question words stay where the answer goes**. The answer replaces 什么: 我叫安娜。 No reordering, unlike English "What is your name?".
- 认识你很高兴 is literally "knowing you, (I'm) very happy". You will hear it in every first meeting.
- Chinese names put the **family name first**: 王小明 is Mr Wang, given name Xiaoming. A teacher called Wang is 王老师, "Wang teacher".

:::culture
你好 is the textbook greeting, but between friends it can sound a little stiff. People who know each other often greet with the person's name or a question instead: 吃了吗？ (eaten yet?) is a classic, and 去哪儿？ (where are you off to?) is another. Nobody expects a real answer. To a teacher, an older person or a customer, use the polite 您好 (nín hǎo).
:::

## Thank you, sorry, and their answers

Four phrases you will use every single day, each with its standard reply:

```dialogue
title: Polite reflexes
A: 谢谢！ | Thank you!
B: 不客气。 | You're welcome. (literally "don't be polite")
A: 对不起！ | Sorry!
B: 没关系。 | It doesn't matter. / No problem.
A: 请问，你是老师吗？ | Excuse me, are you a teacher?
B: 不是，我是学生。 | No, I'm a student.
```

```words
谢谢 | thank you
不客气 | you're welcome
对不起 | sorry
没关系 | it doesn't matter
请问 | excuse me, may I ask…
```


## A is B: the verb 是

是 (shì) means "to be" when it links two nouns: "I am a student", "she is a teacher". It never changes form.

| Chinese | Literally | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 我是学生。 | I be student. | I'm a student. |
| 她是老师。 | She be teacher. | She's a teacher. |
| 我们是朋友。 | We be friend. | We're friends. |
| 他不是中国人。 | He not be China person. | He's not Chinese. |

To say "not", put 不 in front of the verb: 不是 (bú shì, remember the tone change).

Nationalities are country + 人 (person): 中国人 Chinese, 英国人 British, 美国人 American, 法国人 French. To ask, use 哪 (which): 你是哪国人？ "You are which-country person?"

```words
是 | to be
不 | not, no
人 | person
中国 | China
中国人 | Chinese person
老师 | teacher
学生 | student
朋友 | friend
同学 | classmate
哪 | which
国 | country
他 | he, him
她 | she, her
```

:::grammar[One word for he, she and it]
他 (he) and 她 (she) are both pronounced *tā*. The difference only exists in writing, and only since the 1920s, when writers invented 她 (with the 女 woman radical) to translate European languages. In speech, you only know from context. 它 (it), also *tā*, is for animals and things.
:::

```order
    zh: 我们 是 朋友

  - en: What's your name?

```

## Questions with 吗 and 呢

Turning a statement into a yes-or-no question could not be simpler: add 吗 at the end.

> 你是老师。 You are a teacher. → 你是老师吗？ Are you a teacher?

There is no word for "yes" or "no" as such. You answer with the verb itself: 是 (yes, I am) or 不是 (no, I'm not). Asked 你是学生吗？, reply 是 or 不是, or the full 我是学生 / 我不是学生.

呢 (ne) asks "and you?" or "what about…?". It bounces the same question back without repeating it:

> 我是英国人，你呢？ I'm British, and you?

```dialogue
title: Where are you from?
马克: 你好！你是中国人吗？ | Hi! Are you Chinese?
小红: 是，我是中国人。你呢？ | Yes, I'm Chinese. And you?
马克: 我是美国人。 | I'm American.
小红: 你是学生吗？ | Are you a student?
马克: 不是，我是老师。你也是老师吗？ | No, I'm a teacher. Are you a teacher too?
小红: 不，我是学生。 | No, I'm a student.
```

也 (also) and 都 (all, both) go **before the verb**, never at the end: 我也是学生 (I also am a student), 我们都是学生 (we all are students).

```words
吗 | yes/no question particle
呢 | and you? what about…?
都 | all, both
你们 | you (plural)
我们 | we, us
他们 | they (male or mixed)
她们 | they (female)
您 | you (polite)
谁 | who
```

```fill
items:

    options: [吗, 呢, 也]
    answer: 0
  - zh: 我很好，你___？

  - zh: 我是老师，他___是老师。

  - zh: 你叫___名字？

    options: [什么, 谁, 哪]
    answer: 0
  - zh: 他是___？

```

:::mistake
English speakers often say 我是很好 for "I am very well". But 是 only links nouns. With an adjective like 好, 高兴 or 忙, there is no 是: just 我很好. The 很 is usually there even when you don't mean "very"; it makes the sentence sound complete. More on this in lesson 14.
:::

## Put it all together

You walk into a language exchange in Beijing. Someone comes over to say hello. Choose what you say; if you choose wrong, you will find out how they react.

```scene
title: At a language exchange
setting: A café in Beijing. A young woman with a name badge comes over to your table.
partner: 小红
turns:
  - they: 你好！
    theyEn: Hello!
    options:
      - zh: 你好！
        en: Hello!
        ok: true
      - zh: 再见！
        en: Goodbye!
        reply: 啊？你要走吗？
        replyEn: Huh? You're leaving?
  - they: 我叫小红。你叫什么名字？
    theyEn: My name is Xiaohong. What's your name?
    options:
      - zh: 我叫安娜。
        en: My name is Anna.
        ok: true
      - zh: 我是名字。
        en: I am a name.
        reply: 哈哈，你是名字？
        replyEn: Haha, you're a name?
      - zh: 你叫小红。
        en: You are called Xiaohong.
        reply: 对，我叫小红……你呢？
        replyEn: Yes, I'm Xiaohong… and you?
  - they: 你是哪国人？
    theyEn: Where are you from?
    options:
      - zh: 我是英国人。
        en: I'm British.
        ok: true
      - zh: 我是中国。
        en: I am China.
        reply: 你是中国？你是一个国家吗？
        replyEn: You're China? Are you a country?
  - they: 认识你很高兴！
    theyEn: Nice to meet you!
    options:
      - zh: 认识你，我也很高兴。
        en: Nice to meet you too.
        ok: true
      - zh: 对不起。
        en: Sorry.
        reply: 没关系……为什么对不起？
        replyEn: No problem… but why sorry?
      - zh: 不客气。
        en: You're welcome.
        reply: 嗯……谢谢？
        replyEn: Um… thanks?
end: 小红 smiles and pulls up a chair. Your first conversation in Chinese!
```
