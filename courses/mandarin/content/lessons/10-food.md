In China, food is a love language. 吃了吗？ "Have you eaten?" is a greeting. A good meal is shared, ordered for the table, and argued over at the end when everyone tries to pay. This lesson gets you through a restaurant, a breakfast stall and a friend's kitchen.

Some of these are clever compounds: 鸡蛋 is "chicken egg", 牛奶 "cow milk", 面包 "flour wrap", 水果 "water fruit" (juicy fruit, as opposed to nuts). 菜 means both "vegetable" and "dish": a Chinese menu is a list of 菜.

:::culture
Meals are named after rice: 早饭 (morning rice) is breakfast, 午饭 lunch, 晚饭 dinner. In the north, wheat is the staple (noodles, dumplings, steamed buns); in the south, rice. Breakfast is often savoury and bought from a stall on the way to work: 包子, soy milk, fried dough sticks, rice porridge.
:::

## Wanting: 想 and 要

Two verbs do almost all the ordering:

- **想** (xiǎng) is "would like to", soft and polite: 我想喝茶 (I'd like to drink tea).
- **要** (yào) is "want", direct: 我要一杯茶 (I want a cup of tea). In shops and restaurants, 要 is completely normal and not rude at all.

Both can take a verb (想吃, 要喝) or a thing (要米饭). The negative of "want" is usually **不想** (don't feel like) or **不要** (don't want; also "don't!").

```words
想 | to want to, would like to; to think
要 | to want; to need
不要 | to not want; don’t!
做 | to do; to make
做饭 | to cook
好吃 | tasty, delicious
饿 | hungry
```

## Measure words of the table

In the last lessons you met 个 and 口. Food brings its own set: what you count with depends on the container.

| Measure word | For | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 杯 (cup, glass) | drinks | 一杯茶 a cup of tea |
| 碗 (bowl) | rice, noodles, soup | 两碗米饭 two bowls of rice |
| 个 | most things | 三个包子 three steamed buns |
| 盘 (plate) | dishes | 一盘饺子 a plate of dumplings |

## At the restaurant

```dialogue
title: Ordering
服务员: 你好！几位？ | Hello! How many of you?
马克: 两个人。 | Two.
服务员: 请坐。你们想吃什么？ | Please sit down. What would you like to eat?
马克: 我要一碗面条儿，一盘饺子。 | I'll have a bowl of noodles and a plate of dumplings.
小红: 我要米饭和这个菜。 | I'll have rice and this dish.
服务员: 喝什么？ | Anything to drink?
小红: 两杯茶，谢谢。 | Two cups of tea, thanks.
服务员: 好的，还要别的吗？ | Sure, anything else?
马克: 不要了，谢谢。 | No, that's all, thanks.
```

A few restaurant phrases are hidden in there:

- 几位？ How many (people)? 位 is the polite measure word for people.
- 还要别的吗？ Anything else? (still want other?) The answer 不要了 means "that's all": the 了 marks a change, "no more".
- 请坐 Please sit; 请 + verb is "please do".

```words
杯子 | cup, glass
桌子 | table
椅子 | chair
饭店 | restaurant; hotel
早饭 | breakfast
午饭 | lunch
晚饭 | dinner
请 | please; to invite
还 | still; also, in addition
给 | to give; for
```

:::fun
Paying is a sport. When friends eat together in China, the bill is usually paid by one person, and there is often a friendly fight over who gets to pay: grabbing the bill, sneaking off to pay at the counter "to go to the bathroom". Splitting the bill (AA制, "AA system") is getting more common among young people.
:::

:::key
- 想 = would like to (soft); 要 = want (direct, normal when ordering). 不要了 = that's all.
- Food has its own measure words: 杯 for drinks, 碗 for bowls, 个 for most things.
- 请 + verb = please do: 请坐, 请给我…
- 早饭, 午饭, 晚饭: breakfast, lunch, dinner.
:::
