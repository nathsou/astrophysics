Is the red one cheaper than the black one? Is Shanghai bigger than Beijing? Who is the tallest in your family? Comparisons are everywhere in conversation, and Chinese builds them with one small word and the adjective you already know how to use.

## A 比 B + adjective

To say A is more … than B:

> A + **比** + B + adjective
> 哥哥比我高。 My brother is taller than me. (brother compared-to me tall)

No 很 here, and no "more": 比 already does the comparing. To add how much, put the amount **after** the adjective:

- 哥哥比我高**一点儿**。 My brother is a bit taller than me.
- 这个比那个贵**多了**。 This one is much more expensive than that one.
- 他比我大**两岁**。 He's two years older than me.

To say "even more", use 更 (gèng) before the adjective: 今天比昨天更冷 (today is even colder than yesterday).

The negative is usually 没有…(那么)…, "not as … as": 我没有哥哥高 (I'm not as tall as my brother).

```words
比 | than; compared with
高 | tall, high
长 | long
快 | fast
慢 | slow
个子 | height, build
多了 | much (more)
这么 | so, this (much)
那么 | so, that (much)
```

:::mistake
我比他很高 ✗. With 比, drop the 很: 我比他高 ✓. If you want "much taller", it's 我比他高多了 or 我比他高得多, with the amount after the adjective.
:::

## The same: 跟…一样

A **跟** B **一样** means "A is the same as B". Add an adjective for "as … as":

- 我的手机跟你的一样。 My phone is the same as yours.
- 他跟我一样高。 He's as tall as me.
- 我们不一样。 We're different.

```words
跟 | with; (in comparisons) as
一样 | the same
更 | even more
最 | the most
```

## The best: 最

最 (zuì) before an adjective or verb makes a superlative: 最好 (the best), 最大 (the biggest), 我最喜欢… (I like … most).

- 她是我们班最高的。 She's the tallest in our class.
- 你最喜欢什么颜色？ What's your favourite colour?

## Colours

Colour words are often the colour + 色 (colour): 红色, 黑色. As adjectives, the 色 often drops: 红衣服 (red clothes).

```words
颜色 | colour
红色 | red
黑色 | black
白色 | white
绿色 | green
不错 | not bad, pretty good
```

:::culture
Red (红) is the colour of luck, celebration and weddings in China: red envelopes of money at New Year, red lanterns, red wedding dresses. White is traditionally the colour of mourning, though white wedding dresses have become popular too. And on Chinese stock market screens, rising prices are shown in red and falling ones in green: the opposite of the West.
:::

## This or that? 还是

还是 (háishi) asks "or?" in questions, offering a choice:

- 你要红色的还是黑色的？ Do you want the red one or the black one?
- 你喝茶还是喝咖啡？ Will you drink tea or coffee?

The noun after 的 can be dropped when it's obvious: 红色的 (the red one).

```words
还是 | or (in questions)
这样 | like this, this way
那样 | like that, that way
坏 | bad; broken
错 | wrong
```

## Shopping, compared

```dialogue
title: Which one?
小红: 这两件衣服，你觉得哪件好看？ | These two tops, which do you think looks nicer?
安娜: 红色的比黑色的好看。 | The red one looks nicer than the black one.
小红: 可是红色的比黑色的贵一百块。 | But the red one is 100 kuai more expensive than the black one.
安娜: 那你最喜欢哪个颜色？ | Then which colour do you like best?
小红: 我最喜欢绿色！ | Green, I like green best!
安娜: 这儿没有绿色的……白色的怎么样？跟红色的一样好看。 | There's no green one… how about the white? It looks as nice as the red one.
```


:::key
- A 比 B + adjective (no 很); the amount goes after: 高一点儿, 贵多了, 大两岁.
- 更 = even more. Negative: A 没有 B + adjective.
- A 跟 B 一样 (+ adjective) = the same (as).
- 最 + adjective = the most.
- 还是 = "or" in questions.
:::
