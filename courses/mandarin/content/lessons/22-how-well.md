"You speak Chinese very well!" (你汉语说得很好！) is the compliment every learner hears, often after saying nothing more than 你好. By the end of this lesson you'll understand exactly how it's built, and you'll know the modest reply.

## How well: verb + 得 + description

To describe **how** an action is done, put 得 (de) after the verb and the description after that:

- 他跑**得**很快。 He runs fast.
- 你说**得**很好！ You speak very well!
- 我写**得**不好。 I don't write well.
- 她唱**得**怎么样？ How well does she sing?

If the verb has an object, say the verb twice, or move the object to the front:

- 他**说汉语说**得很好。 He speaks Chinese well. (speaks Chinese, speaks well)
- 他**汉语**说得很好。 (same, object first: the most common form)

```words
得 | links a verb to how well it is done
错 | wrong; mistake
懂 | to understand
自己 | oneself
```

:::culture
The polite answer to a compliment is to deflect it. 哪里哪里 (nǎli nǎli, "where? where?") is the classic, a bit old-fashioned now; 还不错 (still not bad) or 还差得远 (still far off) are common too. A simple 谢谢 is perfectly fine these days, especially from foreigners.
:::

## A bit: 有点儿 vs 一点儿

Both mean "a bit", and they're one of the most common mix-ups in Chinese:

| | Position | Feeling | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **有点儿** | **before** the adjective | usually unwelcome | 这个有点儿贵。 This is a bit expensive. |
| **一点儿** | **after** the adjective (or verb) | comparison, request | 便宜一点儿吧。 A bit cheaper, please. |

一点儿 also goes after verbs for "a little": 我会说一点儿汉语 (I can speak a little Chinese), 多吃一点儿！ (eat a bit more!).

:::fun
The 高考 (gāokǎo), China's university entrance exam, is a national event. Over thirteen million students sit it each June. Construction sites near schools go quiet, police escort late students, and mothers wear qipao dresses (旗袍) for luck, because 旗开得胜 means "win as soon as the flag is raised".
:::

## Introducing yourself formally

```words
介绍 | to introduce
姓 | surname; to be surnamed
姓名 | full name
名 | name; place (in a ranking)
位 | polite measure word for people
从小 | since childhood
小时候 | as a child
```

In formal situations, people ask your surname, politely: 您贵姓？ "Your honourable surname?" The answer is 我姓王 (my surname is Wang), never 我贵姓 (you don't honour yourself). 位 is the polite measure word for people: 这位是王老师 (this is Teacher Wang).

```dialogue
title: Meeting the new teacher
校长: 大家好！我来介绍一下，这位是我们的新老师。 | Hello everyone! Let me introduce our new teacher.
新老师: 大家好，我姓陈，叫陈小红。 | Hello everyone, my surname is Chen, my name is Chen Xiaohong.
学生: 陈老师好！您教什么？ | Hello Teacher Chen! What do you teach?
新老师: 我教汉语。我从小就喜欢汉字。 | I teach Chinese. I've loved characters since I was little.
学生: 陈老师，您的汉字写得真漂亮！ | Teacher Chen, your characters are beautiful!
新老师: 哪里哪里，谢谢！ | Oh, not at all, thank you!
```

:::key
- Verb + 得 + description = how well: 他跑得很快. With an object: 他汉语说得很好.
- 有点儿 + adjective (unwelcome); adjective / verb + 一点儿 (a bit more, a little).
- 您贵姓？ — 我姓…; 位 is the polite measure word for people.
:::
