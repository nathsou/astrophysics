---
title: Rectangle contained by two lines
---
A rectangle is *contained by* the two sides that meet at one of its right angles. So a rectangle is determined, up to position, by two lengths $a$ and $b$, and Euclid can speak of "the rectangle contained by $AB$, $BC$" without drawing it.

In modern terms this rectangle is the product $AB \cdot BC$, and "the square on $AB$" is $AB^2$. This definition is what lets Book II be read as algebra. Note that Euclid never multiplies the lengths: the rectangle is a figure, and two rectangles are "equal" when they have the same area, not when they are congruent.
