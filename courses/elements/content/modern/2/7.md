---
title: Square of a difference
---
**Statement.** If $AB$ is cut at $C$, then
$$AB^2 + BC^2 = 2\,AB \cdot BC + AC^2.$$
With $AB = a$ and $BC = b$: $a^2 + b^2 = 2ab + (a-b)^2$, which is the familiar $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$.

**Proof.** Draw the square $ADEB$ with the same construction as [[2.4]] ("let the figure be drawn"). The two rectangles @figure(AF) and @figure(CE) are each $AB \cdot BC$, since $BF = BC$. Together they cover the gnomon @figure(KLM) once and the square @figure(CF) twice. Add the square @figure(DG), which is the square on $AC$: the gnomon and @figure(DG) make the square on @AB, and the extra @figure(CF) is the square on @BC.

**In the figure.** Drag @C. The overlap of the two rectangles is always the square @figure(CF).

:::leads Where it leads
[[2.13]] uses this identity, with $CB$ cut at the foot of a perpendicular, to prove the law of cosines for an acute angle.
:::
