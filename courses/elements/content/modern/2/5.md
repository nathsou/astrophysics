---
title: Product as a difference of squares
---
**Statement.** Let $AB$ be bisected at $C$ and cut unequally at $D$. Then
$$AD \cdot DB + CD^2 = CB^2.$$
With $CB = h$ and $CD = x$, this is $(h+x)(h-x) + x^2 = h^2$, the difference of two squares. With $AD = a$ and $DB = b$ it reads
$$ab = \left(\frac{a+b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{a-b}{2}\right)^2 .$$

**Proof.** Draw the square $CEFB$ on @CB, its diagonal @BE, and, as in [[2.4]], the parallels through @D and through the point @H where @DG meets the diagonal. The idea is to turn the rectangle @figure(AH) (which is $AD \cdot DB$, since $DH = DB$) into a gnomon.

- The complements @figure(CH) and @figure(HF) are equal ([[1.43]]). Add the square @figure(DM) to both: the rectangle @figure(CM) equals the rectangle @figure(DF).
- @figure(CM) equals @figure(AL), since they have equal bases $AC = CB$ and lie between the same parallels ([[1.36]]). So @figure(AL) equals @figure(DF).
- Add @figure(CH) to both: @figure(AH) equals the gnomon @figure(NOP).

So $AD \cdot DB$ is the gnomon. The gnomon and the small square @figure(LG), which is the square on $CD$, make up the square on @CB.

**In the figure.** Drag @D between @C and @B. The rectangle @figure(AH) and the gnomon @figure(NOP) change shape but stay equal.

:::leads Solving quadratics
II.5 is how Greek geometry solves the equation $x(b - x) = c$: find two lengths with a given sum $b$ and a given product $c$. Put $AB = b$, and look for $D$ with $AD \cdot DB = c$. By II.5, $CD^2 = (b/2)^2 - c$, so $CD$ is the side of a known square, and
$$x = DB = \frac b2 - \sqrt{\left(\frac b2\right)^2 - c}.$$
This is the quadratic formula. It also shows the condition: a solution exists only if $c \le (b/2)^2$, the largest rectangle with a given perimeter being the square. Euclid states that limit in [[6.27]], and solves the general problem, "applying" an area to a line so that it falls short by a square, in [[6.28]].
