---
title: Gnomon
---
Take a parallelogram and a point on its diagonal, and draw the two lines through the point parallel to the sides. This cuts the parallelogram into four pieces: two parallelograms about the diagonal and two *complements* ([[1.43]]). A **gnomon** is one of the parallelograms about the diagonal together with the two complements: an L-shaped region.

For a square it is the difference of two squares, $a^2 - b^2$, as a shape. This is the tool of [[2.5]], [[2.6]], [[2.7]] and [[2.8]]: an area is moved into a gnomon, and the gnomon plus the small square completes the big one. The word first meant the upright pointer of a sundial, and then the carpenter's square, which has the same L shape.
