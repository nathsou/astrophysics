---
title: The square as a sum of two rectangles
---
**Statement.** If $AB$ is cut at $C$, then
$$AB \cdot BC + AB \cdot AC = AB^2.$$
With $AC = a$ and $CB = b$: $(a+b)b + (a+b)a = (a+b)^2$.

**Proof.** Draw the square @figure(AE) on @AB and the line @CF through @C parallel to its sides. It cuts the square into @figure(AF), with sides $AD = AB$ and $AC$, and @figure(CE), with sides $BE = AB$ and $BC$. This is [[2.1]] with the uncut line equal to the whole.

**In the figure.** Drag @C along @AB: the two rectangles trade area, and they always make up the square.
