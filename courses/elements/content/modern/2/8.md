title: Four rectangles and a square
---
**Statement.** If $AB$ is cut at $C$, then
$$4\,AB \cdot BC + AC^2 = (AB + BC)^2.$$
With $AB = a$ and $BC = b$: $4ab + (a - b)^2 = (a + b)^2$.

**Proof.** Produce @AB to @D with $BD = BC$, draw the square $AEFD$ on @AD, and "draw the figure double": two diagonal constructions of [[2.4]], through @K and through @Q. The eight rectangles that make up the gnomon @figure(STU) come in two families of four equal pieces.

- @figure(CK), @figure(KD), @figure(GR) and @figure(RN) are equal, by equal bases ([[1.36]]) and complements ([[1.43]]).
- @figure(AG), @figure(MQ), @figure(QL) and @figure(RF) are equal for the same reasons.

One piece of each family makes the rectangle @figure(AK), which is $AB \cdot BD = AB \cdot BC$. So the gnomon is four times $AB \cdot BC$, and with the square @figure(OH) on $AC$ it fills the square on @AD.

**In the figure.** Drag @C along @AB.

:::code A second proof
Four copies of an $a \times b$ rectangle arranged around a square of side $a - b$ make a square of side $a + b$. This is the "pinwheel" arrangement often used to prove Pythagoras. Euclid's version is longer because he builds everything from the diagonal construction of [[2.4]].
:::
