---
title: Distributive law
---
**Statement.** If the line $BC$ is cut at $D$ and $E$, then for any line $A$,
$$A \cdot BC = A \cdot BD + A \cdot DE + A \cdot EC.$$
With $BC = b + c + d$, this is $a(b+c+d) = ab + ac + ad$. It holds for any number of pieces.

**Proof.** Stand the rectangle up: draw @BG at right angles to @BC with $BG = A$, and complete the rectangle @figure(BH) on @BC. The lines through @D and @E parallel to @BG cut it into @figure(BK), @figure(DL) and @figure(EH). Each piece has height $A$, because opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal ([[1.34]]), so each piece is the rectangle of $A$ with its part of $BC$. The whole is the sum of its parts.

**In the figure.** Drag @D and @E along @BC, or change the length of @A with the slider. The pieces change, but their sum is always the whole.
