---
title: Least common multiple of three numbers
---
**Statement.** Given $a, b, c$, find the least number they all divide:
$$\operatorname{lcm}(a, b, c) = \operatorname{lcm}(\operatorname{lcm}(a, b), c).$$

**Proof.** Let @D $= \operatorname{lcm}(A, B)$ ([[7.34]]).

- If @C divides $D$, then $D$ is the answer. Any smaller common multiple @E of the three would be a common multiple of $A, B$, hence divisible by $D$ ([[7.35]]), which is impossible.
- Otherwise (the figure) let @E $= \operatorname{lcm}(C, D)$. $A$ and $B$ divide $D$, hence $E$, and so does $C$. If @F were a smaller common multiple of the three, then $D \mid F$ ([[7.35]]) and $C \mid F$, so $E \mid F$ ([[7.35]] again), which is impossible.

This is the mirror image of [[7.3]], with [[7.35]] in the role of the porism of [[7.2]]. As there, the argument extends to any number of terms: `xs.reduce(lcm)`.
