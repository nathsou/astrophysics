---
title: Reducing to lowest terms
---
**Statement.** Given numbers $a, b, c, \dots$, find the least numbers in the same ratio. The answer is to divide each by their greatest common divisor $d$:
$$(a, b, c) \mapsto \left(\tfrac ad, \tfrac bd, \tfrac cd\right).$$

**Proof.** If $a, b, c$ are prime to one another, they are already least ([[7.21]]). Otherwise let @D be their gcd ([[7.3]]), and let @E, @F, @G be the quotients: $A = D \cdot E$, and so on. By [[7.16]], @E, @F, @G divide @A, @B, @C the same number of times (namely $D$), so they are in the same ratio.

They are the least. Suppose @H, @K, @L were smaller numbers in the same ratio. Then they would divide @A, @B, @C the same number of times, say @M times, so $M$ would divide all three ([[7.16]]). From $E \cdot D = A = H \cdot M$ we get $E : H = M : D$ ([[7.19]]), and $E > H$ gives $M > D$. So $M$ would be a common divisor greater than the greatest, which is impossible.

For two numbers, this is reducing a fraction: $\frac{12}{18} \mapsto \frac 23$.
