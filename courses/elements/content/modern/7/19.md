---
title: Cross-multiplication
---
**Statement.** $a : b = c : d$ if and only if $a d = b c$. In the figure, @E $= A \cdot D$ (the extremes) and @F $= B \cdot C$ (the means).

**Proof.** Let @G $= A \cdot C$.

- By [[7.17]], $C : D = G : E$ (both multiplied by $A$). By [[7.18]], $A : B = G : F$ (both multiplying $C$).
- ($\Rightarrow$) If $A : B = C : D$, then $G : E = G : F$, so $E = F$.
- ($\Leftarrow$) If $E = F$, then $G : E = G : F$, which gives $C : D = A : B$.

This is the modern *definition* of equality of fractions: $\frac ab = \frac cd$ means $ad = bc$. From here on, Euclid uses this proposition whenever he needs to turn a product equation into a proportion, notably in [[7.20]]–[[7.30]].

:::gap Borrowing from Book V
The steps "$G : E = G : F$, so $E = F$", and its converse, are [[5.9]] and [[5.7]], proved for magnitudes with Book V's definition of ratio. Book VII has its own definition of proportion ([[7.def.20]]), and Euclid never shows that the two agree for numbers. For numbers the step is easy to prove directly: if $e$ is the same fraction of $g$ as $f$ is, then $e = f$. But Euclid does not prove it.
:::
