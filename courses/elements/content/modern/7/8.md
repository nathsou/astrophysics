---
title: Subtracting equal fractions
---
**Statement.** If $a = \tfrac mn b$ and $a' = \tfrac mn b'$ with $a' < a$, then $a - a' = \tfrac mn (b - b')$. In the figure, $AB = \tfrac mn CD$ and $AE = \tfrac mn CF$, and the claim is $EB = \tfrac mn FD$.

**Proof.** Copy @AB as @GH and split it into its parts of @CD: @GK and @KH. Split @AE into its parts of @CF: @AL and @LE. Mark @GM equal to @AL, and @KN equal to @LE. Now each piece is a single part:

- $GK = \tfrac1n CD$ and $GM = \tfrac1n CF$, so the remainder $MK = \tfrac1n FD$ by [[7.7]];
- in the same way, $NH = \tfrac1n FD$.

So $MK + NH = EB$ is $m$ of the $n$-th parts of $FD$, which is the claim.

This is the same reduction as in [[7.6]]: split the fraction $\tfrac mn$ into $m$ copies of $\tfrac1n$ and apply the unit-fraction result to each copy.
