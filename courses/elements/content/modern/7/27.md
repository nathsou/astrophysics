---
title: Powers of coprime numbers are coprime
---
**Statement.** If $\gcd(a, b) = 1$, then $\gcd(a^2, b^2) = 1$ and $\gcd(a^3, b^3) = 1$.

**Proof.** @C $= A^2$ is prime to @B by [[7.25]], and then, by [[7.25]] again, $C$ is prime to @E $= B^2$. Likewise @A is prime to $E$. So @A and @C are each prime to @B and @E, and by [[7.26]] the products @D $= A^3$ and @F $= B^3$ are prime to one another.

The same induction gives $\gcd(a^m, b^n) = 1$ for all exponents. The bracketed words in the enunciation, "this is always the case with the extremes", point at this generalization; Heath prints them in brackets as doubtful. Book VIII uses the result for the extremes of geometric sequences, whose terms are powers.
