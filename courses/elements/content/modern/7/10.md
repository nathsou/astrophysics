---
title: Alternation for fractions
---
**Statement.** If $a = \tfrac mn b$ and $c = \tfrac mn d$, then $\dfrac ac = \dfrac bd$. In the figure, @AB is the same parts of @C that @DE is of @F, and the claim is that $AB$ is the same fraction of $DE$ that $C$ is of $F$.

**Proof.** Split @AB into its parts of @C (@AG, @GB) and @DE into its parts of @F (@DH, @HE). For each matching pair, $AG = \tfrac1n C$ and $DH = \tfrac1n F$, so by [[7.9]] $AG$ is the same fraction of $DH$ that $C$ is of $F$. The same holds for $GB$ and $HE$. Adding the pairs back together with [[7.5]] and [[7.6]] gives the same fraction for $AB$ and $DE$.

This completes the preparation. [[7.13]] now states alternation for proportions in general.
