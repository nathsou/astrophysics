---
title: Least common multiple of two numbers
---
**Statement.** Given $a, b$, find the least number they both divide, $\operatorname{lcm}(a, b)$.

- If $\gcd(a, b) = 1$, it is $ab$.
- Otherwise, let $f : e$ be $a : b$ in lowest terms ([[7.33]]). It is $c = a e = b f$.

Both cases are the formula $\operatorname{lcm}(a, b) = \dfrac{ab}{\gcd(a, b)}$.

**Proof (second case, the figure).** Since @F $: $ @E $= A : B$, we have $A \cdot E = B \cdot F$ ([[7.19]]). Call this @C. Both @A and @B divide it. Suppose they both divide a smaller number @D, with $D = A \cdot G = B \cdot H$. Then $A : B = H : G$ ([[7.19]]), so $F : E = H : G$. Since $F, E$ are least, $E \mid G$ ([[7.20]]). But $C : D = E : G$ ([[7.17]], both are multiples of $A$), so $C$ divides the smaller number $D$, which is impossible.

The first case is the same argument with $A, B$ playing the role of $F, E$ (by [[7.21]], coprime numbers are already least).

:::code Divide first
```ts
const lcm = (a: number, b: number) => (a / gcd(a, b)) * b;
```
The quotient $a / \gcd(a, b)$ is the $f$ of the proposition, and $b / \gcd(a,b)$ is $e$. Computing $\frac{a}{\gcd}\cdot b$ instead of $\frac{ab}{\gcd}$ keeps the intermediate value no larger than the result, which matters for fixed-width integers.
:::
