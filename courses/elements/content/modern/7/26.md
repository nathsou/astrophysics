---
title: Products of coprime numbers
---
**Statement.** If each of $a, b$ is prime to each of $c, d$, then $ab$ is prime to $cd$.

**Proof.** Apply [[7.24]] twice. Since @A and @B are both prime to @C, their product @E is prime to $C$. In the same way, $E$ is prime to @D. So @C and @D are both prime to $E$, and by [[7.24]] again, their product @F is prime to $E$.

In terms of prime factors, it is obvious: no prime in $a$ or $b$ appears in $c$ or $d$, so none of the primes of $ab$ appears in $cd$. But Euclid has no prime factorization. He has to work with coprimality directly, one product at a time.
