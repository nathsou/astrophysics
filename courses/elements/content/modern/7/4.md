---
title: Every smaller number is a fraction of a larger one
---
**Statement.** If $b < a$, then $b$ is either a part of $a$ ($b = \tfrac1n a$) or parts of $a$ ($b = \tfrac mn a$). In modern terms, $b/a$ is always a fraction $m/n$ with $m < n$.

**Proof.** Three cases.

1. *$a, b$ coprime.* Split $b$ into its units. Each unit is the $a$-th part of $a$, so $b$ is "$b$ of the $a$-th parts" of $a$: $b = \tfrac ba\, a$. Here the unit counts as a part.
2. *$b \mid a$.* Then $b$ is a part of $a$.
3. *Otherwise* (the figure), let $d = \gcd(a, b)$ (@D), by [[7.2]]. Split @BC into pieces equal to $d$: @BE, @EF, @FC. Each piece divides $a$, so each is a part of $a$, and $b$ is several such parts. With $a = n d$ and $b = m d$ this says $b = \tfrac mn a$.

The gcd makes the fraction canonical: $m/n$ is in lowest terms. This is what [[7.def.20]] relies on when it compares "the same parts".
