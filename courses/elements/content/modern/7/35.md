---
title: Every common multiple is a multiple of the lcm
---
**Statement.** If $a \mid n$ and $b \mid n$, then $\operatorname{lcm}(a, b) \mid n$.

**Proof.** Let @E be the least number measured by @A and @B, and let them both measure @CD. If $E \nmid CD$, lay off copies of $E$ from $D$ as far as they go (@DF) and let @CF be the remainder, less than $E$. $A$ and $B$ divide $E$, hence $DF$. They also divide $CD$, hence the remainder $CF$. So $CF$ is a common multiple of $A$ and $B$ smaller than $E$, which is impossible. So the remainder is empty and $E \mid CD$.

In the figure, @CD is a common multiple and the copies of @E fill it exactly. The last piece, @CF, is a whole copy of $E$ rather than a smaller remainder.

This is the dual of the porism of [[7.2]] (every common divisor divides the gcd), and it has the same proof: whatever divides the pair also divides the remainder. In modern language, the common multiples of $a$ and $b$ form the ideal $\operatorname{lcm}(a, b)\,\mathbb{Z}$.
