---
title: Coprime numbers are least in their ratio
---
**Statement.** If $\gcd(a, b) = 1$, then no smaller pair of numbers has the ratio $a : b$. In fractions: a fraction whose numerator and denominator are coprime is in lowest terms.

**Proof.** Suppose @C, @D are less than @A, @B with $C : D = A : B$. We may take them to be least in their ratio. By [[7.20]], $C$ measures $A$ as many times as $D$ measures $B$; call that number of times @E. By [[7.16]], $E$ then measures $A$ (according to the units of $C$) and $B$ (according to the units of $D$). So $E$ is a common measure of $A$ and $B$. It is a number, not the unit, because $C < A$. This contradicts $\gcd(A, B) = 1$.

**Remark.** The written proof applies [[7.20]] to $C, D$ as if they were least in their ratio, which the reductio does not state. That is harmless: if there is any smaller pair in the ratio, there is a least one, by well-ordering.

[[7.22]] is the converse. Together they say:
$$\frac ab \text{ is in lowest terms} \iff \gcd(a, b) = 1.$$
