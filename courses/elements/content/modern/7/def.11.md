---
title: Prime number
---
A number $p$ is **prime** when the unit is the only thing that measures it (apart from $p$ itself, which Euclid does not count). In modern terms, $p \ge 2$ and its only divisors are $1$ and $p$.

Since $1$ is not a number, the question whether $1$ is prime does not arise for Euclid. Modern mathematics excludes $1$ for a different reason: including it would destroy the uniqueness of factorization ($6 = 2 \cdot 3 = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3$). The primes are the atoms of multiplication; see [[7.30]] and [[7.31]].
