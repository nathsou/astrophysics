---
title: Alternation for parts
---
**Statement.** If $a = \tfrac1n b$ and $c = \tfrac1n d$, then whatever fraction $a$ is of $c$, $b$ is the same fraction of $d$: $\dfrac ac = \dfrac bd$.

**Proof.** @BC is $n$ copies of @A (@BG, @GC), and @EF is $n$ copies of @D (@EH, @HF). Each copy in $BC$ is the same fraction of the matching copy in $EF$, namely $a/c$. Adding them up with [[7.5]] (if $a/c$ is a part) or [[7.6]] (if it is parts) shows that $BC$ is that same fraction of $EF$.

This is the first *alternation* ("alternately") in Book VII: from $a : b = c : d$ to $a : c = b : d$, here in the special case where the ratio is $1 : n$. [[7.10]] handles $m : n$, and [[7.13]] states the general law.
