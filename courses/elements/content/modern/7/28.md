---
title: Coprimality and sums
---
**Statement.** $\gcd(a, b) = 1$ if and only if $\gcd(a + b, a) = 1$ (and then also $\gcd(a + b, b) = 1$).

**Proof.**
- ($\Rightarrow$) A number @D dividing @AC and @AB divides the remainder @BC $= AC - AB$. So it divides both @AB and @BC, which are prime to one another. The same argument works for @AC and @BC.
- ($\Leftarrow$) A number $D$ dividing $AB$ and $BC$ divides the whole $AC$. So it divides $AC$ and $AB$, which are prime to one another.

It is the step of the subtractive algorithm ([[7.1]]) read as a statement: $\gcd(a + b, a) = \gcd(a, b)$. The common divisors do not change when one number is added to or subtracted from the other.
