---
title: A lemma on the unit
---
**Statement.** If the unit measures $b$ as many times as $c$ measures $d$, then the unit measures $c$ as many times as $b$ measures $d$. In modern terms: if $d$ is $b$ copies of $c$, then $d$ is also $c$ copies of $b$.

**Proof.** Split @BC into its units @BG, @GH, @HC, and @EF into copies of @D: @EK, @KL, @LF. There are as many units as copies. Each unit is to its copy of $D$ as the unit @A is to @D. By [[7.12]], the sum of the units is to the sum of the copies in the same ratio: $1 : D = BC : EF$. That says the unit measures @D as many times as $BC$ measures $EF$.

This is alternation ([[7.13]]) with the unit as one of the four terms. It needs its own proposition because the unit is not a number ([[7.def.1]]), so [[7.13]] does not cover it. It is the key step toward [[7.16]].
