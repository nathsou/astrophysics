---
title: Parts (proper fraction)
---
A smaller number $a$ is **parts** of $b$ when it does not divide $b$. For example, $6$ is parts of $9$: it is two thirds of it.

[[7.4]] shows that this is always meaningful. If $d = \gcd(a, b)$, then $a = m d$ and $b = n d$, and $a$ is "$m$ of the $n$-th parts" of $b$, that is, $a = \tfrac{m}{n} b$ with $m \ge 2$. So "part" and "parts" are the two kinds of proper fraction, $\tfrac1n$ and $\tfrac mn$. Euclid has no word for the fraction itself, only for the relation between the two numbers.
