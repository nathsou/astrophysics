---
title: A prime is coprime to every number it does not divide
---
**Statement.** If $p$ is prime and $p \nmid b$, then $\gcd(p, b) = 1$.

**Proof.** Suppose a number @C divides both @A $= p$ and @B. Since $C \mid B$ and $A \nmid B$, $C$ is not $A$. So $C$ is a divisor of the prime $A$ other than $A$ itself, and other than the unit, which is impossible ([[7.def.11]]).

The only divisors of $p$ are $1$ and $p$, so $\gcd(p, b)$ is one of them. It is $p$ exactly when $p \mid b$. This dichotomy is the first step of Euclid's lemma, [[7.30]].
