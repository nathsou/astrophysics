---
title: Adding equal fractions
---
**Statement.** If $a = \tfrac mn b$ and $c = \tfrac mn d$, then $a + c = \tfrac mn (b + d)$.

**Proof.** Split @AB into its $m$ parts of @C (@AG and @GB in the figure, so $m = 2$), and @DE into its $m$ parts of @F (@DH, @HE). Each piece of $AB$ is the same part ($\tfrac1n$) of $C$ that the matching piece of $DE$ is of $F$. So by [[7.5]], each sum $AG + DH$ and $GB + HE$ is $\tfrac1n$ of $C + F$. Adding the $m$ sums gives $a + c = \tfrac mn(b + d)$.

"Parts" reduces to "part" by splitting into pieces, and this is the pattern of [[7.6]]–[[7.10]]: each result about $\tfrac1n$ is followed by the same result about $\tfrac mn$.

**In the figure.** $k$ is odd, so $AB = \tfrac2k\,C$ is genuinely "parts" and not a part.
