---
title: The least pair in a ratio divides every pair
---
**Statement.** Let $(c, e)$ be the least numbers in the ratio $a : b$. Then $c$ divides $a$ and $e$ divides $b$, the same number of times: $a = k c$ and $b = k e$.

In fractions: if $\frac ce$ is in lowest terms, every fraction equal to it is $\frac{kc}{ke}$.

**Proof.** @CD is less than @A, so by [[7.4]] it is either a part or parts of $A$. Suppose it were parts, say $m$ of the $n$-th parts with $m \ge 2$. Then @EF would be the same parts of @B ([[7.13]], [[7.def.20]]). Split $CD$ into its parts of $A$ (@CG, @GD) and $EF$ into its parts of $B$ (@EH, @HF). The pieces are pairwise in the same ratio, so by [[7.12]], $CG : EH = CD : EF$. But $CG, EH$ are smaller than $CD, EF$, and $CD, EF$ were the least in that ratio. Contradiction. So $CD$ is a part of $A$, and $EF$ is the same part of $B$.

The argument is a minimality argument: from any "parts" relation, it cuts out a smaller pair in the same ratio.
