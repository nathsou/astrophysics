---
title: Even-times even number
---
A number $n$ is **even-times even** when $n = a b$ with both $a$ and $b$ even, for example $16 = 4 \cdot 4$ or $24 = 4 \cdot 6$.

Taken literally, this classification overlaps with the next one: $24 = 4 \cdot 6$ is even-times even, and $24 = 8 \cdot 3$ is even-times odd. In Book IX Euclid needs the sharper notion "even-times even *only*", and the numbers of that kind are the powers of two, $2^k$ ([[9.32]]).
