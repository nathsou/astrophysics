---
title: A product of numbers prime to c is prime to c
---
**Statement.** If $\gcd(a, c) = 1$ and $\gcd(b, c) = 1$, then $\gcd(ab, c) = 1$.

**Proof.** Let $D = A \cdot B$, and suppose a number @E divides both @C and @D. By [[7.23]], $E$ is prime to @A (since $E \mid C$). Let @F be the quotient, $D = E \cdot F$. Then $E \cdot F = A \cdot B$, so $E : A = B : F$ ([[7.19]]). Since $E, A$ are coprime, they are least in their ratio ([[7.21]]), so $E$ divides $B$ ([[7.20]]). But then $E$ divides both $B$ and $C$, which are prime to one another.

This is the first time Euclid combines the whole chain [[7.19]] → [[7.21]] → [[7.20]]. The same chain proves Euclid's lemma, [[7.30]]. Stripped to its core, the chain says: *if $e \mid ab$ and $\gcd(e, a) = 1$, then $e \mid b$*. Modern texts usually prove that from Bézout's identity instead.
