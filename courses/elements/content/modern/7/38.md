---
title: A part names a divisor
---
**Statement.** If $a$ has a $c$-th part $b$, then $c \mid a$. For example, if $a$ has a third, then $3 \mid a$.

**Proof.** The unit @D is the $c$-th part of @C, and @B is the $c$-th part of @A. So $D$ measures $C$ as many times as $B$ measures $A$. Alternating ([[7.15]]), $D$ measures $B$ as many times as $C$ measures $A$. So $C$ measures $A$, namely $B$ times.

It is the converse of [[7.37]], and both reduce to $a = b \cdot c = c \cdot b$ ([[7.16]]). In the figure, @A is cut into @C copies of @B, and so @C divides @A, @B times.
