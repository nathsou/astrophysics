---
title: Least number with given parts (common denominator)
---
**Statement.** Find the least number that has given parts, say a $d$-th, an $e$-th and an $f$-th part. The answer is $g = \operatorname{lcm}(d, e, f)$.

For example, the least number that has a half, a third and a quarter is $12$.

**Proof.** Let @D, @E, @F be the numbers the parts @A, @B, @C are named after, and let @G be the least number they all divide ([[7.36]]). Each of them divides $G$, so $G$ has the corresponding parts ([[7.37]]). If a smaller number @H had all three parts, then $D$, $E$ and $F$ would divide it ([[7.38]]), contradicting the minimality of $G$.

This is the least common denominator. To add $\frac12 + \frac13 + \frac14$, take the least number with all three parts, $12$: its half, third and quarter are $6$, $4$ and $3$, so the sum is $\frac{13}{12}$. In the figure, @A, @B and @C are drawn as those parts of @G.

The proposition closes Book VII by returning to its opening vocabulary. Parts, multiples and measuring, defined in [[7.def.3]]–[[7.def.5]], turn out to be the arithmetic of fractions.
