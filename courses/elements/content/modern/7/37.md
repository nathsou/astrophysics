---
title: A divisor names a part
---
**Statement.** If $b \mid a$, then $a$ has a "$b$-th part": a number $c$ with $a = b \cdot c$. For example, if $3 \mid a$, then $a$ has a third.

**Proof.** Let @C be the number of times @B measures @A. The unit @D measures $C$ as many times as $B$ measures $A$. Alternating ([[7.15]]), $D$ measures $B$ as many times as $C$ measures $A$. So $C$ is the same part of $A$ that the unit is of $B$, namely the $b$-th part.

Greek names fractions after numbers: a third after three, a quarter after four. This proposition and [[7.38]] say that "$b$ divides $a$" and "$a$ has a $b$-th part" are the same statement: $b \mid a \iff \frac ab$ is a whole number. In the figure, @A is cut into @B copies of @C.
