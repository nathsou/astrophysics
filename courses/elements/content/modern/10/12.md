---
title: Commensurability is transitive
---
**Statement.** If $a$ and $b$ are each commensurable with $c$, then $a, b$ are commensurable.

**Proof.** Write $A : C = D : E$ and $C : B = F : G$ with numbers ([[10.5]]). To compound the two ratios, Euclid needs numbers in continued proportion ([[8.4]]): $H = DF$, $K = EF$, $L = EG$ give $H : K = D : E$ and $K : L = F : G$. Then ex aequali ([[5.22]]), $A : B = H : L$, a ratio of numbers, so @A and @B are commensurable ([[10.6]]).

In modern terms: $a/b = (a/c)(c/b)$, and a product of rationals is rational. Together with reflexivity and symmetry, X.12 makes commensurability an equivalence relation. The equivalence class of a line $x$ is the set $\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\, x$.
