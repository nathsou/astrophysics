---
title: Constructing a first binomial
---
**Statement.** A first binomial ([[10.def2.1]]) exists, and here is a recipe. Take $\rho = 1$ and integers $m > n$ with $m^2 - n^2$ not a square. Then
$$1 + \sqrt{1 - n^2/m^2}$$
is a first binomial. With $m = 3$, $n = 2$ it is $1 + \tfrac{\sqrt5}{3}$, the figure's default.

**Construction (Euclid's).** Take numbers @AC, @CB with $AB = AC + CB$ in the ratio of two squares to $BC$ but not to $AC$ (so $AB = m^2$, $BC = n^2$; [[10.28]] and its lemmas supply such numbers). Take @EF commensurable with the rational line @D, and make $EF^2 : FG^2 = AB : AC$ ([[10.6]]).

- $FG^2$ is a rational multiple of $EF^2$, so @FG is rational. $AB : AC$ is not a ratio of squares, so $EF$ and $FG$ are incommensurable in length ([[10.9]]). Hence @EG is a binomial ([[10.36]]).
- Let $EF^2 = FG^2 + H^2$. Then $EF^2 : H^2 = AB : BC$ (convertendo, [[5.19]]), a ratio of squares, so @H is commensurable with @EF ([[10.9]]).
- $EF$, the greater term, is commensurable with $D$. So $EG$ is a first binomial.

The tool used throughout [[10.48]]–[[10.53]] is [[10.9]]: two lines are commensurable in length exactly when their squares are in the ratio of a square number to a square number. Every property of the binomial is read off from ratios of whole numbers.
