---
title: The first apotome of a medial
---
**Statement.** Let @AB and @BC be medial lines, commensurable in square only, containing a rational rectangle. The remainder @AC $= AB - BC$ is irrational, a **first apotome of a medial**. It is the first bimedial ([[10.37]]) with a minus sign; for example $\sqrt[4]{27} - \sqrt[4]{3}$, whose parts have squares $3\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt3$ and rectangle $3$.

**Proof.** $AB^2$ and $BC^2$ are medial and commensurable, so their sum is medial ([[10.15]], [[10.23]]). $2\,AB\cdot BC$ is rational, so it is incommensurable with that sum. By [[2.7]], $AB^2 + BC^2 = 2\,AB\cdot BC + AC^2$, and a whole incommensurable with one part is incommensurable with the other ([[10.16]]): $2\,AB\cdot BC$ is incommensurable with $AC^2$. Since $2\,AB\cdot BC$ is rational, $AC^2$ is not, and $AC$ is irrational.

In algebra, $AC^2 = (x^2 + y^2) - 2xy$ is a medial area minus a rational one.
