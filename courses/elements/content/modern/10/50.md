---
title: Constructing a third binomial
---
**Statement.** Take $AB = m^2$, $BC = n^2$ as in [[10.48]], and a number $D$ that is not a square and is in the ratio of two squares neither to $AB$ nor to $AC$. With the rational line $E = 1$,
$$FG + GH = \sqrt{AB/D} + \sqrt{AC/D}$$
is a third binomial ([[10.def2.3]]). For $m = 3$, $n = 2$, $D = 2$: $\tfrac{3}{\sqrt2} + \sqrt{\tfrac52}$, half of $3\sqrt2 + \sqrt{10}$.

**Proof.** Make $E^2 : FG^2 = D : AB$ and $FG^2 : GH^2 = BA : AC$ ([[10.6]]). Both @FG and @GH are rational, and incommensurable in length with each other and with @E, because none of $D : AB$, $AB : AC$ and $D : AC$ (ex aequali, [[5.22]]) is a ratio of squares ([[10.9]]). If $FG^2 = GH^2 + K^2$, then $FG^2 : K^2 = AB : BC$, a ratio of squares, so @K is commensurable with $FG$. So @FH is a third binomial.
