---
title: Medial pairs with a medial rectangle (two kinds)
---
**Statement.** To find medial lines $D, E$, commensurable in square only, containing a medial rectangle, such that $\sqrt{D^2 - E^2}$ is commensurable with $D$ (and, at the end, incommensurable).

With $\rho = 1$: take rationals $A, B, C$, commensurable in square only, with $\sqrt{A^2 - C^2}$ commensurable with $A$ ([[10.29]]), and set $D = \sqrt{AB}$, $E = BC/D$. Then $D : E = A : C$ and $DE = BC$, a medial area.

**Proof.** @D is medial ([[10.21]]). $AB : BC = A : C$, and $AB = D^2$, $BC = DE$, so $A : C = D^2 : DE = D : E$. Hence $D, E$ are commensurable in square only ([[10.11]]), $E$ is medial ([[10.23]]), the excess property passes over by [[10.14]], and $DE = BC$ is medial ([[10.21]]).

**Lemma.** In a right triangle @triangle(ABC) with the right angle at $A$ and the perpendicular @AD:
$$CB \cdot BD = BA^2,\quad BC \cdot CD = CA^2,\quad BD \cdot DC = AD^2,\quad BC \cdot AD = BA \cdot AC.$$
The first three come from the similar triangles of [[6.8]]; the last says that twice the area of the triangle is either leg times leg or hypotenuse times height. The next three propositions use it on a semicircle.
