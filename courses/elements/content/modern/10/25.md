---
title: Medial lines commensurable in square only
---
**Statement.** If $AB, BC$ are medial and commensurable in square only, the rectangle $AB \cdot BC$ is either rational or medial.

With $\rho = 1$: $AB^2 = s\sqrt k$ and $BC^2 = t\sqrt k$ with $s, t$ rational and $s/t$ not a square. Then $AB \cdot BC = \sqrt{st\,k}$: rational if $stk$ is a square, medial otherwise. For example $AB = \sqrt[4]{2}$ and $BC = \sqrt[4]{8}$ give $AB \cdot BC = 2$, a rational area, while $\sqrt[4]3$ and $\sqrt[4]{12}$ give $\sqrt 6$, a medial one. Both cases are needed: they lead to the first and second bimedial lines ([[10.37]], [[10.38]]).

**Proof.** Apply the three areas $AB^2$ (the square @figure(AD)), $AB \cdot BC$ (the rectangle @figure(AC)) and $BC^2$ (the square @figure(BE)) in a row to a rational line @FG, with breadths @FH, @HK, @KL. $FH$ and $KL$ are rational, incommensurable with $FG$ ([[10.22]]), but commensurable with each other, because $AB^2$ and $BC^2$ are. So the rectangle $FH \cdot KL$ is rational ([[10.19]]). The three areas are in continued proportion, $AB^2 : AB\cdot BC = AB \cdot BC : BC^2$, so the breadths are too, and $HK^2 = FH \cdot KL$ is rational ([[6.17]]). So @HK is rational. If it is commensurable in length with $FG$, the area @figure(HN) $= AC$ is rational ([[10.19]]); if in square only, it is medial ([[10.21]]).
