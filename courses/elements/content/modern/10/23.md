---
title: A line commensurable with a medial line is medial
---
**Statement.** If @A is medial and @B is commensurable with $A$, then $B$ is medial. **Porism:** an area commensurable with a medial area is medial.

With $\rho = 1$: if $A = \sqrt[4]{k}$ and $B = rA$ with $r$ rational (or $r = \sqrt s$ with $s$ rational, for "commensurable in square"), then $B^2 = r^2\sqrt k$ is still a rational multiple of a non-rational square root.

**Proof.** Apply $A^2$ and $B^2$ to the rational line @CD, as @figure(CE) and @figure(CF), with breadths @ED and @DF. $ED$ is rational and incommensurable with $CD$ ([[10.22]]). The areas are commensurable, and $CE : CF = ED : DF$ ([[6.1]]), so $ED, DF$ are commensurable ([[10.11]]). Hence $DF$ is also rational and incommensurable with $CD$ ([[10.13]]), and $B^2 = CD \cdot DF$ is a rectangle of rationals commensurable in square only: $B$ is medial by [[10.21]].

Heath's text adds the vocabulary: medial lines can be commensurable "in length" or "in square only", as rational lines can.
