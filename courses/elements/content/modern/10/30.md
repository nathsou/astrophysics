---
title: A rational pair whose excess is incommensurable
---
**Statement.** To find rational lines $AB, AF$, commensurable in square only, such that $\sqrt{AB^2 - AF^2}$ is incommensurable in length with $AB$.

With $\rho = 1$, take squares $CE = p^2$, $ED = q^2$ whose sum $CD = p^2 + q^2$ is not a square ([[10.28]], Lemma 2). Then
$$AB = 1, \quad AF = \frac{p}{\sqrt{p^2 + q^2}}, \quad FB = \frac{q}{\sqrt{p^2 + q^2}}.$$
For $p = 2$, $q = 1$: $AF = 2/\sqrt5$ and $FB = 1/\sqrt5$, incommensurable with $AB = 1$.

**Proof.** As in [[10.29]], with $AB^2 : AF^2 = CD : CE$. Now $AB^2 : FB^2 = CD : DE$ is not a ratio of squares, so @FB is incommensurable with @AB ([[10.9]]). Such pairs make up the binomials of the fourth to sixth orders ([[10.def2.4]]).
