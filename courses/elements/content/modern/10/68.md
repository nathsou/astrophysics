---
title: A line commensurable with a major is a major
---
**Statement.** If @AB is a major and @CD is commensurable with it, then $CD$ is a major.

**Proof.** Cut $CD$ at @F in the ratio in which @E cuts $AB$. The parts keep their ratio, so $CF$, $FD$ are incommensurable in square like $AE$, $EB$. From $AB : BE = CD : DF$ and $AB : AE = CD : CF$ Euclid gets
$$AB^2 : CD^2 = (AE^2 + EB^2) : (CF^2 + FD^2) = AE\cdot EB : CF\cdot FD,$$
so the sum of squares stays rational and the rectangle stays medial ([[10.23]]). In algebra: $\lambda x + \lambda y$ has $\lambda^2(x^2 + y^2)$ and $\lambda^2 xy$, rational multiples of the old areas. So $CD$ is a major ([[10.39]]).
