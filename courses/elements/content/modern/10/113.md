---
title: The inverse of an apotome is a binomial
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$ and a rational line $A$: if $\sqrt X - \sqrt Y$ is an apotome, then
$$\frac{A^2}{\sqrt X - \sqrt Y} = \frac{A^2}{X - Y}\,\big(\sqrt X + \sqrt Y\big),$$
a binomial whose terms are commensurable with $\sqrt X$, $\sqrt Y$, in the same ratio, and of the same order. Example: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2 - 1} = \sqrt2 + 1$.

**Proof.** This is [[10.112]] run backwards. In the figure @BD $= BC - CD$ is the apotome and @KH the breadth of $A^2$ applied to it. Apply $A^2$ also to the whole: $BC \cdot G = A^2$, with @KE $= G$ rational and commensurable with $BC$ ([[10.20]]). Then $CB : BD = HK : KE$, and convertendo $BC : CD = KH : HE$ ([[5.19]]). Choose @F with $KH : HE = HF : FE$; then $KF : FH = BC : CD$, and $KF$, $FH$, $FE$ are in continued proportion, which makes $KF$ commensurable with $FE$ and with $KE$. So $KF$ is rational and commensurable with $BC$, $FH$ is commensurable with $CD$, and $KH = KF + FH$ is a binomial with terms proportional to those of $BD$. [[10.14]], [[10.12]] and [[10.13]] transfer the order.

With [[10.112]]: the reciprocal of a binomial and of an apotome, measured against a rational area, is the conjugate line of the same order. See the code callout there.
