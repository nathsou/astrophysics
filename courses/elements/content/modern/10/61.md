---
title: The square of a first bimedial gives a second binomial
---
**Statement.** If @AB is a first bimedial cut at @C, then $AB^2$ applied to a rational line @DE $= 1$ has as breadth @DG a second binomial. This is the converse of [[10.55]]; with the default sliders, $DG = \tfrac{3}{\sqrt5} + 1$, the binomial of [[10.55]].

**Proof.** Same construction as [[10.60]]. Now $AC^2 + CB^2$ is medial, so @DM is rational but incommensurable with $DE$ ([[10.22]]). And $2\,AC \cdot CB$ is rational, so @MG is commensurable with $DE$ ([[10.20]]). Hence $DM$, $MG$ are rational and commensurable in square only, and $DM > MG$. Since $AC^2$ and $CB^2$ are commensurable, so are @DK and @KM, and [[10.17]] makes $\sqrt{DM^2 - MG^2}$ commensurable with $DM$. The *lesser* term is commensurable with $DE$: a second binomial.
