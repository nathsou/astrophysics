---
title: The square root of a third binomial is a second bimedial
---
**Statement.** If @AB $= 1$ is rational and @AD is a third binomial ([[10.def2.3]]: $\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}$ commensurable with $x$, neither term commensurable with $AB$), the side of the rectangle @AC is a second bimedial ([[10.38]]). Default: $AD = \sqrt2 + \tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{3}$; the parts of the side have squares $\tfrac{5\sqrt2}{6}$ and $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{6}$, and rectangle $\tfrac{\sqrt{10}}{6}$.

**Proof.** With the construction of [[10.54]], @MN and @NO are medial lines commensurable in square only, exactly as in [[10.55]]. The difference is the rectangle $MN \cdot NO = $ @figure(EL). Now $ED$, and so @EF, is incommensurable in length with $AB = EK$. So $EL$ is contained by rational lines commensurable in square only and is medial ([[10.21]]). Hence $MO$ is a second bimedial.
