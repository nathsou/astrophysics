---
title: Lines incommensurable with a given line exist
---
**Statement.** Given a line $a$, there are lines $d$ incommensurable with $a$ in length only, and $e$ incommensurable with $a$ even in square.

With $a = 1$ and whole numbers $B, C$ whose ratio is not a ratio of squares: $d = \sqrt{C/B}$ and $e = \sqrt[4]{C/B}$.

**Proof.** Choose numbers @B, @C not in the ratio of two squares (for example $2, 3$), and construct @D with $A^2 : D^2 = B : C$ ([[10.6]], porism). The squares are commensurable, but by [[10.9]] @A and $D$ are not commensurable in length. Let @E be the mean proportional between $A$ and $D$ ([[6.13]]): then $A^2 : E^2 = A : D$, a ratio of incommensurable lines, so $A^2$ and $E^2$ are incommensurable ([[10.11]]).

This is the existence result promised in [[10.def1.3]]. Note that Euclid cites [[10.11]] here, a later proposition; the order of the text is not the logical order.
