---
title: A major splits in only one way
---
**Statement.** A major ([[10.39]]) is $x + y$ with $x, y$ incommensurable in square, $x^2 + y^2$ rational and $xy$ medial. It can be so cut at one point only.

**Proof.** Suppose @AB is cut so at @C and at @D. By [[2.4]], as in [[10.42]],
$$(AC^2 + CB^2) - (AD^2 + DB^2) = 2\,AD\cdot DB - 2\,AC\cdot CB.$$
The sums of squares are rational, so the left side is rational; the rectangles are medial. A medial area cannot exceed a medial area by a rational one ([[10.26]]).

The argument is word for word that of [[10.42]]: only the sum of squares and the rectangle matter, not whether the parts themselves are rational.
