---
title: Sums of commensurable magnitudes
---
**Statement.** If $a, b$ are commensurable, then $a + b$ is commensurable with each of them. Conversely, if $a + b$ is commensurable with one of them, then $a, b$ are commensurable.

**Proof.** A common measure @D of @AB and @BC measures the whole @AC. Conversely, if $D$ measures $AC$ and $AB$, it measures the remainder $BC$. In modern terms: $(a + b)/a = 1 + b/a$, which is rational exactly when $b/a$ is.
