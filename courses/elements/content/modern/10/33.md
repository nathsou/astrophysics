---
title: The terms of the major line
---
**Statement.** To find lines $x, y$, incommensurable in square, with $x^2 + y^2$ rational and $xy$ medial.

With $\rho = 1$ and $n = p^2 + q^2$ not a square:
$$x^2 = \frac{n + q\sqrt n}{2}, \qquad y^2 = \frac{n - q\sqrt n}{2},$$
so $x^2 + y^2 = n$ and $xy = \tfrac12 p\sqrt n$.

**Construction.** Take rational @AB, @BC as in [[10.30]] ($AB = \sqrt n$, $BC = p$, excess $q$ incommensurable with $AB$). Bisect $BC$ at @point(D) and apply to $AB$ the rectangle $AE \cdot EB = BD^2$ falling short by a square ([[6.28]]). Draw the semicircle on $AB$ and the perpendicular @EF. Then $x = AF$ and $y = FB$.

**Proof.** $AE, EB$ are incommensurable ([[10.18]]), and $AF^2 : FB^2 = AB \cdot AE : AB \cdot EB = AE : EB$ (lemma of [[10.32]]), so $AF, FB$ are incommensurable in square. $AF^2 + FB^2 = AB^2$ is rational ([[1.47]]). $EF^2 = AE \cdot EB = BD^2$, so $EF = \tfrac12 BC$, and $AF \cdot FB = AB \cdot EF = \tfrac12 AB \cdot BC$ is medial ([[10.21]]).

In algebra, $x^2$ and $y^2$ are the roots of $t^2 - n t + \tfrac14 p^2 n = 0$. Their sum is the rational $n$ and their product is $\tfrac14 p^2 n$, whose square root $\tfrac12 p\sqrt n$ is medial. The sum $x + y$ is the *major* line ([[10.39]]).
