---
title: The square on a medial line, applied to a rational line
---
**Statement.** If the square on a medial line is applied to a rational line $BC$, the breadth $CD$ is rational and incommensurable with $BC$ in length.

With $\rho = 1$: a medial line $A$ has $A^2 = \sqrt{pq}$ (with $pq$ not a square). Applied to $BC = \sqrt c$, the breadth is $CD = \sqrt{pq}/\sqrt c = \sqrt{pq/c}$, whose square $pq/c$ is rational, while $CD : BC = \sqrt{pq} : c$ is not a ratio of numbers.

**Proof.** Write $A^2$ as the rectangle @figure(GF) $= EF \cdot EG$ of rationals commensurable in square only ([[10.21]]). It equals the rectangle @figure(BD), so the sides are reciprocally proportional, $BC : EG = EF : CD$ ([[6.14]]). Squaring, $BC^2 : EG^2 = EF^2 : CD^2$; the left ratio is rational, so $CD^2$ is commensurable with the rational $EF^2$ and @CD is rational. For the incommensurability, the lemma of [[10.21]] turns $EF : EG$ (irrational) into $EF^2 : EF \cdot EG$, and then into $CD^2 : CD \cdot CB$, which equals $CD : CB$.

So the characteristic property of a medial area: on a rational line it produces a breadth that is rational in square only. [[10.20]] was the same for rational areas with "in length".
