---
title: A rational multiple of a binomial is a binomial of the same order
---
**Statement.** If @AB is a binomial and @CD is commensurable in length with it, then $CD$ is a binomial of the same order. In algebra: if $x + y$ is a binomial of order $k$ and $\lambda$ is a positive rational, then $\lambda x + \lambda y$ is a binomial of order $k$.

**Proof.** Cut $AB$ into its terms at @E and cut $CD$ in the same ratio at @F ([[6.12]]), so $AE : CF = EB : FD = AB : CD = \lambda$ ([[5.19]]). Then $CF$, $FD$ are commensurable with $AE$, $EB$, hence rational, and alternately $AE : EB = CF : FD$ ([[5.16]]), so they are commensurable in square only: $CD$ is a binomial. Every property that fixes the order is preserved by the common factor: $\sqrt{AE^2 - EB^2}$ commensurable with $AE$ or not ([[10.14]]), and each term commensurable with the rational line or not ([[10.12]], [[10.13]]). Euclid runs through the six cases one by one.
