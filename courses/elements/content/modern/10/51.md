---
title: Constructing a fourth binomial
---
**Statement.** Take numbers @AC, @CB with $AB$ in the ratio of two squares neither to $BC$ nor to $AC$ (for example $3$ and $2$, with $AB = 5$). With $EF = 1$ and $EF^2 : FG^2 = AB : AC$,
$$EF + FG = 1 + \sqrt{AC/AB}$$
is a fourth binomial ([[10.def2.4]]). For $3$ and $2$: $1 + \sqrt{3/5}$.

**Proof.** As in [[10.48]], @EG is a binomial with greater term @EF commensurable with the rational line @D. The difference is in the side @H of $EF^2 - FG^2$: now $EF^2 : H^2 = AB : BC$ is *not* a ratio of squares, so $H$ is incommensurable with $EF$ ([[10.9]]). That makes $EG$ a fourth binomial rather than a first.
