---
title: Commensurable in square
---
**Definition.** Lines $a, b$ are *commensurable in square* when their squares $a^2, b^2$ are commensurable areas, that is, when $a^2 : b^2$ is a ratio of whole numbers. Otherwise they are *incommensurable in square*.

This is a weaker relation than commensurability in length. $\sqrt2$ and $1$ are incommensurable in length but commensurable in square, since $2 : 1$ is a ratio of numbers. $\sqrt[4]{2}$ and $1$ are incommensurable even in square. Lines commensurable in length are always commensurable in square ([[10.9]], porism), and Euclid's phrase "commensurable in square only" means: in square, but not in length.
