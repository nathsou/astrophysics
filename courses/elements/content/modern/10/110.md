---
title: Medial minus medial
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$: if from a medial area a medial area incommensurable with it is taken, the side of the remainder is a second apotome of a medial straight line or "that which produces with a medial area a medial whole".

**Proof.** Apply the areas to the rational line @FG. Both breadths @FH and @FK are rational and incommensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.22]]), and they are incommensurable with each other because the areas are ([[6.1]], [[10.11]]). So @KH is an apotome neither of whose terms is commensurable with $\rho$: third or sixth. By [[10.93]] and [[10.96]], the side of @figure(EC) is a second apotome of a medial or the sixth subtractive line.

The hypothesis "incommensurable with the whole" matters. If the two medial areas were commensurable, the remainder would be a rational multiple of a medial area, hence medial, and its side a medial line. This is the twin of [[10.72]], where adding two incommensurable medial areas gives a second bimedial or the side of the sum of two medial areas.
