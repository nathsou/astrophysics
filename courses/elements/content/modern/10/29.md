---
title: A rational pair whose excess is commensurable
---
**Statement.** To find rational lines $AB, AF$, commensurable in square only, such that $\sqrt{AB^2 - AF^2}$ is commensurable in length with $AB$.

With $\rho = 1$, take square numbers $CD = p^2$, $DE = q^2$ whose difference $CE = p^2 - q^2$ is not a square ([[10.28]], Lemma 1). Then
$$AB = 1, \quad AF = \sqrt{1 - q^2/p^2}, \quad FB = q/p.$$
For $p = 3$, $q = 2$: $AF = \sqrt5/3$ and $FB = 2/3$.

**Construction.** On @AB draw a semicircle and fit @AF in it with $AB^2 : AF^2 = CD : CE$ ([[10.6]], porism). $AB$ and $AF$ are rational and, since $CD : CE$ is not a ratio of squares, commensurable in square only ([[10.9]]). The angle at @point(F) is right ([[3.31]]), so $AB^2 - AF^2 = FB^2$, and $AB^2 : FB^2 = CD : DE = p^2 : q^2$ is a ratio of squares: @FB is commensurable in length with $AB$.

This is the kind of pair that makes up a binomial of the first three orders ([[10.def2.1]]): $1 + \sqrt5/3$ is one.
