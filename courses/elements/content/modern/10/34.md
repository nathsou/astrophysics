---
title: The terms of the side of a rational plus a medial area
---
**Statement.** To find lines $x, y$, incommensurable in square, with $x^2 + y^2$ medial and $xy$ rational.

With $\rho = 1$ and $n = p^2 + q^2$ not a square:
$$x^2 = \frac{p(\sqrt n + q)}{2}, \qquad y^2 = \frac{p(\sqrt n - q)}{2},$$
so $x^2 + y^2 = p\sqrt n$ (medial) and $xy = \tfrac12 p^2$ (rational).

**Proof.** The construction of [[10.33]], started from medial lines @AB, @BC with a rational rectangle ([[10.31]], second kind): here $AB = \sqrt{p\sqrt n}$ and $BC = p^2/AB$. With @point(E) the midpoint of $BC$, $AF \cdot FB = BE^2$ and the perpendicular @FD, the lines are $x = AD$ and $y = DB$. $AF, FB$ are incommensurable ([[10.18]]), hence so are $AD^2, DB^2$. $AD^2 + DB^2 = AB^2$ is medial, and $AD \cdot DB = AB \cdot FD = \tfrac12 AB \cdot BC$ is rational.

The sum $x + y$ is the *side of a rational plus a medial area* ([[10.40]]).
