---
title: The side of a rational plus a medial area
---
**Statement.** If two lines, incommensurable in square, with the sum of their squares medial and their rectangle rational, are added, the whole is irrational. It is called the **side of a rational plus a medial area** (literally, "the line that produces a rational and a medial").

With $\rho = 1$ (the pairs of [[10.34]], $n = p^2 + q^2$ not a square):
$$\sqrt{\frac{p(\sqrt n + q)}{2}} + \sqrt{\frac{p(\sqrt n - q)}{2}},$$
whose square is $p^2 + p\sqrt n$: a rational area plus a medial one, hence the name. For $p = 2$, $q = 1$ this is $\sqrt{\sqrt5 + 1} + \sqrt{\sqrt5 - 1} = \sqrt{4 + 2\sqrt 5}$.

**Proof.** $AB^2 + BC^2$ is medial and $2\,AB \cdot BC$ rational, so they are incommensurable, and $AC^2$ is incommensurable with the rational $2\,AB \cdot BC$ ([[10.16]]). So $AC^2$ is irrational.

The sixth and last additive line, the side of the sum of two medial areas, is [[10.41]].
