---
title: Constructing a sixth binomial
---
**Statement.** Take numbers as in [[10.51]], and a non-square number $D$ in the ratio of two squares neither to $AB$ nor to $AC$. With the rational line $E = 1$,
$$FG + GH = \sqrt{AB/D} + \sqrt{AC/D}$$
is a sixth binomial ([[10.def2.6]]). For $AC = 3$, $CB = 2$, $D = 2$: $\sqrt{5/2} + \sqrt{3/2}$.

**Proof.** This is [[10.50]] with the numbers of [[10.51]]. @FG and @GH are rational, commensurable in square only, and neither is commensurable with @E. The side @K of $FG^2 - GH^2$ satisfies $FG^2 : K^2 = AB : BC$, not a ratio of squares, so $K$ is incommensurable with $FG$ ([[10.9]]). So @FH is a sixth binomial.

**Lemma.** Two squares placed corner to corner, with their sides in line, complete to a larger square, and each of the two rectangles between them is the mean proportional between the squares: $a^2 : ab = ab : b^2$. With $a^2$ and $b^2$ given as areas, this is how [[10.54]] builds a square equal to their sum plus twice the mean.
