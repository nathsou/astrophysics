---
title: The square root of a sixth binomial is the side of the sum of two medial areas
---
**Statement.** If @AB $= 1$ is rational and @AD is a sixth binomial ([[10.def2.6]]), the side of the rectangle @AC is the side of the sum of two medial areas ([[10.41]]). Default: $\sqrt{\sqrt5 + \sqrt2}$, with parts of squares $\tfrac{\sqrt5 \pm \sqrt3}{2}$ and rectangle $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$.

**Proof.** In the construction of [[10.54]], @MN and @NO are incommensurable in square, as in [[10.57]]. The sum of their squares, @figure(AK) $= AB \cdot AE$, is medial because $AE$ is commensurable with $AB$ only in square. Their rectangle, @figure(EL) $= EK \cdot EF$, is medial for the same reason. The two are incommensurable because $AE$ is incommensurable with $EF$ ([[6.1]], [[10.11]]). That completes the six cases announced in [[10.54]].

**Lemma.** The appended lemma, which Heath prints in square brackets, shows that for unequal parts $a^2 + b^2 > 2ab$: in modern form, $(a - b)^2 > 0$. [[10.60]] uses it to tell which breadth is greater. It reuses the letters $A$–$D$ for a new line.
