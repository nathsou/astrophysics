---
title: Medial pairs with a rational rectangle (two kinds)
---
**Statement.** To find medial lines $C, D$, commensurable in square only, containing a rational rectangle, such that $\sqrt{C^2 - D^2}$ is commensurable with $C$. The end of the proposition adds the same with "incommensurable".

With $\rho = 1$: start from the pair $A, B$ of [[10.29]] (or [[10.30]] for the second case) and set
$$C = \sqrt{AB}, \qquad D = B^2/C.$$
Then $C : D = A : B$, so the excess $\sqrt{C^2 - D^2}$ is to $C$ as $\sqrt{A^2 - B^2}$ is to $A$ ([[10.14]]).

**Proof.** @C is medial ([[10.21]]). @D satisfies $CD = B^2$, rational. Since $A : B = AB : B^2 = C^2 : CD = C : D$, the lines $C, D$ are commensurable in square only ([[10.11]]) and $D$ is medial ([[10.23]]). The property of the excess transfers by [[10.14]].

The first kind gives the terms of a line whose square is a second-order binomial (the first bimedial); the second kind is used in [[10.34]].
