---
title: No ratio of numbers means incommensurable
---
**Statement.** If $a : b$ is not a ratio of whole numbers, then $a, b$ are incommensurable.

**Proof.** The contrapositive of [[10.5]]. Together, [[10.5]]–[[10.8]] say that for magnitudes of one kind
$$a, b \text{ commensurable} \iff \frac ab \in \mathbb{Q}.$$

**In the figure,** a would-be common measure is @B cut into $k$ equal parts. It measures $B$ by construction, and it measures @A exactly when $k\sqrt n$ is a whole number, that is, when $k^2 n$ is a perfect square. For non-square $n$ that never happens.
