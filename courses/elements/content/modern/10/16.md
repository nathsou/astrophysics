---
title: Sums of incommensurable magnitudes
---
**Statement.** If $a, b$ are incommensurable, then $a + b$ is incommensurable with each of them; and if $a + b$ is incommensurable with one of them, then $a, b$ are incommensurable.

**Proof.** The contrapositives of [[10.15]]: a measure of @AC and @AB would measure @BC. In modern terms, $1 + b/a$ is irrational exactly when $b/a$ is. In the figure $BC : AB = \sqrt n : 2$, so $AC : AB = 1 + \sqrt n / 2$.

**Lemma.** A rectangle applied to a line $AB$ and falling short of it by a square is the rectangle $AC \cdot CB$, where $C$ is where the square begins: its height is the side $DC$ of the square, which equals $CB$. This is the setting of the next two propositions, and it is drawn in the figure of [[10.17]].
