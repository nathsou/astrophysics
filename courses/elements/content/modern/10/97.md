---
title: The square of an apotome, applied to ρ, is a first apotome
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$: if $x - y$ is an apotome of any order, then $(x - y)^2$ is a first apotome ([[10.def3.1]]). Example: $(\sqrt2 - 1)^2 = 3 - 2\sqrt2$.

This is the converse direction of [[10.91]]–[[10.96]]: there the area was given and its side classified; here the side is given and the breadth of its square applied to $\rho$ is classified.

**Proof.** Let @AB $= AG - GB$ with annex @BG. Apply $AG^2$ and $GB^2$ to the rational line @CD, as @figure(CH) and @figure(KL). Then @CM $= AG^2 + GB^2$, and taking away @figure(CE) $= AB^2$ leaves @figure(FL) $= 2\,AG \cdot GB$ ([[2.7]]):
$$CF = (x^2 + y^2) - 2xy.$$
- $x^2 + y^2$ is rational, so $CM$ is rational and commensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.20]]).
- $2xy$ is medial, so @FM is rational but incommensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.22]]).
- A rational area is incommensurable with a medial one, so $CM$, $MF$ are commensurable in square only: @CF is an apotome.

It is first: $CK \cdot KM = NM^2 = (FM/2)^2$ ([[6.17]]), and $CK : KM = x^2 : y^2$ is rational, so by [[10.17]] $CM^2 - MF^2$ is the square of a line commensurable with $CM$. In numbers: $(x^2 + y^2)^2 - 4x^2y^2 = (x^2 - y^2)^2$, a perfect square. Try the sliders: whatever apotome $\sqrt a - \sqrt b$ you choose, $CF$ is first. It mirrors [[10.60]].
