---
title: The side of ρ times a sixth apotome
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$: if $x - y$ is a sixth apotome ([[10.def3.6]]), then $\sqrt{x - y}$ is "that which produces with a medial area a medial whole" ([[10.78]]). Example: $\sqrt{\sqrt3 - \sqrt2}$.

**Proof.** Same construction as [[10.91]]. The parts $AF$, $FG$ are incommensurable ([[10.18]]), so $LP$, $PN$ are incommensurable in square. Neither $AG$ nor $GD$ is commensurable with $\rho$, so both $LP^2 + PN^2 = $ @figure(AK) and $2\,LP \cdot PN = $ @figure(DK) are medial ([[10.21]]). And they are incommensurable with each other, because $AK : DK = AG : GD$ and $AG$, $GD$ are commensurable in square only ([[6.1]], [[10.11]]).

So @LN is the last of the six. Taken together, [[10.91]]–[[10.96]] give a table: the side of $\rho$ times the $k$-th apotome is the $k$-th subtractive irrational of [[10.73]]–[[10.78]], just as [[10.54]]–[[10.59]] do for binomials. It mirrors [[10.59]].
