---
title: Medial areas never differ by a rational area
---
**Statement.** A medial area does not exceed a medial area by a rational area. With $\rho = 1$: if $\sqrt p$ and $\sqrt q$ are medial areas ($p, q$ rational, not squares), then $\sqrt p - \sqrt q$ is not a nonzero rational number.

The algebra is short: if $\sqrt p - \sqrt q = r \ne 0$, squaring $\sqrt p = r + \sqrt q$ gives $p = r^2 + q + 2r\sqrt q$, so $\sqrt q$ would be rational.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Suppose the medial @figure(AB) exceeds the medial @figure(AC) by the rational @figure(DB). Apply $AB$ and $AC$ to a rational line @EF as @figure(FH) and @figure(FG); the remainder @figure(KH) equals $DB$. The breadths @EH and @EG are rational and incommensurable with $EF$ ([[10.22]]), while @GH is rational and commensurable with $EF$ ([[10.20]]). So $EG$ and $GH$ are rational lines commensurable in square only. Then, as [[10.36]] will show in general, $EH^2 = (EG + GH)^2 = EG^2 + GH^2 + 2\,EG \cdot GH$ is a rational area plus an incommensurable medial one, hence irrational. But $EH$ is rational. Contradiction.

Euclid's proof is the algebraic one in disguise: $\sqrt p = r + \sqrt q$ with $r = GH$ and $\sqrt q = EG$, squared. In the figure, whatever $p$ and $q$, the excess $GH$ comes out irrational.

This is used to show that the sum $x + y$ of an additive irrational splits into its parts in only one way ([[10.42]]–[[10.47]]).
