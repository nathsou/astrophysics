---
title: The square root of a fourth binomial is a major
---
**Statement.** If @AB $= 1$ is rational and @AD $= x + \sqrt Y$ is a fourth binomial ([[10.def2.4]]: $x$ rational, $x^2 - Y$ not a square), the side of the rectangle @AC is a major ([[10.39]]). In algebra, the formula of [[10.54]] still holds,
$$\sqrt{x + \sqrt Y} = \sqrt{\tfrac{x + s}{2}} + \sqrt{\tfrac{x - s}{2}}, \qquad s = \sqrt{x^2 - Y},$$
but now $s$ is irrational, so the two parts are not square roots of rationals. Default: $\sqrt{3 + \sqrt7}$, with parts of squares $\tfrac{3 \pm \sqrt2}{2}$.

**Proof.** In the construction of [[10.54]], $AG$ and $GE$ are now incommensurable ([[10.18]]), so $SN = AH$ and $NQ = GK$ are incommensurable: @MN and @NO are incommensurable in square. Their squares add up to @figure(AK) $= AB \cdot AE$, which is rational since $AE$ is commensurable with $AB$. Their rectangle is @figure(EL), contained by the rational lines @EF and @EK, which are commensurable in square only; so it is medial ([[10.21]]). That is the definition of a major.
