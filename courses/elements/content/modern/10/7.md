---
title: Incommensurable means no ratio of numbers
---
**Statement.** If $a, b$ are incommensurable, $a : b$ is not a ratio of whole numbers.

**Proof.** The contrapositive of [[10.6]]: if $a : b = m : n$, the magnitudes would be commensurable.

**In the figure,** $A : B = \sqrt n$ for a non-square $n$. The best candidates for a ratio of numbers are the convergents $p/q$ of the continued fraction of $\sqrt n$, which approach it very fast ($p/q$ is within $1/q^2$). None is exact: $p^2 - nq^2$ is never 0, and for these fractions it takes only a few small values (for $n = 2$ it alternates $\pm1$, the Pell equation).
