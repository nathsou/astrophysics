---
title: A rational area on a rational line has a rational breadth
---
**Statement.** If a rational area is applied to a rational line $AB$, its breadth $BC$ is rational and commensurable with $AB$ in length. With $\rho = 1$: $AB = \sqrt m$ and area $N \in \mathbb{Q}$ give $BC = N/\sqrt m$, and $BC : AB = N : m$.

**Proof.** The converse of [[10.19]]. The square @figure(AD) and the rectangle @figure(AC) are both rational, hence commensurable, and $AD : AC = BD : BC$ ([[6.1]]), so $BD = AB$ and @BC are commensurable in length ([[10.11]]).

"Applying an area to a rational line" is Euclid's way of *dividing* by a rational. Much of the rest of the book measures an area by the breadth it produces on a fixed rational line, as we would read off $A/\rho$.
