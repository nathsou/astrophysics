---
title: Rational lines commensurable in length give a rational rectangle
---
**Statement.** If $AB$, $BC$ are rational and commensurable in length, then the rectangle $AB \cdot BC$ is rational. With $\rho = 1$: $AB = a\sqrt k$, $BC = b\sqrt k$ ($a, b$ rational), and $AB \cdot BC = abk \in \mathbb{Q}$.

**Proof.** Draw the square @figure(AD) on @AB, a rational area ([[10.def1.4]]). It has the same height as the rectangle @figure(AC), so $AD : AC = BD : BC$ ([[6.1]]). $BD = AB$ is commensurable with $BC$, so the areas are commensurable ([[10.11]]), and an area commensurable with a rational area is rational.

The proof pattern (compare an area with the square on one of its sides, via [[6.1]]) recurs through [[10.26]].
