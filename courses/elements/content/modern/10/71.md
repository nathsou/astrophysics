---
title: The side of a rational area plus a medial area
---
**Statement.** If a rational area @AB and a medial area @CD are added, the side of the whole @AD is a binomial, a first bimedial, a major, or the side of a rational plus a medial area. In algebra, with $\rho = 1$: for a rational $r$ and a non-square rational $s$, $\sqrt{r + \sqrt s}$ is one of those four.

**Proof.** Apply both areas to a rational line @EF: @EG $= AB$ with breadth @EH, and @HI $= CD$ with breadth @HK. $EH$ is rational and commensurable with $EF$ ([[10.20]]); $HK$ is rational and incommensurable with it ([[10.22]]); so @EK is a binomial. Which order depends on two questions:

| | $\sqrt{EH^2 - HK^2}$ or $\sqrt{HK^2 - EH^2}$ commensurable with the greater | incommensurable |
|---|---|---|
| $AB > CD$ (greater term rational) | first: binomial ([[10.54]]) | fourth: major ([[10.57]]) |
| $AB < CD$ (lesser term rational) | second: first bimedial ([[10.55]]) | fifth: rational plus medial ([[10.58]]) |

Examples you can set with the sliders: $\sqrt{3 + 2\sqrt2} = 1 + \sqrt2$ is a binomial; $\sqrt{3 + \sqrt2}$ is a major; $\sqrt{4 + 3\sqrt2}$ is a first bimedial; $\sqrt{1 + \sqrt2}$ is the side of a rational plus a medial area. The third and sixth binomials cannot occur, because one term is always commensurable with $EF$.
