---
title: Proportion preserves commensurability
---
**Statement.** If $a : b = c : d$, then $c, d$ are commensurable exactly when $a, b$ are.

**Proof.** Commensurable means that the ratio is a ratio of numbers ([[10.5]], [[10.6]]), and incommensurable means that it is not ([[10.7]], [[10.8]]). $a : b$ and $c : d$ are the same ratio, so they are both ratios of numbers or both not. In modern terms: $a/b = c/d$, and one side is in $\mathbb{Q}$ if and only if the other is.

This is the workhorse of the book. Most later proofs set up a proportion between a pair of lines and a pair of areas (usually by [[6.1]]: rectangles of equal height are as their bases) and use X.11 to move commensurability across it.
