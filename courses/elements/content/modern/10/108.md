---
title: Rational minus medial
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$: if a medial area is taken from a rational area, the side of the remainder is an apotome or a minor line. In numbers: for rational $a$ and a medial area $b$ (so $b^2$ is rational, $b$ is not) with $b < a$, $\sqrt{a - b}$ is an apotome or minor.

**Proof.** Apply both areas to the rational line @FG: @figure(GH) $=$ @figure(BC) and @figure(GK) $=$ @figure(BD), so the remainder @figure(LH) equals @figure(EC). The breadth @FH is commensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.20]]), while @FK is rational but incommensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.22]]), so @KH $= FH - FK$ is an apotome ([[10.73]]) whose whole is commensurable with $\rho$: it is first or fourth, according as $\sqrt{FH^2 - FK^2}$ is commensurable with $FH$ or not. The side of $\rho \cdot KH$ is then an apotome ([[10.91]]) or minor ([[10.94]]).

Example: $3 - \sqrt5$ gives an apotome, since $9 - 5 = 4$ is a square; $3 - \sqrt7$ gives a minor line. [[10.108]] and [[10.109]] together are the subtractive twin of [[10.71]]. There, adding a rational and a medial area gives one of four lines, according to which area is the greater; here the same four cases split according to which area is taken from which.
