---
title: That which produces with a rational area a medial whole
---
**Statement.** Let @AB and @BC be incommensurable in square, with $AB^2 + BC^2$ medial and $2\,AB\cdot BC$ rational ([[10.34]]). The remainder @AC $= AB - BC$ is irrational. Euclid names it by what it does: its square plus the rational area $2\,AB\cdot BC$ makes the medial whole $AB^2 + BC^2$ ([[2.7]]). It is the side of a rational plus a medial area ([[10.40]]) with a minus sign.

**Proof.** $AB^2 + BC^2$ is medial and $2\,AB\cdot BC$ rational, so they are incommensurable, and by [[10.16]] the remainder $AC^2$ is incommensurable with $2\,AB\cdot BC$. Since that is rational, $AC^2$ is not, and $AC$ is irrational.

Default: $AB^2, BC^2 = \tfrac{\sqrt3 \pm \sqrt2}{2}$, so $AC = \sqrt{\sqrt3 - 1}$.
