---
title: The minor
---
**Statement.** Let @AB and @BC be incommensurable in square, with $AB^2 + BC^2$ rational and $AB\cdot BC$ medial (such lines exist by [[10.33]]). The remainder @AC $= AB - BC$ is irrational, and is called **minor**. It is the major ([[10.39]]) with a minus sign. With the default sliders, $AB^2, BC^2 = \tfrac{3 \pm \sqrt2}{2}$, so $AC = \sqrt{3 - \sqrt7}$.

**Proof.** $AB^2 + BC^2$ is rational and $2\,AB\cdot BC$ medial, so they are incommensurable. By [[2.7]] and [[10.16]], $AB^2 + BC^2$ is then incommensurable with the remainder $AC^2$. So $AC^2$ is irrational, and so is $AC$.

The names major and minor come in a pair: the major is $\sqrt{r + \sqrt s}$ and the minor $\sqrt{r - \sqrt s}$ for the same rational $r$ and non-square $s$, with $r^2 - s$ not a square. The side of the regular pentagon in a circle of rational diameter is a minor ([[13.11]]).
