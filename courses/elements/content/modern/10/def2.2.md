---
title: Second binomial
---
A binomial $x + y$ (with $\rho = 1$, $X = x^2$, $Y = y^2$ rational) is **second** when $\sqrt{X - Y}$ is commensurable with the greater term $x$, and the *lesser* term $y$ is commensurable with $\rho$, that is, $y$ is a rational number.

Example: $3\sqrt2 + 4$. Here $X = 18$, $Y = 16$, $\sqrt{18 - 16} = \sqrt2$, and $\sqrt2 / 3\sqrt2 = 1/3$ is rational.

This one is the first binomial $3 + 2\sqrt2$ multiplied by $\sqrt2$: $3\sqrt2 + 4 = \sqrt2\,(3 + 2\sqrt2)$. The square root of $\rho$ times a second binomial is a first bimedial ([[10.55]]). See the table under [[10.def2.1]].
