---
title: The major line
---
**Statement.** If two lines, incommensurable in square, with the sum of their squares rational and their rectangle medial, are added, the whole is irrational. It is called the **major** (*meizōn*).

With $\rho = 1$ (the pairs of [[10.33]], $n = p^2 + q^2$ not a square):
$$\text{major} = \sqrt{\frac{n + q\sqrt n}{2}} + \sqrt{\frac{n - q\sqrt n}{2}},$$
for example $\sqrt{(5 + \sqrt5)/2} + \sqrt{(5 - \sqrt5)/2} = \sqrt{5 + 2\sqrt 5}$. Its square $n + p\sqrt n$ is a fourth-order binomial.

**Proof.** $2\,AB \cdot BC$ is medial and $AB^2 + BC^2$ is rational, so they are incommensurable, and their sum $AC^2$ is incommensurable with the rational $AB^2 + BC^2$ ([[10.16]]). So $AC^2$ is irrational.

The name contrasts with the *minor* $\sqrt{(n + q\sqrt n)/2} - \sqrt{(n - q\sqrt n)/2}$ of [[10.76]]. The side of a regular pentagon inscribed in a circle of rational diameter is a minor line ([[13.11]]).
