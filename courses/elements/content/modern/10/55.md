---
title: The square root of a second binomial is a first bimedial
---
**Statement.** If @AB $= 1$ is rational and @AD $= x + y$ is a second binomial ([[10.def2.2]]: $y$ rational, $\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}$ commensurable with $x$), the side of the rectangle @AC is a first bimedial ([[10.37]]). Default: $AD = \tfrac{3}{\sqrt5} + 1$, whose side has parts with squares $\tfrac{\sqrt5}{2}$ and $\tfrac{\sqrt5}{10}$ and rectangle $\tfrac12$.

**Proof.** Make the construction of [[10.54]]: $AG \cdot GE = EF^2$ and the squares $SN = AH$, $NQ = GK$, so that @MO is the side of $AC$. Only the classification changes.

- $AG$ and $GE$ are commensurable with $AE$ ([[10.17]]), and $AE$ is incommensurable with $AB$. So $AH$, $GK$ are rectangles on rational lines commensurable in square only: medial ([[10.21]]). Hence @MN and @NO are medial, and commensurable in square.
- As in [[10.54]], $MN$ is incommensurable in length with $NO$.
- $ED$ is commensurable with $AB$, so @figure(EL) $= MN \cdot NO$ is rational ([[10.19]]).

Two medials commensurable in square only containing a rational rectangle: $MO$ is a first bimedial.
