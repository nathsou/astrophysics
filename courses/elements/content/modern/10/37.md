---
title: The first bimedial line
---
**Statement.** If two medial lines, commensurable in square only and containing a rational rectangle, are added, the whole is irrational. It is called the **first bimedial**.

With $\rho = 1$ (the pairs of [[10.27]]):
$$\text{first bimedial} = x\sqrt[4]{k} + y\sqrt[4]{k^3} \qquad (k \text{ not a square}),$$
for example $\sqrt[4]3 + \sqrt[4]{27}$. The terms are medial, their squares $x^2\sqrt k$ and $y^2 k\sqrt k$ are in a rational but non-square ratio, and their product $xyk$ is rational.

**Proof.** As in [[10.36]], $AC^2 = (AB^2 + BC^2) + 2\,AB \cdot BC$, and the two parts are incommensurable because @AB, @BC are incommensurable in length. Here $2\,AB \cdot BC$ is rational, so $AC^2$ is irrational. In the figure, $AC^2 = 2xyk + (x^2 + y^2 k)\sqrt k$: a binomial of the second order, which is why [[10.55]] matches the first bimedial with the second binomial.
