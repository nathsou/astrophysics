---
title: The side of the sum of two incommensurable medial areas
---
**Statement.** If two medial areas @AB and @CD, incommensurable with each other, are added, the side of the whole @AD is either a second bimedial or the side of the sum of two medial areas. In algebra: $\sqrt{\sqrt a + \sqrt b}$, with $a$, $b$, $ab$ non-square rationals, is one of these two.

**Proof.** Apply the areas to a rational line @EF as @EG and @HI, with breadths @EH and @HK. Both breadths are rational and incommensurable in length with $EF$ ([[10.22]]), and incommensurable with each other because the areas are ([[6.1]], [[10.11]]). So @EK is a binomial with neither term commensurable with $EF$: a third or a sixth binomial, depending on whether $\sqrt{EH^2 - HK^2}$ is commensurable with $EH$. Then [[10.56]] or [[10.59]] gives the side.

The default, $\sqrt{3\sqrt2 + \sqrt{10}}$, is a second bimedial: $18 - 10 = 8$ and $\sqrt8 / \sqrt{18} = 2/3$. Most other choices, such as $\sqrt{\sqrt3 + \sqrt2}$, give the side of the sum of two medial areas.

With [[10.71]], this accounts for all six additive irrationals as sides of sums of two areas, each rational or medial.
