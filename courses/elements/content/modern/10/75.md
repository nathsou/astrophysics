---
title: The second apotome of a medial
---
**Statement.** Let @AB and @BC be medial lines, commensurable in square only, containing a medial rectangle. The remainder @AC $= AB - BC$ is irrational, a **second apotome of a medial**: the second bimedial ([[10.38]]) with a minus sign.

**Proof.** Both areas are medial now, so Euclid applies them to a rational line @DI. Let @figure(DE) $= AB^2 + BC^2$ have breadth @DG, and @figure(DH) $= 2\,AB\cdot BC$ breadth @DF. The rest, @figure(FE), equals $AC^2$ ([[2.7]]). Both breadths are rational and incommensurable in length with $DI$ ([[10.22]]), and incommensurable with each other, because $AB^2 + BC^2$ is incommensurable with $2\,AB\cdot BC$ (as in [[10.44]]). So @FG $= DG - DF$ is an apotome ([[10.73]]). A rectangle contained by a rational line and an apotome is irrational, so $FE = AC^2$ is irrational, and so is $AC$.
