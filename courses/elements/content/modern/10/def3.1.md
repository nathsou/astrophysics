---
title: First apotome
---
Take the rational line set out as the unit, $\rho = 1$. An apotome ([[10.73]]) is $x - y$ where $x > y$ are rational lines commensurable in square only: $X = x^2$ and $Y = y^2$ are rational numbers and $X/Y$ is not the square of a rational. Euclid calls $x$ the **whole** and $y$ the **annex**. The apotome is **first** when

- $\sqrt{X - Y}$ is commensurable with the whole $x$, that is, $1 - Y/X$ is the square of a rational, and
- the whole $x$ is commensurable with $\rho$, that is, $x$ is a rational number.

Example: $3 - \sqrt5$, since $\sqrt{9 - 5} = 2$ and $2/3$ is rational.

**Comment.** These six definitions copy the six of the binomials ([[10.def2.1]]) word for word, with "whole" and "annex" in place of "greater" and "lesser term". The same two questions sort the apotomes $x - y$:

| | $\sqrt{X-Y}$ commensurable with $x$ | $\sqrt{X-Y}$ incommensurable with $x$ |
|---|---|---|
| $x$ commensurable with $\rho$ | first: $3 - \sqrt5$ | fourth: $2 - \sqrt2$ |
| $y$ commensurable with $\rho$ | second: $3\sqrt2 - 4$ | fifth: $\sqrt2 - 1$ |
| neither | third: $3\sqrt2 - \sqrt{10}$ | sixth: $\sqrt3 - \sqrt2$ |

In each cell the apotome and the binomial with the same terms have the same order. That is no accident: the order depends only on the pair of terms, and [[10.112]]–[[10.113]] show that $x + y$ and $x - y$ are, up to a rational factor, each other's reciprocals. [[10.85]]–[[10.90]] construct one apotome of each order, and [[10.91]]–[[10.96]] show that the six orders give the six subtractive irrationals of [[10.73]]–[[10.78]].
