---
title: The side of the sum of two medial areas splits in only one way
---
**Statement.** The side of the sum of two medial areas ([[10.41]]) is $x + y$ with $x, y$ incommensurable in square, $x^2 + y^2$ and $xy$ both medial and incommensurable with each other. It can be so cut at one point only.

**Proof.** As for the second bimedial ([[10.44]]), both areas are medial, so Euclid applies them to a rational line @EF. Suppose @AB is cut at @C and at @D, with $AC > DB$. Apply $AB^2$ as @EK, with @EG $= AC^2 + CB^2$ and @EL $= AD^2 + DB^2$. The breadths @EH and @HN are rational ([[10.22]]) and incommensurable, because the two areas are incommensurable by hypothesis ([[6.1]], [[10.11]]). So @EN is a binomial cut at @H, and likewise at @M, with $EH \ne MN$. That contradicts [[10.42]].

Here the incommensurability of $EG$ and $HK$ is given directly by the hypothesis; in [[10.44]] it had to be derived.
