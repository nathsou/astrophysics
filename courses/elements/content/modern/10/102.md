---
title: The square of the sixth subtractive line, applied to ρ
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$: if $x - y$ "produces with a medial area a medial whole" ([[10.78]]), then $(x - y)^2$ is a sixth apotome ([[10.def3.6]]).

**Proof.** $CF = CM - MF$ with @CM $= x^2 + y^2$ and @MF $= 2xy$ both medial, so both rational and incommensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.22]]). They are incommensurable with each other by hypothesis, so @CF is an apotome ([[10.73]]). $CK$ and $KM$ are incommensurable, so $\sqrt{CM^2 - MF^2}$ is incommensurable with $CM$ ([[10.18]]): $CF$ is sixth. It mirrors [[10.65]].

Together, [[10.97]]–[[10.102]] give the other half of the correspondence: squaring the $k$-th subtractive line and dividing by $\rho$ gives the $k$-th apotome, and [[10.91]]–[[10.96]] go back. Since the six orders of apotome are mutually exclusive, so are the six lines. [[10.111]] uses this to show that the thirteen irrational lines of the book are all different.
