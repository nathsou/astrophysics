---
title: A line commensurable with an apotome is an apotome of the same order
---
**Statement.** If $x - y$ is an apotome of order $k$ and $\lambda$ is a rational number, then $\lambda x - \lambda y$ is an apotome of order $k$ too. In the figure, @CD $= \lambda \cdot AB$.

**Proof.** Let @BE be the annex of @AB and cut off @DF with $BE : DF = AB : CD$ ([[6.12]]). Then the wholes are in the same ratio too, $AE : CF = AB : CD$ ([[5.12]]), so $CF = \lambda x$ and $FD = \lambda y$.

Multiplying by a rational number changes nothing that the definitions look at:

- $CF$, $FD$ are commensurable with $AE$, $EB$, so they are rational and commensurable in square only ([[10.13]]): $CD$ is an apotome;
- $CF : FD = AE : EB$ ([[5.16]]), so $\sqrt{CF^2 - FD^2}$ is commensurable with $CF$ exactly when $\sqrt{AE^2 - EB^2}$ is with $AE$ ([[10.14]]);
- $CF$ is commensurable with $\rho$ exactly when $AE$ is, and likewise for the annexes ([[10.12]]).

Every condition of [[10.def3.1]]–[[10.def3.6]] is invariant under rational scaling, so the order is too. [[10.104]]–[[10.107]] say the same for the other five subtractive lines, mirroring [[10.66]]–[[10.70]] for the additive ones. In short: every kind of line in the book is closed under multiplication by positive rational numbers.
