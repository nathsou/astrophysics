---
title: Commensurable, incommensurable
---
**Definition.** Magnitudes $a, b$ of one kind are *commensurable* when some magnitude $u$ measures both: $a = mu$ and $b = nu$ for whole numbers $m, n$. Otherwise they are *incommensurable*.

Equivalently ([[10.5]], [[10.6]]), $a, b$ are commensurable exactly when $a : b$ is a ratio of whole numbers. For lines, with $b$ taken as the unit, "commensurable" means "rational" in the modern sense and "incommensurable" means "irrational". The side and diagonal of a square are the classic incommensurable pair.

Commensurability is an equivalence relation ([[10.12]]). A test for it is Euclid's algorithm on magnitudes, which stops for commensurable magnitudes and runs for ever otherwise ([[10.2]], [[10.3]]).
