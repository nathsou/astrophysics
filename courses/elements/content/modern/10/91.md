---
title: The side of ρ times a first apotome is an apotome
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$: if $x - y$ is a first apotome ([[10.def3.1]]), then $\sqrt{x - y}$ is an apotome ([[10.73]]). In the figure @AC $= \rho$, @AD $= AG - GD$ is the first apotome, and the side of the rectangle @figure(AB) is @LN.

**Proof.** Euclid splits the square root by the method of [[10.54]], with a minus sign.

1. Bisect the annex @GD at @E and apply to $AG$ a rectangle $AF \cdot FG = EG^2$ falling short by a square ([[10.17]]). So $AF + FG = x$ and $AF \cdot FG = y^2/4$.
2. Make squares $LP^2 = AF$ and $PN^2 = FG$ about the same angle @angle(LPM) ([[6.26]]). Then $LP \cdot PN = EG = y/2$, because $EG$ is the mean proportional between $AF$ and $FG$.
3. Hence
$$LN^2 = (LP - PN)^2 = LP^2 + PN^2 - 2\,LP \cdot PN = x - y = AD.$$
In the figure: the whole $AK$ equals the two squares, $DK$ equals the gnomon @figure(UVW) together with $NO$, and the remainder $AB$ equals the square @figure(ST) on $LN$.

Why $LN$ is an apotome: the apotome is first, so $AF$ and $FG$ are commensurable with $AG$ ([[10.17]]), hence with $\rho$, so $LP^2$ and $PN^2$ are rational. And $LP \cdot PN = y/2$ is medial, so $LP^2$ is incommensurable with $LP \cdot PN$, and $LP$, $PN$ are commensurable in square only.

:::code Denesting a square root
Steps 1–3 are the denesting formula. With $s = \sqrt{x^2 - y^2}$,
$$\sqrt{x - y} = \sqrt{\frac{x + s}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{x - s}{2}},$$
since the two squares add up to $x$ and their product is $y^2/4$. For $3 - \sqrt5$, $s = 2$ and $\sqrt{3 - \sqrt5} = \sqrt{5/2} - \sqrt{1/2}$. The formula always holds; the apotome being *first* is what makes $s$ commensurable with $x$ and the result simple. [[10.92]]–[[10.96]] run the same computation for the other five orders.
:::
