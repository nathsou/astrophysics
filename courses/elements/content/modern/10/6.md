---
title: A ratio of numbers means commensurable
---
**Statement.** If $a : b = m : n$ with whole numbers $m, n$, then $a, b$ are commensurable. **Porism:** given a line $a$ and numbers $m, n$, one can construct $x$ with $a : x = m : n$, and also $y$ with $a^2 : y^2 = m : n$.

**Proof.** Divide @A into $m$ equal parts ([[6.9]]) and let @C be one of them. Lay off $n$ copies of $C$ to make @F. Then $A : F = m : n = A : B$, so $F = B$ ([[5.9]]). $C$ measures both.

**The porism** is the tool Book X uses to manufacture lines. To get $y$ with $a^2 : y^2 = m : n$, first make $x = \tfrac nm a$, then take the mean proportional $y = \sqrt{a x}$ ([[6.13]]): $a^2 : y^2 = a : x = m : n$ ([[6.20]], porism). With $a = 1$ and $m = 1$ this constructs $\sqrt n$.
