---
title: The square root of a fifth binomial is the side of a rational plus a medial area
---
**Statement.** If @AB $= 1$ is rational and @AD $= x + y$ is a fifth binomial ([[10.def2.5]]: $y$ rational, $\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}$ incommensurable with $x$), the side of the rectangle @AC is the side of a rational plus a medial area ([[10.40]]). Default: $AD = \sqrt3 + 1$; the parts of the side have squares $\tfrac{\sqrt3 \pm \sqrt2}{2}$ and rectangle $\tfrac12$.

**Proof.** In the construction of [[10.54]], $AG$ and $GE$ are incommensurable ([[10.18]]), so @MN and @NO are incommensurable in square. (Heath's text reads "commensurable" at this step, a slip: the argument needs "incommensurable".) The sum of their squares is @figure(AK) $= AB \cdot AE$, and $AE$ is commensurable with $AB$ only in square, so it is medial ([[10.21]]). Their rectangle is @figure(EL) $= EK \cdot EF$, and $EF$ is commensurable with $EK = AB$, so it is rational ([[10.19]]). This is the definition in [[10.40]].
