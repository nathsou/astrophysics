---
title: A second bimedial splits in only one way
---
**Statement.** A second bimedial ([[10.38]]) is $x + y$ with $x, y$ medial, commensurable in square only, and $xy$ medial. It can be so cut at one point only.

**Proof.** Now both the sums of squares and the rectangles are medial, so [[10.26]] cannot be applied directly. Euclid moves the problem to a rational line @EF and reduces it to [[10.42]].

Suppose @AB is cut at @C and at @D, with $AC > DB$, so $D$ is nearer the middle and $AD^2 + DB^2 < AC^2 + CB^2$ (the lemma after [[10.41]]). Apply $AB^2$ to $EF$ as @EK, with @EG $= AC^2 + CB^2$ and @EL $= AD^2 + DB^2$. The breadths @EH and @HN (of $EG$ and of $HK = 2\,AC\cdot CB$) are rational, since the areas are medial ([[10.22]]), and they are incommensurable because $AC^2 + CB^2$ is incommensurable with $2\,AC\cdot CB$ (this is where $AC$, $CB$ being incommensurable in length is used). So @EN is a binomial cut at @H. The same argument makes it a binomial cut at @M. And $EH \ne MN$, because $EG = AC^2 + CB^2$ exceeds $MK = 2\,AD\cdot DB$. Two different cuts contradict [[10.42]].
