---
title: The terms of the side of two medial areas
---
**Statement.** To find lines $x, y$, incommensurable in square, with $x^2 + y^2$ medial, $xy$ medial, and $x^2 + y^2$ incommensurable with $xy$.

With $\rho = 1$, $s = \sqrt{p^2 + 1}$ irrational and $b$ a suitable non-square: start from $AB = \sqrt{s\sqrt b}$ and $BC = p\sqrt b / AB$, medial lines with a medial rectangle ([[10.32]], second kind), and repeat the construction of [[10.34]].

**Proof.** As before, $x = AD$ and $y = DB$ are incommensurable in square, $AD^2 + DB^2 = AB^2$ is medial, and $AD \cdot DB = AB \cdot FD = AB \cdot BE$ is medial ([[10.32]], porism). The new condition: $AB$ is incommensurable in length with $BC$, hence with @BE, so $AB^2 : AB \cdot BE = AB : BE$ is not a ratio of numbers ([[10.11]]). In the figure $AB^2 : AD \cdot DB = 2s : p$.

The sum $x + y$ is the *side of the sum of two medial areas* ([[10.41]]).
