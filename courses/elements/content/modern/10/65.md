---
title: The square of the side of the sum of two medial areas gives a sixth binomial
---
**Statement.** If @AB is the side of the sum of two medial areas, cut at @C, then $AB^2$ applied to a rational line @DE $= 1$ has as breadth @DG a sixth binomial. Converse of [[10.59]].

**Proof.** Same construction as [[10.60]]. Both $AC^2 + CB^2$ and $2\,AC\cdot CB$ are medial, so @DM and @MG are rational and incommensurable with $DE$ ([[10.22]]); they are incommensurable with each other because the two areas are, by hypothesis. @DK and @KM are incommensurable, so $\sqrt{DM^2 - MG^2}$ is incommensurable with $DM$ ([[10.18]]). Neither term is commensurable with $DE$: a sixth binomial. With [[10.54]]–[[10.59]], this makes the correspondence between the six binomials and the six additive irrationals complete in both directions.
