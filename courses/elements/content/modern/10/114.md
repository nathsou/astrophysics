---
title: Apotome times conjugate binomial is rational
---
**Statement.** If an apotome and a binomial have terms commensurable and in the same ratio, the side of their rectangle is rational. With $\rho = 1$ and rationals $\lambda, \mu$:
$$\lambda(\sqrt X - \sqrt Y) \cdot \mu(\sqrt X + \sqrt Y) = \lambda\mu\,(X - Y),$$
a rational area, so its side $\sqrt{\lambda\mu(X - Y)}$ is a rational line.

**Proof (Euclid's).** He avoids multiplying the lines out. Set out a rational line @H and apply $H^2$ to the binomial @CD, with breadth @KL. By [[10.112]], $KL = KM - ML$ is an apotome with terms proportional to $CE$, $ED$, hence to the terms $AF$, $FB$ of @AB. So $AF : FB = KM : ML$, and alternately and by subtraction ([[5.19]]) $AB : KL = AF : KM$, a rational ratio. So $AB \cdot CD$ is commensurable with $KL \cdot CD = H^2$ ([[6.1]], [[10.11]]), which is rational. Its side @G is rational.

**Porism.** A rational area can be contained by two irrational lines. Example: $(\sqrt2 - 1)(\sqrt2 + 1) = 1$. In the language of [[10.112]], this is the norm of a quadratic irrational: $z \bar z$ is rational although $z$ and $\bar z$ are not.
