---
title: The quadratic with an incommensurable root difference
---
**Statement.** With the same set-up as [[10.17]] ($x + y = a$, $xy = c^2/4$, so $x - y = \sqrt{a^2 - c^2}$): $x, y$ are incommensurable exactly when $\sqrt{a^2 - c^2}$ is incommensurable with $a$.

**Proof.** The contrapositive of [[10.17]], proved by the same steps with [[10.13]] and [[10.16]] in place of [[10.12]] and [[10.15]]. In the figure $BD : DC = \sqrt n$, and $DF : BC = (\sqrt n - 1) : (\sqrt n + 1)$, which is irrational.

**The lemma after it** fixes the vocabulary. A line commensurable in length with the rational line $\rho$ is "rational and commensurable in length and in square"; a line commensurable with $\rho$ in square only is still called rational, "commensurable in square only". With $\rho = 1$: $\tfrac32$ is of the first sort, $\sqrt 3$ of the second.
