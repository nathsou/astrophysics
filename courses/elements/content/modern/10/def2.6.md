---
title: Sixth binomial
---
A binomial $x + y$ is **sixth** when $\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}$ is incommensurable with $x$, and neither term is commensurable with the rational line $\rho$.

Example: $\sqrt3 + \sqrt2$. Here $\sqrt{3 - 2} = 1$, and $1/\sqrt3$ is irrational; neither $\sqrt3$ nor $\sqrt2$ is rational.

This is the generic case: pick two non-square rationals $X > Y$ at random and $\sqrt X + \sqrt Y$ is almost always sixth. The square root of $\rho$ times a sixth binomial is the side of the sum of two medial areas ([[10.59]]). See the table under [[10.def2.1]].
