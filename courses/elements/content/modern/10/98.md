---
title: The square of a first apotome of a medial, applied to ρ
---
**Statement.** With $\rho = 1$: if $x - y$ is a first apotome of a medial straight line ([[10.74]]), then $(x - y)^2$ is a second apotome ([[10.def3.2]]). Example: $(\sqrt[4]8 - \sqrt[4]2)^2 = 3\sqrt2 - 4$.

**Proof.** As in [[10.97]], $CF = CM - MF$ with @CM $= x^2 + y^2$ and @MF $= 2xy$. Now the roles swap: $x^2$, $y^2$ are commensurable medial areas, so their sum is medial and $CM$ is rational but incommensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.22]]); while $2xy$ is rational, so $MF$ is commensurable with $\rho$ ([[10.20]]). They are incommensurable in length, so @CF is an apotome, and as before $CK \cdot KM = NM^2$ with $CK$, $KM$ commensurable, which makes $CM^2 - MF^2$ the square of a line commensurable with $CM$ ([[10.17]]). The annex $MF$ is the rational one: $CF$ is second. It mirrors [[10.61]].
