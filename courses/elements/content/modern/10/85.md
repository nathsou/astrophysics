---
title: Constructing a first apotome
---
**Statement.** A first apotome ([[10.def3.1]]) exists. Take $\rho = 1$, a rational number $r$, and integers $m > n$ with $m^2 - n^2$ not a square. Then
$$r\Big(1 - \sqrt{1 - n^2/m^2}\,\Big)$$
is a first apotome. The figure's default, $r = \tfrac32$, $m = 3$, $n = 2$, gives $\tfrac32 - \tfrac{\sqrt5}{2}$.

**Construction (Euclid's).** This is [[10.48]] with the sign changed: the same two lines are found, and this time the smaller is taken away from the greater. Take @BG commensurable with the rational line @A, square numbers @DE $= m^2$ and @EF $= n^2$ whose difference @DF is not a square, and make $ED : DF = BG^2 : GC^2$ ([[10.6]]).

- $GC^2$ is a rational multiple of $BG^2$, so @GC is rational. $ED : DF$ is not a ratio of squares, so $BG$ and $GC$ are incommensurable in length ([[10.9]]). So @BC $= BG - GC$ is an apotome ([[10.73]]).
- Let $BG^2 = GC^2 + H^2$. Convertendo ([[5.19]]), $DE : EF = BG^2 : H^2$, a ratio of squares, so @H is commensurable with the whole $BG$.
- The whole $BG$ is commensurable with $A$. So $BC$ is a first apotome.

All six constructions, [[10.85]]–[[10.90]], work like this. Every commensurability question is turned into a question about whole numbers by [[10.9]]: two lines are commensurable in length exactly when their squares have the ratio of a square number to a square number.
