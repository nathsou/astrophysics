---
title: A first bimedial splits in only one way
---
**Statement.** A first bimedial ([[10.37]]) is $x + y$ with $x, y$ medial, commensurable in square only, and $xy$ rational; for example $\sqrt[4]{3} + \sqrt[4]{27}$, where $xy = \sqrt[4]{81} = 3$. Such a line can be cut into two parts with these properties at one point only.

**Proof.** Suppose @AB is cut so at @C and also at @D. As in [[10.42]], squaring gives
$$2\,AD\cdot DB - 2\,AC\cdot CB = (AC^2 + CB^2) - (AD^2 + DB^2).$$
This time the roles are reversed. Both rectangles are rational, so the left side is rational. Each sum of squares is medial: $AC^2$ is medial and $CB^2$ is commensurable with it, so their sum is too ([[10.15]], [[10.23]]). A medial area does not exceed a medial area by a rational one ([[10.26]]), so no second cut exists.
