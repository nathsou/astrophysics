---
title: Cutting a pyramid into two pyramids and two prisms
---
**Statement.** Bisect the six edges of a triangular pyramid $ABCD$ at @E, @F, @G, @H, @K, @L and join the midpoints. The pyramid falls into

- two pyramids, @solid(AEGH) and @solid(HKLD), equal to each other and similar to the whole, each $\tfrac18$ of it;
- two prisms, equal to each other, each $\tfrac14$ of it.

So the two prisms together are $\tfrac34$ of the pyramid, which is more than half.

**Proof.** The midpoint theorem ([[6.2]]) makes @EH parallel to @DB and half of it, and so for every segment joining two midpoints. Hence the faces of the corner pyramid $AEGH$ are congruent to those of $HKLD$, and both are similar to the faces of $ABCD$ with ratio $\tfrac12$; the two small pyramids are equal and similar to the whole ([[11.def.10]]).

For the prisms: the parallelogram @figure(EBFG) is twice the triangle @triangle(GFC), and the prism with base $EBFG$ and top edge $HK$ has the same height as the prism with base $GFC$ and top $HKL$. By [[11.39]] the two prisms are equal. Each prism is greater than a small pyramid, since the prism on $EBFG$ contains the pyramid $EBFK$, which is congruent to $AEGH$. So the prisms exceed the pyramids, and together they are more than half of $ABCD$.

Euclid only needs "more than half", which he gets by comparing pieces. The exact fractions $\tfrac18$ and $\tfrac14$ follow from similarity ([[12.8]]) once the theory is complete.

**In the figure.** The shaded pieces are the two prisms; the thin outlines are the small pyramids. Change the height and the lean of the vertex @D: the readouts check the equalities and the fraction $\tfrac34$, which does not depend on the shape.

:::leads Where it leads
The two small pyramids are what is left over, and they are copies of the whole at half size. Cutting them the same way leaves four pyramids with $\tfrac1{64}$ each, and so on. After $n$ rounds the leftover is $(\tfrac14)^n$ of the pyramid, which [[10.1]] turns into "less than any given solid". This is the engine of [[12.4]] and [[12.5]].
:::
