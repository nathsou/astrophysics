---
title: A polygon between two concentric circles
---
**Statement.** Given two concentric circles, one can inscribe in the greater an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides that does not touch the lesser.

**Construction.** Draw the diameter @BD through the centre @K, and the chord @AC perpendicular to it at @G, the point where the lesser circle meets $KD$. The chord @AC touches the lesser circle ([[3.16]]). Now halve the semicircle $BAD$, halve the half, and so on. By [[10.1]] this eventually leaves an arc @LD less than the arc $AD$. The chord @LD is the side of the polygon: fitted round the circle, it closes up after $2^{k+1}$ steps.

**Proof.** Drop @LM perpendicular to @BD and extend it to @N, so that $LD = DN$. Since the arc $LD$ is less than the arc $AD$, the chord @LN is farther from the centre than @AC, and so misses the lesser circle. The sides $LD$, $DN$ lie even farther out. Every side of the polygon is congruent to $LD$ and at the same distance from $K$, so none touches the lesser circle.

**In the figure.** The slider sets the ratio of the radii. The readouts count the halvings needed and the sides of the polygon: as the circles get closer, the polygon needs more sides.

:::leads Where it leads
This is the plane version of [[12.17]], which fits a polyhedron between two concentric spheres. It is what "the circle is exhausted by polygons" means in a form Euclid can state: for every gap, however thin, some polygon fits inside it.
:::
