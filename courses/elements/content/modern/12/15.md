---
title: Equal cylinders have reciprocal bases and heights
---
**Statement.** Two cones or cylinders are equal exactly when their bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights:
$$AO = EP \iff \text{circle } ABCD : \text{circle } EFGH = MN : KL.$$

**Proof.** If the heights are equal, the bases are equal by [[12.11]], and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let @MN be the greater height. Cut off $QN = KL$ and cut the cylinder @EP by the plane through @Q, giving the cylinder @ES with the same height as @AO. Then
$$AO : ES = ABCD : EFGH, \qquad EP : ES = MN : QN,$$
the first because the cylinders have the same height ([[12.11]]), the second because a plane parallel to the bases cuts $EP$ ([[12.13]]).
If $AO = EP$, the left sides are equal, so $ABCD : EFGH = MN : QN = MN : KL$. Conversely, if the bases are reciprocal to the heights, the right sides are equal, so $AO : ES = EP : ES$ and $AO = EP$ ([[5.9]]).
