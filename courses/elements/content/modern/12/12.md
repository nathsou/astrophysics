---
title: Similar cones scale with the cube
---
**Statement.** Similar cones (and cylinders) are in the triplicate ratio of the diameters of their bases:
$$\text{cone } ABCDL : \text{cone } EFGHN = (BD : FH)^3.$$
Two cones are *similar* when their axes and the diameters of their bases are proportional ([[11.def.24]]).

**Proof.** First the polygonal version. Inscribe similar octagons @figure(ATBUCVDW) and @figure(EPFQGRHS) and raise pyramids on them with the vertices @L and @N. Join the centres @K and @M to the vertices of the polygons, cutting each pyramid into small triangular pyramids like @solid(BKTL) and @solid(FMPN). Euclid checks, triangle by triangle, that the faces of $BKTL$ and $FMPN$ are similar: $BKL \sim FMN$ (the axes and radii are proportional and make right angles), $BKT \sim FMP$ (the same central angle), $TKL \sim PMN$, and finally $LTB \sim NPF$ from proportional sides. So the small pyramids are similar, and by [[12.8]] they are in the ratio $(BK : FM)^3 = (BD : FH)^3$. Adding up ([[5.12]]), so are the whole pyramids.

Then exhaustion passes the ratio to the cones, exactly as in [[12.11]], and the cylinders follow from [[12.10]].

**In the figure.** The slider scales the second cone. The shaded small pyramids are $BKTL$ and $FMPN$; the readouts show the ratio of their volumes, and of the pyramids on the whole octagons, following the cube of the ratio of the diameters.
