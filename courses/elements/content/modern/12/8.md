---
title: Similar pyramids scale with the cube
---
**Statement.** Similar triangular pyramids are in the triplicate ratio of corresponding sides:
$$ABCG : DEFH = (BC : EF)^3.$$
The porism extends this to pyramids on similar polygons.

**Proof.** Complete the parallelepipeds @solid(BGML) and @solid(EHQP) on the edges from @B and @E. Similar pyramids have equal angles and proportional edges at these corners, so the faces @figure(BM), @figure(BK), @figure(BN) are similar to @figure(EQ), @figure(EO), @figure(ER), and the parallelepipeds are similar. Similar parallelepipeds are in the triplicate ratio of their sides ([[11.33]]). Each pyramid is a sixth of its parallelepiped: the prism on the triangle is half the parallelepiped ([[11.28]]) and three times the pyramid ([[12.7]]). Sixths are in the same ratio as the wholes.

"Triplicate ratio" is Book V's name for the ratio compounded three times ([[5.def.10]]); with numbers, $r^3$.

**In the figure.** The slider scales @solid(DEFH) against @solid(ABCG). The readouts show the ratio of the volumes tracking the cube of the ratio of the sides, and the pyramid staying a sixth of its parallelepiped.

:::leads Where it leads
Area scales with the square of length and volume with the cube: this is why a mouse's legs can be thin and an elephant's cannot, and why heat loss (surface) and heat production (volume) set limits on the size of animals. In the other direction, [[12.12]] and [[12.18]] use this proposition to show that cones and spheres also scale with the cube.
:::
