---
title: Pyramids of equal height are as their bases
---
**Statement.** Triangular pyramids of the same height are to one another as their bases:
$$ABCG : DEFH = \triangle ABC : \triangle DEF.$$

**Proof.** This is [[12.2]]'s argument for solids. Suppose $\triangle ABC : \triangle DEF = ABCG : W$ for a solid $W$.

1. *$W$ is not less than @solid(DEFH).* If it were, divide @solid(DEFH) as in [[12.3]], then divide the leftover pyramids, and so on. Each round removes more than half of what is left, so by [[10.1]] the leftover pyramids (such as @solid(DQRS) and @solid(STUH) after one round) eventually total less than $DEFH - W$. Then the prisms in $DEFH$ exceed $W$. Divide $ABCG$ the same number of times. By [[12.4]] and the hypothesis,
$$\text{prisms in } ABCG : \text{prisms in } DEFH = ABC : DEF = ABCG : W,$$
and alternately $ABCG : \text{its prisms} = W : \text{prisms in } DEFH$. The first ratio is greater than one, so $W$ exceeds the prisms in $DEFH$. Contradiction.
2. *$W$ is not greater.* Invert, and use the lemma of [[12.2]] to reduce to case 1 with the pyramids exchanged.

So $W = DEFH$.

**In the figure.** The first slider sets the common height, the second divides @solid(DEFH) again and again: the shaded pyramids are what is left, a quarter of the volume after each round. The third slider sets the supposed solid $W$ as a fraction of $DEFH$; once the leftover is less than $DEFH - W$, the prisms beat $W$.

:::leads Where it leads
In modern terms, $V = \tfrac13 B h$ for a pyramid needs two facts: volume is proportional to the base at fixed height (this proposition and [[12.6]]), and the constant is $\tfrac13$ ([[12.7]]). Calculus gets both at once from $\int_0^h B (x/h)^2\,dx$, which is exactly the "slices scale with the square of the distance to the vertex" that underlies [[12.3]].
:::
