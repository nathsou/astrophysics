---
title: Pyramids on polygons
---
**Statement.** Pyramids of the same height with polygonal bases are as their bases:
$$ABCDEM : FGHKLN = ABCDE : FGHKL.$$

**Proof.** Triangulate the bases from one vertex with the diagonals @AC, @AD and @FH, @FK. This splits each pyramid into triangular pyramids of the same height, to which [[12.5]] applies: $ABCM : ACDM = ABC : ACD$, and so on. Euclid then adds the proportions one piece at a time with *componendo* and *ex aequali* ([[5.18]], [[5.22]]), first within each pyramid and then across the two.

In modern terms, each triangular pyramid has volume $c \cdot \text{base}$ for a constant $c$ that depends only on the height, so the same holds for any sum of them. Book V has no multiplication of magnitudes by numbers like $c$, which is why the bookkeeping takes seven steps.

**In the figure.** The two pyramids share their height. Change it, the size of @figure(FGHKL) or the lean of @N: the ratio of the volumes follows the ratio of the bases.
