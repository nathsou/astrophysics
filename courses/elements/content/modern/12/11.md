---
title: Cones of equal height are as their bases
---
**Statement.** Cones (and cylinders) of the same height are to one another as their bases:
$$\text{cone } AL : \text{cone } EN = \text{circle } ABCD : \text{circle } EFGH.$$

**Proof.** The same double reductio as in [[12.2]] and [[12.5]]. Suppose $ABCD : EFGH = \text{cone } AL : O$ with a solid $O$ less than the cone @solid(EN). Exhaust @solid(EN) with pyramids on the square, then on the octagon @figure(HPEQFRGS), and so on, until the pyramid in it exceeds $O$. Inscribe the similar octagon @figure(DTAUBVCW) in the other base. Now three proportions line up:

- the polygons are as the squares on the diameters @AC and @EG ([[12.1]]);
- the circles are as the same squares ([[12.2]]);
- pyramids of equal height are as their bases ([[12.6]]).

So $\text{cone } AL : O = \text{pyramid in } AL : \text{pyramid in } EN$, and alternating, $O$ would exceed the pyramid in $EN$. Contradiction. The case "$O$ greater" is reduced to this one as before. Cylinders follow, being three times the cones ([[12.10]]).

**In the figure.** The two cones share their height. Change the radius of @circle(EFGH): the pyramids on the similar polygons, and the polygons themselves, stay in the ratio of the squares on the diameters, whatever the number of sides.
