---
title: Cylinders on equal bases are as their heights
---
**Statement.** Cones and cylinders on equal bases are to one another as their heights:
$$\text{cylinder } EB : \text{cylinder } FD = GH : KL.$$

**Proof.** Extend the axis @KL beyond @L by $LN = GH$ and imagine the cylinder @CM on it. It stands on a base equal to that of @EB and has the same height, so it equals $EB$ ([[12.11]]). The cylinder @FM is cut by the plane of the circle $CD$, so $CM : FD = LN : KL$ ([[12.13]]). Replace $CM$ by $EB$ and $LN$ by $GH$. The cones, a third of the cylinders ([[12.10]]), are in the same ratio.

**In the figure.** The two sliders set the axes @GH and @KL. The dashed cylinder is the auxiliary cylinder $CM$.

:::leads Where it leads
Together with [[12.11]], this says the volume of a cylinder is proportional to base times height, the two variables separately. [[12.15]] combines them.
:::
