---
title: The prisms in two pyramids are as the bases
---
**Statement.** Let @solid(ABCG) and @solid(DEFH) be triangular pyramids of the same height, each divided as in [[12.3]] (and the resulting small pyramids divided again, the same number of times). Then
$$\text{prisms in } ABCG : \text{prisms in } DEFH = \triangle ABC : \triangle DEF.$$

**Proof.** Take one division. The triangle @triangle(LOC) is the base triangle scaled by $\tfrac12$, and so is @triangle(RVF), so $\triangle ABC : \triangle DEF = \triangle LOC : \triangle RVF$ ([[6.22]], [[5.16]]).

The prism on @triangle(LOC) with top $PMN$ and the prism on @triangle(RVF) with top $STU$ have equal heights, half the common height of the pyramids (the lemma, via [[11.17]]). Prisms of equal height are as their bases ([[11.32]], halving the parallelepipeds), so these two prisms are as $LOC : RVF$, that is, as $ABC : DEF$. The other prism in each pyramid equals this one ([[12.3]]), so the pairs of prisms are in the same ratio ([[5.12]]).

At the next division the small pyramids $PMNG$ and $STUH$ have bases $PMN = LOC$ and $STU = RVF$, so their prisms are again in the ratio $ABC : DEF$, and adding ratios that are equal keeps the ratio. By induction, the prisms after any number of divisions are as the bases.

**In the figure.** The slider changes the size of @triangle(DEF) while the pyramids keep a common height. The readouts compare the prisms after one and two divisions with the ratio of the bases; the pyramids themselves are also in that ratio, but that is [[12.5]].

:::leads Where it leads
This is the finite, exact half of an exhaustion argument: an identity that holds at every stage $n$. [[12.5]] combines it with the fact that the leftover pyramids shrink to nothing.
:::
