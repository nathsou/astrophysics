---
title: Similar inscribed polygons scale with the diameter squared
---
**Statement.** If similar polygons $P$ and $P'$ are inscribed in circles with diameters $d$ and $d'$, then
$$P : P' = d^2 : d'^2.$$

**Proof.** By [[6.20]], similar polygons are to one another as the squares on corresponding sides, so it is enough to show that a side is to the corresponding side as diameter to diameter: $BA : GF = BM : GN$.

Draw the diameters @BM and @GN and join @BE, @AM, @GL, @FN.

1. The polygons are similar, so $\angle BAE = \angle GFL$ and $BA : AE = GF : FL$. By [[6.6]], the triangles @triangle(ABE) and @triangle(FGL) are equiangular, and $\angle AEB = \angle FLG$.
2. Angles on the same arc are equal ([[3.27]]): $\angle AMB = \angle AEB$ and $\angle FNG = \angle FLG$. So $\angle AMB = \angle FNG$.
3. The angles @angle(BAM) and @angle(GFN) are right, being in semicircles ([[3.31]]). So the triangles @triangle(ABM) and @triangle(FGN) are equiangular, and $BM : GN = BA : GF$ ([[6.4]]).

Squaring, $d^2 : d'^2 = BA^2 : GF^2 = P : P'$.

:::leads Where it leads
This is the polygonal half of [[12.2]]. The polygons are exact, so the proportion holds for each of them. [[12.2]] passes it to the circles, which are the limit of such polygons, without ever speaking of a limit.
:::
