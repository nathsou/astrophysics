---
title: Equal pyramids have reciprocal bases and heights
---
**Statement.** Two triangular pyramids are equal in volume exactly when their bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights:
$$ABCG = DEFH \iff \triangle ABC : \triangle DEF = h_{DEFH} : h_{ABCG}.$$

**Proof.** Complete the parallelepipeds @solid(BGML) and @solid(EHQP) as in [[12.8]]. Each pyramid is a sixth of its parallelepiped, so the pyramids are equal exactly when the parallelepipeds are. For parallelepipeds this is [[11.34]], with bases @figure(BM) and @figure(EQ), which are double the triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ and so in the same ratio. The heights of the parallelepipeds are those of the pyramids.

With $V = \tfrac13 B h$ the statement is immediate: $B_1 h_1 = B_2 h_2 \iff B_1 : B_2 = h_2 : h_1$. Euclid proves the result without that formula, which he never states.

**In the figure.** The second slider shrinks the base @triangle(DEF); the height of @solid(DEFH) grows by the inverse factor squared, so that its volume stays that of @solid(ABCG). The readouts check both directions: the volumes are equal, and the bases are as the heights inverted.
