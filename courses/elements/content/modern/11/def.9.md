---
title: Similar solids
---
Similar solid figures are those bounded by the same number of similar planes (faces).

This is not a sound definition. Similarity should mean that one solid is a scaled copy of the other, and having similar faces does not guarantee it. The faces have to be arranged in the same way, and even then, for non-convex solids, a region of the boundary can sometimes be pushed in without changing the shape of any face. For *convex* polyhedra the definition does work, once the faces are required to be arranged in the same pattern: this follows from Cauchy's rigidity theorem (1813).

Book XI uses the definition only for parallelepipeds ([[11.27]], [[11.33]]), where it causes no harm.
