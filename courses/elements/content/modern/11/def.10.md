---
title: Equal and similar solids
---
Equal and similar solids are those bounded by similar and equal faces, the same number of each. In modern terms: congruent solids.

The definition inherits the problems of [[11.def.9]], and it adds one of its own. A solid and its mirror image have equal and similar faces, but in general neither can be moved onto the other. Euclid treats them as equal. For volume this is correct, since reflections preserve volume. But it is a real assumption, and [[11.28]] relies on it.
