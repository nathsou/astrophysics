---
title: Solid angle
---
A solid angle is the figure formed at a point by three or more plane angles that meet there and do not all lie in one plane. Examples are the corner of a cube (three right angles) and the apex of a pyramid.

Euclid's solid angle is the *polyhedral* angle: a cone over a polygon, described by its faces. The modern solid angle as a number (the area it cuts out of the unit sphere, measured in steradians) does not appear. Euclid compares solid angles only through their face angles: [[11.20]] and [[11.21]] give the constraints the face angles must satisfy, and [[11.23]] and [[11.26]] construct solid angles from them.
