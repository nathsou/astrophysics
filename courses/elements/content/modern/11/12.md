---
title: Erecting a perpendicular to a plane
---
**Statement.** At a given point @A of a plane, set up a line perpendicular to the plane.

**Construction (Euclid's).** Take any point @B above the plane and drop the perpendicular @BC to the plane ([[11.11]]). Through @A draw @AD parallel to @BC ([[1.31]]). Then @AD is perpendicular to the plane by [[11.8]].

The problem is the reverse of [[11.11]], and it is reduced to it: a perpendicular exists somewhere, so a parallel to it works everywhere. [[11.13]] shows that the perpendicular at @A is unique.

:::code In vectors
The answer is $A + t\,n$ for a normal $n$. The construction obtains a normal as $n = B - C$, the difference between a point and its projection.
:::
