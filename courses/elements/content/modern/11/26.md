---
title: Copying a solid angle
---
**Statement.** At a point @A of a given line @AB, construct a solid angle equal to a given solid angle at @D, contained by $\angle EDC$, $\angle EDF$, $\angle FDC$.

**Construction (Euclid's).** From a point @F on the edge @DF drop the perpendicular @FG to the plane $EDC$ ([[11.11]]) and join @DG. At @A, in a plane, make $\angle BAL = \angle EDC$ and $\angle BAK = \angle EDG$ ([[1.23]]), with $AK = DG$. Set up @KH perpendicular to that plane ([[11.12]]) with $KH = GF$, and join @HA.

**Why it works.** Take $AB = DE$. The right triangles $AKH$ and $DGF$ are congruent, so $AH = DF$. The triangles $KAB$ and $GDE$ are congruent (SAS), so $KB = GE$, and then the right triangles $HKB$ and $FGE$ give $HB = FE$. Now $\triangle HAB \cong \triangle FDE$ (SSS), so $\angle BAH = \angle EDF$. In the same way $\angle HAL = \angle FDC$.

The data that pin down the solid angle are the angle $EDC$ in the base, and the position of @F given by its projection @G and height $FG$. Copy those and you copy the solid angle.

:::gap Mirror images
Euclid calls two solid angles equal when their face angles are equal. But a solid angle and its mirror image have the same face angles, and cannot in general be moved onto each other. The construction produces one of the two, depending on which side of the plane $BAL$ the perpendicular $KH$ is set up and on which side of @AB the angle $BAK$ is placed. The figure places them the same way as at @D, so the copy is a rotation of the original, not a reflection.
:::
