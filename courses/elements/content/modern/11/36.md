---
title: The solid on three proportionals
---
**Statement.** If three lines are in continued proportion, $A : B = B : C$, then the parallelepiped with edges $A$, $B$, $C$ equals the equilateral parallelepiped with all edges $B$ and the same solid angles.

This is the solid analogue of [[6.17]]: there, the rectangle on the extremes equals the square on the mean.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Build the equilateral solid @solid(EK) with edges $ED = EG = EF = B$, and at @L the solid @solid(LH) with the same solid angle ([[11.26]]) and edges $LM = A$, $LO = B$, $LN = C$. Since $A : B = B : C$, the sides about the equal angles $\angle NLM$ and $\angle DEF$ are reciprocally proportional, so the base parallelograms @figure(MN) and @figure(DF) are equal ([[6.14]]). The edges @LO and @EG are equal and make equal angles with the base edges, so by the porism of [[11.35]] the two solids have the same height. Equal bases and equal heights give equal solids ([[11.31]]).

:::code
For fixed angles, the volume is $V = abc \cdot \kappa$, where $\kappa$ is the volume of the solid with those angles and unit edges (the square root of the Gram determinant of three unit vectors along the edges). The proposition says $b^2 = ac \Rightarrow abc = b^3$. The rods at the left are $A$, $B$ and $C = B^2 / A$.
:::
