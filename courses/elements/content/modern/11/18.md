---
title: A plane through a perpendicular is perpendicular
---
**Statement.** If @AB is perpendicular to a plane, every plane through @AB is perpendicular to that plane.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Let @plane(DE) be a plane through @AB, meeting the plane of reference in @CE. Take @F on @CE and draw @FG in @plane(DE) perpendicular to @CE. Since $\angle ABF$ and $\angle GFB$ are both right, @FG ∥ @AB ([[1.28]]), so @FG is perpendicular to the plane of reference ([[11.8]]). Every line drawn in @plane(DE) perpendicular to the section is therefore perpendicular to the other plane, which is the definition of perpendicular planes ([[11.def.4]]).

:::code In vectors
The plane @plane(DE) contains the normal $n$ of the reference plane, so its own normal $m$ satisfies $m \cdot n = 0$: the planes are perpendicular. Turn the plane with the slider; @FG stays parallel to @AB.
:::
