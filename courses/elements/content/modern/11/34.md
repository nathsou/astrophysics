---
title: Equal volumes, reciprocal bases and heights
---
**Statement.** Two parallelepipeds @solid(AB) and @solid(CD) are equal exactly when their bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights:
$$\text{base } EH : \text{base } NQ = \text{height of } CD : \text{height of } AB.$$

In modern terms, $B_1 h_1 = B_2 h_2$. This is the solid version of [[6.14]] and [[6.15]].

**Proof (Euclid's), with upright edges.** If the bases are equal, so are the heights, by [[11.32]] and [[11.31]]. Otherwise let @figure(EH) be the greater. Then @CM is greater than @AG; cut off $CT = AG$ and complete the solid $CV$ on @figure(NQ) with height @CT. Now
$$AB : CV = EH : NQ \quad ([[11.32]], \text{ same height}), \qquad CD : CV = CM : CT \quad ([[11.25]], [[6.1]]).$$
If $AB = CD$, the left sides are equal ([[5.7]]), so $EH : NQ = CM : CT = CM : AG$. Conversely, if $EH : NQ = CM : AG$, the right sides are equal, so $AB : CV = CD : CV$ and $AB = CD$ ([[5.9]]).

For oblique edges Euclid drops perpendiculars from the tops and applies the first case to the upright solids, which are equal to the oblique ones by [[11.29]] and [[11.30]]. The figure shows the upright case; the second case reuses letters for the feet of the perpendiculars.

:::code
The figure fixes the volume of @solid(CD) equal to that of @solid(AB) and computes its height as volume ÷ base. It then checks $EH : NQ = CM : AG$ and $AB : CV = EH : NQ$.
:::
