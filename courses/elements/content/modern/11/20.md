---
title: Triangle inequality for face angles
---
**Statement.** If a solid angle at @A is contained by three plane angles $\angle BAC$, $\angle CAD$, $\angle DAB$, any two of them together are greater than the third.

**Proof (Euclid's).** If the three angles are equal there is nothing to prove. Otherwise let $\angle BAC$ be the greatest. In its plane construct $\angle BAE = \angle DAB$ with $AE = AD$, and draw a line through @E meeting @AB at @B and @AC at @C.

1. $\triangle DAB \cong \triangle EAB$ (SAS), so $DB = BE$.
2. $BD + DC > BC$ ([[1.20]]); subtracting $DB = BE$ leaves $DC > EC$.
3. The triangles $DAC$ and $EAC$ have two sides equal ($AD = AE$, $AC$ common) and $DC > EC$, so $\angle DAC > \angle EAC$ ([[1.25]]).
4. Add $\angle DAB = \angle BAE$: $\angle DAB + \angle DAC > \angle BAE + \angle EAC = \angle BAC$.

The two smaller angles exceed the largest, and the other two inequalities are trivial.

:::code A metric on directions
Represent each edge of the solid angle by a unit vector. The angle between two unit vectors, $d(u, v) = \arccos(u \cdot v)$, is the great-circle distance on the unit sphere. [[11.20]] is the triangle inequality for this distance:
$$d(u, w) \le d(u, v) + d(v, w),$$
with strict inequality when the three vectors are not coplanar. The face angles of a solid angle are the sides of a *spherical triangle*.
:::

:::leads Where it leads
[[11.21]] adds the second condition on the face angles, and [[11.23]] proves that the two together are sufficient to build the solid angle. Spherical trigonometry, developed by Menelaus and later by astronomers, starts from these facts.
:::
