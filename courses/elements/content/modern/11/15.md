---
title: Parallel angles span parallel planes
---
**Statement.** If two intersecting lines @AB, @BC are parallel to two intersecting lines @DE, @EF in another plane, the plane through @AB, @BC is parallel to the plane through @DE, @EF.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Drop the perpendicular @BG from @B to the plane $DEF$ ([[11.11]]), and draw @GH ∥ @ED and @GK ∥ @EF. Then @BA ∥ @GH ([[11.9]]). Since $\angle BGH$ is right, so is $\angle GBA$ ([[1.29]]). Likewise $\angle GBC$ is right. So @GB is perpendicular to both planes ([[11.4]]), and planes with a common perpendicular are parallel ([[11.14]]).

:::code In vectors
A plane through $P$ spanned by directions $u$, $w$ is $\{P + su + tw\}$, with normal $u \times w$. Parallel lines give the same $u$, $w$ up to scale, so both planes have the same normal and are parallel (or equal, if they share a point). The figure checks that @BG is orthogonal to @BA and @BC.
:::
