---
title: The diagonal plane halves a parallelepiped
---
**Statement.** A parallelepiped is bisected by the plane @plane(CE) through the diagonals @CF and @DE of two opposite faces.

**Proof (Euclid's).** The diagonal @CF halves the top face into the triangles $CGF$ and $CFB$, and @DE halves the bottom face into $ADE$ and $DEH$ ([[1.34]]). The two prisms have equal triangles as ends, and their side faces are equal in pairs: @plane(CA) and @plane(EB) are opposite faces, as are @plane(GE) and @plane(CH), and the cutting parallelogram is common. So the prisms are "contained by equal and similar planes equal in multitude" and are equal ([[11.def.10]]).

The figure computes both prism volumes (each as three tetrahedra) and checks that each is half the solid.

:::gap The two halves are mirror images
The point reflection through the centre of the parallelepiped maps it onto itself, maps the cutting plane onto itself, and swaps the two prisms. In three dimensions a point reflection reverses orientation: its matrix is $-I$, with determinant $-1$. So the two halves are mirror images, and in general no rigid motion carries one onto the other. Euclid's appeal to [[11.def.10]] passes over this. The conclusion is right, because volume is invariant under reflections too ($|\det| = 1$), but a modern proof has to say so, or cut the prisms into pieces that can be matched by rigid motions.
:::

The proposition is used for the triangular prisms of [[11.39]] and of Book XII.
