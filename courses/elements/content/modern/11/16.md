---
title: A plane cuts parallel planes in parallel lines
---
**Statement.** If the parallel planes @plane(AB) and @plane(CD) are cut by a plane @plane(EFGH), the sections @EF and @GH are parallel.

**Proof (Euclid's).** @EF and @GH lie in one plane, the cutting plane. If they met when produced, at $K$ say, then $K$ would lie on @EF, hence in @plane(AB) ([[11.1]]), and on @GH, hence in @plane(CD). The parallel planes would meet, contrary to hypothesis. Lines in one plane that do not meet are parallel ([[1.def.23]]).

:::code In vectors
The section of the plane with normal $n_1$ by the plane with normal $n_3$ has direction $n_1 \times n_3$. For parallel planes $n_2 = \lambda n_1$, so the second section has direction $n_2 \times n_3 = \lambda\, n_1 \times n_3$: the same.
:::

[[11.17]] is the metric version: parallel planes cut all lines in the same ratios.
