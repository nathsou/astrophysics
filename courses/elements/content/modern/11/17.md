---
title: Parallel planes cut lines proportionally
---
**Statement.** If two lines are cut by three parallel planes, @AB at @A, @E, @B and @CD at @C, @F, @D, then
$$AE : EB = CF : FD.$$

This is the three-dimensional form of [[6.2]]. The two lines may be skew.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Join @AD, meeting the middle plane at @O. The plane of the triangle $ABD$ cuts the two lower planes in @EO and @BD, which are parallel by [[11.16]]. So $AE : EB = AO : OD$ by [[6.2]]. Likewise the plane of $ADC$ cuts the two upper planes in @AC and @OF, which are parallel, so $AO : OD = CF : FD$. Chain the ratios ([[5.11]]).

The diagonal @AD is the trick: it links the two lines through a third line that lies in a plane with each of them.

:::code A linear function along a line
The parallel planes are level sets $\varphi(X) = c_1, c_2, c_3$ of one linear function $\varphi(X) = n\cdot X$. Along any line $X(t) = P + tv$, $\varphi$ is affine in $t$, so the parameter values where it takes the levels $c_1, c_2, c_3$ divide in the ratio $(c_2 - c_1) : (c_3 - c_2)$, whatever the line. The figure checks both ratios as you move the planes and the lines.
:::
