---
title: Shearing a parallelepiped along a line
---
**Statement.** Parallelepipeds @solid(CM) and @solid(CN) on the same base @figure(AB) and of the same height, whose top vertices lie on the same two lines @FN and @DK, are equal.

This is the three-dimensional [[1.35]]. Slide the second top along the lines with the slider: the solid leans, its faces change shape, and its volume does not change.

**Proof (Euclid's).** The tops $FDHM$ and $GEKN$ overlap in the parallelogram $GEHM$. On the line @DK, $DH = EK$ (each equals @CB), so subtracting $EH$ gives $DE = HK$; then the triangles $DCE$ and $HBK$ are congruent, and the parallelograms @figure(DG) and @figure(HN) are equal ([[1.36]]). The same holds on the other side for $AFG$ and $MLN$. So the prism with ends $AFG$ and $DCE$ is equal to the prism with ends $MLN$ and $HBK$: its faces are equal and similar, one for one, and it is in fact a translate of it. Now
$$\text{solid } CM = \text{common part} + \text{prism } MLN\text{–}HBK, \qquad \text{solid } CN = \text{common part} + \text{prism } AFG\text{–}DCE,$$
where the common part is the solid with base @figure(AB) and top $GEHM$. So the solids are equal.

It is a cut-and-paste proof: cut a prism off one end and glue it on the other.

:::code A shear has determinant 1
In coordinates with the base in the plane $z = 0$, sliding the top along the line direction $q$ is the linear map
$$S = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & \lambda q_x / h \\ 0 & 1 & \lambda q_y/h \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},$$
which moves each point horizontally in proportion to its height. It is triangular with ones on the diagonal, so $\det S = 1$, and volumes are preserved. In terms of edge vectors: $\det[a,\ b,\ c + \lambda b] = \det[a, b, c]$, because the determinant is linear in the last column and vanishes when two columns are equal.
:::

:::leads Where it leads
[[11.30]] removes the condition on the lines, and [[11.31]] allows different bases of equal area. Together they say that the volume of a parallelepiped depends only on base area and height.
:::
