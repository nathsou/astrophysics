---
title: A parallel to a perpendicular is perpendicular
---
**Statement.** If @AB and @CD are parallel and @AB is perpendicular to a plane, then @CD is perpendicular to it as well.

This is the converse of [[11.6]].

**Proof (Euclid's).** Let the lines meet the plane at @B and @D. Then @AB, @CD and @BD lie in one plane ([[11.7]]). Since @AB ⊥ @BD and the lines are parallel, @CD ⊥ @BD ([[1.29]]). Now draw @DE in the plane perpendicular to @BD with $DE = AB$. As in [[11.6]], $AD = BE$ and then $\angle EDA$ is right. So @ED is perpendicular to @DB and @DA, hence to their plane ([[11.4]]), which contains @DC. So $CD \perp DE$ as well as $CD \perp DB$, and by [[11.4]] @CD is perpendicular to the plane.

:::code In vectors
Perpendicularity to a plane depends only on the direction of the line, and parallel lines have the same direction. The figure checks $\vec{DC} \cdot \vec{DB} = \vec{DC} \cdot \vec{DE} = 0$.
:::
