---
title: Angles with parallel arms are equal
---
**Statement.** If @AB ∥ @DE and @BC ∥ @EF, pointing the same way, and the two angles are not in one plane, then $\angle ABC = \angle DEF$.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Cut off $BA = BC = ED = EF$ and join @AD, @CF, @BE, @AC, @DF. Since @BA is equal and parallel to @ED, $ABED$ is a parallelogram, so @AD is equal and parallel to @BE ([[1.33]]). Likewise @CF. By [[11.9]], @AD and @CF are parallel, and they are equal, so $ACFD$ is a parallelogram and $AC = DF$. Then $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$ (SSS, [[1.8]]).

:::code A translation
The second angle is the first moved by the translation $X \mapsto X + \vec{BE}$. A translation preserves distances, hence angles. In vectors the claim is immediate: the angle is $\arccos \frac{u\cdot w}{|u||w|}$, and it depends only on the directions of the arms.
:::

The same-way condition matters: if one arm is reversed, the angles are supplementary instead of equal. Euclid leaves this to the figure.
