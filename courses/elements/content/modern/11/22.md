---
title: Chords of the face angles form a triangle
---
**Statement.** Let three plane angles $\angle ABC$, $\angle DEF$, $\angle GHK$ be such that any two together exceed the third, and let their arms all be equal. Then the chords @AC, @DF, @GK satisfy the triangle inequality, so a triangle can be made from them ([[1.22]]).

**Proof (Euclid's).** At @H construct $\angle KHL = \angle ABC$ with $HL$ equal to the arms, and join @KL, @GL. Then $KL = AC$ (SAS). Since $\angle GHK + \angle ABC > \angle DEF$, the angle $\angle GHL$ exceeds $\angle DEF$, so $GL > DF$ ([[1.24]], the hinge theorem). Then $GK + KL > GL > DF$ ([[1.20]]), that is $AC + GK > DF$. The other two inequalities are proved the same way.

:::code With the chord formula
With arms of length $r$, the chord of an angle $\theta$ is $2r\sin(\theta/2)$, which increases with $\theta$ on $[0^\circ, 180^\circ]$. The claim is that $\sin\frac{\alpha}{2} + \sin\frac{\gamma}{2} > \sin\frac{\beta}{2}$ whenever $\alpha + \gamma > \beta$. Euclid gets it from the hinge theorem, without trigonometry. The figure draws the resulting triangle below the angles.
:::

The lemma prepares [[11.23]], which uses this triangle as the base of the solid angle it builds.
