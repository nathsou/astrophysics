---
title: Boundary of a solid
---
The boundary of a solid is a surface, just as the boundary of a surface is a line ([[1.def.6]]) and the ends of a line are points ([[1.def.3]]).

In topological language, the boundary of a reasonable region of $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a two-dimensional surface. For the solids of Books XI–XIII (polyhedra, cones, cylinders, spheres) this is clear from the pictures. In general it is a theorem that needs hypotheses: there are regions whose boundary is far wilder than a surface.
