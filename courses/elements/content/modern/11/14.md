---
title: Planes perpendicular to one line are parallel
---
**Statement.** Two planes @plane(CD) and @plane(EF) to which the same line @AB is perpendicular are parallel.

**Proof (Euclid's).** If they met, they would meet in a line $GH$ ([[11.3]]). Take a point $K$ on it and join $AK$, $BK$. Since @AB is perpendicular to both planes, $\angle ABK$ and $\angle BAK$ are both right, and the triangle $ABK$ has two right angles, contradicting [[1.17]].

The figure cannot draw $K$. It draws instead the two lines from @A and @B that would have to meet at $K$: both perpendicular to @AB, they run side by side.

:::code In vectors
Both planes have normal $\vec{AB}$, so their equations are $n\cdot X = c_1$ and $n \cdot X = c_2$ with $c_1 \ne c_2$. No point satisfies both. The distance between them is $|c_1 - c_2|/|n| = AB$, which the figure checks at several points of @plane(EF).
:::
