---
title: Shearing a parallelepiped in any direction
---
**Statement.** Parallelepipeds @solid(CM) and @solid(CN) on the same base $ACBL$ and of the same height are equal, even when their tops @figure(FDHM) and @figure(GEKN) do not lie between the same two lines.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Produce the sides of the two tops until they meet: $NK$ and $DH$ at @R, $FM$ and $GE$ at @O and @P, and so on, giving a third top $OQRP$ over the same base. The solid $CP$ with this top shares the lines @FP and @DR with $CM$, so $CM = CP$ by [[11.29]]. It shares the lines @GQ and @NR with $CN$, so $CP = CN$ by [[11.29]] again.

:::code Two shears make a translation
Moving the top by an arbitrary horizontal vector $t$ is the composition of two shears: write $t = \lambda p + \mu q$ in terms of the base edges $p = \vec{AC}$ and $q = \vec{AL}$, and slide first along $q$, then along $p$. The intermediate solid $CP$ is the one after the first slide. In matrix terms, the upper unitriangular matrices form a group, and each one is a product of elementary ones. All have determinant $1$.
:::

The figure's three sliders move the tops independently; the three volumes stay equal.
