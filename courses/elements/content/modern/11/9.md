---
title: Parallelism in space is transitive
---
**Statement.** If @AB and @CD are each parallel to @EF, and not in one plane with it, then @AB is parallel to @CD.

In the plane this is [[1.30]]. In space it needs a new proof, because the three lines do not lie in one plane.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Take @G on @EF. In the plane of @EF and @AB draw @GH perpendicular to @EF, and in the plane of @EF and @CD draw @GK perpendicular to @EF. By [[11.4]], @EF is perpendicular to the plane @triangle(HGK). By [[11.8]], so are its parallels @AB and @CD. By [[11.6]], two perpendiculars to one plane are parallel.

The proof turns parallelism into perpendicularity to a common plane, and back.

:::code In vectors
Parallel means "same direction up to a nonzero scalar": $u = \lambda w$ and $w = \mu v$ give $u = \lambda\mu\, v$. The relation is transitive with no work at all, which is one measure of how much vectors simplify solid geometry.
:::
