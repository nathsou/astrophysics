---
title: The common section of two perpendicular planes
---
**Statement.** If two planes @plane(AB) and @plane(BC) are both perpendicular to a plane, their common section @BD is perpendicular to it as well.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Suppose not. At @D draw @DE in @plane(AB) perpendicular to its trace @AD, and @DF in @plane(BC) perpendicular to its trace @CD. By [[11.def.4]], both are perpendicular to the plane of reference. Two perpendiculars at one point on the same side are impossible ([[11.13]]), so @DE and @DF coincide with each other and with the only line common to both planes, @BD.

The figure builds each plane from its trace and the condition of perpendicularity, and computes the section @BD rather than assuming it vertical. @E and @F then land on @BD, as the proof says they must.

:::code In vectors
A plane perpendicular to the reference plane contains its normal $n$. Two such planes both contain $n$, so their intersection, with direction $n_1 \times n_2$, is parallel to $n$.
:::
