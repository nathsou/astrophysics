---
title: The centre of a cube
---
**Statement.** In a cube, let the planes @plane(KN) and @plane(OR) pass through the midpoints of the sides of two opposite faces @plane(CF) and @plane(AH). Their common section @US and the diagonal @DG of the cube bisect each other at @T.

**Proof (Euclid's).** In the top face, $\triangle DOU \cong \triangle EPU$ (SAS, alternate angles), so $DU = UE$ and $\angle OUD = \angle PUE$; hence $DUE$ is a straight line ([[1.14]]) and @U is the midpoint of the face diagonal @DE. Likewise @S is the midpoint of @BG. Since @DB and @EG are equal and parallel ([[11.9]]), $DBGE$ is a parallelogram, so @DE ∥ @BG ([[1.33]]). Then $\triangle DTU \cong \triangle GTS$ (AAS, [[1.26]]), which gives $DT = TG$ and $UT = TS$.

:::code The centroid
With the centre of the cube at the origin, the vertices come in opposite pairs $\pm v$, and so do the face centres. Every segment joining a point to its opposite passes through the origin and is bisected there. The theorem holds for any parallelepiped, since only parallelism was used.
:::

This lemma is used in [[13.17]], where a dodecahedron is built on a cube.
