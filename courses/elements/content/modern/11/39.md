---
title: Two prisms of equal volume
---
**Statement.** Let two prisms have the same height: one, $ABCDEF$, stands on a parallelogram @figure(AF), and the other, $GHKLMN$, on a triangle $GHK$. If @figure(AF) is double the triangle $GHK$, the prisms are equal.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Complete each prism to a parallelepiped: $AO$ and $GP$. The parallelogram @figure(HK) on the triangle $GHK$ is double the triangle ([[1.34]]), so it equals @figure(AF). The parallelepipeds have equal bases and the same height, so they are equal ([[11.31]]). Each prism is half its parallelepiped ([[11.28]]).

Note which face of the first prism is its "base": the parallelogram, with the height measured from it to the opposite edge @DC. A triangular prism can stand on either kind of face.

:::code
With base area $B$ and height $h$ the volume of a prism is $B h$ when it stands on a triangle, and $\tfrac12 B h$ when it stands on a parallelogram face. The figure computes both prisms as sums of tetrahedra and checks that they agree.
:::

The proposition is used in [[12.3]] and [[12.4]], where a pyramid is cut into two prisms and two smaller pyramids.
