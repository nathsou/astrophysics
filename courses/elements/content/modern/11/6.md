---
title: Two perpendiculars to a plane are parallel
---
**Statement.** If @AB and @CD are both perpendicular to the same plane, they are parallel.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Let them meet the plane at @B and @D. In the plane draw @DE perpendicular to @BD with $DE = AB$, and join @BE, @AE, @AD.

1. $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle EDB$ (SAS, right angles at @B and @D), so $AD = BE$.
2. $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle EDA$ (SSS, common side @AE), so $\angle EDA = \angle ABE$, a right angle.
3. So @ED is perpendicular to @DB, @DA and @DC at @D, and by [[11.5]] these three lie in one plane. That plane contains @AB as well ([[11.2]]).
4. In that plane @AB and @CD are both perpendicular to @BD, so they are parallel ([[1.28]]).

The hard part is step 3: showing that the two lines are coplanar, which parallel lines have to be ([[1.def.23]]).

:::code In vectors
Both lines have the normal of the plane as their direction, up to sign. Two lines with proportional directions are parallel (or the same line). The figure checks that $(B-A) \times (D-C) = 0$.
:::
