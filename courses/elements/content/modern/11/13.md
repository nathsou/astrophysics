---
title: The perpendicular at a point is unique
---
**Statement.** At a point @A of a plane, only one line can be set up perpendicular to the plane on a given side.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Suppose @AB and @AC were both perpendicular. The plane through them meets the given plane in a line $DAE$ ([[11.3]]). By [[11.def.3]], $\angle BAE$ and $\angle CAE$ are both right angles. They lie in one plane on the same side of $DAE$, so the part equals the whole: impossible. The figure draws @AC as it would have to be, and $\angle CAE$ is not right.

:::code In vectors
Perpendicular lines at $A$ have direction $\pm n$, where the normal $n$ is unique up to scale because the orthogonal complement of a plane in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is one-dimensional. "On the same side" fixes the sign.
:::
