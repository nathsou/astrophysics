---
title: Equal angles determine the inclination
---
**Statement.** Let $\angle BAC = \angle EDF$, and let lines @AG and @DM rise from the vertices, making equal angles with the corresponding arms: $\angle GAB = \angle MDE$ and $\angle GAC = \angle MDF$. Drop perpendiculars @GL and @MN to the planes of the angles. Then the lines make equal angles with their planes: $\angle GAL = \angle MDN$.

In other words, the inclination of a line to a plane ([[11.def.5]]) is determined by the angles it makes with two lines of the plane.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Cut off $AH = DM$ and let @HK be parallel to @GL, so perpendicular to the plane ([[11.8]]). From @K drop perpendiculars to the arms, with feet @B and @C, and from @N likewise, with feet @E and @F.

1. $HA^2 = HK^2 + KC^2 + CA^2 = HC^2 + CA^2$ ([[1.47]] twice), so $\angle HCA$ is right ([[1.48]]). Likewise $\angle MFD$ is right.
2. The right triangles $HAC$ and $MDF$ have equal hypotenuses and an equal angle, so $AC = DF$ ([[1.26]]); likewise $AB = DE$.
3. Then $\triangle CAB \cong \triangle FDE$ (SAS), and, subtracting from the right angles at @C and @F, the triangles $BCK$ and $EFN$ are congruent (ASA). So $CK = FN$, and $AK = DN$ ([[1.47]]).
4. Then $HK = MN$ ([[1.47]] once more), and $\triangle HAK \cong \triangle MDN$ (SSS).

**Porism.** If moreover $AG = DM$, the perpendiculars @GL and @MN are equal. [[11.36]] uses this to show that two solids have the same height.

:::code Coordinates in a frame
Write the direction $m$ of the elevated line in the frame $(e, f, n)$, where $e$, $f$ are unit vectors along the arms and $n$ is the unit normal. The two given cosines $m \cdot e$ and $m \cdot f$ are two linear equations in the components of $m$ in the plane, and they have a unique solution when $e \ne \pm f$. The normal component is then $\sqrt{1 - |m_{\text{plane}}|^2}$, and the inclination follows. The figure's second line @DM is computed exactly this way from the angles of @AG, and the claims check the conclusion.
:::
