---
title: Separation (separando)
---
**Statement.** If $AB : BE = CD : DF$ (where $E$ lies on $AB$ and $F$ on $CD$), then
$$AE : EB = CF : FD.$$
With $a = AE$, $b = EB$, $c = CF$, $d = FD$: $(a + b) : b = (c + d) : d \Rightarrow a : b = c : d$. For fractions, subtract 1 from both sides.

**Proof.** Take equimultiples of the four parts, @GH $= m \cdot AE$, @HK $= m \cdot EB$, @LM $= m \cdot CF$, @MN $= m \cdot FD$, and other multiples @KO $= n \cdot EB$, @NP $= n \cdot FD$. Then
- @GK $= m \cdot AB$ and @LN $= m \cdot CD$ ([[5.1]]);
- @HO $= (m + n) \cdot EB$ and @MP $= (m + n) \cdot FD$ ([[5.2]]).

By the hypothesis and [[5.def.5]], $GK \gtrless HO$ and $LN \gtrless MP$ agree. Subtract the common part @HK from $GK$ and $HO$, and @MN from $LN$ and $MP$: the comparisons $GH \gtrless KO$ and $LM \gtrless NP$ agree too. These are $m \cdot AE \gtrless n \cdot EB$ and $m \cdot CF \gtrless n \cdot FD$, for arbitrary $m, n$, so $AE : EB = CF : FD$.

The trick is to choose the multiples of the whole and of the part so that the common piece $m \cdot EB$ can be cancelled. The figure lays out all four rows: move $m$ and $n$ and watch the two comparisons stay alike.
