---
title: Ex aequali, the order lemma
---
**Statement.** Let $a : b = d : e$ and $b : c = e : f$. Then
$$a > c \iff d > f, \qquad a = c \iff d = f, \qquad a < c \iff d < f.$$

**Proof.** Suppose @A $>$ @C. By [[5.8]], $A : B > C : B$. Now $A : B = D : E$ by hypothesis, and $C : B = F : E$ by inverting the second hypothesis ([[5.7]], porism). So $D : E > F : E$ ([[5.13]]), and therefore $D > F$ ([[5.10]]). The cases of equality and "less" are the same.

This lemma is what [[5.22]] needs. It is the ex aequali rule in a weak form: it compares $a$ with $c$ and $d$ with $f$, without yet saying $a : c = d : f$. In the figure, $D, E, F$ are the same multiple $s$ of $A, B, C$ (the most general way to satisfy both hypotheses); the comparisons of @A with @C and of @D with @F always agree.
