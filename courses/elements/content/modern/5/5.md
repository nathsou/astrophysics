---
title: Subtracting multiples
---
**Statement.** If $AB = m \cdot CD$ and the part $AE = m \cdot CF$, then the remainders satisfy $EB = m \cdot FD$:
$$ma - mb = m\,(a - b) \qquad (a > b).$$

**Proof.** Construct @CG with $EB = m \cdot CG$, laid off beyond @C. By [[5.1]], $AE + EB = m\,(CF + CG)$, that is, $AB = m \cdot GF$. But also $AB = m \cdot CD$. Equal multiples of two magnitudes are equal only if the magnitudes are equal, so $GF = CD$. Subtract @CF from both: $GC = FD$, and therefore $EB = m \cdot GC = m \cdot FD$.

:::gap Dividing a magnitude into equal parts
The construction asks for @CG such that a given magnitude @EB is $m$ times it: that is, it divides @EB into $m$ equal parts. For straight lines this is possible ([[6.9]]), but for magnitudes in general Book V has no such operation, and Euclid assumes it. The step "$m\,x = m\,y \Rightarrow x = y$" is also used without proof; it follows from trichotomy, since $x < y$ would give $mx < my$.
:::
