---
title: Equimultiples keep a proportion
---
**Statement.** If $a : b = c : d$, then for all whole numbers $m, n$,
$$ma : nb = mc : nd.$$
In the figure @E, @F are $m\,A$, $m\,C$, and @G, @H are $n\,B$, $n\,D$; the claim is $E : G = F : H$.

**Proof.** Apply the test of [[5.def.5]] to the new ratio. Take any multiples $K = pE$, $L = pF$ and $M = qG$, $N = qH$. By [[5.3]], $K = (pm)A$ and $L = (pm)C$ are equimultiples of $A$ and $C$, and $M = (qn)B$, $N = (qn)D$ are equimultiples of $B$ and $D$. Since $A : B = C : D$, the comparisons $K \gtrless M$ and $L \gtrless N$ come out alike. That holds for every $p, q$, so $E : G = F : H$.

The proof is a pattern the whole book repeats: to show a proportion, take arbitrary equimultiples, reduce them to equimultiples of the original terms, and apply the hypothesis. The figure checks it with sliders for all four multipliers.

:::code Why this is not trivial
For fractions, $\frac{ma}{nb} = \frac mn \cdot \frac ab$, so the statement is obvious. But Euclid has no product of a ratio with a fraction. All he can do is test the new ratio against every fraction $q/p$, and the test against $q/p$ reduces to the test of the old ratio against $qn/pm$.
:::
