---
title: The extremes exceed the means
---
**Statement.** If $a : b = c : d$ with $a$ the greatest and $d$ the least of the four, then
$$a + d > b + c.$$
In Heath's letters, $AB : CD = E : F$ gives $AB + F > CD + E$.

**Proof.** Cut off @AG $= E$ from @AB and @CH $= F$ from @CD. Then $AB : CD = AG : CH$, so the remainders are in the same ratio: $GB : HD = AB : CD$ ([[5.19]]). Since $AB > CD$, [[5.14]] gives $GB > HD$. Now add $AG + F$ to $GB$ and $CH + E$ to $HD$; these additions are equal, since $AG = E$ and $CH = F$. So $AB + F > CD + E$.

For numbers: with $a/b = c/d = r > 1$, $(a + d) - (b + c) = (r - 1)(b - d) > 0$. The special case $a : b = b : d$ says that the arithmetic mean of two magnitudes exceeds their geometric mean: $a + d > 2b$ when $b^2 = ad$ and $a \ne d$. The figure reports both sums.
