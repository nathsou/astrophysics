---
title: Multiple
---
**Definition.** $b$ is a *multiple* of $a$ when $a$ measures $b$: $b = na$ for a whole number $n$. The multiple $na$ is $a + a + \dots + a$ ($n$ times), which needs nothing but addition of magnitudes of one kind.

This is the only way whole numbers enter Book V. Euclid never multiplies two magnitudes. He multiplies a magnitude by a count, and the phrase "the same multiple" (*equimultiples*) means the same count $n$ applied to different magnitudes: $na$ and $nc$.
