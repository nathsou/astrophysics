---
title: Same ratio is transitive
---
**Statement.** If $a : b = c : d$ and $c : d = e : f$, then $a : b = e : f$.

**Proof.** Take equimultiples @G, @H, @K of @A, @C, @E and equimultiples @L, @M, @N of @B, @D, @F. The first proportion says that $G \gtrless L$ and $H \gtrless M$ come out alike; the second says that $H \gtrless M$ and $K \gtrless N$ come out alike. So $G \gtrless L$ and $K \gtrless N$ come out alike, for every choice of multiples. That is $a : b = e : f$.

In the figure the three comparisons are shown together; change $m$ and $n$ to see them agree.

"Same ratio" is defined by a condition, not as equality of objects, so its transitivity is a theorem. Together with reflexivity and symmetry, which are immediate from [[5.def.5]], it makes "same ratio" an equivalence relation. In modern terms a ratio *is* its equivalence class, and [[5.def.5]] is the definition of equality on the quotient.
