---
title: Multiplication distributes over sums
---
**Statement.** For magnitudes $e_1, \dots, e_k$ and a whole number $m$,
$$m e_1 + m e_2 + \dots + m e_k = m\,(e_1 + e_2 + \dots + e_k).$$
With Heath's letters, if $AB = m\,E$ and $CD = m\,F$, then $AB + CD = m\,(E + F)$.

**Proof.** Cut @AB into $m$ pieces equal to @E and @CD into $m$ pieces equal to @F (@AG, @GB and @CH, @HD in the figure, where $m = 2$). Pair the pieces off: each pair $AG + CH$, $GB + HD$ equals $E + F$, and there are $m$ pairs. So $AB + CD$ consists of $m$ copies of $E + F$.

Euclid's proof is a counting argument: "as many as there are in the one, so many in the other." Whole numbers appear only as counts of pieces, never as magnitudes.

:::code In code
Multiples are repeated addition, `times(m, e) = e + e + … + e`. The proposition is the law `times(m, a) + times(m, b) == times(m, a + b)`, which holds because addition of magnitudes is commutative and associative, and those properties are used here without comment.
:::
