---
title: Multiples of multiples
---
**Statement.** If $a = m b$ and $c = m d$, and $e = n a$, $g = n c$, then $e$ and $g$ are the same multiple of $b$ and $d$:
$$n\,(m b) = (nm)\, b.$$
In Heath's letters: @A $= m\,B$, @C $= m\,D$, and @EF, @GH are equimultiples of @A, @C; then @EF and @GH are equimultiples of @B and @D.

**Proof.** Cut @EF into $n$ pieces equal to @A (@EK, @KF when $n = 2$) and @GH into $n$ pieces equal to @C (@GL, @LH). Each piece of @EF is $m\,B$ and the matching piece of @GH is $m\,D$. By [[5.2]], applied $n - 1$ times, the sums @EF and @GH are the same multiple of @B and @D.

Euclid does not compute the multiple, $nm$. He only needs that it is the *same* for both, which is all [[5.def.5]] ever asks about multiples. This is the associativity of multiplication by integers, and [[5.4]] uses it immediately.
