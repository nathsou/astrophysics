---
title: Subtracting multiples of the same
---
**Statement.** If $AB = m\,E$, $CD = m\,F$ and the parts $AG = n\,E$, $CH = n\,F$ with $n < m$, then the remainders are $GB = (m - n)\,E$ and $HD = (m - n)\,F$: equal to $E$, $F$ when $m - n = 1$, and equimultiples of them otherwise.
$$ma - na = (m - n)\,a.$$

**Proof** (the case $GB = E$, which Heath draws). Lay off @CK equal to @F beyond @C. By [[5.2]], $KH = CK + CH = (n + 1)F$, and $AB = GB + AG = (n + 1)E$. But $AB = mE$ and $CD = mF$, so $KH$ and $CD$ are the same multiple of $F$ and hence equal. Subtracting @CH from both leaves $KC = HD$, that is, $HD = F$. The case of a larger remainder is the same with $CK = (m - n)F$.

With [[5.1]]–[[5.5]], this completes the rules for integer multiples: $m(a \pm b) = ma \pm mb$, $(m \pm n)a = ma \pm na$ and $n(ma) = (nm)a$. The slider $r$ in the figure is $m - n$.
