---
title: Diameter perpendicular to a chord
---
**Statement.** Let a line through the centre @E meet a chord @AB (not a diameter) at @F. Then it bisects @AB if and only if it is perpendicular to @AB.

**Proof.**
- *Bisects ⇒ perpendicular.* If $AF = FB$, the triangles $AFE$ and $BFE$ have three sides equal ($EA = EB$ are radii, $FE$ is common), so they are congruent ([[1.8]]). The adjacent angles at @F are equal, hence right ([[1.def.10]]).
- *Perpendicular ⇒ bisects.* If the angles at @F are right, the triangles $EAF$ and $EBF$ have $\angle EAF = \angle EBF$ ([[1.5]]), equal right angles at @F, and the common side $EF$. By AAS ([[1.26]]) they are congruent, so $AF = FB$.

In coordinates: the foot of the perpendicular from the centre to a chord is the chord's midpoint. This fact is used again and again, in [[3.4]], [[3.14]], [[3.35]] and [[3.36]].

**In the figure.** Drag @A and @B. The line @CD is always the diameter through the midpoint @F, and the angle at @F stays right.
