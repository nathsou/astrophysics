---
title: Equal arcs subtend equal angles
---
**Statement.** In equal circles, angles standing on equal arcs are equal, whether at the centres ($\angle BGC = \angle EHF$) or at the circumferences ($\angle BAC = \angle EDF$).

**Proof.** This is the converse of [[3.26]], proved by contradiction. If $\angle BGC > \angle EHF$, copy $\angle EHF$ at @G inside $\angle BGC$ ([[1.23]]), making $\angle BGK = \angle EHF$. By [[3.26]] the arc $BK$ equals the arc $EF$, which equals the arc $BC$. But $BK$ is part of $BC$. So the central angles are equal, and the inscribed angles are their halves ([[3.20]]).

The pattern "prove the direct statement, then get the converse by contradiction using trichotomy" recurs throughout the Elements (compare [[1.6]] with [[1.5]], or [[1.19]] with [[1.18]]). It is sound because the map from angles to arcs is strictly monotonic: a larger angle stands on a larger arc.

**In the figure.** @F is placed so that the arcs @arc(BC) and @arc(EF) are equal. Drag the points: the angles at the centres, and those at the circumferences, stay equal.
