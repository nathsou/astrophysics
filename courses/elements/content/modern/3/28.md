---
title: Equal chords cut off equal arcs
---
**Statement.** In equal circles, equal chords $AB = DE$ cut off equal arcs: the greater arc $ACB$ equals the greater arc $DFE$, and the less $AGB$ equals the less $DHE$.

**Proof.** With centres @K and @L, the triangles $AKB$ and $DLE$ have three sides equal, so $\angle AKB = \angle DLE$ ([[1.8]]). Equal central angles stand on equal arcs ([[3.26]]), so the arc $AGB$ equals the arc $DHE$. Subtracting from equal whole circles, the greater arcs are equal too.

A chord does not determine *one* arc but two, and the proposition is careful to pair them: the less with the less. In modern terms, a chord of length $c$ in a circle of radius $r$ subtends the central angle $\theta = 2\arcsin\frac{c}{2r}$ and its complement $2\pi - \theta$.

**In the figure.** @E is placed on the second circle at distance $AB$ from @D, so the chords are equal. Drag @A, @B and @D: both pairs of arcs stay equal.
