---
title: Intersecting chords
---
**Statement.** If two chords @AC and @BD of a circle cross at @E, then
$$AE \cdot EC = DE \cdot EB.$$
For Euclid, both sides are rectangles, and "equal" means equal in area.

**Proof.** If both chords pass through the centre, all four segments are radii and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, let @F be the centre and @G the foot of the perpendicular from @F to @AC. The perpendicular bisects the chord ([[3.3]]), so @AC is cut equally at @G and unequally at @E. By [[2.5]],
$$AE \cdot EC + EG^2 = GC^2.$$
Add $GF^2$ to both sides and use Pythagoras twice ([[1.47]]): $EG^2 + GF^2 = FE^2$ and $GC^2 + GF^2 = FC^2$. So
$$AE \cdot EC + FE^2 = FC^2 = r^2.$$
The same reasoning with @H on the other chord gives $DE \cdot EB + FE^2 = r^2$. Subtract $FE^2$.

So the product does not depend on the chord at all:
$$AE \cdot EC = r^2 - FE^2 \quad\text{for every chord through } E.$$

**In the figure.** Drag @E, @A or @B. The two products change together, and both equal $r^2 - FE^2$: they depend only on how far @E is from the centre.

:::code The power of a point
Define, for a point $P$ and a circle with centre $O$ and radius $r$,
$$\operatorname{pow}(P) = |PO|^2 - r^2.$$
If a line through $P$ with unit direction $u$ meets the circle at $P + t_1u$ and $P + t_2u$, then $t_1, t_2$ are the roots of $|P + tu - O|^2 = r^2$, that is, of
$$t^2 + 2t\,u\cdot(P - O) + \operatorname{pow}(P) = 0.$$
By Vieta, $t_1t_2 = \operatorname{pow}(P)$, whatever the direction $u$. Inside the circle the roots have opposite signs, and $|t_1||t_2| = r^2 - |PO|^2$: this proposition. Outside they have the same sign, and the product is $|PO|^2 - r^2$: [[3.36]]. A tangent is a double root, $t_1 = t_2$, so the product is the square of the tangent. One quadratic, three propositions.
:::

:::leads Where it leads
The name *power of a point* is Jakob Steiner's (1826). The set of points with equal power with respect to two circles is a line, the radical axis, which appeared in [[3.10]]'s algebra. The proposition also gives a quick construction of the geometric mean: in a semicircle on a diameter $a + b$, the perpendicular at the division point has length $\sqrt{ab}$ ([[2.14]], [[6.13]]).
:::
