---
title: Equal chords, equal distances
---
**Statement.** In a circle with centre @E, the chords @AB and @CD are equal if and only if they are equally distant from the centre, that is, iff the perpendiculars @EF and @EG are equal.

**Proof.** The perpendicular from the centre bisects each chord ([[3.3]]), so $AB = 2AF$ and $CD = 2CG$. The angles at @F and @G are right, so by Pythagoras ([[1.47]])
$$AF^2 + EF^2 = EA^2 = EC^2 = CG^2 + EG^2.$$
If $AF = CG$, subtracting gives $EF^2 = EG^2$, so $EF = EG$. If $EF = EG$, subtracting gives $AF = CG$ and so $AB = CD$.

In one formula, a chord at distance $d$ from the centre has length $2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$. That function is injective on $0 \le d \le r$, which is the whole proposition. Euclid's "squares on" are areas, but the subtraction works just as well with numbers.

**In the figure.** Drag @A, @B or @C. @D follows so that $CD = AB$ always, and the perpendiculars stay equal.
