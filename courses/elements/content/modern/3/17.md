---
title: Tangent from a point
---
**Statement.** Given a point @A outside the circle $BCD$ with centre @E, construct a line from @A that touches the circle.

**Construction.** Join @EA; it cuts the circle at @D. Draw the circle $AFG$ with centre @E through @A. At @D erect the perpendicular to @EA, meeting the big circle at @F. Join @EF, cutting the small circle at @B. Then @AB is tangent.

**Proof.** The triangles $AEB$ and $FED$ have $EA = EF$ and $EB = ED$ (radii of the two circles) and share the angle at @E. By SAS ([[1.4]]) they are congruent, so $\angle EBA = \angle EDF$, which is right. A line perpendicular to a radius at its end is tangent ([[3.16]], porism).

The construction is a rotation in disguise: turning the right triangle $EDF$ about @E, so that @D goes to @B, carries @F to @A.

:::code The modern recipe
Today one usually finds the tangent points as the intersections of the circle with the circle on @EA as diameter: by [[3.31]] the angle at each such point is right. Euclid could not use that here, because [[3.31]] comes later. Both give the same two tangents; Euclid draws one, and the other is its mirror image in @EA.
:::

**In the figure.** Drag @A. The angle $\angle EBA$ stays right, and $DF = AB$.
