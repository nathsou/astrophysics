---
title: Internal contact lies on the line of centres
---
**Statement.** If two circles touch internally at @A, the line through their centres @F and @G, extended, passes through @A.

**Proof.** Suppose the line @FG, extended beyond @G, met the outer circle at a point @H other than @A. In the triangle $AGF$, $AG + GF > FA = FH$ ([[1.20]]). Subtract $FG$: $AG > GH$. But $AG = GD$, where @D is where the line meets the inner circle, so $GD > GH$: the inner circle reaches beyond the outer one, which is impossible for circles that touch internally.

**In the figure.** The figure draws the argument rather than the impossible configuration. The circle $ADE$ is drawn with centre @G through @A. Drag @G off the line @FA and @D falls outside the outer circle: the two circles cut instead of touching. Only when @G is on the line @FA does @D reach @H, and then the circles touch at @A.

In modern terms: the circles touch internally exactly when $FG = FA - GA$, the equality case of the triangle inequality, which holds when $F$, $G$ and $A$ are collinear.

:::gap Only one case
Euclid supposes that the line of centres, extended beyond @G, misses @A. He does not discuss the line extended the other way, beyond @F, nor explain why the circle $ADE$ lies inside $ABC$ in the first place, which is what "touching internally" is taken to mean.
:::
