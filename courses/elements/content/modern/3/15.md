---
title: The diameter is the longest chord
---
**Statement.** Of the chords of a circle, the diameter @AD is the longest, and a chord nearer to the centre is longer than one farther away: if $EH < EK$ then $BC > FG$.

**Proof.** Mark @L on @EK with $EL = EH$ and draw the chord @MN through @L perpendicular to @EK. It is at the same distance from the centre as @BC, so $MN = BC$ ([[3.14]]).
- $AD = AE + ED = ME + EN > MN$ by the triangle inequality ([[1.20]]), so $AD > BC$.
- The triangles $MEN$ and $FEG$ have two sides equal (all radii), and the angle at @E is greater in $MEN$. By the hinge theorem ([[1.24]]), $MN > FG$.

So $AD > BC > FG$. Euclid's statement says "nearer to the diameter", but since @BC and @FG need not be parallel to @AD, "nearer to the centre" is what is meant, as the text itself shows.

With the formula of [[3.14]], chord length $2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$ is strictly decreasing in the distance $d$, and $d = 0$ gives the diameter.

**In the figure.** Drag the four points on the circle (the view refuses a drag that would bring @FG nearer than @BC). $MN$ always equals $BC$.
