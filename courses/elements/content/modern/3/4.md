---
title: Two chords do not bisect each other
---
**Statement.** If two chords @AC and @BD of a circle cross at @E, and neither passes through the centre, they do not bisect each other.

**Proof.** Suppose $AE = EC$ and $BE = ED$. Let @F be the centre. Since @FE bisects @AC, it is perpendicular to it ([[3.3]]), so $\angle FEA$ is right. For the same reason $\angle FEB$ is right. But one of these angles is part of the other, so they cannot be equal.

Put differently, the only chords through a point $E$ that are bisected at $E$ are perpendicular to $FE$, and there is only one line through $E$ perpendicular to $FE$. So at most one chord through $E$ is bisected there, unless $E$ is the centre, where every chord is.

**In the figure.** Drag the four points. The readouts show the ratios $AE : EC$ and $BE : ED$; you can make either one equal to 1, but not both at once.

:::leads Where it leads
A quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other is a parallelogram. So the proposition says that the only parallelograms inscribed in a circle are those whose diagonals are diameters, that is, rectangles.
:::
