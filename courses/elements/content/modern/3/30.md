---
title: Bisecting an arc
---
**Statement.** Bisect a given arc $ADB$.

**Construction.** Join @AB and bisect it at @C. Draw the perpendicular to @AB at @C, meeting the arc at @D. Then @D is the midpoint of the arc.

**Proof.** The triangles $ACD$ and $BCD$ are congruent by SAS ([[1.4]]), so $AD = DB$. Equal chords cut off equal arcs ([[3.28]]), and since the two arcs $AD$ and $DB$ are both less than a semicircle, they are the corresponding (lesser) arcs. So $\text{arc } AD = \text{arc } DB$.

The side condition matters: a chord cuts off two arcs, and [[3.28]] pairs the less with the less. Here each half of the given arc is less than a semicircle, because the whole given arc is less than a full circle.

Repeating the construction bisects an arc into $2^n$ equal parts. Trisection is another matter: there is no general construction with straightedge and compass, as Wantzel proved in 1837.

**In the figure.** Drag @A and @B along the circle. Only the given arc is drawn; @D stays at its midpoint.
