---
title: Cutting off a segment admitting a given angle
---
**Statement.** From a given circle, cut off a segment admitting a given angle @D.

**Construction.** Draw the tangent @EF at any point @B of the circle ([[3.17]], or the porism of [[3.16]]). At @B construct $\angle FBC = \angle D$ ([[1.23]]), with @C on the circle. The segment $BAC$ on the other side of @BC admits $\angle D$.

**Proof.** By the tangent–chord theorem ([[3.32]]), $\angle FBC$ equals the angle in the alternate segment $BAC$. And $\angle FBC = \angle D$.

This is [[3.33]] with the circle given instead of the chord. There, the circle was built on the chord; here the chord is found in the circle. Every chord of the circle of length $2r\sin D$ would do.

**In the figure.** The slider sets the given angle. Drag @B round the circle and @A along the segment: $\angle BAC$ stays equal to $\angle D$.
