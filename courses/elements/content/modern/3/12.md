---
title: External contact lies on the line of centres
---
**Statement.** If two circles touch externally at @A, the segment @FG joining their centres passes through @A.

**Proof.** Suppose @FG missed @A, crossing the circles at @C and @D. Then $FC = FA$ and $GD = GA$, so $FA + AG = FC + GD$. As the circles touch externally they do not overlap, so $FC + GD \le FG$. That gives $FA + AG \le FG$, but the triangle inequality ([[1.20]]) says $FG < FA + AG$. Contradiction.

In modern terms: the circles touch externally exactly when $FG = r_1 + r_2$, the equality case of the triangle inequality, which forces $A$ onto the segment $FG$.

**In the figure.** The circle $ADE$ is drawn about @G through @A. Drag @G: unless it lies on the line @FA, $FG < FA + AG$, and the readouts show it. Then @C and @D have crossed over, and the circles overlap instead of touching.
