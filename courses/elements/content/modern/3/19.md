---
title: The centre lies on the normal
---
**Statement.** If a line @DE touches a circle at @C, the perpendicular @CA to @DE at @C passes through the centre.

**Proof.** Suppose the centre were a point @F off @CA. Then @FC is the radius to the point of contact, so it is perpendicular to @DE ([[3.18]]), and $\angle FCE$ is right. But $\angle ACE$ is right too, and one is part of the other. Contradiction.

This is [[3.18]] read backwards: there is only one perpendicular to @DE at @C ([[1.11]] and uniqueness), and the radius is one, so the perpendicular contains the centre. It is used in [[3.32]] to make the perpendicular to a tangent a diameter.

**In the figure.** Drag @F: $\angle FCE$ is right only when @F is on @CA.
