---
title: Circles touch in at most one point
---
**Statement.** Two circles touch at most at one point, whether internally or externally.

**Proof.**
- *Internally.* Suppose the circle $EBFD$ touched the circle $ABDC$ internally at two points @B and @D. By [[3.11]] the line of centres @G, @H passes through both points of contact, so @B, @G, @H, @D are on one line. Since @G is the centre of $ABDC$, $BG = GD$, so $BH > HD$. But @H is the centre of $EBFD$, so $BH = HD$. Contradiction.
- *Externally.* Suppose the circle $ACK$ touched $ABDC$ externally at @A and @C. The chord @AC lies inside each circle ([[3.2]]). But circles that touch externally have no inside points in common. Contradiction.

With [[3.11]] and [[3.12]] this settles what contact looks like: one point, on the line of centres. The internal case leans on [[3.11]] harder than it can bear: that proposition says the line of centres passes through *a* point of contact, and here it is applied to two at once.

**In the figure.** Inside: the dashed oval touches the circle at @B and @D, and it cannot be a circle. Slide @H along @BD: $BH > HD$ always. Outside: a circle through @A and @C (dashed) must cross $ABDC$, because both contain the chord @AC.
