---
title: Tangent line
---
A straight line **touches** a circle (is tangent to it) if it meets the circle but, when extended, does not cut it: it has a point in common with the circle and never passes inside.

Modern equivalent: a line is tangent to a circle when they have exactly one common point. Euclid proves the useful characterization later. The line through a point of the circle perpendicular to the radius there is tangent ([[3.16]], porism), and conversely the radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent ([[3.18]]). In coordinates: the line is tangent when its distance from the centre equals the radius.
