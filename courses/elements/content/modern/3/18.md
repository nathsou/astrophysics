---
title: The radius is perpendicular to the tangent
---
**Statement.** If a line @DE touches a circle at @C, the radius @FC is perpendicular to @DE.

**Proof.** Suppose not. Drop the perpendicular @FG from the centre to @DE, with @G different from @C. In the triangle $FGC$ the angle at @G is right, so the angle at @C is acute ([[1.17]]), and the side opposite the larger angle is larger ([[1.19]]): $FC > FG$. But $FC = FB$, where @B is the point where @FG meets the circle, so $FB > FG$, and @G would be inside the circle. A point of the tangent inside the circle contradicts the definition of touching ([[3.def.2]]).

With the porism of [[3.16]], this gives the modern characterization: a line through a point of a circle is tangent exactly when it is perpendicular to the radius there.

**In the figure.** Drag @G along the tangent: $FG > FB$ always, since every point of the tangent except @C is outside the circle.
