---
title: Equal circles
---
Two circles are **equal** when their radii are equal (equivalently, their diameters). In modern terms: circles are determined up to congruence by their radius, so "equal" here means *congruent*, not "equal in area".

One might expect this to be proved, by placing one circle on the other so that the centres coincide. Euclid instead takes it as the meaning of the word. From [[3.26]] to [[3.29]] "in equal circles" is the hypothesis, and it lets him carry angles, arcs and chords from one circle to another.
