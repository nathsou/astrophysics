---
title: Circles that cut have different centres
---
**Statement.** Two circles that cut one another do not have the same centre.

**Proof.** Suppose the circles $ABC$ and $CDG$ meet at @B and @C and have a common centre @E. Draw @EC, and any other line from @E, meeting the first circle at @F and the second at @G. As @E is the centre of the first circle, $EC = EF$; as it is the centre of the second, $EC = EG$. So $EF = EG$, although @F lies strictly between @E and @G.

The modern version is one line: concentric circles are $\{|x - e| = r_1\}$ and $\{|x - e| = r_2\}$. If they share a point then $r_1 = r_2$ and they are the same circle, so they do not *cut*.

:::gap What makes EF shorter than EG?
The proof needs the line from @E to meet the two circles at different points, one beyond the other. That holds only if the circles are not the same circle, which is part of what "cut one another" means. Euclid takes the order of @F and @G from the figure.
:::

**In the figure.** Drag @E anywhere inside both circles, and @G around the second circle. $EF$ and $EG$ never agree, so @E is never a common centre.
