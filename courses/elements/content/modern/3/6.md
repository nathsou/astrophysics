---
title: Circles that touch have different centres
---
**Statement.** Two circles that touch one another do not have the same centre.

**Proof.** This is the argument of [[3.5]] again. If the circles $ABC$ and $CDE$ touch at @C and had a common centre @F, then for any line @FEB from the centre, $FE = FC = FB$, although @E lies between @F and @B.

Euclid's figure shows internal contact, which is the only case that needs an argument. Two circles touching externally obviously cannot share a centre, since each centre is inside its own circle and outside the other.

**In the figure.** Drag @F and @B. The readouts compare $FC$, $FE$ and $FB$: they are never all equal.
