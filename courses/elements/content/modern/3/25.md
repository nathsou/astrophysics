---
title: Completing a circle from a segment
---
**Statement.** Given a segment $ABC$ of a circle (an arc and its chord), construct the whole circle.

**Construction.** Bisect the chord @AC at @D and erect the perpendicular @DB to the arc. Construct $\angle BAE = \angle ABD$ at @A ([[1.23]]), with @E on the line @DB. Then @E is the centre.

**Proof.** In the triangle $ABE$ the base angles at @A and @B are equal, so $EA = EB$ ([[1.6]]). The triangles $ADE$ and $CDE$ are congruent by SAS ([[1.4]]), so $EA = EC$. The three distances are equal, and the circle with centre @E through @A passes through @B and @C.

Three cases, depending on the shape of the segment:
- $\angle ABD > \angle BAD$: @E lies beyond @D, outside the segment, which is less than a semicircle (Heath's figure);
- $\angle ABD = \angle BAD$: @E is @D, and the segment is a semicircle;
- $\angle ABD < \angle BAD$: @E lies between @D and @B, and the segment is greater than a semicircle.

In modern terms, @E is the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors of @AB and @AC, the circumcentre of the triangle $ABC$. Euclid gets the perpendicular bisector of @AB by copying an angle.

**In the figure.** Drag @B up and down the perpendicular: the readout names the case, and @E crosses @D when the segment becomes a semicircle. Drag @A and @C to change the chord.
