---
title: Touching circles
---
Two circles **touch** when they meet but do not cut one another: they share a point, and neither crosses into the other's inside or outside there. They touch *internally* when one lies inside the other, and *externally* otherwise.

With centres $O_1, O_2$ and radii $r_1, r_2$, the circles touch externally when $O_1O_2 = r_1 + r_2$, and internally when $O_1O_2 = |r_1 - r_2| > 0$. Euclid gets there by the propositions: the line of centres passes through the point of contact ([[3.11]], [[3.12]]), and there is only one such point ([[3.13]]).
