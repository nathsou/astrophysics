---
title: Three equal lines determine the centre
---
**Statement.** If from a point @D inside a circle more than two equal segments can be drawn to the circle, say $DA = DB = DC$, then @D is the centre.

**Proof.** Bisect @AB at @E and @BC at @F. Since $DA = DB$, the triangles $AED$ and $BED$ are congruent by SSS ([[1.8]]), so @DE is perpendicular to @AB. By the porism of [[3.1]] the centre lies on the line @GK through @E and @D. For the same reason it lies on the line @HL through @F and @D. Two distinct lines meet in at most one point, and here they meet at @D. So @D is the centre.

In modern terms: the set of points equidistant from $A$ and $B$ is the perpendicular bisector of $AB$. A point equidistant from three points of a circle lies on two such bisectors, and they meet only at the circumcentre.

:::gap Are the two lines really distinct?
The proof needs @GK and @HL to be different lines. If @A, @B, @C are distinct points of a circle, the chords @AB and @BC are not parallel, so neither are their perpendicular bisectors. Euclid does not say this; it follows from [[3.2]] (three points of a circle are never collinear).
:::

There is also a shorter route through [[3.7]]: from a point that is not the centre, at most two equal lines fall on the circle.
