---
title: Angles in the same segment are equal
---
**Statement.** If @A and @E lie on the same arc cut off by the chord $BD$, then $\angle BAD = \angle BED$.

**Proof.** Let @F be the centre. Both angles stand on the arc @arc(BCD), so each is half the central angle $\angle BFD$ ([[3.20]]). Halves of the same thing are equal.

This is the proposition that makes [[3.def.8]] (the angle *in* a segment) meaningful: the angle does not depend on which point of the arc you choose.

**In the figure.** Drag @A and @E along the arc: both angles stay equal, and each is half of the arc @arc(BCD) as measured at the centre. Drag @B and @D to change the segment. The view refuses to let @A or @E leave the segment; on the other arc the angle would be the supplement.

:::gap The case Euclid's proof does not cover
Euclid draws a segment greater than a semicircle. Then $\angle BFD$ is less than two right angles, and [[3.20]] applies as proved. If the segment is less than a semicircle, the angle at the centre on the arc @arc(BCD) is reflex, which Euclid's [[3.20]] does not handle. The standard repair joins @AE and splits the figure into triangles whose angles lie in a segment greater than a semicircle.
:::
