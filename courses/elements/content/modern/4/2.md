---
title: Inscribing a triangle of given shape
---
**Statement.** In a given circle, inscribe a triangle with the same angles as a given triangle @triangle(DEF).

**Construction.** Draw the tangent @GH at any point @A of the circle ([[3.16]]). At @A make $\angle HAC = \angle DEF$ and $\angle GAB = \angle DFE$ ([[1.23]]), with @B and @C on the circle, and join @BC.

**Proof.** The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment ([[3.32]]). So $\angle ABC = \angle HAC = \angle DEF$, and likewise $\angle ACB = \angle GAB = \angle DFE$. The third angles are then equal as well, because the angles of each triangle add up to two right angles ([[1.32]]).

**In the figure.** Drag the vertices of @triangle(DEF): the inscribed triangle changes shape with it. Drag @A around the circle: the triangle turns, but its angles do not change.

:::leads Where it leads
A triangle is fixed up to similarity by its angles, and a circle fixes the size. So this construction produces the unique triangle (up to rotation and reflection) with the given shape and circumcircle. [[4.11]] uses it to inscribe a golden triangle, the first step towards the pentagon.
:::
