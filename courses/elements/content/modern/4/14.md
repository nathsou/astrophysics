---
title: The circle circumscribed about a regular pentagon
---
**Statement.** About a given regular pentagon, circumscribe a circle.

**Construction.** Bisect the angles at @C and @D by @CF and @DF, meeting at @F. The circle with centre @F through @A is the required circle.

**Proof.** As in [[4.13]], @FB, @FA and @FE bisect the angles at @B, @A and @E. The angles $FCD$ and $CDF$ are halves of equal angles, so they are equal and $FC = FD$ ([[1.6]]). In the same way all five lines from @F to the vertices are equal.

This is [[4.5]] for the pentagon, proved with angle bisectors instead of perpendicular bisectors. In a regular polygon the two centres coincide: the incentre of [[4.13]] and the circumcentre found here are the same point.

**In the figure.** Drag @C or @D.
