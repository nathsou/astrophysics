---
title: Fitting a chord of given length
---
**Statement.** Given a circle and a length $D$ not greater than its diameter, there is a chord of the circle equal to $D$.

**Construction.** Draw a diameter @BC. If it equals @D, it is the chord. Otherwise cut off @CE equal to @D ([[1.3]]), draw the circle with centre @C through @E, and let it meet the given circle at @A. Then @CA is a radius of the second circle, so $CA = CE = D$.

**In the figure.** Change $D$ with the slider, or drag @B and @C. The figure refuses a value of $D$ larger than the diameter @BC, and the hypothesis is exactly what makes the two circles meet.

:::gap Do the circles meet?
As in [[1.1]], Euclid does not prove that the circle @circle(EAF) meets the circle $ABC$. It does: the second circle passes through @E, which is inside the first circle, and it contains points outside it, since its radius $CE$ is positive and @C is on the first circle. By the circle–circle continuity principle the two circles meet. The condition $D \le BC$ is what keeps @E inside (or on) the given circle.
:::
