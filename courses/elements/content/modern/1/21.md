---
title: A triangle inside a triangle
---
**Statement.** If $D$ is inside triangle $ABC$, then
$$BD + DC < BA + AC \qquad\text{and}\qquad \angle BDC > \angle BAC.$$

**Proof (Euclid's).** Produce @BD to meet @AC at @E.

*Lengths.* In @triangle(ABE), $BA + AE > BE$ ([[1.20]]); add $EC$: $BA + AC > BE + EC$. In @triangle(CED), $CE + ED > CD$; add $DB$: $BE + EC > BD + DC$. Chain the two inequalities.

*Angles.* $\angle BDC$ is exterior to @triangle(CDE), so $\angle BDC > \angle CED$ ([[1.16]]). And $\angle CEB$ is exterior to @triangle(ABE), so $\angle CEB > \angle BAC$. Chain them.

So as the apex moves *inward* over the same base, the two sides shrink and the angle opens. The lengths and the angle move in opposite directions.

:::gap
That @BD produced meets @AC between @A and @C is assumed: it is the crossbar theorem, a consequence of Pasch's axiom. "Inside the triangle" is also used without definition.
:::

**In the figure.** Drag @D around inside the triangle; dragging it outside is refused, since the proposition says nothing then.
