---
title: Cutting off a shorter length
---
**Statement.** Given segments $AB$ and $C$ with $C < AB$, there is a point $E$ on $AB$ with $AE = C$.

**Construction.** Copy @C to @A as @AD ([[1.2]]). Draw the circle @circle(DEF) with centre @A through @D ([[1.post.3]]). It meets @AB at @E.

**Why it works.** $AE = AD$ as radii ([[1.def.15]]), and $AD = C$, so $AE = C$ ([[1.cn.1]]).

With I.2 and I.3, lengths can be moved and compared freely: "cut off from the greater a part equal to the less" becomes a basic operation, used throughout Book I (in [[1.5]], [[1.6]], [[1.9]], [[1.11]], [[1.18]], [[1.20]], [[1.22]]) and again for magnitudes in Books V and X.

:::gap
Two facts are used without proof: that the circle meets the ray from @A through @B, and that the meeting point @E lies *between* @A and @B because $C < AB$. The first is a continuity fact, the second a betweenness fact. Hilbert makes this proposition an axiom: on any ray from a point a given length can be laid off ("segment construction", axiom III.1).
:::

**In the figure.** The slider changes the length of @C. If it becomes longer than @AB the construction is refused: there is nothing to cut off.
