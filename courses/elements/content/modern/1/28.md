---
title: Corresponding angles give parallels
---
**Statement.** Let a transversal $EF$ meet $AB$ at $G$ and $CD$ at $H$. If either

- the corresponding angles are equal, $\angle EGB = \angle GHD$, or
- the co-interior angles are supplementary, $\angle BGH + \angle GHD = 180^\circ$,

then $AB \parallel CD$.

**Proof.** Both cases reduce to equal alternate angles and [[1.27]].

- $\angle EGB = \angle AGH$ (vertical angles, [[1.15]]), so $\angle AGH = \angle GHD$.
- $\angle AGH + \angle BGH = 180^\circ$ ([[1.13]]). Subtracting $\angle BGH$ from both sums gives $\angle AGH = \angle GHD$.

:::gap Still neutral
Like [[1.27]], this uses no parallel postulate, so it holds in hyperbolic geometry. The three conditions (alternate angles equal, corresponding angles equal, co-interior angles supplementary) are equivalent in any geometry, by vertical angles and [[1.13]]. What neutral geometry cannot prove is the converse: that parallel lines *must* make these angles. That is [[1.29]].
:::
