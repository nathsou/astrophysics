---
title: Converse of angles on a straight line
---
**Statement.** If rays $BC$ and $BD$ lie on opposite sides of a line $AB$ and $\angle ABC + \angle ABD = 2$ right angles, then $C$, $B$, $D$ are collinear.

**Proof (Euclid's), by contradiction.** Suppose not, and let @BE be the continuation of @CB beyond @B. By [[1.13]], $\angle ABC + \angle ABE = 2$ right angles. So $\angle ABC + \angle ABE = \angle ABC + \angle ABD$ (all right angles are equal, [[1.post.4]]), and subtracting $\angle ABC$ gives $\angle ABE = \angle ABD$. But one of the two angles is part of the other, so they cannot be equal ([[1.cn.5]]).

This is the first real use of Postulate 4. "Two right angles" in [[1.13]] means two right angles *at B formed by CD*; to compare them with two right angles formed elsewhere, one needs to know that right angles are all the same size.

:::gap
"Opposite sides" matters: on the same side, the two rays would coincide instead. The proof also uses the uniqueness of the continuation of @CB, which is the unstated half of Postulate 2 ([[1.post.2]]).
:::

**In the figure.** @D glides on the continuation of @CB, so the hypothesis holds; @BE, dashed, is the other ray of the reductio.
