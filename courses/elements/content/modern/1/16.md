---
title: Exterior angle inequality
---
**Statement.** In any triangle $ABC$ with $BC$ produced to $D$, the exterior angle $\angle ACD$ is greater than each of the interior opposite angles $\angle CBA$ and $\angle BAC$.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Let @E be the midpoint of @AC ([[1.10]]). Produce @BE to @F with $EF = BE$ ([[1.3]]) and join @FC.

- $\triangle ABE \cong \triangle CFE$ by SAS ([[1.4]]): $AE = EC$, $BE = EF$, and $\angle AEB = \angle FEC$ are vertical angles ([[1.15]]). So $\angle BAE = \angle ECF$.
- @F lies inside @angle(ACD), so $\angle ACD > \angle ECF = \angle BAC$ ([[1.cn.5]]).

For $\angle ABC$, do the same with the midpoint of @BC, using $\angle BCG$ (the vertical angle of $\angle ACD$).

The proof works by *moving the angle*: the rotation by half a turn about @E sends @A to @C and @B to @F, so it carries the angle at @A onto an angle at @C that fits inside the exterior angle.

:::gap The proof needs lines to be infinite and betweenness to be axiomatic
Two steps are carried by the picture. First, that @F lies inside @angle(ACD). It does, because @F is on the other side of @AC from @B (since $E$ is between $B$ and $F$), and on the same side of @BD as @A; but Euclid has no axioms for "side". Second, that @F exists at all: @BE has to be produced to twice its length. On a sphere, where "lines" are great circles of finite length, both steps can fail. Take a triangle with a vertex at the north pole and two vertices on the equator: its exterior angle at the equator is a right angle, equal to the interior opposite angle there. I.16 is false on the sphere. What rescues it in the plane is the unboundedness of lines (Postulate 2, read strongly) together with the order axioms.
:::

:::leads Neutral geometry
I.16 does not use Postulate 5, and it holds in hyperbolic geometry too. It is the most important theorem of *neutral geometry*, the geometry of Book I up to [[1.28]]. From it comes [[1.17]] (two angles of a triangle are less than two right angles) and [[1.27]] (alternate angles equal imply parallel), and so the *existence* of parallels. The Euclidean strengthening is [[1.32]]: the exterior angle *equals* the sum of the two opposite angles. That needs [[1.29]] and therefore Postulate 5.
:::
