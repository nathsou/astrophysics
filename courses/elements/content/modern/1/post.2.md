---
title: Extending a segment
---
A finite straight line can be extended continuously in a straight line.

Two readings are possible, and Euclid needs the stronger. The weak one: every segment is part of a longer segment. The strong one: the extension can be made as long as you like, and in only one way. [[1.16]] uses the length (a segment is doubled in length) and [[1.14]] the uniqueness.

Riemann distinguished *unbounded* from *infinite*. A great circle on a sphere is unbounded, since you can always go further, but finite in length. On the sphere, Postulate 2 holds in the weak sense but not the strong one, and that is why [[1.16]] fails there. In elliptic geometry, lines are closed and of finite length, and only the weak reading of Postulate 2 survives.
