---
title: Triangles on equal bases
---
**Statement.** Triangles on equal bases and between the same parallels have equal area: if $BC = EF$, both on one line, and $A$, $D$ lie on a parallel to it, then $\triangle ABC = \triangle DEF$.

**Proof.** As in [[1.37]], complete each triangle to a parallelogram: @figure(GBCA) with $BG \parallel CA$, and @figure(DEFH) with $FH \parallel DE$. These are on equal bases and between the same parallels, so they are equal ([[1.36]]). Each triangle is half of its parallelogram ([[1.34]]).

**A consequence.** A median splits a triangle into two triangles of equal area: they have equal bases (the two halves of a side) and the same apex. [[1.42]] uses exactly this.

**In the figure.** Drag @point(E) along the base line and @point(D) along the top line. The two shaded triangles keep equal areas.
