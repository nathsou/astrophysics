---
title: The whole is greater than the part
---
The whole is greater than the part.

For lengths, angles and areas it is the principle behind every proof by contradiction of the form "the less equal to the greater: which is impossible" ([[1.6]], [[1.7]], [[1.14]]) and behind [[1.16]]'s inequality.

For areas it is a real statement: a polygon cannot be equal in area to a polygon lying strictly inside it. This is called De Zolt's axiom, and Hilbert shows that it can be proved once area has been defined.

For infinite sets it fails. The even numbers are a part of the natural numbers, yet the map $n \mapsto 2n$ pairs them off one to one. Galileo noticed this paradox; Dedekind turned it into a definition: a set is *infinite* when it can be put in one-to-one correspondence with a proper part of itself.
