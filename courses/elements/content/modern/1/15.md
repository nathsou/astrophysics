---
title: Vertical angles
---
**Statement.** If lines $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $E$, then $\angle AEC = \angle DEB$ and $\angle CEB = \angle AED$.

**Proof (Euclid's).** By [[1.13]], $\angle CEA + \angle AED$ and $\angle AED + \angle DEB$ both equal two right angles. Subtract the common $\angle AED$ ([[1.cn.3]]): $\angle CEA = \angle DEB$. The other pair is the same.

**Porism.** The four angles at @E add up to four right angles.

The porism (a corollary) generalises: all the angles around a point add up to four right angles, however many lines pass through it. It is the angular version of "a full turn is $360^\circ$".

:::code The proof as a cancellation law
With $s$ for two right angles, the argument is $x + y = s$, $y + z = s \Rightarrow x = z$: cancellation in an ordered abelian group of angle magnitudes. Much of Book I is this kind of bookkeeping, and the common notions are its rules.
:::
