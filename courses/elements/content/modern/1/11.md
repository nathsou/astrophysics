---
title: Perpendicular at a point of a line
---
**Statement.** Given a line $AB$ and a point $C$ on it, construct a line $CF$ perpendicular to $AB$.

**Construction.** Take any @D on @AC, make $CE = CD$ on the other side of @C ([[1.3]]), build the equilateral triangle @triangle(FDE) on @DE ([[1.1]]), and join @FC.

**Why it works.** In triangles $DCF$ and $ECF$: $DC = CE$, $CF$ is common, $DF = FE$. By SSS ([[1.8]]), $\angle DCF = \angle ECF$. These are adjacent angles on a line, and equal adjacent angles are right angles by definition ([[1.def.10]]).

Notice what the definition does: a right angle is not "ninety degrees". It is an angle equal to its own supplement. Euclid has no degrees; angles are compared, not measured.

**In the figure.** Drag @C along the line, or @D along @AC: the angle at @C stays right.
