---
title: Bisecting a segment
---
**Statement.** Given a segment $AB$, construct its midpoint $D$.

**Construction.** Build the equilateral triangle @triangle(ABC) on @AB ([[1.1]]) and bisect the angle @angle(ACB) by @CD ([[1.9]]).

**Why it works.** In triangles $ACD$ and $BCD$: $AC = BC$, $CD$ is common, and $\angle ACD = \angle BCD$. By SAS ([[1.4]]), $AD = BD$.

:::gap
That @CD meets @AB at all, and between @A and @B, is assumed. It follows from the crossbar theorem (a ray inside an angle of a triangle meets the opposite side), which is a consequence of Pasch's axiom.
:::

:::code Composition
Written out fully, I.10 calls I.9, which calls I.3, I.1 and I.8, and I.3 calls I.2, which calls I.1 again. The modern textbook construction is shorter: the two circles about @A and @B through each other meet in two points, and the line through them bisects @AB. Euclid's version is longer but uses only functions already in the library.
:::
