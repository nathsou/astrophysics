---
title: Parallelogram is double the triangle
---
**Statement.** If a parallelogram $ABCD$ and a triangle $EBC$ share the base $BC$ and lie between the same parallels, then
$$\text{area}(ABCD) = 2\cdot\text{area}(EBC).$$

**Proof.** Join the diagonal @AC. Then $\triangle ABC = \triangle EBC$, since they have the same base and lie between the same parallels ([[1.37]]). And $ABCD = 2\,\triangle ABC$, since the diagonal bisects the parallelogram ([[1.34]]).

In modern terms: a parallelogram has area $bh$, and a triangle with the same base and height has area $\tfrac12 bh$. Euclid states the relation between the two without any multiplication.

**Where it is used.** This is the step [[1.47]] relies on: the rectangle $BL$ and the triangle $ABD$ share the base $BD$ and lie between the parallels $BD$ and $AL$. It is also what makes [[1.42]] work.
