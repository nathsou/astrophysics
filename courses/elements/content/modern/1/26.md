---
title: ASA and AAS congruence
---
**Statement.** Suppose triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ have $\angle ABC = \angle DEF$ and $\angle BCA = \angle EFD$, and one pair of corresponding sides equal: either the side between the two angles (ASA, $BC = EF$) or a side opposite one of them (AAS, $AB = DE$). Then the triangles are congruent: $AB = DE$, $AC = DF$, $BC = EF$ and $\angle BAC = \angle EDF$.

**Proof (ASA).** Suppose $AB \ne DE$, say $AB > DE$. Cut off $BG = DE$ on $BA$ ([[1.3]]). Then $\triangle GBC \cong \triangle DEF$ by SAS ([[1.4]]), so $\angle GCB = \angle DFE = \angle ACB$. But @point(G) lies strictly inside the segment $AB$, so $\angle GCB$ is a proper part of $\angle ACB$. The part equals the whole, which is impossible. So $AB = DE$, and SAS finishes the proof.

**Proof (AAS).** Suppose $BC > EF$ and cut off $BH = EF$. SAS gives $\angle BHA = \angle EFD = \angle BCA$. But in $\triangle AHC$, $\angle BHA$ is an exterior angle and $\angle BCA$ is an interior opposite angle, so $\angle BHA > \angle BCA$ by [[1.16]]. Contradiction.

**In the figure.** Triangle $DEF$ is built from the hypothesis: @point(F) is at distance $BC$ from @point(E), and @point(D) is where the two arms meet after the angles at @point(E) and @point(F) are copied from @point(B) and @point(C). The points @point(G) and @point(H) are the impossible points of the two reductios. They are drawn at supposed positions, with dashed joins. Only the ASA configuration is drawn. AAS then holds automatically, since $AB = DE$ comes out as a consequence.

:::gap Neutral, and careful about it
The AAS case is proved with the exterior angle theorem [[1.16]], not with the angle sum [[1.32]]. With the angle sum it would be one line: the third angles are equal, so it reduces to ASA. Euclid's order keeps I.26 free of the parallel postulate, so both cases hold in hyperbolic geometry too.

The step "the part equals the whole" also needs betweenness: $G$ must lie inside $AB$ so that the ray $CG$ lies inside $\angle ACB$. Hilbert's axioms of order supply this.
:::

:::gap SSA is missing, for a reason
Two sides and a non-included angle do not determine a triangle. The circle about $A$ with radius $AC$ can meet the line $BC$ twice, and gives two different triangles. Euclid states only the cases that are true.
:::

:::history Thales
Proclus reports that Eudemus credited Thales with this theorem, reasoning that Thales' method of finding the distance of a ship at sea needed it.
:::
