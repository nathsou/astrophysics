---
title: Straight line
---
A straight line is a line that "lies evenly with the points on itself".

No one knows exactly what this means, and nothing in the Elements depends on it. One reading: a straight line looks the same from each of its points, with no side privileged. Another, going back to Plato's *Parmenides*, is that a straight line is one whose middle hides its ends from an eye placed at one end: it is the line of sight. Archimedes used a property instead of a definition: of all lines with the same endpoints, the straight one is the shortest.

What Euclid actually uses about straight lines is in the postulates: two points can be joined ([[1.post.1]]), a segment can be extended ([[1.post.2]]), and, silently, two straight lines cannot enclose a space, so that two points determine one line.
