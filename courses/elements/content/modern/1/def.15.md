---
title: Circle
---
A circle is a plane figure bounded by one line (the *circumference*) such that all the straight lines drawn to it from one inside point are equal.

In modern notation, with centre $O$ ([[1.def.16]]) and radius $r$, the circumference is
$$\{\,P : |OP| = r\,\},$$
and the circle, the figure, is the disk $\{P : |OP| \le r\}$.

This is the definition the Elements uses most. Every construction with circles, starting with [[1.1]], rests on one consequence: *all radii are equal*. The postulate that lets us draw circles is [[1.post.3]]. Nothing guarantees that two circles, or a circle and a line, actually meet; that is the gap discussed in [[1.1]] and [[1.22]].
