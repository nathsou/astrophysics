---
title: All right angles are equal
---
All right angles are equal to one another.

It sounds trivial, but it says something about space: the plane is *homogeneous*. A right angle is defined locally, as an angle equal to its neighbour on a line ([[1.def.10]]). Postulate 4 says that the right angles at two different points, or on two different lines, are also equal to each other. That makes "two right angles" a fixed yardstick, which [[1.13]], [[1.14]], [[1.17]] and Postulate 5 all rely on.

Hilbert proved it as a theorem from his congruence axioms, which include SAS. Euclid could have argued for it by superposition ([[1.cn.4]]), moving one right angle onto the other; he chose to state it as a postulate instead.
