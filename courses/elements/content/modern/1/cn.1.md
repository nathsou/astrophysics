---
title: Transitivity of equality
---
Things equal to the same thing are equal to each other: if $a = c$ and $b = c$, then $a = b$.

Together with the evident symmetry of "equal", this makes equality transitive. Euclid's "equal" is not identity: equal segments are different segments of the same length, and equal triangles are different triangles with the same area. The common notions say that each such "equal" is an equivalence relation, compatible with addition and subtraction. [[1.1]] uses this one to finish its proof.
