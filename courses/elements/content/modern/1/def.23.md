---
title: Parallel lines
---
Two straight lines in the same plane are *parallel* if, extended indefinitely in both directions, they never meet.

The definition is purely negative: parallel means *disjoint*. It says nothing about direction or constant distance. That matters. "Everywhere the same distance apart" would be a tempting definition, but that such lines exist is equivalent to Postulate 5 ([[1.post.5]]), so taking it as a definition would hide the postulate in the definitions.

With Euclid's definition, the existence of parallels is a theorem of neutral geometry ([[1.27]], [[1.31]]). Their *uniqueness*, one parallel through a given point, is Postulate 5 in Playfair's form. In hyperbolic geometry there are infinitely many; on the sphere, where every two great circles meet, there are none.
