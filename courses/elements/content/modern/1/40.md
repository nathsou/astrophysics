---
title: Equal triangles on equal bases
---
**Statement.** If triangles $ABC$ and $CDE$ have equal areas, stand on equal bases $BC$ and $CE$ of one line and lie on the same side of it, then $AD \parallel BE$. This is the converse of [[1.38]].

**Proof.** It is the same argument as in [[1.39]]. If $AD$ were not parallel to $BE$, the parallel through $A$ would meet $CD$ at a point $F \ne D$. [[1.38]] gives $\triangle FCE = \triangle ABC = \triangle DCE$, and one of $FCE$ and $DCE$ lies inside the other. That is impossible.

Heath prints this proposition in square brackets (the closing one is visible after "Q. E. D."), marking it as a suspected interpolation: a later editor's converse, added to match [[1.38]] and written exactly on the model of [[1.39]].
:::
