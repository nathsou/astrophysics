---
title: Parallelogram equal to a triangle
---
**Construction.** Given a triangle $ABC$ and an angle $D$, construct a parallelogram with one angle equal to $D$ and area equal to the triangle.

1. Bisect @BC at @point(E) ([[1.10]]) and join @AE.
2. At @point(E), make @angle(CEF) equal to the angle $D$ ([[1.23]]).
3. Through @point(A) draw @AG parallel to @BC, and through @point(C) draw @CG parallel to @EF ([[1.31]]). Then @figure(FECG) is the parallelogram.

**Why it works.** The median $AE$ halves the triangle: $\triangle ABE = \triangle AEC$, since they have equal bases $BE = EC$ and the same apex ([[1.38]]). So $\triangle ABC = 2\,\triangle AEC$. The parallelogram $FECG$ has the same base $EC$ as $\triangle AEC$ and lies between the same parallels, so it is also $2\,\triangle AEC$ ([[1.41]]). Hence $FECG = \triangle ABC$, and its angle at $E$ is $D$.

In modern terms: halve the base, keep the height. A parallelogram on half the base with the same height has area $\tfrac b2 \cdot h$, the same as the triangle's $\tfrac12 bh$. Changing the angle only shears the parallelogram, which does not change its area ([[1.35]]).

:::code Normalizing a representation
This is the first of four problems, [[1.42]], [[1.44]], [[1.45]] and [[2.14]], that turn an arbitrary figure into a standard one of the same area. Here a triangle becomes a parallelogram with a prescribed angle. [[1.44]] also prescribes one side, [[1.45]] accepts any polygon, and [[2.14]] finally produces a square. It is a normalization pipeline. Once every figure can be put in a canonical form, comparing two areas reduces to comparing two canonical forms.
:::
