---
title: The greater angle is subtended by the greater side
---
**Statement.** In triangle $ABC$, if $\angle ABC > \angle BCA$, then $AC > AB$.

**Proof (Euclid's).** By trichotomy, $AC = AB$, $AC < AB$ or $AC > AB$. If $AC = AB$, the angles would be equal ([[1.5]]). If $AC < AB$, then $\angle ABC < \angle ACB$ ([[1.18]]). Both contradict the hypothesis, so $AC > AB$.

:::code Converse for free
This is a general pattern. Suppose $f$ maps side comparisons to angle comparisons, and you know
$$a = b \Rightarrow \alpha = \beta, \qquad a < b \Rightarrow \alpha < \beta, \qquad a > b \Rightarrow \alpha > \beta.$$
Since exactly one case holds on each side (trichotomy), each implication reverses automatically. [[1.5]]–[[1.6]] and [[1.18]]–[[1.19]] together say that in a triangle, the order of the sides equals the order of the opposite angles. A sorting routine could use either.
:::
