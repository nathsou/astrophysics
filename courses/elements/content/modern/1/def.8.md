---
title: Plane angle
---
An angle is the "inclination" of two lines that meet at a point and do not lie in one straight line.

Three things stand out.

- The lines can be curves: Euclid admits angles between a circle and a line (the "horn angle" of [[3.16]]), which cannot be compared with rectilinear angles in the usual way.
- A straight angle is excluded ("do not lie in a straight line"), and so is the zero angle. Euclid's angles lie strictly between nothing and two right angles, so a sum like "the angles $ABC$, $ABD$" of [[1.13]] is not itself an angle.
- An angle is a magnitude that can be compared and added, but it is never given a number. Degrees and radians are not Euclid's.

Modern treatments define the angle $\angle BAC$ as the pair of rays $AB$, $AC$, and compare angles by congruence.
