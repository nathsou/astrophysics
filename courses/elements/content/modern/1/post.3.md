---
title: Drawing a circle
---
A circle can be drawn with any centre and any radius ("distance").

Read literally in the Greek, the radius is given as a segment from the centre: the circle with centre $A$ *through* $B$. Euclid's compass may therefore "collapse" when lifted: it cannot carry a distance from one place to another. [[1.2]] shows that it does not need to, since a length can be copied with the collapsing compass alone.

:::code The primitive operations
Postulates 1–3 are the instruction set of straightedge-and-compass geometry: `join`, `extend`, `circle`. Every construction in the Elements is a program over them, plus one operation they do not provide: taking the intersection points of what has been drawn. That missing primitive, intersection, is the continuity gap of [[1.1]].
:::
