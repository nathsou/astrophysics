---
title: The greater side subtends the greater angle
---
**Statement.** In triangle $ABC$, if $AC > AB$, then $\angle ABC > \angle BCA$.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Cut off $AD = AB$ on @AC ([[1.3]]) and join @BD.

- $\angle ADB$ is an exterior angle of @triangle(BCD), so $\angle ADB > \angle DCB$ ([[1.16]]).
- $\triangle ABD$ is isosceles, so $\angle ABD = \angle ADB$ ([[1.5]]).
- @D lies between @A and @C, so @BD lies inside @angle(ABC), and $\angle ABC > \angle ABD$.

Together: $\angle ABC > \angle ABD = \angle ADB > \angle ACB$.

The proof is a chain of three comparisons, and I.16 is the link that makes it work. A neutral-geometry result: nothing here needs parallels.

**In the figure.** Dragging so that $AC \le AB$ is refused, since the hypothesis would fail.
