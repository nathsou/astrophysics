---
title: Cutting off a given part of a segment
---
**Construction.** To cut off a third of @AB: draw any line @AC through @A, mark off three equal steps $AD = DE = EC$ on it with any step length ([[1.3]]), join @BC, and draw @DF parallel to @BC ([[1.31]]). Then $AF = \tfrac13 AB$.

**Why it works.** In triangle $ABC$, $DF \parallel BC$, so $CD : DA = BF : FA$ ([[6.2]]). Since $CD = 2\,DA$, also $BF = 2\,FA$, and $AB = 3\,AF$.

The same recipe cuts off an $n$-th part for any $n$: lay off $n$ equal steps. This is the standard way to divide a segment into equal parts with ruler and compass, and it is still taught that way. The equal steps can have any length because [[6.2]] depends only on ratios, not on sizes.

**In the figure.** Drag @D to change the direction and the step length; @F stays at the third.
