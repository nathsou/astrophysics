---
title: SAS similarity
---
**Statement.** If $\angle BAC = \angle EDF$ and $BA : AC = ED : DF$, then triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ are equiangular: $\angle ABC = \angle DEF$ and $\angle ACB = \angle DFE$.

**Proof (Euclid's).** It follows the pattern of [[6.5]]. On @DF construct the triangle $DGF$ with $\angle FDG = \angle BAC$ and $\angle DFG = \angle ACB$ ([[1.23]]).

- $DGF$ is equiangular with $ABC$, so $BA : AC = GD : DF$ ([[6.4]]).
- By hypothesis $BA : AC = ED : DF$, so $ED = GD$ ([[5.9]]).
- Triangles $EDF$ and $GDF$ have $ED = GD$, the side $DF$ in common, and equal angles at $D$. By SAS ([[1.4]]) they are congruent, so $\angle DFE = \angle DFG = \angle ACB$ and $\angle DEF = \angle DGF = \angle ABC$.

The criteria AA ([[6.4]]), SSS ([[6.5]]) and SAS (this proposition) are the similarity versions of the congruence theorems [[1.26]], [[1.8]] and [[1.4]]. In each, "equal sides" is replaced by "proportional sides". [[6.20]] and [[6.32]] use this one.
