---
title: Parallelograms about the diameter
---
**Statement.** In a parallelogram $ABCD$ with diameter (diagonal) @AC, take a point @F on the diagonal and draw through it the parallels to the sides. The two parallelograms about the diameter, @figure(EG) at $A$ and @figure(HK) at $C$, are similar to $ABCD$ and to each other.

In modern terms, $EG$ is the image of $ABCD$ under the dilation about @A with factor $AF : AC$, and $HK$ is its image under the dilation about @C with factor $CF : CA$.

**Proof (Euclid's).** $EF \parallel BC$ gives $BE : EA = CF : FA$, and $FG \parallel CD$ gives $CF : FA = DG : GA$ ([[6.2]]). Hence $BE : EA = DG : GA$, and adding the parts ([[5.18]]) and swapping means ([[5.16]]) gives $BA : AD = EA : AG$. Those are the sides about the common angle at $A$. For the other sides, the triangles $ADC$ and $AGF$ are equiangular, and so are $ACB$ and $AFE$; [[6.4]] and *ex aequali* ([[5.22]]) give the remaining proportions. $HK$ is handled the same way, and $EG \sim HK$ follows by [[6.21]].

The two other parallelograms through $F$, the *complements*, are equal ([[1.43]]). [[6.26]] proves the converse of this proposition, and [[6.27]]–[[6.29]] use the whole configuration.
