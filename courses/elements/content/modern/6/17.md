---
title: Mean proportional as a square
---
**Statement.** For three segments,
$$A : B = B : C \iff A \cdot C = B^2.$$
The rectangle of the extremes equals the square on the mean.

**Proof.** Let @D be a copy of @B. Then $A : B = D : C$ is a four-term proportion, and [[6.16]] turns it into $A\cdot C = B \cdot D = B^2$. The converse runs the same way backwards.

This is the form in which the mean proportional of [[6.13]] is usually used: $B = \sqrt{AC}$. It also connects Book VI with Book II. The square equal to a given rectangle, found in [[2.14]], has as its side the mean proportional between the rectangle's sides.
