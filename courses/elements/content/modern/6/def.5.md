---
title: Compound ratio
---
The text printed here has no definition 5. Some manuscripts have one, generally taken to be a later addition, which says that a ratio is **compounded** of ratios when their "sizes", multiplied together, make it.

In modern notation, the ratio compounded of $a:b$ and $c:d$ is the product
$$\frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{c}{d}.$$
Euclid does not have products of ratios, because for him ratios are not numbers. He works with chains instead: if $a : b = K : L$ and $c : d = L : M$, the ratio compounded of $a:b$ and $c:d$ is $K : M$. This is how [[6.23]] handles it, and it is the reason for the fourth proportional of [[6.12]]. The special case of compounding a ratio with itself is the "duplicate ratio" of [[5.def.9]], which is a square.
