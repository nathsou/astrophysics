---
title: Proportional lines, proportional figures
---
**Statement.** Let figures be drawn on four segments: similar and similarly situated figures @figure(KAB), @figure(LCD) on $AB$, $CD$, and similar and similarly situated figures @figure(MF), @figure(NH) on $EF$, $GH$. Then
$$AB : CD = EF : GH \iff KAB : LCD = MF : NH.$$
In modern terms: $a/b = c/d \iff a^2/b^2 = c^2/d^2$ for positive quantities, dressed up with any shapes.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Take the third proportionals @O to $AB, CD$ and @P to $EF, GH$ ([[6.11]]). *Ex aequali*, $AB : O = EF : P$ ([[5.22]]). By the porism of [[6.19]], $AB : O = KAB : LCD$ and $EF : P = MF : NH$. So the figures are proportional.

For the converse Euclid argues by contradiction. Take @QR with $AB : CD = EF : QR$ ([[6.12]]) and draw on it a figure @figure(SR) similar to $MF$ ([[6.18]]). By the first part, $KAB : LCD = MF : SR$, so $SR = NH$. Two similar and similarly situated figures that are equal must have equal corresponding sides, so $QR = GH$.

:::gap Equal similar figures have equal sides
The last step is not proved anywhere. It follows from [[6.19]] and [[6.20]]: equal figures have ratio $1$, the duplicate of $QR : GH$ is then $1$, so $QR : GH$ is $1$. That needs the fact that a ratio whose duplicate is equality is itself equality, which is true (if $QR > GH$ then its duplicate is greater than equality) but unstated. In the figure, $QR$ is drawn dashed; it always has the length of $GH$.
:::
