---
title: Application with defect (solving x(a − x) = b²)
---
**Problem.** Given a segment $AB$, a figure @C and a parallelogram @D, apply to $AB$ a parallelogram equal to $C$ and deficient by a parallelogram similar to $D$. The **condition** is that $C$ must not exceed the parallelogram described on half of $AB$ similar to the defect ([[6.27]]).

In the simplest case (rectangles, square defect), with $AB = a$, $C = b^2$ and defect side $x$:
$$x(a - x) = b^2, \qquad \text{solvable iff } b \le \tfrac a2,\qquad x = \tfrac a2 - \sqrt{\left(\tfrac a2\right)^2 - b^2}.$$
This is the quadratic formula, built with ruler and compass.

**Construction (Euclid's).**

1. Bisect $AB$ at @E, and on $EB$ describe @figure(GB) $= EBFG$ similar to $D$ ([[6.18]]). Complete @figure(AG). If $AG = C$, done.
2. Otherwise $GB > C$, by the condition. Construct @figure(KM) $= KLMN$ similar to $D$ and equal to the excess $GB - C$ ([[6.25]]).
3. $KM$ is smaller than $GB$ and similar to it, so its sides are smaller. Cut off @GO $= KL$ and @GP $= LM$ and complete the parallelogram $OGPQ$. It is a copy of $KM$ in the corner $G$, so @Q lies on the diameter $GB$ ([[6.26]]).
4. Draw the parallels through $Q$. The answer is the parallelogram @figure(ST) $= ASQT$, with defect @figure(QB), which is similar to $D$ ([[6.24]]).

**Why it works.** The gnomon @figure(UWV) is $GB - GQ = GB - KM = C$. The complements @figure(PR) and @figure(OS) are equal ([[1.43]]), and @figure(OB) $=$ @figure(TE) because $AE = EB$ ([[1.36]]). Adding and subtracting these pieces shows that $ST$ equals the gnomon, which equals $C$.

:::modern The algebra behind the figure
In the square case, $GB = (a/2)^2$ and $KM = (a/2)^2 - b^2$, so $GO = \sqrt{(a/2)^2 - b^2}$. The defect has side $SB = EB - ES = \tfrac a2 - \sqrt{(a/2)^2 - b^2}$, which is the smaller root. Every step is a line of the algebraic solution:

| algebra | geometry |
|---|---|
| $x^2 - ax + b^2 = 0$ | apply $C$ to $AB$ with a square defect |
| $\left(\tfrac a2\right)^2 - b^2$ | the excess $GB - C$ |
| $\sqrt{\left(\tfrac a2\right)^2 - b^2}$ | the side of $KM$ ([[6.25]], via [[6.13]]) |
| $\tfrac a2 \pm \sqrt{\cdots}$ | the points $S$ on either side of $E$ |
| discriminant $\ge 0$ | the condition $C \le GB$ |

Euclid gives only one root. The other, $x' = a - x$, is the same rectangle with the roles of applied figure and defect exchanged.
:::

::application-of-areas{mode="defect"}

The slider $c$ in the figure is $C : GB$. At $c = 1$ the answer is $AG$ itself, and the condition forbids $c > 1$: the excess $GB - C$ would be negative.

:::history Geometric algebra
Heath and the historians of his time (Zeuthen, Tannery) read VI.27–29 as the Greek solution of the quadratic equation, and called this body of results "geometric algebra". Later historians have questioned how far the Greeks thought of it as algebra. Either way, these propositions do the work of the quadratic formula, including the condition for real roots, and Apollonius built the names of the conic sections on them: *ellipse* ("defect") and *hyperbola* ("excess") come from the application with defect and with excess.
:::
