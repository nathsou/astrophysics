---
title: Similar figures
---
Two polygons $P_1 \dots P_n$ and $Q_1 \dots Q_n$ are **similar** when, with the vertices matched up in order,

- the corresponding angles are equal: $\angle P_i = \angle Q_i$, and
- the sides about equal angles are proportional: $P_{i-1}P_i : P_iP_{i+1} = Q_{i-1}Q_i : Q_iQ_{i+1}$.

Equivalently, all sides share one scale factor: $Q_iQ_{i+1} = k \cdot P_iP_{i+1}$. In modern terms the two figures differ by a *similarity transformation*, which is a composition of an isometry and a dilation $x \mapsto kx$.

For polygons with more than three sides both conditions are needed. A square and a non-square rectangle are equiangular but not similar, and a square and a rhombus have proportional sides but are not similar. For triangles, [[6.4]] and [[6.5]] show that either condition implies the other.
