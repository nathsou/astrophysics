---
title: Converse of the parallelograms about the diameter
---
**Statement.** If a parallelogram @figure(AF) $= AEFG$ is cut from the corner @A of a parallelogram $ABCD$, sharing the angle $DAB$ and similar and similarly situated to $ABCD$, then its far corner @F lies on the diameter @AC.

This is the converse of [[6.24]]. In modern terms: a figure similar to $ABCD$ in the corner at $A$ is the image of $ABCD$ under a dilation about $A$, and a dilation about $A$ maps $C$ to a point of the line $AC$.

**Proof (Euclid's), by contradiction.** Suppose the diameter misses $F$ and cuts $GF$ produced at some point $H$. Draw through $H$ the parallel to $AD$, meeting $AB$ at $K$. The parallelogram with corner $H$ is about the diameter, so by [[6.24]] it is similar to $ABCD$, and $DA : AB = GA : AK$. By hypothesis $AF$ is similar to $ABCD$ too, so $DA : AB = GA : AE$. Hence $AK = AE$ ([[5.9]]), the less equal to the greater, which is impossible.

:::gap Only one case
Euclid assumes that the diameter cuts $GF$ *produced*, beyond $F$. It could also cut $GF$ between $G$ and $F$. The same argument works, with the inequality reversed. The points $H$ and $K$ exist only under the false assumption, so the figure does not draw them; it shows that $F$ stays on the dashed diagonal $AC$ as you drag @E.
:::
