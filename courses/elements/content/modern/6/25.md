---
title: A figure of given shape and given size
---
**Construction.** Given a polygon $ABC$ (the shape) and a polygon @D (the size), construct a polygon @triangle(KGH) similar to $ABC$ and equal in area to $D$.

The idea: similar figures are in the duplicate ratio of their sides ([[6.19]]), so the side of the answer must be the mean proportional between $BC$ and a segment that turns $D$ into a ratio.

1. On @BC, apply a parallelogram @figure(BE) equal to the triangle $ABC$ ([[1.44]]).
2. On @CE, apply a parallelogram @figure(CM) equal to $D$ in the same angle ([[1.45]]). It has the same height as $BE$, and its base @CF continues $BC$.
3. Find @GH, the mean proportional between $BC$ and $CF$ ([[6.13]]).
4. On $GH$, describe $KGH$ similar to $ABC$ ([[6.18]]).

**Why it works.** $BC : GH = GH : CF$, so by the porism of [[6.19]], $ABC : KGH = BC : CF$. The parallelograms $BE$ and $EF$ have equal heights, so $BC : CF = BE : EF$ ([[6.1]]). Therefore $ABC : KGH = BE : EF$, and since $ABC = BE$, also $KGH = EF = D$.

In modern terms, the answer is $ABC$ scaled by $k = \sqrt{D/\triangle ABC}$. Euclid produces $\sqrt{\ }$ of a ratio of areas as a ratio of lines: the areas become the lengths $BC$ and $CF$ over a common height, and [[6.13]] takes the square root.
