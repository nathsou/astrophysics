---
title: Fourth proportional
---
**Construction.** Given three segments @A, @B, @C, find a fourth, $HF$, with
$$A : B = C : HF, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad HF = \frac{B \cdot C}{A}.$$
Draw two lines @DE and @DF at any angle. On the first, mark $DG = A$ and $GE = B$. On the second, mark $DH = C$. Join @GH and draw @EF parallel to it.

**Why it works.** In triangle $DEF$, $GH \parallel EF$, so $DG : GE = DH : HF$ ([[6.2]]), which is $A : B = C : HF$.

:::code Multiplication and division with a ruler
With a fixed unit segment, this construction is arithmetic. Take $A = 1$ and it computes the product $B \cdot C$. Take $B = 1$ and it computes the quotient $C / A$. With [[6.13]] for square roots, the lengths constructible by ruler and compass from a unit form a field closed under $\sqrt{\ }$. Descartes started his *Géométrie* (1637) from these constructions, and they are the reason why ruler-and-compass constructibility is a question about field extensions of degree $2^k$.
:::

The fourth proportional is also how Euclid handles compound ratios in [[6.23]]: to chain $a:b$ and $c:d$, he finds $L$ with $a : b = K : L$ and then $M$ with $c : d = L : M$.
