---
title: Triangles with parallel sides
---
**Statement.** If triangles $ABC$ and $DCE$ are placed together at the vertex @C with $AB \parallel DC$, $AC \parallel DE$ and $AB : AC = DC : DE$, then $BC$ and $CE$ lie on one straight line.

**Proof (Euclid's).** $AB \parallel DC$ and $AC \parallel DE$ give $\angle BAC = \angle ACD = \angle CDE$ (alternate angles, [[1.29]]). The sides about the equal angles at $A$ and $D$ are proportional, so the triangles are equiangular ([[6.6]]) and $\angle ABC = \angle DCE$. Then
$$\angle ACE = \angle ACD + \angle DCE = \angle BAC + \angle ABC,$$
and adding $\angle ACB$ gives $\angle ACE + \angle ACB = $ two right angles ([[1.32]]). By [[1.14]], $BC$ and $CE$ are in one line.

In the figure, $DCE$ is $ABC$ turned through two right angles and scaled by $k$ (the slider), which keeps the hypothesis true. The conclusion shows that such a map sends the line $BC$ to itself.

The enunciation leaves something implicit: the triangles must be placed so that the parallel sides point the right way, with $ and $ on opposite sides of $ as in the figure. If $ is flipped to the other side of $, the conclusion fails.
