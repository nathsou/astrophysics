---
title: Equal triangles have reciprocal sides
---
**Statement.** Let triangles $ABC$ and $ADE$ have equal angles at @A, placed with $CA$ in line with $AD$ and $EA$ in line with $AB$. Then
$$\triangle ABC = \triangle ADE \iff CA : AD = EA : AB.$$

This is [[6.14]] for triangles, and the proof is the same. The auxiliary figure is the triangle $BAD$, formed by joining @BD. It shares the side $AD$ with $ADE$ and the side $AB$ with $ABC$.

**Proof.** $\triangle CAB : \triangle BAD = CA : AD$ and $\triangle EAD : \triangle BAD = EA : AB$, because each pair has the same apex and bases on one line ([[6.1]]). If the triangles are equal, the left-hand sides are equal ([[5.7]]), and so are the right-hand sides. Conversely, if $CA : AD = EA : AB$, the triangles have the same ratio to $BAD$, so they are equal ([[5.9]]).

In modern terms, area $= \tfrac12\, AB\cdot AC \sin A$, so with $\angle A$ fixed, equal area means $AB \cdot AC = AD \cdot AE$. [[6.19]] uses this proposition to compare the areas of similar triangles.
