---
title: The maximum of x(a − x)
---
**Statement.** Among all parallelograms *applied* to a segment $AB$ and *deficient* by a parallelogram of a fixed shape, the greatest is the one applied to half the segment.

The vocabulary needs unpacking. To **apply** a parallelogram to $AB$ means to build it on part of $AB$, from $A$ to some point $K$. It is **deficient** by the parallelogram on the rest, $KB$, which completes it to a parallelogram on the whole of $AB$. Here the defect must have a given shape: that of @figure(DB), the parallelogram on the half $CB$.

In the simplest case the parallelograms are rectangles and the defect is a square. With $AB = a$ and defect side $KB = x$, the applied rectangle is $AK \cdot KB = x(a - x)$. The proposition then says:
$$x(a - x) \le \left(\tfrac a2\right)^2, \quad \text{with equality only at } x = \tfrac a2.$$
The general case only multiplies both sides by a constant that depends on the shape.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Let @figure(AF) be applied to $AB$ with defect @figure(FB) similar to $DB$. Similar parallelograms in a common corner lie about the same diameter ([[6.26]]), so @F lies on the diagonal @DB. Complete the figure with parallels through $F$.

- The complements are equal ([[1.43]]): @figure(CF) $=$ @figure(FE). Adding $FB$ to each gives @figure(CH) $=$ @figure(KE).
- $CH = CG$, since they have equal bases $AC = CB$ and the same height ([[1.36]]). So $CG = KE$.
- Add $CF$ to both: $AF = CG + CF$ equals $KE + CF$, which is the gnomon @figure(LMN), the part of $DB$ outside the parallelogram at $D$.

The gnomon is less than $DB$, and $DB = AD$ ([[1.36]]). So $AF < AD$.

:::modern The discriminant condition
Read as algebra, this proposition is the solvability condition for the next one. The equation
$$x(a - x) = b^2 \quad\iff\quad x^2 - ax + b^2 = 0$$
asks for a rectangle equal to the square $b^2$, applied to $a$ and deficient by a square. Since $x(a-x)$ never exceeds $(a/2)^2$, a solution exists only if
$$b^2 \le \left(\tfrac a2\right)^2 \quad\iff\quad a^2 - 4b^2 \ge 0,$$
which is exactly the condition that the discriminant is non-negative. This is the *diorismos* (condition of possibility) that [[6.28]] states in its enunciation. The widget above solves the equation with a semicircle: the perpendicular from where the level $b$ meets the semicircle on $AB$ gives $AK\cdot KB = b^2$ by [[6.13]], and when $b > a/2$ the level misses the semicircle.
:::

:::code Completing the square
The gnomon argument is completing the square:
$$x(a-x) = \left(\tfrac a2\right)^2 - \left(\tfrac a2 - x\right)^2.$$
The applied figure equals the big square on the half minus a small square, and the small square is the parallelogram on $DF$. It is zero only when $x = a/2$. [[2.5]] proves this identity for rectangles; Book VI extends it to any shape of defect.
:::
