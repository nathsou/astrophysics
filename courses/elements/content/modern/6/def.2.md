---
title: Reciprocally related figures
---
Heath prints only the name, in brackets, because the definition is generally taken to be a later addition, and its wording in the manuscripts is obscure. Euclid never uses the term.

What he does use is the idea behind it, in [[6.14]] and [[6.15]]. Two figures with an equal angle have their sides about that angle **reciprocally proportional** when
$$a : b = d : c, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad a c = b d,$$
where $a, c$ are the sides of one figure and $b, d$ those of the other. This is the condition for two equiangular parallelograms, or two triangles with an equal angle, to have equal area.
