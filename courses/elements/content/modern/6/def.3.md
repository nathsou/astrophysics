---
title: Extreme and mean ratio
---
A segment $AB$ is cut in **extreme and mean ratio** at $E$ when
$$AB : AE = AE : EB,$$
that is, the whole is to the greater part as the greater part is to the smaller.

Set $AE = 1$ and $AB = \varphi$. Then $\varphi : 1 = 1 : (\varphi - 1)$, so $\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1$ and
$$\varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt5}{2} \approx 1.618.$$
This is the golden ratio. Euclid constructs the cut in [[2.11]] as a problem about areas, and again in [[6.30]] as a proportion. He uses it in [[4.10]] for the regular pentagon, and in Book XIII for the icosahedron and the dodecahedron.
