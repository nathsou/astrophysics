---
title: Third proportional
---
**Construction.** Given two segments @AB and @AC, find $CE$ with
$$AB : AC = AC : CE, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad CE = \frac{AC^2}{AB}.$$
Put $AB$ and $AC$ at an angle at @A. Produce $AB$ to @D with $BD = AC$, join @BC, and draw @DE parallel to $BC$, meeting $AC$ produced at @E.

**Why it works.** In triangle $ADE$, $BC \parallel DE$, so $AB : BD = AC : CE$ ([[6.2]]). Since $BD = AC$, $AB : AC = AC : CE$.

In the language of arithmetic on lengths, with $AB$ as the unit this constructs $AC^2$, and in general it computes $b^2/a$ from $a$ and $b$. Three segments $a, b, c$ with $a : b = b : c$ form a *continued proportion*, and $c$ is the **third proportional** to $a$ and $b$. [[6.19]] uses it to prove that similar triangles are in the duplicate ratio of their sides.
