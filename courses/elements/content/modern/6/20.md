---
title: Areas of similar polygons
---
**Statement.** Similar polygons $ABCDE$ and $FGHKL$ can be cut into the same number of triangles, similar in pairs and with areas in the same ratio as the wholes. The polygons are to one another in the duplicate ratio of corresponding sides:
$$ABCDE : FGHKL = (AB : FG)^2.$$

**Proof (Euclid's).** Draw the diagonals @BE, @EC and @GL, @LH.

1. *Similar triangles.* The angles at $A$ and $F$ are equal and the sides about them are proportional, so $ABE \sim FGL$ by SAS ([[6.6]]). Subtracting the equal angles $ABE$ and $FGL$ from the equal angles $ABC$ and $FGH$ leaves $\angle EBC = \angle LGH$, and the sides about them are proportional (*ex aequali*, [[5.22]]). So $EBC \sim LGH$, and in the same way $ECD \sim LHK$.
2. *Proportional triangles.* Euclid shows that $\triangle ABE : \triangle EBC = \triangle FGL : \triangle LGH$ using the auxiliary diagonals @AC, @FH and their intersections @M, @N with the first diagonals. Swapping means, every triangle of the first polygon is to its partner in one common ratio.
3. *Sum.* If $a_1 : b_1 = a_2 : b_2 = a_3 : b_3$, then $(a_1 + a_2 + a_3) : (b_1+b_2+b_3)$ is the same ratio ([[5.12]]). So the polygons are in the ratio of $\triangle ABE : \triangle FGL$, which is $(AB : FG)^2$ by [[6.19]].

Step 2 is more work than necessary. Once the triangles are similar in pairs, [[6.19]] already gives each pair the ratio $(AB : FG)^2$, since the corresponding sides are all in the ratio $AB : FG$. Euclid's longer route through $M$ and $N$ uses only [[6.1]].

**Porism.** The same holds for quadrilaterals and hence for all similar polygons: they are in the duplicate ratio of corresponding sides.
