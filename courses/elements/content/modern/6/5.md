---
title: SSS similarity
---
**Statement.** If $AB : BC = DE : EF$, $BC : CA = EF : FD$ and $BA : AC = ED : DF$, then triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ are equiangular, with $\angle ABC = \angle DEF$, $\angle BCA = \angle EFD$ and $\angle BAC = \angle EDF$.

This is the converse of [[6.4]]: proportional sides force equal angles.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Construct on @EF a triangle $GEF$ that has the angles of $ABC$: $\angle FEG = \angle ABC$ and $\angle EFG = \angle ACB$ ([[1.23]]). It is shown dashed in the figure.

- $GEF$ is equiangular with $ABC$, so its sides are proportional to those of $ABC$ ([[6.4]]): $AB : BC = GE : EF$.
- By hypothesis $AB : BC = DE : EF$. So $DE : EF = GE : EF$, and $DE = GE$ ([[5.9]]). In the same way $DF = GF$.
- Triangles $DEF$ and $GEF$ now have three equal sides, so they are congruent ([[1.8]]). Hence $\angle DEF = \angle GEF = \angle ABC$, and so on.

This pattern recurs in [[6.6]] and [[6.7]]: build a triangle that is known to be similar, show it has the same sides as the given one, and conclude by a congruence theorem of Book I.

In the figure, @D is built from the side lengths alone, and the angle readouts confirm the theorem. @G is the reflection of @D in @EF.
