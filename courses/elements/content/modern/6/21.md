---
title: Similarity is transitive
---
**Statement.** If the figures @A and @B are each similar to @C, then $A$ is similar to $B$.

**Proof.** By [[6.def.1]], $A$ is equiangular with $C$ and has its sides about equal angles proportional to those of $C$; so does $B$. Equal angles are equal to each other ([[1.cn.1]]), and ratios equal to the same ratio are equal to each other ([[5.11]]). So $A$ and $B$ are equiangular with proportional sides.

Together with the obvious reflexivity and symmetry, this makes similarity an **equivalence relation**. Its classes are "shapes". In modern terms, the similarity transformations form a group, and two figures are similar when one is the image of the other under an element of the group; transitivity is closure under composition.
