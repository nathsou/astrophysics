---
title: Progressions from the unit
---
**Statement.** If $n$ numbers fall in continued proportion between the unit and $a$, and $n$ between the unit and $b$, then $n$ numbers fall in continued proportion between $a$ and $b$. This is the converse of [[8.9]], and it needs no coprimality.

In modern terms: $1, d, d^2, d^3 = a$ and $1, f, f^2, f^3 = b$. Then
$$d^3,\; d^2 f,\; d f^2,\; f^3$$
is a progression in the ratio $d : f$.

**Proof.** Since the unit @C is to @D as $D$ is to @E, $D$ measures $E$ as often as the unit measures $D$, that is, $E = D \cdot D$. In the same way $A = D \cdot E$, $G = F \cdot F$, and $B = F \cdot G$. So the progressions from the unit are powers.

Put @H $= D \cdot F$, @K $= D \cdot H$, @L $= F \cdot H$. Then [[7.17]] and [[7.18]], applied as in [[8.2]], give
$$E : H = H : G = D : F, \qquad A : K = K : L = L : B = D : F.$$
With the default sliders, $D = 2$ and $F = 3$, and $8, 12, 18, 27$ is the progression.
