---
title: Similar solid numbers are as cube to cube
---
**Statement.** If $A$ and $B$ are similar solid numbers, then $A : B = s^3 : t^3$ for some numbers $s, t$.

**Proof.** Two means @C, @D fall between @A and @B ([[8.19]]). Take the least numbers @E, @F, @G, @H in the ratio of $A, C, D, B$ ([[7.33]] or [[8.2]]). By the porism of [[8.2]], the extremes of a least progression of four terms are cubes, and $E : H = A : B$ ex aequali. So $A : B$ is as the cube $E$ to the cube $H$.

In the figure, the sides of $A$ are $s, s, sk$ and those of $B$ are $t, t, tk$. So $A = s^3 k$ and $B = t^3 k$, and the ratio reduces to that of the cubes of $s, t$ in lowest terms.

This closes the book. For two numbers, one mean, similar plane numbers, and the ratio of two squares all mean the same thing; two means, similar solid numbers, and the ratio of two cubes do too. Book IX begins by drawing consequences for products: for example, the product of two similar plane numbers is a square ([[9.1]]).
