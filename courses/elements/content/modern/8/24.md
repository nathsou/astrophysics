---
title: In the ratio of two squares
---
**Statement.** If $a : b = c^2 : d^2$ and $a$ is a square, then $b$ is a square.

**Proof.** $C$ and $D$ are squares, hence similar plane numbers, so one mean falls between them ([[8.18]]). Numbers in the same ratio admit the same number of means ([[8.8]]), so one mean falls between @A and @B as well. $A, (\text{mean}), B$ is a progression starting with a square, so $B$ is a square ([[8.22]]).

The thin rods are the two means, which the text does not name. With the defaults, $C : D = 16 : 36$, the mean is $24$; $A : B = 4 : 9$, the mean is $6$.

In fractions: if $\frac ab = \left(\frac cd\right)^2$ and $a$ is a perfect square, so is $b$. Reduce $\frac cd$ to lowest terms $\frac{c'}{d'}$. Then $a = m c'^2$ and $b = m d'^2$ for some $m$, and $a$ being a square forces $m$ to be a square.
