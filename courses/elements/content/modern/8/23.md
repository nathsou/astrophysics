---
title: A progression that starts with a cube
---
**Statement.** If $a, b, c, d$ are in continued proportion and $a$ is a cube, then $d$ is a cube.

**Proof (Euclid's).** Two means, @B and @C, fall between @A and @D, so they are similar solid numbers ([[8.21]]). $A$ is a cube, so $D$ is a cube.

As in [[8.22]], the last step needs more than Euclid gives. In a progression $a, ar, ar^2, ar^3$ of four terms, $b^3 = a^2 d$ (both equal $a^3 r^3$). Write $a = s^3$. Then $d = b^3 / s^6$, so $(s^2)^3 \mid b^3$, hence $s^2 \mid b$ by [[8.15]], and $d = (b / s^2)^3$ is the cube of a whole number.

With the default sliders, $8, 12, 18, 27$: $8 = 2^3$ and $27 = 3^3$.
