---
title: Two means force similar solid numbers
---
**Statement.** If two mean proportionals fall between $A$ and $B$, then $A$ and $B$ are similar solid numbers. This is the converse of [[8.19]].

In modern terms, the progression $A, C, D, B$ is $m x^3, m x^2 y, m x y^2, m y^3$ with $x : y$ in lowest terms, so $A = x \cdot x \cdot mx$ and $B = y \cdot y \cdot my$.

**Proof.** Take the least numbers @E, @F, @G in the ratio of @A, @C, @D ([[8.2]]). Their extremes are prime to one another ([[8.3]]), and one mean falls between them, so they are similar plane numbers ([[8.20]]). Call the sides @H, @K of $E$ and @L, @M of $G$. From [[8.20]]'s construction, $E, F, G$ is a progression in the ratio $H : L$ and also $K : M$.

Ex aequali $E : G = A : D$ ([[7.14]]). $E, G$ are prime to one another, hence least, so they divide $A$ and $D$ the same number of times ([[7.20]], [[7.21]]). Call it @N: $A = N \cdot E = H \cdot K \cdot N$, a solid number. In the same way $E, F, G$ is in the ratio of $C, D, B$, so $G$ divides @B as often as $E$ divides $C$. Call it @O: $B = O \cdot G = L \cdot M \cdot O$.

Finally $N : O = A : C$ ([[7.18]]), which is $E : F$, which is $H : L$ and $K : M$. So the sides $H, K, N$ of $A$ and $L, M, O$ of $B$ are proportional.

In the figure, the least progression is $x^2, xy, y^2$, whose sides from [[8.20]] are $H = K = x$ and $L = M = y$.
