---
title: Similar plane numbers are as square to square
---
**Statement.** If $A$ and $B$ are similar plane numbers, then $A : B = s^2 : t^2$ for some numbers $s, t$.

In modern terms, $A = s^2 k$ and $B = t^2 k$ (sides $s, sk$ and $t, tk$ in the figure), so the common factor $k$ cancels.

**Proof.** One mean @C falls between @A and @B ([[8.18]]). Take the least numbers @D, @E, @F in the ratio of $A, C, B$ ([[7.33]]). By the porism of [[8.2]], the extremes of a least progression of three terms are squares. And $D : F = A : B$ ex aequali. So $A : B$ is the ratio of the square $D$ to the square $F$.

Together with [[8.20]] and [[8.24]], this completes the picture for two numbers $a, b$:
$$\text{one mean between } a, b \iff a, b \text{ similar plane} \iff a : b = s^2 : t^2 \iff ab \text{ is a square}.$$
The last form is the modern one: $ab = (\text{mean})^2$.

:::leads Where it leads
[[10.9]] uses this proposition and [[8.11]] to show that two lengths are commensurable in square exactly when their squares are in the ratio of a square number to a square number. That is Euclid's version of "$\sqrt{n}$ is rational only when $n$ is a perfect square". Book IX uses it in [[9.10]].
:::
