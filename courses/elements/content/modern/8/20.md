---
title: One mean forces similar plane numbers
---
**Statement.** If one mean proportional falls between $A$ and $B$, then $A$ and $B$ are similar plane numbers. This is the converse of [[8.18]].

In modern terms, if $A : C = C : B$ with $A : C = d : e$ in lowest terms, then $A = m d^2$, $C = m d e$, $B = m e^2$. So $A = d \cdot (md)$ and $B = e \cdot (me)$ have proportional sides.

**Proof.** Let @D, @E be the least numbers in the ratio $A : C$ ([[7.33]]). They divide @A and @C the same number of times; call that number @F, so $A = D \cdot F$ ([[7.20]]). Since also $C : B = D : E$, the least numbers divide $C$ and @B the same number of times; call the number of times that $E$ divides $B$ @G, so $B = E \cdot G$.

It remains to see that the sides are proportional. $F$ multiplies $D$ and $E$ to give $A$ and $C$, so $D : E = A : C = C : B$. $E$ multiplies $F$ and $G$ to give $C$ and $B$, so $F : G = C : B$ ([[7.17]]). Hence $D : E = F : G$, and alternately $D : F = E : G$ ([[7.13]]).

:::gap A corrupted step
Heath notes that the text is corrupt at the step marked †. It infers $D : E = A : C$, which is part of the hypothesis. What it needs, and does not justify, is that $F \cdot E = C$. That follows because $E$ divides $C$ as many times as $D$ divides $A$, which is $F$ times. Heath suggests a replacement for the passage. The argument above uses the repaired version.
:::
