---
title: Squares and cubes of a progression
---
**Statement.** If $a, b, c$ are in continued proportion, so are their squares $a^2, b^2, c^2$ and their cubes $a^3, b^3, c^3$.

With a common ratio $r$, this is the statement that $a, ar, ar^2$ becomes $a^2, a^2 r^2, a^2 r^4$, a progression with ratio $r^2$. Euclid has no numbers for ratios, so he has to go the long way round.

**Proof.** Put @L $= A \cdot B$, @M $= A \cdot L$, @N $= B \cdot L$, and on the other side @O $= B \cdot C$, @P $= B \cdot O$, @Q $= C \cdot O$. As in [[8.2]] and [[8.12]]:

- @D, @L, @E and @G, @M, @N, @H are progressions in the ratio $A : B$;
- @E, @O, @F and @H, @P, @Q, @K are progressions in the ratio $B : C$.

Since $A : B = B : C$, the two chains have the same ratios step by step. Ex aequali ([[7.14]]), the first term is to the last in both chains alike: $D : E = E : F$ and $G : H = H : K$.

The figure draws the three groups to different scales, since the cubes quickly dwarf the numbers they come from.
