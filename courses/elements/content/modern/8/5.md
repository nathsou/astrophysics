---
title: The ratio of plane numbers
---
**Statement.** If $A = C \cdot D$ and $B = E \cdot F$, then $A : B$ is the ratio compounded of $C : E$ and $D : F$. In fractions:
$$\frac{CD}{EF} = \frac CE \cdot \frac DF.$$

Euclid cannot multiply ratios. A ratio for him is not a number. "Compounding" is his substitute: if $G : H = C : E$ and $H : K = D : F$, the ratio $G : K$ is the ratio *compounded* of $C : E$ and $D : F$. The shared middle term $H$ is what makes it work, and [[8.4]] provides it.

**Proof.** Let @G, @H, @K be the least numbers in the ratios $C : E$ and $D : F$ ([[8.4]]), and put @L $= D \cdot E$, a go-between that shares one factor with $A$ and one with $B$.

- $A = D \cdot C$ and $L = D \cdot E$, so $A : L = C : E = G : H$ ([[7.17]]).
- $L = E \cdot D$ and $B = E \cdot F$, so $L : B = D : F = H : K$ ([[7.17]]).

Ex aequali ([[7.14]]), $A : B = G : K$, which is the compounded ratio.

The same idea, with a go-between rectangle, is [[6.23]]: equiangular parallelograms have the ratio compounded of the ratios of their sides.

:::history
The definition of compounded ratio that appears in some manuscripts as VI. Def. 5 ([[6.def.5]]) is generally regarded as an interpolation. Euclid uses the notion here and in [[6.23]] without a definition.
:::
