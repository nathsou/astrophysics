---
title: Coprime extremes make a progression least
---
**Statement.** Let $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_n$ be in continued proportion, with $\gcd(a_0, a_n) = 1$. Then no progression of smaller numbers has the same ratios: $a_0, \dots, a_n$ are *the least* in their ratio.

**Proof.** Suppose @E, @F, @G, @H are less than @A, @B, @C, @D and in the same ratio. Taking the ratios in turn (ex aequali, [[7.14]]),
$$A : D = E : H.$$
$A$ and $D$ are prime to one another, so they are the least numbers in their ratio ([[7.21]]). The least numbers in a ratio divide every other pair in that ratio ([[7.20]]), so $A \mid E$. But $E < A$. So no such smaller numbers exist.

The only things the proof uses about the middle terms are that they exist and carry the same ratios. All the work is done by the two extremes.

**In the figure.** The sliders choose the ratio $a : b$ with $a, b$ prime to one another, and $A, B, C, D = a^3, a^2 b, a b^2, b^3$. The dashed rods are the smaller progression that the reductio supposes.

:::leads Least means primitive
Two progressions with the same ratios differ by a rational factor. So the least progression in a given ratio is the one whose terms have greatest common divisor $1$, a *primitive* progression, and every other progression in that ratio is a whole multiple of it. This proposition gives a quick test for primitivity: look at the extremes only. [[8.3]] proves the converse, and [[8.2]] shows what the least progression looks like.
:::
