---
title: One mean between similar plane numbers
---
**Statement.** Let $A = CD$ and $B = EF$ be *similar plane numbers*, that is, their sides are proportional, $C : D = E : F$ ([[7.def.21]]). Then one mean proportional falls between $A$ and $B$, and $A : B$ is the duplicate of the ratio $C : E$ of corresponding sides.

In modern terms, if $C : D = E : F$ then $CF = DE$, so
$$AB = CD \cdot EF = (DE)^2,$$
and $DE$ is the mean. Similar plane numbers are the numbers $k p q$ with the same "shape" $p : q$ at two scales $s$ and $t$: $A = s^2 pq$, $B = t^2 pq$.

**Proof.** Alternately, $C : E = D : F$ ([[7.13]]). Let @G $= D \cdot E$. Then:

- $D$ multiplies @C and @E to give @A and $G$, so $A : G = C : E$ ([[7.17]]).
- $E$ multiplies @D and @F to give $G$ and @B, so $G : B = D : F$ ([[7.17]]), which is again $C : E$.

So $A, G, B$ is a progression in the ratio $C : E$, and $A : B$ is that ratio duplicated ([[5.def.9]]). [[8.11]] is the special case of two squares.

The grey marks show each product as copies of one of its sides.
