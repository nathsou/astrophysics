---
title: Coprime ends of a progression are powers
---
**Statement.** If $a, b$ are prime to one another and $n$ numbers fall between them in continued proportion, then $n$ numbers also fall in continued proportion between the unit and each of $a$ and $b$.

In modern terms: the progression is $x^{n+1}, x^n y, \dots, y^{n+1}$, so $a = x^{n+1}$ and $b = y^{n+1}$. The means between $1$ and $a$ are $x, x^2, \dots, x^n$.

**Proof.** Build the least progressions in the ratio of @A, @C, @D, @B, by [[8.2]]: @F, @G; then @H, @K, @L; then @M, @N, @O, @P. $A$ and $B$ are prime to one another, so $A, C, D, B$ is already least ([[8.1]]), and it equals $M, N, O, P$. By the construction of [[8.2]], $H = F^2$ and $M = F \cdot H$.

Now compare with the unit @E. The unit measures $F$ as many times as there are units in $F$, and $F$ measures $H = F \cdot F$ the same number of times. So $E : F = F : H$ ([[7.def.20]]). Likewise $H$ measures $M = F \cdot H$ as many times as the unit measures $F$, so $E : F = H : M$, and $M = A$. So $E, F, H, A$ is a progression, that is $1, x, x^2, x^3 = A$. The same holds for $E, G, L, B$.

The hypothesis that $A, B$ are coprime matters. Between $16$ and $54$ two means fall ($24, 36$), but $16$ is not a cube. See [[8.8]].
