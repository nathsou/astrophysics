---
title: Interpolation depends only on the ratio
---
**Statement.** If $n$ numbers fall in continued proportion between $a$ and $b$, and $e : f = a : b$, then $n$ numbers fall in continued proportion between $e$ and $f$. ("Fall between" is Heath's rendering of a Greek verb meaning *can be interpolated*.)

**Proof.** Let @C, @D fall between @A and @B. Take the least numbers @G, @H, @K, @L in the ratio of $A, C, D, B$ ([[7.33]]). Their extremes are prime to one another ([[8.3]]), and ex aequali $G : L = A : B = E : F$ ([[7.14]]).

Since $G, L$ are prime to one another, they are the least in their ratio, and they divide @E and @F the same number of times, say $m$ ([[7.20]], [[7.21]]). Put @M $= m H$ and @N $= m K$. Then $E, M, N, F$ is $m$ times the progression $G, H, K, L$, so it is a progression with the same ratios.

In short: every progression is a multiple of the least one, and whether one fits between two numbers depends only on the least form of their ratio.

**Criterion.** Combining this with [[8.2]] and [[8.3]]: $n$ means fall between $a$ and $b$ exactly when, with $a : b = p : q$ in lowest terms, both $p$ and $q$ are $(n+1)$-th powers. Try it:

::mean-proportionals{a="16" b="54" n="2"}

$16 : 54 = 8 : 27 = 2^3 : 3^3$, so two means fit ($24, 36$), but one does not, since $8$ and $27$ are not squares.
