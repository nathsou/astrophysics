---
title: Squares fail to divide iff their sides fail
---
**Statement.** For numbers $c, d$:
$$c^2 \nmid d^2 \iff c \nmid d.$$

**Proof.** This is [[8.14]] negated on both sides. If @C divided @D, @A would divide @B; it does not, so $C \nmid D$. If $A$ divided $B$, $C$ would divide $D$; it does not, so $A \nmid B$.

Euclid states the contrapositive as a separate proposition because Book IX and Book X cite the negative form. With the default sliders, $16 \nmid 36$ and $4 \nmid 6$. The grey marks show copies of $A$ laid along $B$, and they do not end at its end. The two numbers share the factor $4$, and still neither square divides the other.
