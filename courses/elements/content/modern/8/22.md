---
title: A progression that starts with a square
---
**Statement.** If $a, b, c$ are in continued proportion and $a$ is a square, then $c$ is a square.

**Proof (Euclid's).** One mean, @B, falls between @A and @C, so they are similar plane numbers ([[8.20]]). $A$ is a square, so $C$ is a square.

:::gap The last step
"A is square, therefore C is also square" is not justified by what precedes it. It needs an argument such as this one. From $b^2 = ac$ and $a = s^2$ we get $c = (b/s)^2$. So $s^2 \mid b^2$, hence $s \mid b$ by [[8.14]], and $c$ is the square of the whole number $b/s$. Alternatively, write the progression as $m d^2, m de, m e^2$ with $d, e$ prime to one another, as in [[8.20]]. Then $m d^2$ is a square, so $m$ is a square (by unique factorization: every prime exponent of $m d^2$ is even, and those of $d^2$ are even), and so is $m e^2$.
:::

In the figure the sliders build $A, B, C = n^2 d^2,\; n^2 de,\; n^2 e^2$, so $A$ is always a square, and the readout shows the square root of $C$.
