---
title: Two means between similar solid numbers
---
**Statement.** Let $A = CDE$ and $B = FGH$ be *similar solid numbers*: $C : D = F : G$ and $D : E = G : H$ ([[7.def.21]]), or equivalently $C : F = D : G = E : H$. Then two mean proportionals fall between $A$ and $B$, and $A : B$ is the triplicate of the ratio of corresponding sides.

In modern terms, with sides $s(p, q, r)$ and $t(p, q, r)$, the numbers are $A = s^3 pqr$ and $B = t^3 pqr$, and the means are $s^2 t\, pqr$ and $s t^2 pqr$.

**Proof.** Put @K $= C \cdot D$ and @L $= F \cdot G$. These are similar plane numbers, so one mean @M $= D \cdot F$ falls between them ([[8.18]]), in the ratio $C : F$. Then multiply by the third sides: @N $= E \cdot M$ and @O $= H \cdot M$. Using [[7.17]] and [[7.18]]:

- $A = E \cdot K$ and $N = E \cdot M$, so $A : N = K : M = C : F$;
- $N : O = E : H$ ($E$ and $H$ both multiply $M$), which is also $C : F$;
- $O = H \cdot M$ and $B = H \cdot L$, so $O : B = M : L = C : F$.

So $A, N, O, B$ is a progression in the ratio of corresponding sides, and $A : B$ is that ratio triplicated ([[5.def.10]]). With the defaults, $16, 24, 36, 54$.
