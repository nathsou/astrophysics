---
title: Golden section, extended
---
**Statement.** If @AB is cut in extreme and mean ratio at @C (@AC greater) and @AD, equal to $AC$, is added beyond @A, then $DB$ is cut in extreme and mean ratio at $A$, with $AB$ the greater segment.

**Proof.** In the square @AE on $AB$, the rectangle @CE is $AB \cdot BC = AC^2$, and so is @HE. The square @figure(DH) on $AD$ equals the square @HC on $AC$. So the rectangle @figure(DK), which is $BD \cdot DA$ (its short side @DL equals $AD$), equals $DH + AK = HE + AK = AE = AB^2$. Hence $DB : BA = BA : AD$ ([[6.17]]).

In modern terms: if $a : b = \varphi$, then $(a + b) : a = \varphi$ too, because $\varphi = 1 + 1/\varphi$. Extending a golden cut by its greater segment gives another golden cut, and so on forever.

:::code A golden sequence
Starting from the two segments of a golden cut and adding repeatedly produces a geometric sequence:
```ts
let [a, b] = [1, 1 / PHI]; // greater, lesser segment
for (let i = 0; i < 5; i++) [a, b] = [a + b, a]; // XIII.5: still a golden cut
// a = PHI^5, b = PHI^4
```
With integer starting values the same rule $x_{n+1} = x_n + x_{n-1}$ gives the Fibonacci numbers, whose ratios approach $\varphi$.
:::
