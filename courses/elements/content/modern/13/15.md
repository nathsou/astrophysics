---
title: The cube in a sphere
---
**Statement.** Construct a cube inscribed in a sphere of diameter $d$, and show that
$$d^2 = 3\,e^2, \qquad e = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\, d \approx 0.5774\, d.$$

**Construction.** Cut the diameter @AB at @C with $AC = 2CB$, draw the semicircle @circle(ADB) and the perpendicular @CD, and join @DB. Set out the square @figure(EFGH) with side $DB$, and at its corners set up @EK, @FL, @GM, @HN perpendicular to it and equal to its side. This is the cube @solid(FN).

**Proof.** *It fits the sphere.* The edge @KE is perpendicular to the base, so the angle $KEG$ is right, and $E$ lies on the sphere with diameter @KG ([[3.31]]). The edge $GF$ is perpendicular to the face containing $F$, $E$, $K$, $L$, so the angle $KFG$ is right too, and so on for every corner: all eight lie on the sphere with diameter $KG$.

*The ratio.* $EG^2 = 2EF^2$ and $KG^2 = EG^2 + EK^2 = 3EK^2$. On the other side, $AB : BC = 3 : 1$, so $AB^2 = 3BD^2$ ([[6.8]]). With $EK = DB$, $KG = AB$.

This is the three-dimensional Pythagorean theorem: the space diagonal of a box with edges $a, b, c$ has $\delta^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2$, here $3e^2$.

**In the figure.** Turn the cube; the dashed diagonals are @KG, @EG and @FK. The claims check that all eight corners lie on the sphere and that the twelve edges are equal.

:::leads Where it leads
[[13.17]] builds the dodecahedron on this cube: its twenty vertices are the cube's eight corners and twelve more points, one pair above each face, and they all lie on the same sphere.
:::
