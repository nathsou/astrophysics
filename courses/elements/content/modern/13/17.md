---
title: The dodecahedron on the cube
---
**Statement.** Construct a regular dodecahedron inscribed in a sphere of diameter $d$. Its edge is the greater segment of the cube's edge cut in extreme and mean ratio, and it is an *apotome*:
$$e = \frac{a}{\varphi} = \frac{\sqrt{15} - \sqrt3}{6}\, d \approx 0.3568\, d,$$
where $a = d/\sqrt3$ is the edge of the cube in the same sphere.

**Construction.** Start from the cube of [[13.15]], with centre @Z. On two adjacent faces @figure(ABCD) and @figure(CBEF), bisect the edges and draw the midlines: they meet at the face centres @Q and @P. Cut @NP, @PO and @HQ in extreme and mean ratio at @R, @S, @T (greater segments $RP$, $PS$, $QT$), and raise @RU, @SV, @TW perpendicular to the faces, outward, equal to those greater segments. The pentagon @figure(UBWCV) stands on the edge @BC. Do the same on all twelve edges.

**Proof.** Write $a$ for the edge of the cube.

1. *Equilateral.* $BU^2 = BN^2 + NR^2 + RU^2 = 4\,RU^2$ by [[13.4]], so $BU = 2RU = UV$. Similarly for the other sides.
2. *Plane.* Take @X on the axis through @P with $PX = QT$. The triangles $XPH$ and $HTW$ have proportional sides and parallel legs, so @XH and @HW are in a straight line ([[6.32]]), and the pentagon lies in one plane.
3. *Equiangular.* By [[13.5]] and [[13.4]], $BV = BC$, so the triangles $BUV$ and $BWC$ have three equal sides and $\angle BUV = \angle BWC$; likewise $\angle UVC$. Three equal angles suffice ([[13.7]]).
4. *In the sphere.* $UZ^2 = ZX^2 + XU^2 = 3\,NP^2$ by [[13.4]] again, which is the square on the radius of the cube's sphere ([[13.15]]).
5. *The edge.* $UV = RS$, and doubling the golden cut of $NP$ at $R$ shows that $RS$ is the greater segment of $NO = a$ ([[5.15]]); and the cube's edge is rational when the diameter is, so $UV$ is an apotome ([[13.6]]).

**In the figure.** The shaded face is $UBWCV$ on the edge $BC$; the thin outlines are the other eleven faces, one on each edge of the cube (dashed). The claims check that there are twelve pentagonal faces, that all twenty vertices lie on the sphere and all thirty edges are equal, and that $XHW$ is straight.

:::leads Where it leads
The dodecahedron is the dual of the icosahedron, as the octahedron is of the cube, and the tetrahedron is its own dual. Its twenty vertices are the cube's eight, $(\pm1, \pm1, \pm1)$, and twelve of the form $(0, \pm\tfrac1\varphi, \pm\varphi)$ and cyclic permutations. Euclid's "roof" over each face of the cube is exactly these extra points, two per face.
:::
