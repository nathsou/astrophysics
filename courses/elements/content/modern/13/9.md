---
title: Hexagon plus decagon is a golden cut
---
**Statement.** In one circle, put the side @BC of the inscribed decagon and the side @CD of the inscribed hexagon in a straight line. Then the whole @BD is cut in extreme and mean ratio at @C, and $CD$, the hexagon's side (equal to the radius), is the greater segment.

**Proof.** Let @E be the centre, and extend $BE$ to @A. The arc $BC$ is a tenth of the circle, so $\angle CEB = 36°$ and $\angle AEC = 144°$, four times as much ([[6.33]]). In the isosceles triangle $EBC$, $\angle ECB = 72°$; in the isosceles triangle $ECD$ ($EC = CD$, both equal to the radius, [[4.15]]), $\angle EDC = 36°$. So $\angle EDC = \angle BEC$, and the triangles $EBD$ and $CBE$, sharing the angle at $B$, are similar. Hence $DB : BE = EB : BC$, and with $BE = CD$, $BD : DC = DC : CB$.

In numbers, with radius 1: the decagon's side is $\frac{\sqrt5 - 1}{2} = 1/\varphi$, so $BD = 1 + 1/\varphi = \varphi$, and $\varphi : 1 = 1 : 1/\varphi$.

:::leads Where it leads
With [[13.10]] this builds the icosahedron ([[13.16]]): the diameter of its sphere is a hexagon side plus two decagon sides.
:::
