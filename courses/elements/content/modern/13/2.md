---
title: Golden section, converse of the first lemma
---
**Statement.** If the square on @AB is five times the square on its part @AC, and @CD is twice $AC$, then $CD$ is cut in extreme and mean ratio at @B, with @CB the greater segment.

**Proof.** The converse of [[13.1]], with the same figure read backwards. The square @figure(AF) on $AB$ is five times the square @AH on $AC$, so the gnomon @MNO is four times $AH$, which is the square @CG on $CD = 2AC$. The pieces of the gnomon are rearranged as in [[13.1]], and what is left is $HF = BG$, that is, $CB^2 = CD \cdot DB$. So $DC : CB = CB : BD$ ([[6.17]]), and since $DC > CB$, also $CB > BD$.

The lemma checks that $B$ really lies between $C$ and $D$, that is, $2AC > CB$: if $CB$ were $2AC$ or more, then $AB^2 = (AC + CB)^2$ would exceed $CB^2 + AC^2 \ge 5AC^2$ ([[2.4]]), against the hypothesis.

In numbers: with $AC = 1$, $AB = \sqrt5$, $CB = \sqrt5 - 1$, $CD = 2$, and $\frac{2}{\sqrt5 - 1} = \frac{\sqrt5 + 1}{2} = \varphi$.
