---
title: Golden section, first lemma
---
**Statement.** If @AB is cut in extreme and mean ratio at @C, with @AC the greater segment, and @AD is half of $AB$ laid off beyond @A, then
$$CD^2 = 5\,AD^2.$$

**Proof.** The golden cut means $AB \cdot BC = AC^2$ ([[6.def.3]], [[6.17]]). Draw the square $AE$ on $AB$ and the square $DF$ on $DC$, divided by the lines through $A$ and @H into the square on $AD$, the square @FH on $AC$ and two equal rectangles. The rectangle $CE$ is $AB \cdot BC$, so it equals the square $FH$. Since $AB = 2AD$, the rectangle $CK$ (sides $AC$ and $AB$) is twice $CH$ (sides $AC$ and $AD$), that is, it equals the two rectangles @LH and @HC. So the whole square $AE$ equals the gnomon @MNO, and the square $DF$ is the gnomon plus the square on $AD$: $AB^2 + AD^2 = 4AD^2 + AD^2$.

With $AB = 1$: $AC = x$ satisfies $x^2 = 1 - x$, so $x = \frac{\sqrt5 - 1}{2}$, and $CD = x + \tfrac12 = \frac{\sqrt5}{2}$, whose square is $\tfrac54 = 5 \cdot \tfrac14$. This is completing the square: $x^2 + x = 1$ becomes $(x + \tfrac12)^2 = \tfrac54$.

**In the figure.** Drag @A and @B. The gnomon (shaded) always equals the square on $AB$, and the readouts check $CD^2 = 5AD^2$.

:::leads Where it leads
[[13.2]] is the converse, and [[13.11]] uses this lemma to identify the side of the pentagon as a *minor* line. The identity $(x + \tfrac12)^2 = \tfrac54$ is the source of every $\sqrt5$ in the book.
:::
