---
title: Golden section, second lemma
---
**Statement.** If @AB is cut in extreme and mean ratio at @C (@AC greater) and @D bisects $AC$, then
$$BD^2 = 5\,DC^2.$$
The lesser segment plus half the greater behaves like the greater plus half the whole in [[13.1]].

**Proof.** In the square on $AB$, draw the diagonal from @B and the lines through $C$ and $D$ and their mirror images ("the figure drawn double"). The rectangle @figure(CE) is $AB \cdot BC = AC^2$, and $AC^2$ is four times $DC^2$, the small square @figure(FG). Rearranging the complements ($CG = MF = FE$), the gnomon @OPQ equals $CE$, so it is four times @figure(FG), and the square @figure(DN) on $DB$, which is the gnomon plus $FG$, is five times $FG$.

With $AB = 1$: $AC = \frac{\sqrt5 - 1}{2}$, $DC = \frac{\sqrt5 - 1}{4}$ and $BD = 1 - DC = \frac{5 - \sqrt5}{4}$. Then $BD^2 = \frac{30 - 10\sqrt5}{16} = 5 \cdot \frac{6 - 2\sqrt5}{16} = 5\,DC^2$.

**In the figure.** Drag @A and @B: the shaded gnomon and square always make up the square on $DB$.

:::leads Where it leads
[[13.16]] uses this lemma to show that the diameter of the icosahedron's sphere is $\sqrt5$ times the radius of the circle it is built on.
:::
