---
title: Three equal angles make an equilateral pentagon regular
---
**Statement.** If an equilateral pentagon has three equal angles, whether consecutive or not, it is equiangular, hence regular.

**Proof.** *Consecutive angles at $A$, $B$, $C$.* The triangles @triangle(ABC) and @triangle(ABE) have two equal sides and the equal angles at $B$ and $A$, so they are congruent ([[1.4]]): @AC $=$ @BE, $\angle BCA = \angle BEA$, and $\angle BAC = \angle ABE$. The last equality makes the triangle $ABF$ isosceles, so @AF $=$ @BF ([[1.6]]), and subtracting, $FC = FE$. Now the triangles $FCD$ and $FED$ have three equal sides, so $\angle FCD = \angle FED$ ([[1.8]]). Adding, $\angle BCD = \angle AED$, so the angle at $E$ equals the angles at $A, B, C$. The same argument from the other end gives the angle at $D$.

*Angles at $A$, $C$, $D$.* Join @BD. The triangles $BAE$ and $BCD$ are congruent (SAS), so $BE = BD$ and $\angle AEB = \angle CDB$; the isosceles triangle $BED$ gives $\angle BED = \angle BDE$, and adding, $\angle AED = \angle CDE$.

**In the figure.** The pentagon is built with equal sides and equal angles θ at @A and @B only. For every θ, Euclid's intermediate steps hold ($AC = BE$, $FC = FE$, $\angle BCD = \angle AED$); the angle at @C varies. Only at θ = 108°, when the angle at $C$ is also θ, does the pentagon become regular.

:::leads Where it leads
Three is the right number. An equilateral pentagon of given side has two degrees of freedom: five edge directions, less one for turning the whole figure and two for making it close up. Two equal angles leave one free (the figure's slider), and a third equal angle fixes it. [[13.17]] uses this proposition: it checks only three angles of the pentagon on each edge of the cube.
:::
