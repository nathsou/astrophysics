---
title: The icosahedron in a sphere
---
**Statement.** Construct a regular icosahedron inscribed in a sphere of diameter $d$. Its edge is the irrational line called *minor*:
$$e = d\sqrt{\frac{5 - \sqrt5}{10}} \approx 0.5257\, d.$$
The porism: $d^2 = 5r^2$, where $r$ is the radius of the circle the icosahedron is built on, and $d$ is one side of the hexagon plus two sides of the decagon in that circle.

**Construction.** Cut @AB at @C with $AC = 4CB$, and take $r = $ @DB, so that $AB^2 = 5\,DB^2$. In the circle @circle(EFGHK) of radius $r$ and centre @V, inscribe the regular pentagon @figure(EFGHK), and let @L, @M, @N, @O, @P halve its arcs; @EP is a side of the decagon. Raise @EQ, @FR, @GS, @HT, @KU perpendicular to the circle, each equal to $r$. On the axis, set up @VW $= r$, then @WZ and, below, @VX, each a side of the decagon.

**Proof.** The whole argument is [[13.10]] used four times: *a hexagon side and a decagon side at right angles make a pentagon side.*

- *Upper and lower pentagons.* $QRSTU$ is congruent to $EFGHK$, and $LMNOP$ is a regular pentagon in the same circle: all their sides are pentagon sides.
- *The band.* $QE = r$ (hexagon) is perpendicular to $EP$ (decagon), so $QP$ is a pentagon side. The ten triangles such as $QLR$ and $QLP$ between the two pentagons are equilateral.
- *The caps.* $QW = EV = r$ is perpendicular to $WZ$ (decagon), so $QZ$ is a pentagon side, and likewise $LX$. The five triangles at @Z and the five at @X are equilateral.

That makes twenty equilateral triangles, five at each vertex.

*It fits the sphere.* $VW$ (hexagon) and $WZ$ (decagon) together form a golden cut of $VZ$ ([[13.9]]), which makes the angles at $E$ and $Q$ in the triangles on @XZ right, so every vertex lies on the sphere with diameter $XZ$. By [[13.3]], $XZ^2 = 5\,VW^2 = 5r^2 = AB^2$.

*The edge is minor.* The diameter is rational, so $r = d/\sqrt5$ is rational in square, the circle's diameter is rational, and the edge is the side of the pentagon in it: minor by [[13.11]].

**In the figure.** The circle $EFGHK$ is horizontal; the pentagon $QRSTU$ floats above it at height $r$, and $LMNOP$ lies in it. The diameter @AB is laid out below. Turn the figure: the claims check that all twelve vertices lie on the sphere and all thirty edges are equal.

:::leads Where it leads
In coordinates, the twelve vertices are the cyclic permutations of $(0, \pm1, \pm\varphi)$: three golden rectangles at right angles. That description is far simpler than Euclid's, but it takes the golden ratio as a number, which Euclid does not have; he has to build it from circles, and [[13.9]] and [[13.10]] are how he does it.
:::
