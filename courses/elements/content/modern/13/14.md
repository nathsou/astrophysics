---
title: The octahedron in a sphere
---
**Statement.** Construct a regular octahedron inscribed in a sphere of diameter $d$, and show that
$$d^2 = 2\,e^2, \qquad e = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\, d \approx 0.7071\, d.$$

**Construction.** Bisect the diameter @AB at @C, draw the semicircle @circle(ADB) and the perpendicular @CD, and join @DB. Set out the square @figure(EFGH) with side $DB$; its diagonals meet at @K. At $K$ set up @KL and @KM perpendicular to the square, on both sides, each equal to $KE$. Join $L$ and $M$ to the four corners.

**Proof.** *The faces are equilateral.* $KE = KH$ and the angle $EKH$ is right, so $HE^2 = 2EK^2$ ([[1.47]]). Also $LK = KE$ with a right angle, so $LE^2 = 2EK^2$. Hence $LE = EH$, and each of the eight triangles such as @triangle(LEH) is equilateral.

*It fits the sphere.* The six points are all at distance $KE$ from $K$, so the sphere with diameter @LM passes through them all.

*The ratio.* $LM^2 = 2LE^2$ (the angle $LEM$ is in a semicircle), and $AB^2 = 2DB^2$ because $AB = 2BC$ ([[6.8]]). With $LE = DB$, $LM = AB$ and $d^2 = 2e^2$.

In coordinates: the six points $(\pm1, 0, 0)$, $(0, \pm1, 0)$, $(0, 0, \pm1)$, with edge $\sqrt2$ and diameter $2$.

**In the figure.** The square @figure(EFGH) is horizontal, with @L above and @M below. Turn the figure: the claims check that the six vertices lie on the sphere and the twelve edges are equal.

:::leads Where it leads
The octahedron is the *dual* of the cube: put a vertex at the centre of each face of a cube and join neighbours, and you get an octahedron (and the other way round). The coordinate descriptions show it: the cube's face centres are exactly $(\pm1,0,0)$, $(0,\pm1,0)$, $(0,0,\pm1)$.
:::
