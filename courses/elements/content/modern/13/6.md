---
title: The golden segments are irrational
---
**Statement.** If a rational line @AB is cut in extreme and mean ratio at @C, each of the segments @AC and @CB is an *apotome*: an irrational line of Book X.

**Proof.** Take $AB = 1$ as the rational unit. Add @AD $= \tfrac12$ beyond @A. By [[13.1]], $CD^2 = 5\,DA^2 = \tfrac54$, so $CD = \frac{\sqrt5}{2}$. Its square is rational, but $5$ is not a square number, so $CD$ is incommensurable in length with $DA$ ([[10.9]]): the two are "rational straight lines commensurable in square only". Their difference
$$AC = CD - DA = \frac{\sqrt5}{2} - \frac12$$
is by definition an apotome ([[10.73]]). For $CB$: $CB = AC^2 / AB$, and the square of an apotome applied to a rational line gives a *first apotome* as breadth ([[10.97]]): $CB = \frac{3 - \sqrt5}{2} = \frac32 - \frac{\sqrt5}{2}$.

**In the figure.** The slider makes $AB$ a whole number of units. However the unit is chosen, the readout $CD : DA = \sqrt5$ is not a ratio of whole numbers, so $AC$ and $CB$ never fall on the unit marks.

:::leads Where it leads
This is the first place Book X's classification is used for a named figure. [[13.11]] and [[13.16]] show the side of the icosahedron is a *minor* line, and [[13.17]] that the side of the dodecahedron is an apotome, using this proposition: the dodecahedron's edge is the greater segment of the cube's edge.
:::
