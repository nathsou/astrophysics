---
title: The product of similar plane numbers is a square
---
**Statement.** If $A = ab$ and $B = cd$ are *similar plane numbers* ([[7.def.21]]), meaning that their sides are proportional, $a : b = c : d$, then $AB$ is a square.

**Modern proof.** $a : b = c : d$ means $ad = bc$. So $AB = (ad)(bc) = (ad)^2$.

**Euclid's proof.** Euclid argues with means instead of sides. Let @D $= A^2$.

1. $A : B = A^2 : AB$, that is, $A : B = D : C$ ([[7.17]]).
2. Similar plane numbers have a mean proportional between them ([[8.18]]). In the default figure, $6$ and $24$ have the mean $12$.
3. Numbers in the same ratio have the same number of means between them ([[8.8]]). So one mean falls between @D and @C.
4. $D$ is a square. If three numbers are in continued proportion and the first is a square, so is the third ([[8.22]]). So $C$ is a square.

In the figure, the rods are drawn on a logarithmic scale and cut at their prime factors. Squares are the rods whose pieces come in pairs.
