---
title: A composite times a number is solid
---
**Statement.** If $A$ is composite and $B$ is any number, then $AB$ is a solid number: a product of three numbers ([[7.def.17]]).

**Proof.** $A$ is composite, so some number @D divides it ([[7.def.13]]), say $A = D \cdot E$ with @E the quotient. Then @C $= AB = D \cdot E \cdot B$, with sides $D$, $E$, $B$.

Every side is at least $2$, because $D$ and $E$ are proper divisors of $A$ and $B$ is a number. That is the point of "composite". A prime times $B$ has only two sides.
