---
title: Powers divide powers by powers
---
**Statement.** In $1, B, C, D, E, \dots$ (continued proportion from the unit @A), a smaller term divides a larger one, and the quotient is again a term of the sequence: $b^m \mid b^n$ with quotient $b^{n-m}$.

**Proof.** $A : B = D : E$. Alternately ([[7.15]]), $A : D = B : E$. The unit measures $D$ exactly $D$ times, so @B measures @E exactly $D$ times: $E = B \cdot D$.

**Porism.** The quotient's place counted from the unit equals the divisor's place counted back from the dividend. $B$ (first) measures $E$ according to $D$ (first before $E$), and $C$ (second) measures $E$ according to $C$ (second before $E$). In exponents, $b^4 = b^1 \cdot b^3 = b^2 \cdot b^2$.
