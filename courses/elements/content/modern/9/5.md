---
title: A cube that multiplies into a cube multiplies a cube
---
**Statement.** If $A$ is a cube and $AB$ is a cube, then $B$ is a cube.

**Proof.** Let @D $= A^2$, a cube ([[9.3]]). Then $A : B = D : C$ with @C $= AB$ ([[7.17]]). $D$ and $C$ are cubes, hence similar solid numbers, so two means fall between them ([[8.19]]). The same number of means falls between $A$ and $B$ ([[8.8]]). $A$ is a cube, so $B$ is a cube ([[8.23]]).

**In modern terms.** $B = (c/a)^3$ is the cube of a fraction. The theorem says that if the cube of a fraction is a whole number, the fraction is a whole number. That is true because of unique factorization: every exponent in the factorization of $B$ is a multiple of $3$.
