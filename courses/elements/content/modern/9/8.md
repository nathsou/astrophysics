---
title: Squares and cubes among the powers
---
**Statement.** Let $1, A, B, C, \dots$ be in continued proportion. Then the terms are the powers $a, a^2, a^3, \dots$ of $a = A$, and:

- every second term ($a^2, a^4, a^6, \dots$) is a square;
- every third term ($a^3, a^6, \dots$) is a cube;
- every sixth term ($a^6, a^{12}, \dots$) is both.

**Proof.** $1 : A = A : B$ means that $A$ divides $B$ exactly $A$ times, so @B $= A^2$, a square. Then $B, C, D$ are in continued proportion with $B$ a square, so @D is a square ([[8.22]]), and so on along the sequence. Similarly $1 : A = B : C$ gives @C $= A \cdot B = A^3$, a cube, and [[8.23]] passes this on to @F. @F, the seventh term counting the unit, is both.

The figure draws the powers on a logarithmic scale, where continued proportion becomes equal steps: $a^k$ is $k$ copies of $a$ laid end to end.
