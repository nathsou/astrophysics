---
title: Other even numbers are both
---
**Statement.** If @A is not a power of 2 and its half is not odd, then $A$ is both even-times even and even-times odd.

**Proof.** Its half is even, so $A = 2 \cdot (A/2)$ is even-times even. Now halve $A$ repeatedly. If this ever reached 2, then $A$ would be a power of 2, contrary to the hypothesis. So it reaches an odd number $m > 1$ first, and $A = 2^k m$ with $2^k$ even: $A$ is even-times odd too.

In modern terms: $n = 2^k m$ with $k \ge 2$ and $m$ odd, $m > 1$. The bisection loop is how one computes $k$ and $m$ (in binary, strip the trailing zeros). The rods below @A are the successive halves.
