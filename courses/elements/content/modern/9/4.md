---
title: The product of two cubes is a cube
---
**Statement.** If $A$ and $B$ are cubes, so is $AB$. In exponents: $a^3 b^3 = (ab)^3$.

**Proof.** Let @D $= A^2$, a cube by [[9.3]]. Then $A : B = D : C$, where @C $= AB$ ([[7.17]]). Two cubes are similar solid numbers, so two means fall between @A and @B ([[8.19]]), and therefore also between @D and @C ([[8.8]]). Since $D$ is a cube, so is $C$ ([[8.23]]).

The same pattern runs through [[9.1]]–[[9.6]]. Euclid turns a statement about products into one about ratios with [[7.17]], moves the mean proportionals across with [[8.8]], and reads off the conclusion with [[8.22]] or [[8.23]].
