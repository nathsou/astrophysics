---
title: Powers of two are even-times even only
---
**Statement.** Each of $4, 8, 16, \dots$ (doubled in turn from the dyad $2$) is *even-times even only*: whenever it is written as a product $d \cdot q$ of two numbers, both $d$ and $q$ are even ([[7.def.8]]).

**Proof.** @A $= 2$ is prime, so by [[9.13]] the only divisors of @D $= 2^k$ are the powers of 2 before it, @A, @B, @C (and the unit). All of these are even, and so is every quotient.

[[9.32]]–[[9.34]] classify the even numbers $n = 2^k m$ (with $m$ odd) by the exponent $k$: here $m = 1$; in [[9.33]] $k = 1$; in [[9.34]] $k \ge 2$ and $m > 1$. Euclid's definitions of "even-times even" and "even-times odd" overlap (24 is both), which is why he needs the word "only".
